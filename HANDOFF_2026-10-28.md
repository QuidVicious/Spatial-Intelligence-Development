## The thread to continue: Google Search grounding and an evidence panel

**Finding, corrected 28 Oct evening (Don checked the record): the 1966 search grounding was a deliberate test, not a contaminated result.**
- The source run is from **7 July 2026 in AI Studio, with no grounding at all**. It produced the "Auld Reekie" patina (period soot on the stone) without naming the Clean Air Act or the words "Auld Reekie". That is the pure LSA result.
- The 1966 session ("View out of the window from the piano nobile, 15 October 1966") was one of many later runs with grounding on and off, built to answer a specific question: Don had found the Clean Air Act and the cleaning himself, so would Search grounding find it too? Don's name for it: the "If I know, Google knows" test. With Search on, it did find the Clean Air Act. The bracketed numbers [1] to [8] are its search citations.
- So the pure claim rests on the 7 July run, and the 1966 run is evidence about what Search adds (named facts and dates, with citations). It is not a flaw in the method.
- Page consequence: the 1966 example is still a grounded run, so its caption must say "Google Search grounding was on for this test" and present it as the comparison. Consider leading with the 7 July run as the pure example. The rerun with Search off is no longer needed.

**Don's observation (log this as its own finding; it shapes the design):** the search citations were often what Don used to find more information himself. They were research leads he followed, not just a check on the model, and more useful than running his own Google search. Grounding gave him sources to read; the model's own knowledge gives none (and when asked to cite, it invents convincing citations: see the confidence finding in the resume and logs).

**Don's earlier argument for grounding:** "If I can find it with Google, the model already has it, so it should find it faster and better." Claude's pushback: Search retrieves today's web pages (their errors, their present tense, other people's descriptions of the place); the model's knowledge is what it absorbed in training. They overlap but are different sources, so Search belongs as evidence, not inside the engine.

**The earlier argument against grounding:** it "stomps on the engine". Miller's Row is the named failure. DECISIONS.md (around line 363) files it as "Google's version of the place overriding the one Don defined", in the same entry as the Street View pano pulling the render toward its own camera position, which gave the rule "Cesium sets the frame; Street View is evidence for detail only". So the record ties Miller's Row to Google's imagery (Maps and Street View) more than to Search, but it never says "search" or "maps" outright: likely, not certain.

**Design direction agreed (28 Oct): the evidence panel, with the director as chair.**
- Search is used again, under the same condition as every other source: it proposes, the director confirms. Its citations are shown, because they are research leads (Don's observation).
- Sources contest; only the director decides. LSA does NOT get a veto over Search: the logs show the model cannot separate what it knows from what it has seen often and invents citations, while a cited search result can be checked. LSA can flag a conflict ("Search says X; what I know of this kind of place says Y"), and the flag goes to the director.
- Authority by type of claim, not one vote each:
  - geometry, framing, what stands where: Cesium 3D tiles;
  - what it looks like now: Street View, read for detail;
  - dates, history, records, named things (the Clean Air Act): Search, with its citations;
  - materials, weathering, plants, how things age: LSA;
  - everything, finally: the director.
- When two sources disagree, the one with authority over that type of claim leads the proposal, the other's view is shown beside it, and the director decides.
- It reuses an existing pattern: the Street View check already reports "where the pano seems to disagree (not applied)". The panel is that report with more voices.
- UI cost: one combined Conflicts list sorted by importance, not a report per source.
- The Evidence card (B5, built 24 Oct) is source-neutral, so Search results can enter it as items with their citations.
