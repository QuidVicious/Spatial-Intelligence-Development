### STAGE 2 & 3: ARCHAEOLOGICAL EXCAVATION & CROSS-DOMAIN SYNERGY

#### 1. Petrology & Pavement Geology (The Ground & Wall Planes)
*   **Terrace Facades:** Constructed from **Craigleith Sandstone** (a hard, pale cream to buff, silica-cemented Carboniferous sandstone quarried nearby) with secondary elements in darker **Hailes Sandstone**. Craigleith is renowned for its quartz-pure matrix, yet under micro-scale inspection, the north-west-facing surfaces exposed to prevailing damp exhibit fine surface granular fretting and granular relief where lime mortars have leached.
*   **Pavements:** Paved in dark, cleaved **Caithness flagstone** slabs (Devonian lacustrine siltstones/sandstones) laid with tight joins. Their micro-rough, cleaved bedding planes are slick with a continuous water film.
*   **Carriageway:** Historic gutters retain **quartz-dolerite ("whinstone")** setts—dense, bluish-grey, igneous stone with rounded shoulders worn smooth by two centuries of iron-shod cart wheels and automotive friction, framed by dressed granite kerbs.

#### 2. Georgian Neoclassical Engineering & Ironwork
*   **Curved Facade Geometry:** James Gillespie Graham’s design (1822) executes a sweeping segmental arc. Rusticated ground-floor masonry features chamfered horizontal V-joints and round-headed openings, transitioning to smooth ashlar on the piano nobile with Greek Doric order porticos.
*   **Ironwork & Lead Caulking:** Continuous cast- and wrought-iron balustrades—featuring classical spearhead and urn finials—enclose the sunken basement areaways. The vertical balusters are bedded into droved sandstone coping stones via molten lead sockets. The mechanical expansion of iron oxidation (rust jacking) has induced hairline fractures in the coping stones at several baluster bases, leaching narrow rust-coloured ferric trails downward across the stone plinth.

#### 3. Atmospheric Chemistry & Pollution Patina
*   **Coal Smoke Legacy:** Ainslie Place preserves the chemical archive of Edinburgh’s "Auld Reekie" coal-burning era. Sandstone under projecting cornices, dentils, window pediments, and platbands forms stable, gypsum-bound **black soot crusts** (soot-gypsum calcification). In contrast, vertical surfaces exposed to direct, wind-driven winter rain reveal pale, salt-scoured wash zones, creating sharp tonal contrast between sheltered carbon encrustations and exposed mineral faces.

#### 4. Winter Urban Epiphytic Ecology
*   **Trees of Ainslie Place Garden:** A dormant canopy of mature **Common Lime (*Tilia × europaea*)**, **Sycamore (*Acer pseudoplatanus*)**, and **Wych Elm (*Ulmus glabra*)**. 
*   **Directional Bio-patina:** Because the standpoint faces South-East (131°), the viewer looks directly at the **north-west-facing aspect** of all surfaces. These faces bear the brunt of moist Atlantic westerlies. Tree trunks are not plain grey: their fissured bark is densely mapped with vivid emerald-green powdery coats of the aerophytic green alga ***Desmococcus olivaceus*** (formerly *Pleurococcus*), interspersed with patches of grey-green foliose lichens (***Physcia tenella***, ***Xanthoria parietina***) and dark cushions of urban-tolerant mosses (***Grimmia pulvinata***, ***Hypnum cupressiforme***) gripping the wet bark ridges and the damp stone garden boundary plinth.
