### STAGE 2: DOMAIN KEY SELECTION & ACTIVATION

*   **Geology & Lithology Lens (Edinburgh Carboniferous Sandstone):** The buildings of the Moray Estate (engineered c. 1822) utilize Carboniferous Craigleith and Hailes sandstone. *   **Historical & Civil Engineering Lens (James Gillespie Graham's Neo-Classical Grammar):** Ainslie Place features a grand curved crescent with a dominant central pavilion: rusticated ashlar ground level, V-shaped rustication joints, piano nobile with giant-order attached columns, and dressed stringcourses. *   **Botanical & Phenological Ecology Lens (Mid-October Microclimate):** The oval garden basin captures damp air. Turf is *Lolium perenne* and *Poa trivialis*, uneven, heavily saturated, with visible earthworm casts.
*   **Chemical Weathering & Atmospheric Degradation Lens:** Two centuries of "Auld Reekie" bituminous coal smoke leave thick, black carbon-gypsum crusts in sheltered undercut moldings (under cornices, window pediments, and V-grooves), contrasting with rain-washed, pale buff-grey projecting stones. Iron-jacking occurs where cast-iron balusters enter the coping stone: oxidized iron expands by up to 600%, causing vertical fractures in the sandstone copestones.

### STAGE 3: CROSS-DOMAIN SYNERGY ANALYSIS
*   **Rain Saturation & Lithic Refractive Index:** Under rainfall, the high porosity of Craigleith sandstone causes light to penetrate the surface water layer, dramatically dropping its diffuse reflectance and shifting its perceived color from pale matte cement-grey/buff to a deep, dark slate-ochre. Runoff water concentrates along historic sooty drip lines, turning vertical tracks nearly charcoal-black.
*   **South-East Exposure Epiphytes:** Looking north-westward, the camera captures the south-east facing faces of objects. The trunks and stone dwarf wall receive the majority of solar light earlier in the day, fostering localized crustose lichens (*Lecanora dispersa*) and patches of *Xanthoria parietina* higher on dry bark, but at low levels, continuous damp from the saturated lawn produces a vivid green film of *Desmococcus olivaceus* micro-algae on the lowest 30 cm of trunks and stone copings.
*   **Lead-Iron Galvanic Action:** Rainwater acts as an electrolyte between the lead plugs and iron uprights of the boundary fence, yielding orange-brown ferric oxide staining that bleeds downward over the grey sandstone plinth.
