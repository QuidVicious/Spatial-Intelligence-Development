### STAGE 2: DOMAIN KEY SELECTION & ACTIVATION

#### 1. Petrography & Structural Geology Lens
* **Bedrock & Quarry Source:** The crescent is built primarily of Craigleith sandstone (a highly cemented, quartz-rich Carboniferous arenite with secondary silica cementation), interspersed with subsidiary Hailes sandstone for basement areas. 
* **Surface Response to Moisture:** Dry Craigleith is pale grayish-buff; when saturated at 100% surface moisture during Edinburgh rainfall, its refractive index shifts dramatically, rendering the stone a deep, variegated slate-brown and soot-ochre. 
* **Paving Stratigraphy:** The carriageway margin and gutter feature dense, crystalline olivine-basalt (whinstone) setts bedded in sand-lime mortar, bounded by fine-grained pinkish-grey Corstorphine or Dalbeattie granite kerbstones.

#### 2. Late-Georgian Civil Engineering Lens (Moray Estate, c. 1822–1830)
* **Master Plan Logic:** Designed by James Gillespie Graham under the 10th Earl of Moray. The crescent uses a palatial neoclassical elevation: rusticated V-jointed channel ashlar at ground level, smooth-faced ashlar at the piano nobile and attic storeys, terminated by a continuous blocking course.
* **Fenestration & Ironwork:** Deep-set six-over-six and twelve-pane sash windows with fine timber astragals. The transition from sidewalk to facade is defended by deep sunken basement lightwells ("areas") bridged by cantilevered sandstone platts leading to six-panel timber doors. Cast-iron railings feature Greek spearhead and anthemion motifs set directly into lead-caulked copestone sockets on dwarf sandstone boundary walls.

#### 3. Urban Cryptogamic Ecology & Phenology Lens (October 15, Autumn)
* **Canopy State:** Ainslie Place Garden contains mature *Fagus sylvatica* (European Beech), *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), and *Tilia cordata* (Lime). By mid-October, foliage is at ~40% senescence: translucent bistre-yellow and bronze leaves thinning out, revealing structural branch frameworks.
* **Microclimatic Epiphytes:** The NW-facing aspect receives prevailing wet winds and minimal direct solar radiation. The NW-facing bark of tree trunks, dwarf stone walls, and lower rusticated stone courses exhibit thin carpets of wet, emerald-green aerial microalgae (*Desmococcus olivaceus*) and sprawling pleurocarpous mosses (*Hypnum cupressiforme*), while sheltered vertical joints host dark crustose lichen crusts.

#### 4. Chemical & Industrial Patina Lens
* **Soot Crust Morphology:** Two centuries of burning Scottish coal (specifically midlothian bituminous coal) created recalcitrant gypsum-soot crusts (black crusts) bonded to calcium-rich cementation zones. These crusts remain prominent on sheltered horizontal surfaces—beneath window projecting cills, along mutules of the cornice, and in the undercut channels of rustication.
* **Ferrous Leaching:** Micro-fissures in black bitumen-painted cast-iron railings reveal orange-red ferric oxide (FeOOH) running along the dripping lower rail, causing vertical rust stains down the pale sandstone copings.

### STAGE 3: CROSS-DOMAIN SYNERGY ANALYSIS
* **Hydrological-Chemical Synergism:** Rainwater running down the NW facade picks up soot-borne sulfur residue, causing low-level acid leaching across the Craigleith ashlar, leaving clean, washed pale streaks down central stone faces while concentrating dark soot and algal patches in recessed arrises and joints.
* **Structural-Ecological Interaction:** Senescent, sodden sycamore and beech leaves, shed in the mid-October winds, collect heavily in the whinstone gutter along the crescent's curb and across the cast-iron gully grates, creating localized water damming and shallow, dark puddles reflecting the dull silver-grey northern sky.

### STAGE 4: DOMAIN CONFLICT RESOLUTION
* **Conflict:** Neoclassical formal architectural symmetry demands pristine, uniform surfaces, whereas Edinburgh's damp, soot-laden maritime climate enforces heavy patination and asymmetric biological growth.
* **Resolution:** Structural symmetry is retained purely in the geometric alignment and ashlar drafting, but physically subordinated to authentic environmental decay: NW-facing damp exposure dictates strong directional algal growth on horizontal banding and north-facing tree bark, alongside distinct soot accumulation under downwind cornices.
