# Spatial Domain Analysis: 23 Dublin St Ln N, Edinburgh EH3 6NT, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean strata comprise Lower Carboniferous (Tournaisian/Viséan) sedimentary sequences of the Gullane Formation and Strathclyde Group (pale quartz-arenite sandstones, mudstones, and siltstones) mantled by stiff Devensian glacial till. Primary structural lithic is pale buff-to-grey Craigleith sandstone, a fine-grained, silica-bound quartzose freestone characterized by high compressive strength and low porosity, supplemented with Hailes sandstone for foundation plinths. Mortar chemistry consists of historic non-hydraulic slaked lime putty with coarse river sand and crushed sandstone aggregate, finished in flush or neat penciled lime joints. Hydrogeological flow follows the north-dipping glacial drumlin gradient towards the Water of Leith.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Neoclassical Regency/Georgian residential crescent and garden square urbanism (Drummond Place, Second New Town, Reid & Sibbald layout, c. 1802–1823). Continuous perimeter terraces standing 3 to 4 storeys over sunk basement areaways with retaining walls. Channelled ashlar rustication across ground storeys, droved and polished ashlar on piano nobile and upper floors, articulated by continuous stringcourses, molded band cornices, and parapets. Symmetrical fenestration grids with recessed timber sash-and-case windows (6-over-6 and 12-over-12 configurations). Double-pitch roofs clad in Scottish and Welsh slates, punctuated by ashlar chimney stacks with clay chimney cans and timber-framed dormers. Rectified vertical plumb lines across all curved circus geometry.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Facades: Craigleith fine-grained blonde/buff ashlar sandstone with polished dressings; channelled rustication to basement/ground; coursed rubble to rear elevations.
- Ground Infrastructure: Pavements of split Caithness flagstones, tooled whinstone kerbs, and roadway surfaced in dressed basalt/whinstone setts.
- Metalwork: Cast-iron spearhead boundary railings and gates mounted on droved sandstone plinths with lead sockets.
- Weathering/Patina: Historic coal-smoke carbon encrustations in undersides of dentil moldings and cornices; rain-washed bleached quartz reveals on windward elevations; basal rising damp with micro-algal biofilm colonization on shaded basement stonework.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Mature urban woodland within Drummond Place Gardens. Overstory dominated by *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm), *Tilia × europaea* (Common Lime), and *Fagus sylvatica* (European Beech). Phenological state for November 11 (late autumn): advanced senescence with 75–85% defoliation; remaining foliage displays dull russet, ochre, and golden-brown tones; stark silhouette of bare scaffold limbs and intricate branch tracery; deep organic mulch and fallen leaf litter distributed across enclosed central lawn and gravel walks.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient vehicles, parked automobiles, delivery vans, pedestrians, wheelie bins, and temporary traffic signage are removed. Permanent historic civil fabric—including wrought-iron railings, cast-iron Edinburgh-pattern streetlamps, stone kerb lines, and cobbled setts—is fully retained and structurally restored.
