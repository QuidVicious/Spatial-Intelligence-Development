# Spatial Domain Analysis: West End, Atholl Cres, Edinburgh EH3 8HA, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The subterranean bedrock consists of Lower Carboniferous sedimentary formations of the Strathclyde Group (Gullane and Wardie Formations), overlain by stiff glacial till (boulder clay). The dominant structural stone across the entire corridor is Scottish Lower Carboniferous quartzose sandstone, primarily quarried from Craigleith, Hailes, and Binny beds. The masonry employs historic non-hydraulic to weakly hydraulic slaked lime mortar (*Calx*) mixed with coarse river sand, allowing continuous vapor permeability. Subterranean moisture is managed via legacy stone-vaulted coal cellars beneath the sidewalk flagstones and sub-pavement drains leading towards the Water of Leith catchment basin.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
A high-angle northeast axial view overlooking Shandwick Place (A8) as it merges into the New Town street grid towards Charlotte Square and George Street.
- **Foreground / Midground Left (Prominent Landmark):** St George's West Church (now Charlotte Chapel), designed by David Bryce (1866–1869) with a towering 56-meter Venetian/Italianate Romanesque campanile tower designed by Sir Robert Rowland Anderson (completed 1881). The church features a multi-tiered ashlar tower with vermiculated base bands, giant-order Corinthian pilasters, pedimented clock dials, an arcaded belfry, and a crowning stone/lead lantern.
- **Street Flanks (Left & Right):** Classical Georgian and Victorian tenement blocks ranging from 4 to 5 storeys, incorporating ground-floor retail shopfronts beneath rusticated basements, followed by ashlar upper levels with regular fenestration patterns, timber sash-and-case windows (12-pane and 2-over-2 configurations), projecting cornices, parapet gutters, and extensive pitched slate roofs interrupted by dense arrangements of stone chimney stacks with ceramic pots.
- **Background Vista:** In the far distance to the northeast, the distinct green copper drum and dome of West Register House (formerly St George's Church, Charlotte Square, by Robert Reid / Robert Adam, 1811–1814) anchors the horizon. Further east, the street perspective terminates against the massive classical stone blocks of the Caledonian Hotel and Lothian Road intersection.
- **Planar Rectification:** All vertical load-bearing lines, tower shafts, and tenement elevations are plumbed strictly to gravity vertical (90.0° relative to true horizon). All horizontal stringcourses, entablatures, parapet copings, and slate roof ridges are flattened into rectilinear architectural planes.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Primary Masonry:** Fine-grained, pale buff-to-honey Craigleith and Hailes sandstone dressed in fine ashlar, channelled rustication, and droved/tooled ashlar finishes. 
- **Roofing & Flashings:** Dark blue-grey Scottish Ballachulish and Welsh roofing slates laid in diminishing courses; rolled lead (*plumbum*) sheet valleys, ridges, gutters, and chimney flashings.
- **Domes & Lanterns:** Oxidized verdigris sheet copper on the distant Charlotte Square dome and church cupola elements.
- **Environmental Weathering & Patina:** Decades of legacy maritime damp and coal smoke have produced characteristic dark atmospheric sulphate crusting (soot-blackening) across sheltered sandstone undercuts, cornice soffits, and window drip moulds, contrasted with clean rain-washed buff faces on windward surfaces. Subtle green micro-algal dusting appears on northern damp stringcourses and damp stone joints.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Along the distant background and fringe urban courtyards, mature urban deciduous tree canopies are present, primarily *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), *Fraxinus excelsior* (Common Ash), and *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm), featuring dense, organic leafy branch structures and natural summer foliar canopies.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient visual noise—including moving and parked buses, cars, delivery vans, pedestrians, temporary road cones, waste bins, and paper street clutter—has been fully removed. The entire roadway surface of Shandwick Place, including asphalt lanes, tram rail tracks, granite curbs, and stone flagstone pavements, is rendered completely clear and static.
