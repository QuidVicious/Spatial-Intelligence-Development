# Spatial Domain Analysis: Forth House, Edinburgh EH1 2BW, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The subterranean strata beneath Edinburgh’s West End comprise Lower Carboniferous sedimentary formations of the Strathclyde and Inverclyde Groups, dominated by quartzose sandstones interbedded with siltstones and mudstones, situated atop glacial till (boulder clay). 

The above-ground masonry is quarried locally:
- **Craigleith Sandstone**: A resilient, dense, pale blonde-to-buff quartz arenite defining the ashlar facades of the surrounding Georgian and Victorian tenements and the dominant campanile of St George's West Church.
- **Hailes Sandstone**: Characteristic dark, carbonaceous-banded sandstone utilized extensively in secondary coursed rubble rear elevations and boundary walls.
- **Mortar Lithology**: Traditional non-hydraulic and feebly hydraulic lime mortars (`Ca(OH)₂`), displaying historic lime leaching, aggregate exposure, and carbonation over successive wet-dry cycles.
- **Subterranean Drainage**: Natural gravitational runoff slopes northwestward down towards the Water of Leith basin, with groundwater channeled through permeable glacial gravels.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Looking northwest across Edinburgh's West End (Shandwick Place / Stafford Street axis):
- **Central Monument**: **St George's West Church (Free Church)**, designed by David Bryce (1866–1869) with its landmark 56-metre (184-foot) Italian Romanesque / Venetian-inspired campanile designed by Sir Robert Rowand Anderson (1879–1881). The tower features a square ashlar shaft, belfry arcade with triple round-arched louvres, classical clock stage, and a verdigris copper pyramidal spire.
- **Foreground**: Four-storey Victorian/Georgian red-orange brick and buff sandstone tenement rear blocks with hipped/pitched leaded and slated roof planes, prominent stone chimney stacks, and clay chimneypots.
- **Flanking Terraces & Tenements**: 3.5 to 5-storey classical Scottish Georgian and Victorian tenement blocks arranged in continuous street grids, characterized by regular rhythms of vertical 2-over-2 and 6-over-6 timber sash-and-case windows, projecting eaves, canted bays, dormered mansard roofs, and heavy dressed ashlar parapets.
- **Planar Rectification & Anti-Warp Protocol**: All vertical tenement facades, corner quoins, and the soaring church tower are plumbed to absolute gravity-vertical alignment; all slated roof ridges, stringcourses, and cornice lines are rectified into true planar geometries, eliminating photogrammetry tilt and distorted polygon artifacts.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Primary Facades**: Honed ashlar and rock-faced rusticated Craigleith sandstone, bearing authentic maritime-temperate environmental patina—diffuse atmospheric carbon crusting under cornices and window hoods, rain-washed blonde faces, and subtle mineral oxidation.
- **Spire & Roofing**: Weathered oxidised copper sheet on the church campanile displaying a distinctive pale verdigris-green patina; Scottish Ballachulish and Welsh roofing slate across tenements with subtle blue-grey sheen, lead ridge flashing, zinc valley gutters, and terracotta/fireclay chimney pots.
- **Foreground Infill**: Exposed weathered red clay brick masonry with lime mortar repointing on mid-terrace additions.
- **Glazing & Openings**: Cast-iron rainwater hoppers, painted dark cast-iron drainpipes, and crisp painted timber sash window frames set deep into dressed reveals.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Urban vegetation interspersed along rear garden plots, shared greens, and distant street corridors:
- **Canopy Trees**: Mature London Planes (*Platanus x acerifolia*), European Sycamores (*Acer pseudoplatanus*), and Silver Birches (*Betula pendula*) exhibiting full, dense organic leafy branching, textured bark, and natural crown volume—clearly disambiguated from adjacent masonry.
- **Understory & Distant Parks**: English Ivy (*Hedera helix*) clinging to sheltered courtyard rubble walls, ornamental garden shrubs, and the rolling green drumlins of the distant Lothian hills and the Firth of Forth horizon line.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient visual noise has been strictly removed:
- **Removed**: Zero motor vehicles (no moving or parked buses, trams, or cars on Shandwick Place/Maitland Street), zero pedestrians, zero trash bins/dumpsters, and zero transient signage.
- **Preserved Civil Infrastructure**: Dressed granite kerbs, flagstone pedestrian pavements, cast-iron street lamps, wrought-iron area railings, and permanent municipal utility access covers.
