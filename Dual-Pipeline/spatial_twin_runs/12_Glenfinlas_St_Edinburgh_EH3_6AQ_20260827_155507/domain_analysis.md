# Spatial Domain Analysis: 12 Glenfinlas St, Edinburgh EH3 6AQ, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- Subterranean Bedrock: Underlain by Carboniferous sedimentary formations (Gullane Formation and Wardie Shales) intersected by glacial till deposits from the Devensian glaciation, structured atop the broader volcanic and sedimentary crag-and-tail bedrock of the Edinburgh basin.
- Regional Lithics: Primary load-bearing masonry is extracted from the local Lower Carboniferous Craigleith Quarry (fine-grained, highly siliceous, compact 'liver rock' sandstone), with supplementary grey-blonde Carboniferous sandstones across neighboring residential terraces.
- Mortar & Jointing: Traditional lime-based mortars composed of non-hydraulic lime and sharp local sand, forming tightly pointed ashlar joints with minimal bedding margins on formal classical facades.
- Hydro-geology: Stable subterranean urban drainage along the gently descending slope of the New Town terrace towards the Water of Leith basin.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- Typology: Neoclassical monumental civic architecture (former St George’s Church / West Register House, designed by Robert Reid, 1811–1814) flanked by unified Georgian residential and commercial ashlar blocks (New Town masterplan by James Craig, facades by Robert Adam).
- Massing & Storey Heights: The central neoclassical edifice features a square-plan base rising into an elevated peristyle drum with engaged Corinthian/Ionic colonnades, surmounted by a ribbed dome with an open tempietto lantern and finial (reaching 49 meters / 160 feet in height). Flanking New Town terraces comprise 3 to 4 storeys plus attic dormers and sunken basement lightwells.
- Roofscapes & Fenestration: Complex multi-flued masonry chimney stacks, hipped slate mansard pitches, lead-lined parapet gutters, vertical sliding sash windows (six-over-six and twelve-over-twelve timber frames), deep rusticated voussoirs at ground levels, and crisp projecting ashlar entablatures.
- Planar Rectification: All vertical masonry lines, colonnade shafts, drum pilasters, and tenement corner quoinings are rectified to true gravity vertical; curved 3D mesh artifacts on dome ribs, balustrades, and cornice modillions are resolved into crisp planar surfaces.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- West Register House Dome & Drum: Heavily patinated verdigris copper sheeting on the outer ribbed dome shell with localized turquoise and pale-green oxidation; fine-dressed Craigleith cream-grey ashlar drum and lantern with gilded copper/brass apex finial.
- Wall Facades: Dressed blonde-to-soot-stained Craigleith sandstone ashlar blocks with fine-tooled draughted margins.
- Roof Coverings: Dark Scottish Highland (Ballachulish) and Welsh slate roofing tiles, cast-iron rainwater goods, and milled sheet lead flashing on valleys and ridges.
- Environmental Patina: Authentic maritime and urban micro-climate weathering, including carbonaceous soot crusting beneath projecting cornices, water run-off wash tracks on stone faces, subtle calcium silicate efflorescence, and microscopic lichen growth in shaded joints.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy

- Species Composition: *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm), *Tilia x europaea* (Common Lime), and *Sorbus aucuparia* (Rowan).
- Foliage Rendering: Dense, naturally stratified organic leafy canopies with structural woody branching and distinct leaf clusters, fully disambiguated from solid stonework.
