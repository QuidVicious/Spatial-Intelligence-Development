# Spatial Domain Analysis: 1540 Maine Ave SW, Washington, DC 20004, USA

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- **Bedrock & Subterranean Foundation**: Underlain by the Atlantic Coastal Plain margin overlapping Piedmont crystalline bedrock (metamorphic schist and gneiss) buried beneath deep Quaternary alluvial deposits and Potomac Formation clay, silt, and gravel. Because the original 19th-century marshland soils had a high water table and low bearing capacity, the Washington Monument rests on an engineered underpinning: a massive subterranean concrete slab and buttress foundation system extending 11.2 m (36.8 ft) deep to stabilize settling.
- **Lithology & Quarry Provenance**:
  - *Lower Shaft (0–46.3 m / 0–152 ft)*: Coarse-grained white calcitic Cockeysville Marble quarried from the Texas Quarry in Baltimore County, Maryland, backed by blue gneiss and Seneca red sandstone masonry.
  - *Intermediate Transition (approx. 46–49 m)*: Four courses of fine-grained white marble quarried in Lee/Sheffield, Berkshire County, Massachusetts.
  - *Upper Shaft & Pyramidion (49–169 m)*: Finer-grained dolomitic Cockeysville Marble (Maryland) backed by New England granites and blue gneiss rubble.
  - *Apex*: 100-ounce cast solid aluminum pyramidion tip.
  - *Plaza & Curbs*: Fine-to-medium grained grey and charcoal granites (including Mesabi Black and regional Appalachian granites).
- **Mortar Chemistry**: 19th-century hydraulic lime-sand mortar in the lower tier, transitioning to early Portland cement and natural hydraulic cement blends in the upper sections.
- **Hydrology**: High water table mitigated by subterranean radial French drains, gravitational sumps, and surface grading that channels storm runoff away from the monument mound into National Mall drainage basins.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- **Typology & Geometric Rectification**: 
  - *Center*: Egyptian-revival hollow load-bearing stone masonry obelisk standing at an official geodetic height of 169.046 m (554 ft 7 11/32 in) with a base width of 16.8 m tapering to 10.5 m below the 55-degree apex pyramidion. The 3D photogrammetric taper is rectified to true planar vertical alignment with laser-straight plumb lines, crisp 90-degree corner arrises, and symmetrical 8-window observation apertures at the pyramidion base.
  - *Monument Plaza*: Symmetrical circular granite terrace and dual concentric flag rings surrounding the monument base, accessed by rectilinear and arcuate crushed-gravel pathways.
  - *Northeast Flank (National Museum of African American History and Culture)*: Contemporary 5-storey above-grade civic institutional pavilion featuring an inverted, three-tiered corona profile angled at 17 degrees (mirroring the monument’s pyramidion angle). Rectilinear glass curtain wall beneath a cantilevered, laser-plumbed cast bronze-aluminum screen.
  - *Southeast Flank (Institutional Buildings)*: Neoclassical/federalist multi-storey administrative brick and limestone ashlar office wings with symmetrical fenestration and flat/mansard rooflines.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Washington Monument Facade**:
  - Distinct horizontal color-shift demarcation line at the 46-meter mark, delineating the lower weathered 1848–1854 Texas calcitic marble from the lighter, slightly cooler-toned 1879–1884 Cockeysville dolomitic marble above.
  - Mildew/algae micro-film along lower plinth mortar recesses, softened historic ashlar joints, minor atmospheric gypsum wash patination along drip edges, and slight micro-fracture mortar repointing lines.
- **NMAAHC Facade**:
  - 3,600 intricate bronze-coated cast-aluminum filigree corona panels with dark metallic warm-brown sheen, backed by low-iron architectural structural glazing and exposed dark structural steel frames.
- **Ground Hardscape**:
  - Crushed reddish-tan Virginia gravel/stabilized aggregate paths, sawn-face grey granite pavers, dark granite security bollards, concrete curbs, and asphalt roadway surfaces with painted civil markings.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- **Overstory & Canopy**: Mature deciduous trees forming dense, rounded organic canopies; primary species include American elm (*Ulmus americana*), willow oak (*Quercus phellos*), red oak (*Quercus rubra*), London plane trees (*Platanus × acerifolia*), and flowering Yoshino cherry (*Prunus × yedoensis*). Canopies exhibit complex branching with dense leafy foliage, distinct from underlying stone/earth textures.
- **Groundcover**: High-traffic manicured turfgrass lawn (Kentucky bluegrass *Poa pratensis* and turf-type tall fescue *Festuca arundinacea* mix) exhibiting radial mower stripes, subtle compaction wear along footpath margins, and healthy emerald-to-olive green seasonal saturation.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
- All transient vehicular traffic, tour buses, commuter cars, delivery vans, and temporary maintenance vehicles are eliminated from Constitution Avenue, 15th Street, and 17th Street.
- All pedestrians, tourists, queue lines, security tents, temporary barriers, trash receptacles, dumpsters, and loose street debris are stripped.
- Permanent static infrastructure is strictly retained: circular perimeter flagpole ring, bronze orientation plaques, granite bollards, cast-iron roadway light fixtures, traffic signals, painted asphalt road crosswalks, stone boundary curbs, and manicured park path borders.
