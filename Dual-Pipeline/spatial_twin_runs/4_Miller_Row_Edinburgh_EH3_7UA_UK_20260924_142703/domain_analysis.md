# Spatial Domain Analysis: 4 Miller Row, Edinburgh EH3 7UA, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- Bedrock: Carboniferous sedimentary strata (Wardie Shales and sandstone beds) incised by the Water of Leith, flanked by basaltic volcanic volcanic plugs (Castle Rock, Arthur's Seat).
- Lithics: Craigleith and Hailes pale blonde-to-grey carboniferous sandstone for Georgian terraces and bridge piers; Scottish slate roofing (Ballachulish).
- Mortar: Historic non-hydraulic and feebly hydraulic lime mortars; natural stone bedding joints with ashlar fine-lime pointing.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- Civil Infrastructure: Dean Bridge (Thomas Telford, 1831); four four-centred main ashlar arches carrying cantilevered side footpaths on slender piers.
- Residential: 4-to-5-storey Georgian classical and Victorian New Town tenements; rusticated basements, planar ashlar upper storeys, sash-and-case fenestration grids (mostly 6-over-6 and 2-over-2 timber frames), continuous string courses, cornices, and pitched slate mansards/attics with stone dormers.
- Skyline Monuments: St George's West dome and distant Edinburgh Castle fortress complex atop volcanic crag.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Primary: Fine-tooled droved ashlar and squared rubble Craigleith sandstone, weathered to uniform leaden-grey with carbon encrustation on sheltered cornices.
- Roofs: Ballachulish blue-grey slates laid in diminishing courses, lead flashing, zinc valley gutters, cast-iron rainwater goods, and stone chimney stacks with terracotta pots.
- Bridge Elements: Heavy tooled sandstone blockwork, cutwaters, and dressed stone parapets.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- Riparian/Gorge Canopy: *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), *Fraxinus excelsior* (European Ash), *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm), and *Alnus glutinosa* (Alder) lining the Water of Leith gorge.
- Phenology (Late September): Dense, mature broadleaf crowns transitioning with localized foliar chlorosis, patchy yellow-ochre edges, and early leaf drop along the upper riverbank margins.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient traffic, moving vehicles on Dean Bridge and Queensferry Road, pedestrians, modern road signage, and temporary waste receptacles are entirely removed. Static infrastructure (kerbs, stone balustrades, cast-iron street lamps) remains.
