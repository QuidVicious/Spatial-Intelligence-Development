# Spatial Domain Analysis: 7 Ainslie Pl, Edinburgh EH3 6AS, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean Carboniferous sedimentary strata and basaltic volcanic basement. Superficial deposits of dense glacial till. Primary masonry composed of buff-grey Craigleith and Hailes carboniferous quartz-arenite sandstone. Traditional lime-sand mortar beds and pointing.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Early 19th-century Georgian New Town residential terrace (Ainslie Place, Moray Estate, designed by James Gillespie Graham, c. 1822). Classical composition with planar vertical masonry walls, rusticated ashlar ground floors, polished ashlar upper storeys, continuous stringcourses, and moulded eaves cornices. Three-storey plus attic and sunk basement configurations. Regular fenestration featuring 6-over-6 and 12-pane timber sash-and-case windows. Pitched roofs clad in Scottish Ballachulish slates with dressed stone chimney stacks and ceramic pots. Distant lead-clad classical copper-patinated dome aligned beyond the roofline. Symmetrical central private communal garden with radial gravel paths and perimeter ironwork.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Facades: Fine-grained Craigleith sandstone ashlar, V-joint rustication at base, smooth ashlar above, localized soot-crusted carbon patina and damp run-off staining.
- Roofs: Ballachulish grey-blue slate tiles, lead flashing, ridging, and gutters.
- Fenestration: Painted white timber multi-pane frames, polished glazing.
- Civil Fabric & Boundaries: Hand-tooled stone boundary plinths, cast-iron spearhead railings, Caithness flagstone paving slabs, whinstone kerbing, asphalt road surfaces, and self-binding gravel garden paths.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Central urban garden enclave containing mature *Tilia × europaea* (Common Lime), *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), and *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm). Late-autumn/early-winter phenology for late November: predominantly defoliated canopies with bare, intricate crown architecture, sparse clusters of dry brown senescent leaves clinging to sheltered inner branches, and leaf litter over mown rye-grass turf.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient vehicles, pedestrians, modern traffic signage, waste receptacles, and street clutter are eliminated. Static architectural, lithic, and botanical elements remain fully plumbed, rectified, and preserved.
