# Spatial Domain Analysis: 15 Marchmont Cres, Edinburgh EH9 1HL, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The subterranean geology of Marchmont rests upon the Upper Oil-Shale and Gullane Formations of the Carboniferous sedimentary sequence, overlain by dense glacial till. The superstructures are constructed predominantly of indigenous Lower Carboniferous quartz sandstones, featuring pale grey-buff and carbon-flecked laminated stone extracted from Hailes and Craigleith quarries, alongside Permo-Triassic New Red/Locharbriggs sandstones deployed in late Victorian phases. Mortar chemistry consists of traditional lime mortar binders (slaked hydraulic lime with sharp quartz river sand aggregate) providing essential vapor permeability. Natural subterranean drainage flows northwards down the glacial slope toward the historic Burgh Loch basin (The Meadows).

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Classic late-Victorian Scottish Baronial / Renaissance revival tenement blocks (c. 1876–1890) flanking the junction of Marchmont Crescent, Roseneath Street, and Warrender Park Road. 
- **Typology & Height:** Solid four-to-five-storey load-bearing masonry blocks (16–20m elevation to ridge) above raised plinths and ground-floor commercial units.
- **Corner Features & Articulation:** Prominent corner conical turrets (tourelles) with corbelled ashlar bases, crow-stepped and pedimented nepus gables, decorative stringcourses, and heavy ashlar wallhead chimney stacks with terracotta chimney pots.
- **Fenestration:** Rectilinear vertical timber sash-and-case windows (one-over-one and two-over-two patterns) recessed in crisp, roll-moulded stone reveals.
- **Planar Rectification:** All exterior load-bearing ashlar and snecked rubble walls are plumbed to absolute gravitational vertical; horizontal stringcourses, lead roof valleys, and Ballachulish slate pitched roof planes are rectified into true planar geometries.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Masonry:** Snecked rock-faced rubble walling with polished ashlar stone dressings, quoin blocks, and corbel tables. 
- **Color & Texture:** Ranging from honey-buff to ash-grey Hailes stone, interwoven with warm pinkish-red Locharbriggs sandstone.
- **Climate-Adaptive Patina:** Temperate maritime atmospheric weathering exhibiting localized gypsum crusting, historic industrial carbon/soot accumulation embedded in sheltered window architraves, cornice recesses, and rainwater run-off channels across upper friezes. 
- **Roofing:** Scottish dark blue-grey Ballachulish slate tiles, zinc/lead flashing, ridge valleys, and cast-iron rainwater downpipes clamped against the stone courses.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Urban temperate vegetation in rear courtyard plots and sidewalk verges. Mature leafy canopy of *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm), and *Betula pendula* (Silver Birch), displaying organic deciduous branching, deep green serrated foliage, and natural irregular crowns distinct from building masonry.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient motorized traffic, parked cars, vans, dumpsters, recycling bins, pedestrians, and temporary roadworks visible in the raw survey have been completely excised. Static urban infrastructure—historic whinstone kerbs, asphalt roadways with faded white junction markings, sandstone boundary copings, cast-iron railings, and municipal streetlamps—is fully preserved in crisp structural stillness.
