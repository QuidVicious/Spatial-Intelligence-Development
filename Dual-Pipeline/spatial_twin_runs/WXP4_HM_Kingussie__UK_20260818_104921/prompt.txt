High-angle 35mm documentary still of the remote estate steading deep within Glen Tromie near Kingussie, captured looking northwards down the glacial valley axis at an elevation of 525 meters AGL. A prominent 2-storey Scottish Highland estate lodge rising to 7.8m in height forms the core of the central compound, constructed from local grey granite and harled metamorphic stone under pitched Ballachulish slate roofs, accompanied by lower 1-storey ancillary steadings and timber-clad outbuildings reaching 3.8m to 4.2m. The architectural compound sits enclosed within fenced pasture fields and bordered by two dense, rectangular dark green shelterbelt copses of mature Scots pine and larch. To the right, a pale compacted gravel access track meanders alongside the compound before tracking north toward the distant, dark glacial loch nestled between the towering valley walls. Flanking the glen, massive steep mountain ridges composed of weathered psammite and heather-clad scree rise dramatically on both sides, their lower talus slopes covered in tawny graminoid moorland, sedge, and patchy peat bog. Crisp, clear late-morning mountain daylight under a soft North Atlantic sky casts defined, natural directional shadows to the northwest, illuminating the drystone field boundaries, the rough texture of the mountain slopes, and the vast scale of the Cairngorms wilderness. Captured on 35mm Kodak Portra 400 film, natural color grade, razor-sharp architectural rectilinearity, authentic atmospheric depth, and physical spatial realism.
