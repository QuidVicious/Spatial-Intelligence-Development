## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The West End of Edinburgh rests upon Early Carboniferous sedimentary bedrock of the Strathclyde Group (sandstones, siltstones, and oil-shales) overlying glacial till, shaped into classic crag-and-tail landforms by Quaternary glacial flows. In the far left background rises the basaltic volcanic plug of Castle Rock and the distant silhouette of Arthur's Seat / Salisbury Crags (Carboniferous volcanic neck and basaltic sill system).
- Primary Architectural Lithics: High-quartz fine-grained Craigleith Sandstone (Carboniferous age, pale blonde to warm buff-grey) forming the monumental Gothic fabric of St Mary's Episcopal Cathedral, complemented by Dunmore and Hailes quarry sandstones across the surrounding Victorian residential terraces.
- Mortar Chemistry: Traditional hot-mixed Scottish non-hydraulic and feebly hydraulic lime mortars with coarse grit sand, providing moisture breathability across dense ashlar joints.
