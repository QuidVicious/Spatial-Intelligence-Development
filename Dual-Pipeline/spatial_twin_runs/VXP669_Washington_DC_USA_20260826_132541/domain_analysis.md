## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- **Subterranean Bedrock & Subsurface Dynamics:** The site occupies alluvial terrace deposits of the Atlantic Coastal Plain overlying crystalline metamorphic bedrock (gneiss/schist) of the Piedmont Fall Zone. The foundation rests on massive stepped bluestone gneiss masonry reinforced with concrete sub-footings to stabilize the weight above high Potomac River alluvial water tables.
- **Indigenous Masonry Lithics:**
  - *Washington Monument:* Three distinct lithic strata across its vertical rise. The lower ~152 feet (1848–1854) is faced with coarse-grained calcitic marble quarried from Texas, Baltimore County, Maryland. Above a distinct horizontal shift line, four transitional courses consist of fine Stockbridge dolomitic marble from Sheffield/Lee, Massachusetts. The upper two-thirds (1879–1884 completion) is faced with fine-grained dolomitic Cockeysville marble (Beaver Dam Quarry, Maryland), backed by Maine granite and Seneca red sandstone.
  - *Federal Triangle & Background Civic Buildings:* Clad extensively in Salem Formation Indiana oolitic limestone, with lower rusticated basements set in gray crystalline granite.
- **Mortar Chemistry:** Traditional non-hydraulic to semi-hydraulic high-calcium lime mortar in the historic masonry seams, with repointed elastomeric and natural cementitious formulations at high-exposure joints.
