# Spatial Domain Analysis: 1 Greenside Row, Edinburgh EH1 3AN, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The subterranean geology belongs to the Lower Carboniferous Calton Hill Volcanic Formation, comprising interbedded basaltic lavas, tuffs, and volcanic agglomerates resting upon Lower Old Red and Carboniferous sedimentary strata. Structural foundations are anchored into competent basaltic bedrock and dense overconsolidated glacial till. Primary historic load-bearing masonry consists of Lower Carboniferous Craigleith and Hailes quartz-arenite sandstone—dense, fine-grained, silica-cemented pale buff to carbon-grey sandstone bonded with historic non-hydraulic to weakly hydraulic slaked lime mortar (*Calx*).

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
The scene features a high-density, multi-tiered urban intersection characterized by steep topography and divergent architectural epochs:
- **Central Wedge Tenement (Calton Road / Greenside Place Junction):** Acute-angle triangular "flat-iron" tenement block, 4 storeys plus attic mansard level. True plumbed vertical wall planes, rectilinear floor registers, 2-over-2 and 6-over-6 double-hung timber sash windows, ashlar sandstone lintels and continuous projecting string courses, terminating in pitched slate roofs with dressed masonry chimney stacks and cylindrical terracotta chimney pots.
- **Background Monumental Infrastructure (Regent Bridge / Waterloo Place):** Archibald Elliot’s 1816–1819 neoclassical bridge, featuring a monumental round arch spanning the Low Calton ravine surmounted by open Corinthian colonnades and classical entablatures.
- **Right Midground / Foreground (Omni Centre & Rooftop Terrace):** Modern mixed-use complex (Alan Murray Architects, 2003) integrating structural glazed curtain walls, curved zinc standing-seam parapets, precast reconstituted stone spandrels, and a stepped 2-acre rooftop terrace deck.
- **Left Foreground Commercial Massing:** 20th-century modular rectilinear office block defined by a rigid vertical grid of reconstituted sandstone/concrete piers, deeply recessed structural glazing, and flat roof decks.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Historic Tenements & Bridge:** Finely dressed ashlar Craigleith sandstone, droved and rusticated sandstone podiums, Scottish Ballachulish slate roofing, cast-iron rain goods, lead flashing and valleys. 
- **Modern Developments:** Extruded dark anodized aluminum curtain framing, low-emissivity structural glass panels, zinc sheet standing-seam cladding, modular reconstituted stone cladding panels.
- **Pavements & Civil Surfaces:** Dense, multi-coat asphalt road surfacing with painted thermoplastic white and yellow line markings, bordered by monolithic whinstone (quartz-dolerite) and Scottish granite kerbstones, Caithness flagstone pedestrian paving.
- **Pathology & Patina:** Localized atmospheric coal-smoke gypsum crusts adhering to historic sandstone cornices, vertical moisture-run staining along ashlar reveals, oxidized lead cappings, and clean rain-washed stone projections.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Urban dendrological specimens comprise mature *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore maple) and *Tilia cordata* (Small-leaved Lime) in street-level alignments, alongside planter-contained *Prunus laurocerasus* and ornamental shrubs on the upper terrace deck. Phenological state for November 11: late-autumn defoliation (~85–95% canopy loss), exposing stark, intricate dark branch architecture with sparse desiccated ochre-brown leaves adhering to terminal twigs.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient dynamic entities—pedestrians, vehicular traffic, public transit buses, delivery trucks, waste receptacles, and loose street furniture—are fully expunged. Fixed civil assets (whinstone kerbs, steel guard rails, signal poles, lane delineators, and mature trees) remain precisely rectified and geometrically anchored.
