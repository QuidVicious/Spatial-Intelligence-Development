# Spatial Domain Analysis: 5 Saxe Coburg Pl, Edinburgh EH3 5BR, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- **Bedrock Lithology & Geomorphology:** The subterranean foundation consists of the Lower Carboniferous Inverclyde and Strathclyde Groups (sedimentary sandstones, siltstones, and mudstones) cut by intrusive volcanic structures. The southward vista spans from the Water of Leith incision in Stockbridge across the glacial crag-and-tail topography of the New Town toward Castle Rock—an eroded Early Carboniferous basaltic/doleritic volcanic plug (~350 Ma) looming on the horizon.
- **Indigenous Masonry Lithics:** The predominant masonry across the Georgian and Victorian urban fabric is indigenous Craigleith sandstone (a highly siliceous, pale-grey to buff Carboniferous quartz arenite prized for exceptional compressive strength and weather resistance), supplemented by Hailes and Ravelston sandstones (laminated, clay-bearing, buff-grey). Ground-level sets and kerbstones feature intrusive quartz-dolerite and Whinstone.
- **Mortar Chemistry & Subterranean Hydrology:** Historic load-bearing masonry utilizes non-hydraulic to feebly hydraulic hot-mixed slaked lime mortars with coarse river sand aggregate. Subterranean moisture moves naturally southward toward the Water of Leith drainage basin via deeply stratified glacial till and alluvial gravels over bedrock.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- **Typologies & Massing:** 
  - *Foreground/Lower Tier:* Mid-to-late Georgian and early Victorian residential tenements and mixed-use commercial blocks along Hamilton Place, St Stephen Place, and Saxe Coburg Place. Typologies range from 3- to 4-storey classical tenements with raised basements to mid-20th-century infill housing (3–4 storeys with horizontal fenestration and light-rendered exterior walls).
  - *Center/Midground:* Prominent Gothic Revival church tower and spire of the Stockbridge Church / St Stephen's community fabric featuring ashlar belfries, lancet openings, and steeply pitched gables. Flanking blocks comprise continuous 4-storey Georgian ashlar tenements punctuated by rhythmic chimney stacks, heavy stone skews, and timber sash-and-case windows (typically twelve-pane 6-over-6 or four-pane 2-over-2 configurations).
  - *Background Horizon:* Distant classical New Town skyline stepping uphill along Heriot Row and Queen Street, framed by the copper-patinated neoclassical dome of West Register House (Charlotte Square), the triple spires of St Mary’s Cathedral to the right, and the fortified mass of Edinburgh Castle crowning Castle Rock on the left skyline.
- **Planar Rectification & Anti-Warp Protocol:** All vertical tenement facades, church spires, and chimney stacks are strictly plumbed to 90° gravity vertical. All horizontal string courses, blocking courses, eaves, and floor plates are rectified into laser-straight parallel planes, fully resolving any photogrammetric facet noise or synthetic bowing. Roof planes, dormers, and lead ridge flashings are crisply delineated.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Per-Structure Facade Discrimination:**
  - *Georgian/Victorian Tenements:* Dressed Craigleith ashlar facing on primary street facades; droved, broached, or stugged coursed rubble on side and rear elevations. Color varies from warm honey-buff to cool silver-grey.
  - *Modern Infill Elements:* Smooth, off-white mineral render over blockwork, dark painted spandrels, and flat-tiled or zinc/slate-clad mansard roofs.
  - *Roofing:* Scotch slate and Welsh slate laid in diminishing courses with hand-dressed edges, secured by copper nails, featuring rolled lead ridges, zinc valleys, and cast-iron rainwater downpipes.
- **Micro-Climate Weathering Pathology (Historic Cold-Temperate Maritime):**
  - Atmospheric carbon and coal-smoke patination (soot deposition) deeply embedded in protected cornices, window reveals, and undersides of string courses from Edinburgh’s historical "Auld Reekie" industrial era.
  - Natural rainwater washing on exposed windward south/west ashlar planes, creating stark contrasts between pale rain-washed quartz faces and dark soot-stained recesses.
  - Localized micro-lichen colonisation, damp-induced dark algal traces around cast-iron gutter spill lines, and subtle sub-surface salt efflorescence along lower basement plinths.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- **Disambiguated Organic Vegetation:** Disambiguated fully from stone geometry; represented with natural open-air branching and distinct foliage layers.
- **Species Composition:** Mature urban deciduous canopy dominating the middle-ground valley (Dean Gardens, Water of Leith corridor, and private tenement back-greens) consisting of *Ulmus glabra* (Wych elm), *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), *Fraxinus excelsior* (European ash), *Fagus sylvatica* (Common beech), and *Tilia x europaea* (Common lime). Leaf clusters show dense, natural organic density with subtle cool-green tones.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
- Complete elimination of all transient moving/parked motor vehicles, pedestrians, waste bins/dumpsters, plastic street signage, and temporary construction hoardings.
- Absolute preservation of permanent civil infrastructure: hand-dressed Whinstone kerbs, caithness flagstone pavements, cast-iron spearhead railings, decorative Georgian street lamps, and stone retaining walls.
