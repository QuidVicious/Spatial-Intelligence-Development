# Spatial Domain Analysis: 1540 Maine Ave SW, Washington, DC 20004, USA

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean geology situated on the Atlantic Coastal Plain-Piedmont fall zone, comprising alluvial silt, sand, and gravel deposits over deep crystalline metamorphic bedrock (gneiss and schist). Monument substructure anchored by an engineered concrete-underpinned gravity foundation. Primary monument lithics comprise Cockeysville calcitic marble (lower 46 meters / 152 feet) backed with Maine granite and blue gneiss rubble, transitioning at the construction demarcation line to lighter Cockeysville/Lee marble ashlar with Massachusetts granite backing up to the 152-meter pyramidion capstone. Surrounding Federal Triangle complexes utilize Salem/Indiana oolitic limestone ashlar over steel and reinforced concrete structural frames. Mortar systems span historic hydraulic lime-sand to modern breathable potassium-silicate restoration mortars.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Washington Monument: 169.29-meter (555 ft) hollow load-bearing stone masonry obelisk, four planar battered facades tapering uniformly to a 55-foot pyramidion apex with aluminum cap point, surrounded by a circular granite-paved plaza ringed by 56 stainless steel flagpoles. National Museum of African American History and Culture: three-tiered inverted pyramidal corona clad in open-patterned bronze-finished architectural cast aluminum lattice panels over a recessed glazed structural pavilion. Federal Triangle: 6-to-8 storey monumental Neoclassical and Beaux-Arts administrative complexes featuring colossal Ionic and Doric colonnades, rusticated ashlar limestone bases, denticulated entablatures, and hipped roofs clad in red clay Spanish mission tiles. Urban DC background: strictly planar 130-foot height-regulated commercial and civic grid with punched-window fenestration and flat roof planes.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
Monument facade exhibits a distinct horizontal material shear/weathering demarcation line at 46 meters height, separating lower weathered off-white Cockeysville marble from upper slightly paler marble ashlar courses; clean raked mortar joints with targeted Jahn M110 conservation fills. Plaza paved in concentric bands of light gray thermal-finish Barre granite slabs. NMAAHC facade composed of 3,600 porous cast-aluminum panels coated in multi-layered metallic bronze finish. Federal Triangle facades built of variegated buff Indiana limestone ashlar with terra cotta barrel roof tiles, copper valley flashing, and bronze window frames. Ground plane consists of manicured Cynodon dactylon / Festuca arundinacea turf, crisply edged compacted tan decomposed granite paths, poured concrete secondary walks, and asphalt radial roadways with split-face granite curbing.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Predominant canopy trees: Ulmus americana (American Elm) lining mall allées, Quercus phellos (Willow Oak), Quercus palustris (Pin Oak), and Acer rubrum (Red Maple) across the monument grounds and perimeter parkland. Botanical phenology for early September: dense mature summer canopies displaying deep olive and emerald foliar color with initial chlorotic tip-drying and faint yellowing on exposed terminal branches; zero significant leaf drop.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All pedestrians, transient tourist foot traffic, vehicles, tour buses, temporary construction staging, ropes, and portable signage are completely removed. Static infrastructure—fixed granite retaining walls, permanent stainless steel flagpoles, bronze perimeter bollards, and cast iron lampposts—is fully preserved.
