# Spatial Domain Analysis: 145 Princes St., Edinburgh EH2 4BL, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Bedrock foundation comprises Lower Carboniferous sedimentary strata of the Inverclyde Group and Gullane Formation sandstones, siltstones, and mudstones, mantled by dense Devensian glacial till. Primary structural masonry is Carboniferous quartz-arenite Craigleith and Hailes sandstone, characterized by high silica content, fine grain, and structural durability. Secondary walling and plinth courses incorporate Pink Corrennie and grey Aberdeen granite. Historic masonry bonding employs hot-mixed calcitic and hydraulic lime mortars with sharp river sand aggregate. Ground plane underlain by compacted road base, gravel sub-base, and consolidated urban fill above subterranean storm drainage vaults.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Linear urban corridor lined with 4-to-5-storey commercial terraces reflecting late Georgian origins remodelled into Victorian baronial and Edwardian classical commercial facades. Street-facing facades feature plumb ashlar elevations, rusticated ground-floor arcades, regular fenestration grids of timber 2-over-2 and 1-over-1 sash-and-case windows, projecting continuous entablatures, dentiled stringcourses, and decorative bracketed cornices. Roofscapes display steeply pitched slate roofs punctuated by pedimented ashlar dormers, stepped Dutch gables, polished sandstone chimney stacks with clay terracotta pots, and lead-lined parapet gutters. Distant western terminus is anchored by a square multi-tiered ashlar church tower capped with a verdigris copper pyramidal spire.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Facades: Fine-grained, droved and polished Craigleith sandstone ashlar in warm buff, honey, and pale grey tones; rusticated ground-floor masonry with timber and bronze storefront architraves.
- Roofs: Ballachulish and Welsh roofing slates laid in diminishing courses; oxidized lead ridge rolls, flashings, and valley troughs; oxidised copper verdigris on distal spire.
- Fenestration: Painted hardwood sash frames with float glass glazing.
- Ground surfacing: Heavy-duty bituminous asphalt carriageway bisected by flush-mounted grooved steel tram rails, bordered by square-dressed grey granite kerbstones and riven Caithness flagstone pedestrian sidewalks.
- Weathering/Patina: Micro-stratified carbon soot encrustations concentrated beneath stringcourses, cornices, and window cills; pale rain-washed surface reveals on exposed relief carvings; minor biological greening and lime leaching at low-level stone joints.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Peripheral urban canopy consisting of mature *Acer pseudoplatanus* (sycamore) and *Tilia × europaea* (common lime). Phenological profile for early September exhibits dense foliage volume, deep green leaf blades with early margins of yellowing chlorosis, localized tar-spotting on sycamore leaves, and seed clusters (samaras) suspended from upper canopy branches.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient entities removed: zero pedestrians, zero buses, zero motor vehicles, and zero street waste. Fixed civil infrastructure retained: embedded steel tram tracks, cast-iron bollards, tubular steel catenary support masts, forged iron basement railings, and municipal granite kerbing. Photogrammetric geometry distortions, floating fragments, and crumpled surface meshes rectified to planar vertical and horizontal architectural lines.
