# Spatial Domain Analysis: 92 Comely Bank Ave, Edinburgh EH4 1HE, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The subterranean foundation rests upon the Carboniferous sedimentary sequence of the Midland Valley of Scotland, dominated by the Gullane Formation (calciferous sandstone measures) interspersed with Lower Carboniferous volcanic dolerite intrusions. The built masonry fabric consists predominantly of fine-to-medium-grained quartzose blonde and honey-colored Scottish Carboniferous sandstones, historically quarried from local Edinburgh beds including Craigleith, Hailes, and Cullalo. Wall structures employ ashlar facing backed by coursed sandstone rubble bound with traditional non-hydraulic to feebly hydraulic lime and sharp river sand mortars. Subterranean moisture is governed by glacial till drift over gently undulating topography sloping towards the Water of Leith basin, driving localized capillary rising damp along ground-level plinths.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Late Victorian and Edwardian 4-storey residential tenements (constructed circa 1894–1910 under Sir James Steel's expansion) arranged in rectilinear perimeter blocks. 
- **Planar Rectification & Geometric Orthogonality**: All building masses rectified to precise structural verticals. Facades feature rhythmic vertical stacks of three-light canted bay windows, projecting polished ashlar cornices, continuous stringcourses, and decorative gablets.
- **Roofscapes**: Steeply pitched double-pitch roofs clad in Scottish and Welsh slates, punctuated by masonry dormers, straight-coped skews with stone kneeler blocks, and monolithic rectangular sandstone chimney stacks complete with cylindrical terracotta clay chimney pots.
- **Fenestration**: Rectilinear vertical timber sash-and-case windows (predominantly 2-over-2 and 1-over-1 configurations) set into rebated stone reveals with clean, planar astragals.
- **Open Space / Grounds**: Rectilinear bowling green and residential shared plots bounded by dwarf ashlar boundary walls, cast-iron railings, and dressed stone copings.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Masonry Lithics**: Blonde Craigleith-type sandstone on primary street frontages showing fine tool markings (droved and polished ashlar finishes), transitioning to warmer buff-rubble sandstone along flank and rear return elevations.
- **Weathering & Environmental Pathology**: 
  - Temperate marine/post-industrial atmospheric patina featuring historic carbon soot incrustation sheltered within window lintels, under projecting bay cornices, and along eaves friezes.
  - Distinct rainwater wash-out tracks across exposed upper ashlar courses.
  - Mild green biological lichen and moss colonization along lower damp stone plinths, base courses, and shaded boundary walls.
  - Lead flashing with subtle white carbonate oxidation at roof valleys, ridge junctions, and dormer aprons.
  - Dark-gray to blue-black roof slates with natural mineral laminations.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Urban landscape ecology of the Stockbridge/Inverleith microclimate. Mature European broadleaf specimens interspersed in private tenement backgreens and roadside verges:
- *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore / Scottish Plane) and *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm) with dense, organic green foliage canopies and articulated, rough-barked trunk branching.
- Fine-textured manicured perennial ryegrass (*Lolium perenne*) turf across the central bowling green and private lawn enclosures.
- *Hedera helix* (English Ivy) and climbing hydrangea lightly adhering to shaded rear stone boundary walls.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient dynamic clutter—including all parked automobiles, moving vehicles, wheeled plastic refuse bins, traffic cones, and pedestrians—has been completely purged. The civil infrastructure remains fully intact: continuous whinstone (dolerite) and granite street kerbs, dressed Caithness flagstone and concrete aggregate footpaths, fixed cast-iron streetlamp columns, street nameplates, cast-iron property railings, and mature rooted street trees.
