# Spatial Domain Analysis: 13 E Basin Dr SW, Washington, DC 20004, USA

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The subterranean foundation rests within the Atlantic Coastal Plain unconsolidated sediments and artificial riverbed fill of the reclaimed Potomac River mudflats. To ensure structural stability across the saturated, dynamic water table adjacent to the Tidal Basin, load-bearing concrete caissons were sunk through alluvial silt and clay down to crystalline metamorphic bedrock (gneiss/schist). 

The superstructure utilizes a diverse palette of North American lithics:
- Exterior envelope, columns, and stepped drum: Vermont Imperial Danby marble (Lower Ordovician Shelburne Formation calcitic marble).
- Basement terraces, retention walls, and foundation podium: Fine-to-medium grained Georgia and New England granite.
- Internal structural elements & backing: Indiana oolitic limestone (Salem Formation) and Portland cement mortar.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- Typology: Neoclassical / Beaux-Arts commemorative rotunda and pantheon-form open-air temple designed by John Russell Pope, Otto R. Eggers, and Daniel P. Higgins (completed 1943).
- Massing & Layout: Symmetrical, circular open colonnade rotunda measuring 165 feet in diameter, surmounted by a stepped shallow masonry dome.
- Elevation & Portico: A north-facing octastyle portico with Ionic colonnade and triangular pediment flanked by circular peristyle colonnade of 26 fluted Ionic columns.
- Planar Rectification: All 26 peristyle columns and 12 portico columns are rectified to plumb vertical alignment. The horizontal entablature, architrave, dentiled cornice, stepped base terraces, and concentric circular parapets are mapped to flat planar geometries without photogrammetry warping.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Facades & Columns: White Imperial Danby marble featuring subtle greyish and pale greenish-brown natural veining, calibrated with fine vertical fluting lines.
- Dome: Segmented concentric limestone courses showing subtle environmental biological patina and mineral leaching typical of high-humidity estuarine microclimates.
- Plaza & Plinth: Axed-finish pale grey granite pavers, granite retaining wall ashlar blocks, and smooth limestone curbs along the curving pedestrian circulation paths.
- Waterway Edges: Cut granite block seawall edge holding back the Tidal Basin waters.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- Mature urban canopy surrounding the memorial: Deciduous Yoshino cherry trees (*Prunus × yedoensis*), Kwanzan cherry (*Prunus serrulata*), American elm (*Ulmus americana*), and pin oaks (*Quercus palustris*).
- Concentric turf terraces composed of managed Kentucky bluegrass (*Poa pratensis*) and tall fescue turfgrass, divided by manicured boxwood (*Buxus sempervirens*) hedges and low yew shrubbery.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All temporary construction fences, site excavation trenches, parked utility trucks, portable equipment, and pedestrians have been systematically eliminated from the view. Retained static civic infrastructure includes permanently fixed bronze bollards, circular stone balustrades, cast-iron park lamps, granite curbs, and asphalt access perimeter roadways.
