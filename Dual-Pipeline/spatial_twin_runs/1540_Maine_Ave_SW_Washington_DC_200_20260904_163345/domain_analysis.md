# Spatial Domain Analysis: 1540 Maine Ave SW, Washington, DC 20004, USA

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean foundation seated upon Atlantic Coastal Plain alluvial terrace deposits (Quaternary silt, sand, gravel) overlying crystalline bedrock of the Piedmont Province (Sykesville Formation granitic gneiss). Monument base stabilized by a mass-concrete underpinning underpinning system over crystalline rock. Primary structural masonry consists of white Cockeysville marble (Maryland calcitic/dolomitic marble) and Texas quarry marble, backed by dense granite and bluestone gneiss ashlar. Surrounding institutional structures utilize Bedford Indiana limestone (Salem Formation calcarenite) with subterranean drainage tied to the reclaimed Tiber Creek basin.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- Washington Monument: Free-standing unreinforced hollow masonry obelisk, 169.04-meter true height, with a 1:10 batter along planar faces terminating in a 16.7-meter pyramidal capstone (pyramidion). Plumbed vertical load-bearing center with visible horizontal demarcation line at the 46-meter mark reflecting the construction hiatus. At the base, a circular granite-paved plaza surrounded by 56 equidistant perimeter flagpoles and a modern low-profile subterranean/ground security screening facility in glass and steel.
- National Museum of African American History and Culture: Three-tiered inverted pyramidal corona clad in perforated cast-aluminum architectural screens atop a subterranean concrete foundation and glazed curtain-wall base.
- Federal Triangle Complex (Herbert C. Hoover Building / Department of Commerce, Ronald Reagan Building): Seven-story monumental Neoclassical and Beaux-Arts administrative complexes featuring continuous rusticated ashlar podiums, giant-order Doric/Ionic colonnades, deep set multipane fenestration grids, and pitched hip roofs clad in Spanish barrel terra cotta tiles.
- Ellipse & Grounds: Geometric concentric and radial crushed-gravel pathways and asphalt service lanes intersecting graded turf berms.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Monument: Fine-grained white Texas marble at the lower third, shifting abruptly to darker, vein-textured Cockeysville dolomitic marble on the upper two-thirds; jointed with historical lime mortar and modern breathable conservation grout. Smooth-faced ashlar dressing, honed planar facets.
- Federal Buildings: Indiana oolitic limestone ashlar facades, red earthenware terra cotta roof tiles, cast-iron spandrel panels, bronze-finished extruded metal window casings.
- Modern Pavilions: Anodized bronze-pigmented cast aluminum filigree panels, low-iron structural glazing, dark patinated steel framing.
- Ground Surfaces: Decomposed granite and crushed bluestone gravel paths, circular granite unit pavers, smooth bituminous asphalt roadways with granite kerbing, manicured cool-season turfgrass (*Poa pratensis* and *Festuca arundinacea*).

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Mature urban forest stands and formal alignments comprising *Ulmus americana* (American elm) bordering the mall avenues, with *Quercus phellos* (willow oak), *Quercus palustris* (pin oak), and *Platanus occidentalis* scattered across the rolling parkland. Early September phenology: full dense canopies in deep late-summer green, minor foliar margin wear, and faint early chlorotic yellowing on interior leaves without full autumn defoliation.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All vehicular traffic, tourist crowds, transient pedestrians, barricades, and temporary park fixtures are removed. Static civil infrastructure including permanent granite bollards, flagpoles, stone curbs, fixed asphalt roadways, and turf topography are preserved.
