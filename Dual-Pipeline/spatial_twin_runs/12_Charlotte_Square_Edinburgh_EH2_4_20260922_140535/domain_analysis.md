# Spatial Domain Analysis: 12 Charlotte Square, Edinburgh EH2 4ET, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- Subterranean Bedrock: Carboniferous sedimentary strata of the Strathclyde Group (Gullane Formation sandstones, siltstones, and mudstones), overlying localized glacial till and basaltic volcanic intrusive sills of the Edinburgh volcanic complex.
- Lithic Sourcing: Primary structural fabric executed in fine-to-medium grained quartzose Craigleith Sandstone (pale buff-grey Carboniferous sandstone from the nearby Craigleith Quarry); secondary rubble masonry and backing in Hailes sandstone. Central monument pedestal composed of polished pink Peterhead granite blocks mounted on grey granite steps.
- Mortar Chemistry: Historic hot-mixed non-hydraulic to feebly hydraulic lime mortar formulated with slaked quicklime (*calcium hydroxide*) and coarse sharp quartzose river sand, with lime-putty pointed ashlar joints.
- Drainage & Substrata: Sloping urban glacial drift over sedimentary bedrock, providing moderate natural sub-surface drainage into the Water of Leith catchment basin.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- Typological Composition: Neoclassical Georgian urban square designed by Robert Adam, anchored axially by West Register House (formerly St George's Church, designed by Robert Reid, 1811–1814), flanked by symmetrical 3-storey, attic, and rusticated basement terraced townhouses.
- Church/Monumental Core: Massive neoclassical dome composition; hexastyle/tetrastyle Roman Ionic entrance portico supporting a heavy triangular pediment; stepping back to a two-tier circular drum flanked by corner piers, supporting a ribbed copper dome topped by a stone lantern with a gilded cross.
- Terrace Grid & Fenestration: Uniform planar ashlar facades; channel-rusticated ground floors with round-arched openings and radiating voussoirs; upper floors divided by giant-order pilasters and continuous stringcourses; fenestration composed of recessed 6-over-6 and 12-pane timber sash-and-case windows set in crisp rectangular dressed surrounds; attic storey featuring lead-flashed slate dormers.
- Central Monument: Scottish National Memorial to Prince Albert (Sir John Steell, 1876); life-size patinated bronze equestrian statue mounted on a multi-tiered polished granite pedestal with inset bronze relief plaques, bronze subsidiary corner figure groups, and a low stone-coped plinth.
- Planar Rectification: All vertical portico columns, pilasters, townhouse party walls, and drum axes rectified to vertical alignment; roof parapets, cornices, and stringcourses flattened into continuous horizontal planes.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Facade Masonry: Pale buff to weathered dark-grey Craigleith sandstone dressed in droved ashlar for main walls, fine-tooled ashlar for columns/entablatures, and channel-grooved rustication on ground storeys.
- Roof & Dome: High-grade dark grey-black Ballachulish slate roof shingles laid in diminishing courses; hemispherical dome clad in standing-seam sheet copper weathered to a uniform pale verdigris-green patina; lead flashing on gutters, dormer cheeks, and pediment copings.
- Metalwork: Dark green-black patinated cast bronze for equestrian figure, bas-reliefs, and corner plinth sculptures; wrought-iron perimeter garden railings with spearhead finials.
- Environmental Weathering & Pathology: Historic industrial coal-soot sulfate encrustation concentrated in sheltered entablatures, column capitals, and rustication reveals; localized pale rain-wash erosion on exposed ashlar surfaces; faint copper carbonate runoff staining on the stone drum base below the copper dome.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- Turf Layer: Mown urban park lawn dominated by perennial ryegrass (*Lolium perenne*) and red fescue (*Festuca rubra*), dense and uniformly green with minimal wear.
- Urban Tree Canopy: Mixed mature parkland specimens comprising Sycamore (*Acer pseudoplatanus*), Common Lime (*Tilia × europaea*), and Wych Elm (*Ulmus glabra*).
- Phenological State (Late September): Canopies remain full to moderately thinned; foliar color transitioning into early autumn senescent chlorosis, characterized by mixed olive-greens, dull lime, and localized margins of ochre-yellow and brown leaf-crisping.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
- Complete elimination of all transient vehicles, street traffic, pedestrians, temporary barriers, municipal road signs, and waste bins from the roadway and foreground lawn.
- Retention strictly confined to immutable civil fabric: permanent stone kerbing, wrought-iron garden enclosure railings, monolithic granite monument base, and mature root systems.
