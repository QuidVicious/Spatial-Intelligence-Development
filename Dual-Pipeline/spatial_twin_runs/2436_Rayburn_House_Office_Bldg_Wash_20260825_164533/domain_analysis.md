# Spatial Domain Analysis: 2436 Rayburn House Office Bldg, Washington, DC 20004, USA

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The site occupies the elevated plateau of Jenkins Hill (Capitol Hill), situated at the Fall Line transition between the crystalline Piedmont province and the Atlantic Coastal Plain unconsolidated sediments (Potomac Formation sands, silts, and clays). Subterranean load-bearing capacity is reinforced by deep gravel-sand strata. The structural lithics of the central complex encompass multiple historic American extraction phases: the original central core features Cretaceous Aquia Creek sandstone (a pale-tan to buff quartzose freestone from Stafford County, Virginia) bonded with historic lime-pozzolan mortar; the flanking House and Senate wings utilize crystalline Lee dolomitic marble from Massachusetts and Cockeysville marble from Maryland; the 20th-century East Front extension is faced with Georgio-Cherokee and Vermont white marble; and the expansive plaza sub-paving and plinths feature pink and gray biotite granite from Mount Airy (North Carolina) and Minnesota.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
The scene captures a vertical nadir/high-angle oblique view centered on the United States Capitol complex (Neoclassical monumental style), flanked to the east by the Supreme Court Building (classical temple pavilion) and the Library of Congress Thomas Jefferson Building (Beaux-Arts Renaissance revival). The Capitol comprises 5 above-ground primary storeys flanking the central rotunda. In the center, the cast-iron double dome designed by Thomas U. Walter rises with a peristyle colonnade, ribbed cupola, and lantern. The Senate (north/left in top-down orientation) and House (south/right) wings present planar rectangular masses with hipped standing-seam copper roofs. All wobbly 3D photogrammetric noise across roof decks, pediments, steps, and balustrades is fully rectified into laser-straight planar parapets, plumb load-bearing exterior walls, true rectangular light wells, and crisp rectilinear East Front plaza paving.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Capitol Dome & Lantern:** 8.9-million-pound multi-tiered structural cast iron, treated in multi-coat warm off-white lead-free protective enamel, displaying soft diffuse luminance and subtle micro-shadowing along decorative castings and iron rib seams.
- **Capitol Roof Surfaces:** 200,000 square feet of multi-panel standing-seam copper sheet, exhibiting variable generations of natural verdigris patina ranging from vivid oxidized sea-green/turquoise to deep sage in older soldered bays.
- **Masonry Facades:** Denser white crystalline marble ashlar blocks along the extensions and East Front, juxtaposed with painted sandstone surfaces; finely weathered with subtle atmospheric carbon wash in sheltered cornices and crisp recessed lime mortar joints.
- **Plazas & Curbs:** Uniform, flame-finished gray-pink granite modular pavers across the East Plaza and East-West promenade, flanked by dressed granite retaining curbs, water-resistant bronze drain grates, and subsurface skylight apertures of structural laminated safety glass.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
The surrounding 58-acre Capitol Square grounds—designed by Frederick Law Olmsted Sr. and maintained as a Level III Arboretum—feature a rich, dense canopy of mature, fully articulated organic deciduous and evergreen trees. Documented botanical species include towering American Elms (*Ulmus americana*), Northern Red Oaks (*Quercus rubra*), Ginkgo biloba, Sugar Maples (*Acer saccharum*), and native flowering Dogwoods (*Cornus florida*). Canopies present organic branch architecture, translucent green foliage, and natural leaf-mass shadowing over meticulously manicured turf lawns and curvilinear crushed-gravel pathways.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All temporary and transient urban noise is excised: zero motor vehicles (passenger sedans, security cruisers, service vans, buses), zero pedestrians or tourists, and zero temporary construction sheds, dumpsters, or loose stanchions. Fully retained are the permanent historic hardscapes: Olmsted's curved aggregate walkways, carved granite perimeter curbs, cast-iron hexagonal bollards, decorative bronze lantern posts, static flagpole bases, and permanent structural tree grates.
