# Spatial Domain Analysis: 53 Old Santa Fe Trail, Santa Fe, NM 87501, USA

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- Bedrock & Lithic Substrate: Unconsolidated alluvial basin fill, sand, gravel, and silt of the Santa Fe Group (Tesuque Formation) overlying Precambrian crystalline basement rock of the Sangre de Cristo uplift.
- Native Structural Material: Sun-dried earthen adobe mud bricks formulated from local sandy-clay soils and straw binders; native river cobbles and volcanic basalt fieldstones used in baseline foundations.
- Finish & Binding Chemistry: Traditional hydraulic lime, sand, and mud plasters over adobe core, overlaid in later interventions by cementitious earth-toned stucco coats.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- Typologies: Spanish-Pueblo Colonial civic architecture and 20th-century Pueblo Revival/Territorial Revival institutional massing (Palace of the Governors and New Mexico Museum of Art complex).
- Storey Heights & Massing: Single-storey elongated colonnaded civic block with stepped planar massing; adjacent multi-level (2 to 3 storeys) Pueblo Revival complex featuring asymmetrical stepped terracing, organic rounded corners, and battered load-bearing profiles.
- Structural Rectification: Vertical wall planes plumbed to true gravity vertical; flat roofs aligned with horizontal parapet capping; portal colonnade rectified along linear structural beam lines with regular post spacing.
- Roof & Drainage Geometry: Flat built-up membrane roofs contained by parapets; integrated timber *canales* (drainage spouts) and sheet metal flashing collectors.
- Fenestration: Deeply recessed multi-pane rectangular timber casement and double-hung sash windows with stained wood lintels.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Facades: Textured ochre, tan, and buff earthen-toned cementitious stucco over historic sun-dried adobe masonry and structural wood frames.
- Colonnade / Portal Fabric: Peeled Ponderosa pine (*Pinus ponderosa*) log posts, hand-carved decorative *zapata* (corbel) capitals, and squared heavy timber lintels.
- Timber Accents: Projecting round timber *vigas* (rafter ends) penetrating the exterior parapet walls.
- Ground Surfaces: Poured concrete sidewalks, split-face flagstone walkways under the covered portal, granite/concrete curbs, and dense asphalt roadway paving.
- Weathering / Patina: Arid-climate weathering, minor efflorescence and thermal hairline fissures in stucco coatings, water-staining under timber canales, and sun-bleached silvering on exposed wooden post bases.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- Specimen Identification: *Populus deltoides subsp. wislizeni* (Rio Grande cottonwood) and *Fraxinus velutina* (velvet ash).
- Canopy Structure & Phenology: Large, high-volume broadleaf crowns with complex multi-stem branching; mid-to-late autumn phenology displaying foliage transition from dull green to uniform golden-yellow senescence with early partial leaf drop.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
- Removal of all transient pedestrians beneath the covered portal and on pedestrian sidewalks.
- Complete purging of all moving and parked motor vehicles from street intersections and roadways.
- Elimination of temporary event signage, trash bins, and street-level clutter; retention of fixed curbed pavements, structural posts, and mature arboriculture.
