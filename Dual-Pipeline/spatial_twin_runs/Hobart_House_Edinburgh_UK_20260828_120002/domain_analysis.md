# Spatial Domain Analysis: Hobart House, Edinburgh, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean formation comprises Lower Carboniferous sedimentary bedrock of the Strathclyde Group (sandstone, siltstone, and mudstone sequences with intrusive quartz-dolerite sills). Superficial deposits consist of dense Quaternary glacial till (boulder clay) overlying well-drained sloping topography between George Street ridge and the North Loch basin. Primary lithic building fabric consists of fine-to-medium grained Carboniferous quartz-arenite sandstone quarried locally from Craigleith and Hailes, complemented by Victorian blonde and Locharbriggs/Dumfriesshire red Permian sandstone variants. Bedding mortar utilizes non-hydraulic lime and sharp pit sand mixes with historic pointing exhibiting porous carbonation. Subterranean drainage routes through engineered stone culverts integrated into Georgian vaulted cellar infrastructure below the street pavements.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Gridiron urban plan conforming to James Craig’s 1767 Edinburgh New Town layout. Prominent corner block exhibits late 19th-century Scots Baronial / Victorian commercial revival adaptations, featuring a polygonal corner turret (tourelle) capped with a conical slate roof, corbelled upper levels, and multi-bay mansard attic storeys punctuated by zinc/lead-clad pitched dormers. Flanking Hanover Street and Rose Street frontages comprise 3-to-4-storey over raised basement Georgian classical tenements and commercial premises. Vertical planes rectified to true gravity vertical, eliminating photogrammetry mesh warping. Rooflines regularized with crisp slate pitches, lead valley gutters, rectilinear stone chimney stacks with terracotta pots, and aligned multi-light fenestration (predominantly 6-over-6 and 2-over-2 timber sash-and-case frames).

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Primary Facades: Droved and polished Craigleith blonde/buff sandstone ashlar, contrasting red sandstone ashlar dressing at corner turret block, rock-faced rustications at basement/ground levels, and droved margin dressings.
- Roofing: Scottish West Highland and Welsh Penrhyn dark blue-grey metamorphic slate tiling laid in diminishing courses; rolled sheet lead flashings, ridges, and valley gutters; flat mineral-felt and zinc roofing over central attic platforms.
- Fenestration & Metalwork: Painted softwood sliding sash-and-case window assemblies, cast-iron rainwater downpipes, hoppers, and wrought/cast-iron perimeter basement area railings.
- Pathology & Weathering: Localized atmospheric coal-smoke crust deposits along protected stringcourses, damp-induced chlorosis and lichen biofilms within recessed stone cornices, granular rain-wash attrition on exposed sandstone friezes, and lead oxidation patination.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Urban core canyon environment with minimal canopy intrusion; isolated courtyard and rear lane specimens include *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore) and *Betula pendula* (Silver Birch). Late-summer phenological state: dense mature foliage displaying deep green chlorophyll saturation, terminal shoot elongation completed, and early micro-stress chlorosis on margins of upper canopy leaves.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient visual clutter removed: zero vehicles, zero pedestrians, zero commercial dumpsters, zero temporary barricades, zero freestanding A-frame signs, and zero discarded refuse. Permanent civil fabric retained: dressed whinstone and granite kerbing, Caithness stone paving flags, historic cast-iron streetlamp posts, and continuous property boundary railings.
