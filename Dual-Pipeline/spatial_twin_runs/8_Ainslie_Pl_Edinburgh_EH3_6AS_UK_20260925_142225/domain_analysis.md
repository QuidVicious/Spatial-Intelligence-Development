# Spatial Domain Analysis: 8 Ainslie Pl, Edinburgh EH3 6AS, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean foundation: Carboniferous sedimentary bedrock sequence of the Calciferous Sandstone Measure, overlain by dense Devensian glacial lodgement till. 
Structural lithics: Fine-grained, silica-cemented pale buff and grey Craigleith quartz-arenite sandstone and Hailes micaceous sandstone, quarried locally for high compressive strength and low porosity.
Paving and kerbing: Basalt and quartz-dolerite (Scottish whinstone) street kerbstones with fissile Caithness flagstone pedestrian footways.
Mortar chemistry: Traditional hot-mixed feebly hydraulic slaked high-calcium lime binder with sharp, graded quartz river aggregate and crushed sandstone fines.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Typology: Late Georgian / Regency Neoclassical residential circus crescent designed by James Gillespie Graham (Moray Estate, c. 1822–1824).
Planar massing: Multi-bay curving ashlar facade arranged across 3 primary storeys, plus a continuous sunk basement lightwell and continuous attic floor behind an ashlar blocking course. Vertical planes rectified to gravity plumb.
Ground floor: Channelled V-jointed ashlar rustication, segmental and round-arched entryways with fanlights, radiated voussoirs, and droved stone platts crossing the lightwells.
Piano nobile (First floor): Tall rectilinear window openings framed by architraved surrounds, supported by a continuous projecting cantilevered stone band course; central bays flanked by engaged fluted Ionic/Doric pilasters.
Second floor and Attic: Diminished-height rectilinear fenestration, crowned by a continuous entablature with moulded architrave, plain frieze, projecting modillion cornice, and stone parapet.
Roof & Chimneys: Double-pitched timber roof structures clad in dark Scottish and Westmorland slates; uniform transverse ashlar masonry party-wall chimney stacks fitted with cylindrical ceramic chimneypots.
Basement lightwell: Continuous dry area retaining wall lined with droved sandstone ashlar, enclosed by cast-iron spear-headed railings with urn-capped standards.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
Façade fabric: Broached, droved, and polished pale blonde Craigleith sandstone ashlar blocks laid in regular ashlar coursing with tight 3mm lime-putty joints.
Weathering pathology: Stable legacy atmospheric carbon encrustation (coal-smoke soot patina) in sheltered architectural recesses, under-cornices, and rustication channels; localized rain-wash leaching and mineral surface exfoliation along windward water-run paths.
Fenestration: Painted timber double-hung sash-and-case frames in 6-over-6 and 2-over-2 configurations set deep in stone reveals with linseed-oil putty glazing.
Ironwork: Wrought and cast-iron decorative balcony railings, basement balustrades, and bracketed over-door lamp irons finished in protective black alkyd paint.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Central garden parkland: Mature specimen trees within Ainslie Place Gardens.
Dominant species: *Ulmus glabra* (Wych elm) exhibiting high-domed open crowns with robust, twisting primary boughs and deeply furrowed bark; *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore) displaying massive single trunks, wide-spreading horizontal branch structure, and dense rounded crown architecture; *Tilia × europaea* (Common lime) with vertical branching, heavy trunk burrs, and high-volume ascending canopies.
Sub-canopy: *Taxus baccata* (European yew) and *Prunus laurocerasus* dense branching framework forming clipped boundary hedgerows inside the perimeter enclosure.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient vehicles, vehicular traffic, parked cars, pedestrians, dumpsters, temporary signage, and photogrammetry polygon fragmentation completely expunged. Static urban fabric, historic cast-iron gas-converted lamp posts, flagstones, stone kerbs, and mature vegetation framework fully preserved.
