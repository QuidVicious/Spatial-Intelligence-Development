# Spatial Domain Analysis: 14 Albany St, Edinburgh EH1 3QB, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The subterranean geology comprises Lower Carboniferous fluvio-deltaic bedrock from the Gullane Formation and Strathclyde Group (sandstone, siltstone, and mudstone intervals) overlain by dense glacial till (boulder clay). Primary load-bearing masonry is extracted from regional quarries: dense, fine-grained buff Craigleith quartzitic sandstone paired with laminated grey Hailes and Binny sandstones. Bedding planes are oriented horizontally to withstand compressive loading, assembled with hot-mixed non-hydraulic to weakly hydraulic lime mortar with washed river aggregate. Natural grade slopes northward toward the Water of Leith basin, engineered with subterranean barrel-vaulted cellars and stone-paved sunken basement lightwells for moisture dissipation.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Classic Regency and Georgian New Town urban composition planned by Robert Reid and William Sibbald (1801–1824). Uniform streetwall blocks, crescent quadrants, and rectilinear terraces feature rigid planar alignments with vertical gravity rectification. Structural configurations consist of 3-to-4-storey elevations over rusticated sunken basements bridged by stone platts with wrought iron spearhead balustrades. Piano nobile and upper floors display polished ashlar stone facades with moulded stringcourses, continuous eaves cornices, and blocking courses. Rooflines are double-pitched timber rafters clad in dark grey Ballachulish slate, punctuated by regular canted and piended slate-clad dormers and massive ashlar wall-head chimney stacks supporting arrays of terracotta chimney pots. Fenestration is strictly aligned to a vertical grid of 6-over-6 and 12-pane timber sash-and-case windows with thin glazing bars (astragals).

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Historic Facades:** Droved and polished Craigleith sandstone ashlar; channelled rustication on ground floors; droved margin and broached/rock-faced basement retaining walls.
- **Roofing & Flashings:** Dark grey Scottish Ballachulish slate tiles laid in diminishing courses; rolled sheet lead flashings, ridges, and valley gutters.
- **Civil Surfaces:** Paved Caithness flagstone footways, polished granite/whinstone kerbing, and squared porphyry and whinstone street setts.
- **Weathering Pathology:** Coal-smoke encrustations and localized carbonaceous crusts in shielded lintel undercuts; pale granular rain-wash patterns across exposed ashlar faces; sub-surface damp staining on lower basement ashlar plinths.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Drummond Place Gardens features mature urban woodland enclosed by perimeter wrought-iron railings. Canopy species: *Ulmus glabra* (Wych elm), *Platanus × acerifolia* (London plane), *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore maple), *Fagus sylvatica* (European beech), and *Tilia × europaea* (Common lime). Date phenology (November 11): Advanced late-autumn senescence with 75% to 85% defoliation; stark branch architectures and fractal limb systems exposed; remaining leaves present as sparse russet, ochre, and deep umber patches; garden lawn surfaces are covered in damp fallen leaf litter.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient visual clutter is purged: zero vehicular traffic, zero parked cars, zero pedestrians, zero trash bins, and zero modern temporary signage. Preserved elements: permanent stone kerbs, iron railings, heritage cast-iron fluted lampposts, and mature vegetation.
