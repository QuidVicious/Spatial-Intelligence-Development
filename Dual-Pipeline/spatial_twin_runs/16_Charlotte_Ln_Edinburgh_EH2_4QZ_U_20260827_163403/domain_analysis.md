# Spatial Domain Analysis: 16 Charlotte Ln, Edinburgh EH2 4QZ, UK

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The subterranean geology of Edinburgh's West End comprises Lower Carboniferous sedimentary formations of the Strathclyde and Inverclyde Groups, dominated by sandstones, siltstones, and mudstones overlain by dense glacial till (boulder clay). Primary structural load-bearing envelopes and ashlar elements are quarried from indigenous Craigleith and Hailes pale blonde-to-buff Carboniferous sandstones, bonded with traditional non-hydraulic to weakly hydraulic lime mortar (slaked quicklime and sharp siliceous sand aggregate). Ground carriage paths and lane setts are composed of durable Whinstone (quartz-dolerite/basalt) paving blocks, directing surface run-off along perimeter stone channels into municipal subsurface culverts.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
A high-density urban intersection situated within Edinburgh’s New Town conservation area, showcasing late Georgian neoclassical terrace tenements seamlessly juxtaposed against late Victorian revivalist corner architecture.
- Northwest Terraced Block (Charlotte Lane / Hope Street Lane): Unified 3-to-4 storey plus basement Georgian ashlar tenement facade; rigorously rectilinear floor plates; regular rhythm of 12-over-12 and 6-over-6 timber sash and case windows; rusticated/channelled ashlar ground storey with projecting string courses; mansard-style slate roof plane broken by regular gabled and lead-cheeked dormers.
- Corner Structure (Centre): A 3-storey late 19th-century Old English / Tudor-revivalist pavilion corner block; masonry-framed ground floor public house frontage featuring pilasters and heavy lintels; upper storeys characterized by decorative exposed half-timbering with diagonal bracing and rendered infill panels; steeply pitched red clay pantile/terracotta multi-gabled roof punctuated by tall, rectilinear red-brick chimney stacks and decorative bargeboards.
- Foreground and Adjacent Blocks: Straightened multi-storey ashlar New Town domestic and institutional tenements with prominent parapets, corniced eaves courses, dressed chimney heads with terracotta pots, and a circular lead-clad dome/cupola pavilion atop the rightmost roofscape.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Facades: Craigleith pale blonde sandstone ashlar with subtle ochre/honey undertones, channelled rustication at lower storeys, showing authentic environmental soot darkening and carbon crusting along drip edges and recessed lintels; upper corner facade finished in dark-stained oak half-timber framing set against off-white lime/cement render.
- Roofscapes: Scottish and Welsh blue-grey roofing slate in regular diminishing courses; terracotta ridges; weathered lead flashings, copper valleys with verdigris patina, and red terracotta clay tiles on the revivalist gables.
- Ground Surface: Dressed whinstone and granite paving setts across the carriageway, bordered by wide, hand-tooled sandstone kerbstones and flagstone pedestrian footpaths.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Sparse, managed urban ecology featuring isolated mature broadleaf street trees (such as *Acer pseudoplatanus* / Sycamore and *Betula pendula* / Silver Birch) visible within rear courtyard enclosures and along lane margins, displaying rich, natural leafy canopies and fine organic branching patterns clearly differentiated from stone masonry.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient visual clutter has been eradicated: zero parked or moving motor vehicles, zero pedestrians, zero rubbish skips/dumpsters, and zero temporary paper signage or street maintenance barriers. Fixed historic infrastructure is retained: cast-iron perimeter area railings, stone kerbing, cast-iron streetlamp columns, and engraved stone signage.
