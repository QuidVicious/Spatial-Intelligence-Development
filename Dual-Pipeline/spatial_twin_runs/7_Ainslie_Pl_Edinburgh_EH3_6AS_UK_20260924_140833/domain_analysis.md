# Spatial Domain Analysis: 7 Ainslie Pl, Edinburgh EH3 6AS, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- Bedrock: Carboniferous sedimentary strata (Calciferous Sandstone Measures) overlaid with glacial till.
- Lithic Fabric: Local pale buff-grey Craigleith and Hailes Carboniferous quartz-arenite sandstones.
- Mortar & Drainage: Traditional hot-mixed non-hydraulic lime mortar; free-draining sloping garden topsoil over gravelly subsoil.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- Typology: Edinburgh New Town Neoclassical Georgian residential circus (Moray Estate, James Gillespie Graham, c. 1822).
- Massing & Profile: 3-storey plus attic and rusticated basement terraces arranged in a curved arc; rear distant copper-clad church dome.
- Facade Grid: Regular multi-bay ashlar facades, 6-over-6 and 12-over-12 timber sash-and-case fenestration, projecting continuous stringcourses, modillion cornices, and pitched Scottish slate roofs punctuated by tall ashlar chimney stacks with terracotta pots.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Primary Facades: Fine-droved and polished Craigleith sandstone ashlar with channelled rustication at ground level.
- Weathering & Patina: Atmospheric coal-smoke carbon crusting in sheltered cornices, rain-washed stone projections, light biological greening on damp basement copings.
- Enclosure & Ground: Wrought and cast-iron perimeter spearhead railings with stone plinths; crushed whinstone gravel paths intersecting natural turf lawns.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- Urban Canopy: Mature *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), *Tilia × europaea* (Lime), and *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm) specimens within central circus garden.
- Phenology (December 24): Full winter dormancy; complete deciduous defoliation exhibiting bare, intricate branch architecture over dormant, damp perennial turf.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
- All photogrammetry polygon fragmentation, vehicle artifacts, pedestrians, and transient street clutter fully removed; static Georgian civil infrastructure, railings, stone curbs, and bare botanical structures preserved.
