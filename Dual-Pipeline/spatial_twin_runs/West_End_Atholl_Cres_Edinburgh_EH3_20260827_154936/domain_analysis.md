# Spatial Domain Analysis: West End, Atholl Cres, Edinburgh EH3 8HA, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The terrain sits atop Lower Carboniferous sedimentary formations of the Inverclyde and Strathclyde Groups, characterized by interbedded sandstones, siltstones, and mudstones with underlying volcanic basalt intrusions typical of the Lothian basin. The primary building stone dominating the structural fabric is pale golden-grey Craigleith and Hailes sandstone (quartz-arenite), bedded with traditional non-hydraulic to weakly hydraulic lime mortar (slaked quicklime with sharp silica aggregate). Subterranean moisture is managed through 19th-century subsurface brick and stone-culvert drainage channels directing runoff toward the Water of Leith catchment.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
A panoramic streetscape looking northeast down Shandwick Place towards Princes Street and the New Town:
- Foreground/Left: Neoclassical curved residential terraces of Atholl Crescent (designed by Thomas Bonnar, c. 1825) rising 3 to 4 storeys over raised basement areas with ashlar frontages, horizontal stringcourses, timber-framed multi-pane sash windows, cantilevered stone steps, and continuous mansard slate roofs punctuated by regularly spaced dormer windows and tall stone chimney stacks.
- Center Midground: The imposing 56-meter Venetian campanile clock tower and Roman Baroque church body of the former St George’s West Church (now Charlotte Chapel, main hall by David Bryce 1866–69, tower by Sir Robert Rowand Anderson 1879–81). The tower features rusticated and vermiculated base courses, smooth ashlar shaft with slit vents, a square louvered belfry stage with clock dials, and a copper-clad pyramidal spire.
- Distant Left/Horizon: The elevated copper-domed rotunda of West Register House (former St George's Church, Charlotte Square, by Robert Reid, 1811–14) framed against the Firth of Forth.
- Foreground/Right: Flanking New Town tenements and commercial frontages along Shandwick Place and Atholl Place, exhibiting classical ashlar orders, rusticated ground-floor masonry, regular 4-storey massing, and lead-flashed parapets. All facades are plumbed to true gravitational vertical, with planar balconies and sharp rectilinear fenestration.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Facades: Craigleith and Hailes sandstone ashlar, with deeply weathered golden-buff to soot-darkened grey patina from two centuries of historic urban coal-smoke exposure. Lower rusticated storeys show fine droved and vermiculated tooling.
- Roofing: Scottish Ballachulish slate roofs, dark blue-grey, laid in diminishing courses; lead valley gutters and ridge flashings; verdigris-oxidized copper cladding on the campanile pyramid and distant West Register House dome.
- Ironwork & Details: Wrought-iron basement railings and cast-iron balcony balustrades coated in matte black bituminous paint; polished granite and sandstone kerbstones bordering asphalt roadways and embedded steel tram rails.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- Atholl Crescent Gardens (foreground left/center) contains mature broadleaf urban vegetation: European limes (*Tilia × europaea*), sycamore maples (*Acer pseudoplatanus*), English elms (*Ulmus minor*), and rowan (*Sorbus aucuparia*), rendered with dense organic leafy canopies, detailed leaf clusters, and natural woody branching, fully disambiguated from adjacent stone walls.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient visual noise has been strictly removed: zero vehicles, zero buses, zero moving or parked automobiles, zero pedestrians, zero dumpsters, and zero temporary construction debris. Permanent civil infrastructure—smooth asphalt street surfaces, embedded double tram lines, granite kerbs, cast-iron streetlamps, and perimeter stone plinths—remains immaculately preserved.
