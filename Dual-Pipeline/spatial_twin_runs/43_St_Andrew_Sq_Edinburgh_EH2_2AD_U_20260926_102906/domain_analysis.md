# Spatial Domain Analysis: 43 St Andrew Sq, Edinburgh EH2 2AD, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean formation composed of Carboniferous sedimentary sequences interbedded with calciferous sandstones and the intrusive igneous volcanic suites of the Arthur's Seat and Calton Hill basaltic/dolerite complex. Primary structural masonry derived from regional Lower Carboniferous quarries, notably fine-grained quartzose Craigleith and Binny sandstones, featuring high silica content and durability. Mortar chemistry comprises traditional non-hydraulic to weakly hydraulic slaked quicklime blended with sharp, coarse local pit sand. Sub-base drainage relies on natural glacial till gradients sloping east-southeast toward the Firth of Forth basin.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Central monumental verticality anchored by the Melville Monument: a 42-metre fluted ashlar Roman Doric column set upon a massive square stepped rusticated plinth, terminating in an abacus, circular drum pedestal, and colossal draped stone statuary. Surrounding urban fabric comprises an orthogonal New Town perimeter: 
- East and North flanks: 4-to-5-storey classical Georgian palazzo blocks and Palladian pavilions with channelled rustication at ground tier, ashlar piano nobile with pedimented window aedicules, 6-over-6 timber sash fenestration, continuous moulded stringcourses, and mansard slate roofs punctuated by dormers and ashlar chimney stacks.
- Distance south-east: Victorian-Edwardian eclectic baronial clock tower rising with stepped bartizans, stone balustrades, and a domed cupola.
- South-east flank: Modern commercial 6-storey civic office block defined by a rigid rectilinear grid of continuous vertical extruded stone/aluminium composite piers and inset glazing.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Monument & Historic Facades: Fine-grained Craigleith buff-grey sandstone ashlar, displaying localized atmospheric coal-smoke crusts, alveolar weathering, and pale sulfate leaching along drip courses; Welsh and Scottish slates on pitched roofs; cast iron railings.
- Modern Infill: Textured precast reconstituted stone fins, dark bronze anodised aluminium curtain-wall profiles, and low-reflectance architectural float glass.
- Ground Plane: Radial self-binding gravel and crushed granite pedestrian paths, fine turf sod, Caithness flagstone paving slabs, dressed whinstone kerbs, and asphalt roadway perimeter.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Civic garden square framed by mature urban specimens:
- *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore): Stout upright boles with scaly, flaking bark, broad multi-stem scaffold branching forming dense globular crowns, 12–18 m height.
- *Tilia × europaea* (Common Lime): Upright trunks with prominent basal epicormic burrs, arching secondary scaffold limbs, pyramidal-to-ovoid crown architecture.
- *Betula pendula* (Silver Birch): Slender pendulous branchlets, peeling white papery bark with dark diamond-shaped fissures, narrow columnar habit.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All vehicular traffic, buses, tram units, temporary construction sheeting, scaffolding, transient pedestrians, commercial street signage, and waste receptacles have been eliminated. Fixed heritage cast-iron lamp posts, stone balustrades, perimeter railings, and permanent architectural surfaces are strictly preserved.
