# Spatial Domain Analysis: 1540 Maine Ave SW, Washington, DC 20004, USA

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean foundation seated on Atlantic Coastal Plain alluvial silt and gravel over dense Piedmont crystalline metamorphic bedrock (Sykesville Formation gneiss and schist). Primary monument lithics comprise Cockeysville white marble ashlar at the lower 46-meter tier, transitioning cleanly to Massachusetts and Maine white marble ashlar on upper courses, backed by coursed granite rubble and bluestone gneiss. Surrounding background architecture features Bedford Indiana oolitic limestone, coarse-grained granite plinths, and structural terracotta tile underlayment.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Central monumental obelisk standing at 169.3 meters, displaying precise 10:1 height-to-base taper terminating in a 16.8-meter pyramidal apex (pyramidion). Plumb vertical masonry courses with rectified planar geometry. Base surrounded by a concentric paved circular plaza with fifty radial flagpole stanchions and a low-profile cuboid security pavilion at the east portal. Midground features the National Museum of African American History and Culture exhibiting a three-tiered inverted trapezoidal corona (ziggurat massing). Federal Triangle urban perimeter comprises Classical Revival and Beaux-Arts federal administrative complexes with rusticated ground stories, monumental Doric/Ionic colonnades, entablatures, and pitched hipped roofs.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
Monument exterior composed of dressed, fine-jointed white marble ashlar with distinct lithic hue variation at the 46-meter construction seam; apex clad in solid marble with a cast aluminum capstone. Museum corona constructed of architectural bronze-coated cast aluminum openwork grilles over dark structural glass curtain walls. Background federal buildings composed of buff Indiana limestone ashlar facades, granite basements, and Spanish barrel red-clay roof tiles. Ground plane composed of crushed granite aggregate walkways, dressed granite curbing, circular scored bluestone plaza pavers, and manicured turf grass.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Surrounding parkland and boulevards populated by mature *Ulmus americana* (American elm), *Quercus phellos* (willow oak), *Quercus alba* (white oak), and *Platanus occidentalis* (American sycamore). Early September phenology exhibits full, dense crown volumes with deep emerald to mature olive-green leaf pigmentation, displaying early late-summer canopy heaviness without active autumnal abscission.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All vehicular traffic on Constitution Avenue and adjacent perimeter roads, pedestrian crowds, tour groups, temporary crowd-control barricades, and maintenance equipment are completely removed. Fixed flagpole rings, permanent granite bollards, paved pathways, and historical masonry surfaces remain intact and unobstructed.
