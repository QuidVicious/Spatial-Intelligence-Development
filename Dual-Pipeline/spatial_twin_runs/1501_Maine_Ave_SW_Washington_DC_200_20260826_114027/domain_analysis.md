## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- **Centerpiece (Washington Monument):** Pure Neoclassical Egyptian-revival obelisk rising to an exact height of 555 feet 5⅛ inches (169.046 m). Rectified vertical lines taper from a 55-foot 1.5-inch square base to a 34-foot 5.5-inch width beneath the 55-foot pyramidion capstone. Solid aluminum pyramidal apex. Planar lines are aligned to true vertical gravity, eliminating mesh tessellation and elevation wobble.
- **Center-Right Flank (National Museum of African American History and Culture - NMAAHC):** Contemporary inverted tiered corona architecture designed by Freelon Adjaye Bond/SmithGroup. Three-tiered outward-sloping trapezoidal volumes derived from traditional Yoruban caryatid capitals, wrapped in 3,600 perforated cast-aluminum architectural screens with deep bronze finish. Rectified horizontal band levels and pristine curtain-wall glazing underneath.
- **Midground / Background Right (Federal Triangle Complex):** Neoclassical and Classical Revival civic massings, notably the Herbert C. Hoover Department of Commerce Building (7 storeys, Doric colonnaded limestone screens, hipped red Spanish clay-tile roofs, multiple internal light courts), the Ronald Reagan Building, and the Internal Revenue Service Building.
- **Midground / Background Left:** Ellipse grounds leading north to the White House complex, flanked by the Eisenhower Executive Office Building (French Second Empire granite mansard massing) and the Treasury Building (Greek Revival colonnade).
- **Urban Grid:** Rigid L'Enfant street grid receding toward downtown DC, strictly rectified with uniform horizontal roof parapets governed by the DC Height of Buildings Act.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Washington Monument:** Differentiated two-tone marble cladding showing an authentic horizontal color transition at the 152-foot level—warmer, weathered, slightly patinated cream-gray marble on the lower third versus brighter, colder, less-weathered marble across the upper two-thirds. Subdued gypsum wash in joints, faint micro-streaking from century-scale atmospheric precipitation, and fine matte crystalline grain.
- **NMAAHC:** Dark matte bronze-PVDF coated architectural cast-aluminum lattice panels with open filigree patterns, low-iron structural curtain wall glazing, dark polished Mesabi black granite plinth, and concrete cantilevered entrance canopy.
- **Federal Triangle Buildings:** Smooth-dressed buff/gray Indiana Bedford limestone facade panels with subtle soot-shadowing along sheltered friezes and dentil cornices; rusticated gray granite bases; unglazed terracotta barrel clay roof tiles; oxidized bronze window sash framing.
- **Hardscape:** Crushed bluestone and aggregate gravel pathways encircling the monument plaza, scored granite pavers, steel flagpoles, and asphalt radial roads edged with dressed granite curbing.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- **Canopy Trees:** Dominated by mature American Elm (*Ulmus americana*), London Plane trees (*Platanus × acerifolia*), Pin Oaks (*Quercus palustris*), Willow Oaks (*Quercus phellos*), and American Sycamores (*Platanus occidentalis*).
- **Botanical Detailing:** Deep summer-green, dense deciduous canopies with intricate branching structures, cast-shadow volume, and individual leaf clusters completely separated from architectural planes.
- **Turfgrass:** Manicured cool-season turf mix consisting of Kentucky bluegrass (*Poa pratensis*) and tall fescue, displaying mown radial maintenance swaths, slight wear along pedestrian pathways, and lush organic variation across the Monument grounds and The Ellipse.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All temporary vehicular traffic, tourist shuttles, buses, parked maintenance vehicles, transient pedestrian crowds, security barricades, temporary signage, event tents, and loose trash receptacles have been systematically removed. The scene is preserved exclusively with static, permanent civil infrastructure: granite curb lines, circular flag arrays with crisp vertical stainless steel masts, fixed street lamp standards, paved pedestrian paths, and permanent architectural bollards.
