# Spatial Domain Analysis: 7 Ainslie Pl, Edinburgh EH3 6AS, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean foundation composed of Lower Carboniferous sedimentary sandstone, siltstone, and mudstone of the Strathclyde and Inverclyde Groups, overlain by dense glacial till. Primary structural lithic is pale buff to light-grey Craigleith quartzose sandstone, supplemented with brown-buff Hailes laminated sandstone for basement retaining walls and rear elevations. Mortar consists of traditional hot-mixed calcitic lime and sharp river sand aggregate.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Moray Estate late-Georgian Neoclassical crescent and circus urbanism designed by James Gillespie Graham (c. 1822–1824). Symmetrical palace-fronted curving terrace: four-storey and basement continuous ashlar facades with pitched attic storeys behind parapets. Ground floor treated with horizontal channeled rustication and segmental-arched or square-headed openings. Piano nobile and upper floors feature smooth ashlar, molded architraves, continuous string courses, and mutule cornice surmounted by a stone blocking course. Slate-clad mansard and pitched roofs punctuated by regularly spaced gabled and segmental-headed timber-framed dormers; regular rhythm of stone-coped ashlar chimney stacks with earthenware cylindrical flues. Fenestration is timber sash-and-case in 6-over-6 and 12-over-12 configurations. Sunk basement lightwells protected by spearhead cast-iron balustrades. In the distant skyline, a classical octagonal drum and ribbed dome clad in standing-seam copper.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
Craigleith fine-grained ashlar sandstone with fine droved and polished dressing; channeled rustication at basement and ground levels. Blue-grey Scottish Ballachulish roof slates with lead flashing, ridging, and valley gutters. Dark green verdigris oxidized copper sheeting on distant drum dome. Structural wrought and cast iron railings, gatepiers, and lampposts with black oil-paint finish. Paved roadways of asphalt bounded by tooled Scottish whinstone kerbing; internal garden circuits surfaced in compacted crushed gravel and hoggin. Weathering manifests as historic localized carbonaceous coal-smoke crusts in sheltered cornices and under-sills, juxtaposed with rain-washed buff stone faces and slight algae patina on low stone basement copings.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Central garden enclosure planted with mature specimen broadleaf urban trees: Sycamore (*Acer pseudoplatanus*), Common Lime (*Tilia × europaea*), and Wych Elm (*Ulmus glabra*). Large ascending trunks with dense multi-tiered ramification, spreading spherical and ovate canopy volumes ranging from 12 to 22 meters in height, rooted in maintained turf with gravel path networks.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All vehicular traffic, parked cars, transient pedestrians, modern street signage, refuse bins, and temporary obstacles are eliminated. Permanent civil infrastructure—stone kerbs, cast-iron railings, stone basement steps, and historical lampposts—is fully preserved.
