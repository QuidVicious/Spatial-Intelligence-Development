# Spatial Domain Analysis: 80 George St, Edinburgh EH2 3BU, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- Bedrock: Carboniferous sedimentary strata of the Gullane Formation (Strathclyde Group), composed of interbedded pale sandstones, siltstones, and mudstones overlying regional volcanic basalt and dolerite sills.
- Facade Lithics: Primary building stone is fine-grained, siliceous Craigleith sandstone ("liver rock" variety), ranging in hue from pale cream to silver-grey, with subordinate Hailes sandstone in rear walls and foundations.
- Statuary Lithics: Monumental pedestal composed of dressed, fine-grained Scottish granite (Peterhead/Aberdeenshire) with fine-honed margins.
- Mortar Chemistry: Traditional hot-mixed slaked non-hydraulic to weakly hydraulic lime mortar with washed quartz aggregate in hairline 2–3mm ashlar joints.
- Hydrology & Subterranean Dynamics: Permeable glacial till overburden over sloping sandstone bedrock, providing natural northern subterranean drainage toward the Firth of Forth basin.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- Typology: Edinburgh New Town late-18th/early-19th-century Georgian neoclassical terraces and Victorian commercial adaptations planned along James Craig’s axial grid.
- Massing & Heights: Symmetrical 3-to-4-storey terraced blocks over sunken basement lightwells, terminated by slate-clad pitched mansard roofs with regular gabled and flat-topped dormer windows.
- Fenestration Grid: Strictly aligned vertical bays featuring timber-framed sash-and-case windows (predominantly 6-over-6 and 2-over-2 glazing pane divisions) within crisp, chamfered ashlar reveals.
- Ground Floor Treatment: Deeply channelled V-groove rustication on sandstone ground floors, punctuated by semicircular arch-headed doorways, classical entablatures, moulded string courses, and modillion eaves cornices.
- Axial Centerpiece: Bronze monument of William Pitt the Younger (Sir Francis Chantrey, 1833) set on a tall, square-section pedestal at the Frederick Street intersection; background axial termination aligns with the fluted Roman Doric column of the Melville Monument (William Burn) in St Andrew Square.
- Planar Rectification: Complete plumb rectification of all facade planes, chimney stacks, and parapet lines; eradication of photogrammetric mesh distortion and facade bowing.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Primary Facades: Polished ashlar blocks of pale Craigleith sandstone displaying typical differential environmental weathering—rain-washed upper tiers exposing pale honey-grey tones, contrasted against deep Carboniferous coal-smoke soot encrustations in undersills, rustication recesses, and dentil courses.
- Statuary & Detailing: Cast bronze statuary exhibiting dark brown patina with olive-green basic copper sulfate verdigris concentrated along upper crevices; wrought-iron perimeter railings painted black.
- Roof Coverings: Ballachulish dark blue-grey roofing slates secured with copper nails, lead ridge flashings, and dressed stone skews with tall ashlar chimneystacks supporting terracotta chimney cans.
- Pavements & Ground Plane: Smooth-faced Caithness flagstones on broad pedestrian walkways, bordered by dressed whinstone and granite kerbstones, flanking a dense, dark bituminous asphalt roadbed.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- Urban Botanical Anchors: Specimen plantings of *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore) and *Tilia × europaea* (Common Lime) along peripheral junctions and distant garden squares.
- Date-Grounded Phenology (Late August): Full, mature summer foliage canopy with dense, dark-green broadleaf laminas showing initial late-summer chlorophyll degradation, localized leaf-margin desiccation, and intact seed samaras/bracts.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
- Total elimination of all vehicular traffic, parked cars, delivery vans, buses, and road surface transit markers.
- Complete removal of all pedestrians, tourists, temporary promotional banners, waste receptacles, traffic cones, and construction hoardings.
- Absolute preservation of fixed architectural ironmongery, stone bollards, traditional cast-iron streetlamps, kerb geometries, and permanent masonry fabric.
