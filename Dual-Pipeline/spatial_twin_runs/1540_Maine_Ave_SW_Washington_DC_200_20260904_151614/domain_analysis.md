# Spatial Domain Analysis: 1540 Maine Ave SW, Washington, DC 20004, USA

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean geology situated on the Atlantic Coastal Plain transition zone near the Potomac Fall Line. Subsurface stratigraphy comprises unconsolidated Cretaceous Potomac Formation sediments (alluvial gravels, interbedded clays, and quartzose sand lenses) over deeply buried crystalline Piedmont metamorphic schist and gneiss. Reclaimed historic marshland engineered with nineteenth-century earth fill and stabilized subterranean concrete underpinning resting on Potomac bluestone gneiss boulders. Primary monument lithics feature fine-to-medium grained Cockeysville calcitic marble (Texas, Maryland quarry) for the lower 152-foot tier, a narrow intermediate course of Sheffield Massachusetts dolomite marble, and Beaver Dam Cockeysville marble for the upper shaft to the pyramidion. Surrounding neoclassical civic facades utilize Mississippian Salem limestone (Bedford, Indiana).

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Robert Mills design completed by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers: a hollow monumental stone masonry obelisk tapering upward 555 feet 5 inches, capped with an 8.9-inch solid cast-aluminum pyramidion set at an apex pitch angle of 17.25 degrees. True plumb masonry envelope with tight mortarless vertical ashlar joints. Modern cultural museum exhibits a three-tiered inverted ziggurat corona form supported by four reinforced concrete shear cores, wrapped in an ornamental latticework screen wall. Surrounding Beaux-Arts and Classical Revival Federal Triangle complexes display monumental 6-to-8 storey massings, rusticated ground-floor arcades, colossal order colonnades, rhythmic punched fenestration grids with bronze multi-pane sashes, and multi-pitched mansard and hipped rooflines clad in terra cotta mission tile.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
Tri-part external marble cladding on the obelisk: creamy white lower calcitic marble with subtle gray veining, a darker gray-weathered dolomite intermediate band, and uniform pale white-buff upper calcitic marble. Granite interior rubble masonry backing. Micro-crystalline Indiana Salem oolitic limestone on neoclassical office blocks, displaying subtle atmospheric particulate patina in protected entablatures and rain-washed limestone reveals on vertical pilasters. High-performance glass curtain wall behind 3,600 bronze-finished cast-aluminum filigree screens on the modern cultural facility. Glazed Spanish clay roofing tile in terra cotta red-orange across federal mansards. Circular granite-paved plaza surrounded by polished stainless steel and aluminum flagpoles. Compacted crushed bluestone gravel paths intersecting maintained turf.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Urban parkland canopy dominated by mature *Quercus phellos* (willow oak), *Ulmus americana* (American elm), *Acer saccharinum* (silver maple), *Quercus palustris* (pin oak), and *Platanus × acerifolia* (London plane). Phenology grounded in early September: full, deep green mature foliage exhibiting late-summer moisture stress, intermittent leaf margins showing early chlorosis and minor tip scorch, dense subcanopy crowns, and low-mown *Festuca arundinacea* and *Poa pratensis* turfgrass fields cross-cut by formal radial pedestrian corridors.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient vehicles, tour buses, commuter traffic, pedestrians, crowd control barricades, temporary event tents, and surface maintenance debris are fully eliminated. Permanent architectural infrastructure—including circular granite kerbs, flag rings, paved access aprons, fixed bollards, asphalt park drives, and mature botanical groupings—remains intact and structurally rectified.
