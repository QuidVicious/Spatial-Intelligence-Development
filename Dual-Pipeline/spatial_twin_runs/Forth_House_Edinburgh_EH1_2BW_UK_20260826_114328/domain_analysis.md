# Spatial Domain Analysis: Forth House, Edinburgh EH1 2BW, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The subterranean strata of central Edinburgh and its West End are founded upon the Carboniferous sedimentary sequence (Gullane Formation and Wardie Shales) interspersed with volcanic basalt intrusions and glacial till/boulder clay deposited during Pleistocene glaciations. The structural fabric is constructed from indigenous Lower Carboniferous Scottish sandstones—primarily fine-to-medium-grained quartzose blonde-to-honey Craigleith and Hailes sandstones, celebrated for silica-cemented durability and fine bedded grain. Subterranean moisture is governed by a deep urban water table descending toward the Water of Leith basin, with historic rubble foundations bedded in non-hydraulic to weakly hydraulic lime mortar (quicklime slaked with regional river aggregate), permitting natural vapor transpiration.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
The scene captures the dense urban morphology of Edinburgh’s West End and New Town. 
- **Centerpiece:** St George's West Church (now Charlotte Chapel, 58 Shandwick Place), a Category A-listed Baroque Revival/Italianate ecclesiastical structure designed by David Bryce (1866–1869) with its soaring 56-metre (185-foot) campanile clock tower completed by Robert Rowand Anderson (c. 1881–1882). Modeled after Palladio’s campanile of San Giorgio Maggiore in Venice, the tower rises through four distinct registers: a rusticated rectangular base with a prominent pedimented clock dial, a towering sheer shaft with corner pilasters and narrow vertical slits, an open classical belfry loggia with arched multi-columned openings, and a stepped verdigris copper pyramid-topped spire.
- **Flanking Fabric:** 4-to-5-storey Georgian and Victorian residential/commercial perimeter blocks and tenements (along Stafford Street, Shandwick Place, and Canning Street) featuring classical proportions, laser-straight ashlar band courses, dentiled cornices, rusticated ground-floor plinths, timber sash-and-case fenestration (twelve-pane and four-pane configurations), and hipped/mansard roofscapes punctuated by rhythmic stone chimneystacks, chimney pots, and dormer projections.
- **Foreground:** Rooftops of proximate tenements showing clean parapet copings, slate pitches, dormers, and a flat lead/felt roof terrace on a brick/rubble rear elevation.
- **Planar Rectification:** All vertical walls, towers, and chimneystacks are plumbed to true gravity perpendicular; distorted photogrammetry angles are corrected to flat, parallel structural planes and crisp horizontal floor plates.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Ashlar & Rubble Facades:** Dressed Craigleith blonde sandstone ashlar facing on primary street facades; coursed and snecked rubble masonry on side/rear tenement walls.
- **Ecclesiastical Spire & Roofs:** Hand-beaten verdigris-oxidized copper sheets clad the campanile pyramid with natural turquoise/pale green carbonate patination. Main pitched roofs are clad in Scottish Ballachulish and Welsh roofing slates (dark blue-gray to anthracite sheen) with lead ridge flashings, zinc valley gutters, and cast-iron rainwater downpipes.
- **Foreground Brick Accent:** Warm terra-cotta/orange clay facing bricks on the foreground rear elevation with lime-pointed joints.
- **Climate-Adaptive Patina & Weathering:** Post-industrial maritime temperate patina characteristic of Edinburgh: authentic historic carbon deposition and dark soot crusting sheltered beneath cornices, pediments, and window hoods; soft rain-washing tracks on windward ashlar courses; subtle lime leaching at mortar joints; and softened, weathered sandstone edges.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
The urban canopy and visible parklands/gardens feature mature deciduous and evergreen specimens:
- European Sycamore (*Acer pseudoplatanus*) and London Plane (*Platanus x acerifolia*) lining avenues and private back-greens with broad, multi-lobed green foliage.
- Wych Elm (*Ulmus glabra*), Silver Birch (*Betula pendula*), and mature European Ash (*Fraxinus excelsior*) forming dense, organic green crowns between perimeter blocks and across the distant New Town gardens and Firth of Forth coastal ridges. Foliage is rendered as discrete, fibrous branching and leafy organic canopies, clearly distinct from structural stone.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient dynamic clutter—including moving and parked automobiles, vans, buses, pedestrians, street bins, construction hoardings, and temporary street clutter—is completely removed. The scene is preserved exclusively as permanent civic infrastructure: hand-dressed granite curbings, Caithness stone paving flags, traditional cast-iron streetlamps, fixed wrought-iron area railings, and authentic historic architectural massing.
