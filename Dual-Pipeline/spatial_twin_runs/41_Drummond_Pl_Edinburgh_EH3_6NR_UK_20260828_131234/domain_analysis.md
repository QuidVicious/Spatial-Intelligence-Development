# Spatial Domain Analysis: 41 Drummond Pl, Edinburgh EH3 6NR, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean strata comprise Carboniferous sedimentary bedrock of the Inverclyde and Strathclyde Groups, interbedded with sandstones, siltstones, and volcanic tuff, mantled by stiff glacial till [1, 2]. Primary superterranean lithic fabric consists of pale buff-to-grey quartzose Craigleith and Hailes sandstone, highly siliceous and micro-laminated, bound with non-hydraulic to weakly hydraulic hot-mixed slaked lime and local sharp river sand mortar [3, 4]. Subterranean drainage flows northward across the gentle declivity of the Broughton/New Town drumlin slope toward the Water of Leith basin [1, 5].

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Neoclassical Georgian residential ensemble designed under the Reid & Sibbald New Town Eastern Extension masterplan (c. 1802–1823) [6, 7]. Rigid symmetrical terrace and pavilion typology featuring:
- Vertical massing: Sunken basement lightwells, rusticated channelled ground storeys, piano nobile first floors, upper attic storeys, and flanking terminal/centre pavilions articulated with giant-order Ionic pilasters [6, 8, 9].
- Roof geometries: Rectified dual-pitch timber trusses clad in West Highland Ballachulish and Welsh slate, segmented dormers, stepped stone skews, and robust ashlar chimney stacks crowned with cylindrical terracotta chimney pots [10, 11].
- Fenestration: Strictly plumbed structural grids with 6-over-6 and 2-over-2 timber sash-and-case windows set into droved stone reveals, embellished with cast-iron geometric balconettes at first-floor level [6, 12, 13].

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Facades: Fine-grained, droved, and ashlar-dressed buff Craigleith sandstone [4, 6]; rusticated horizontal V-grooves on ground levels [6, 14].
- Weathering pathology: Localised gypsum-bound carbonaceous coal-smoke encrustations under projecting eaves and stringcourses [15]; pale rain-washed quartz reveals across windward ashlar faces [15]; biological green algal patinas within shaded sub-street basement lightwells.
- Ground plane: Carriageways paved in dense porphyritic basalt and whinstone setts; pedestrian walkways composed of Caithness flagstone slabs edged with hand-tooled granite kerbstones [16]; boundary perimeters secured by wrought- and cast-iron spearhead railings seated in lead-caulked stone plinths [6].

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Drummond Place Gardens private communal woodland park:
- Canopy dominants: Mature *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm), *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), *Fagus sylvatica* (Common Beech), *Tilia × europaea* (Common Lime), and *Platanus × acerifolia* (London Plane) [17].
- Understory: *Taxus baccata* (English Yew), *Ilex aquifolium*, and mature rhododendron shrubs [18].
- Phenology: Late-summer peak maturity; heavy, fully expanded dark-green foliage massing with incipient chlorosis and drought-bronzing on outer canopy margins of lime and beech; dense interlocking canopy partially screening the garden path networks.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient visual noise completely purged: no moving or parked motor vehicles, no pedestrians, no modern trash receptacles, wheelie bins, construction barriers, or temporary street signage. Only permanent static civil fabric remains: stone setts, flagstone pavements, iron boundary railings, stone lightwells, and cast-iron lampposts [6, 16].
