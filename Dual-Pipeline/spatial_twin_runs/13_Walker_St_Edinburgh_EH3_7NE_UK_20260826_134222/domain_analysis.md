# Spatial Domain Analysis: 13 Walker St, Edinburgh EH3 7NE, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The subterranean substrate consists of Lower Carboniferous sedimentary sequences (sandstones, siltstones, and oil-shales of the Inverclyde and Strathclyde Groups) punctuated by volcanic intrusions—most prominently Castle Rock, an olivine-basalt volcanic plug rising as a crag in the center-left background. The primary structural fabric of the West End Georgian and Victorian tenements, as well as the ecclesiastical architecture, is quarried Carboniferous Craigleith and Hailes sandstone (pale buff, blonde, to cool grey quartz-arenite). Mortar composition across the historic masonry utilizes traditional Scottish non-hydraulic to feebly hydraulic hot-mixed lime mortars with crushed river quartz aggregate. Subterranean moisture regimes are dictated by the natural glacial slope shedding southward towards the Water of Leith and the historic Nor' Loch basin.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
A high-angle oblique rooftop vista facing east-southeast (100.1° heading) over Edinburgh's West End and New Town fringe:
- **Foreground (A):** Multiple pitched and mansard rooflines of 3-to-4-storey Georgian and Early Victorian residential tenement blocks (circa 1820–1860) along Walker Street and William Street, featuring massive rectangular chimney stacks, stone copings, paired cylindrical terracotta flue pots, and zinc/lead flashing on parapets and valleys.
- **Center Focus (B):** The prominent 56-metre (184 ft) Venetian Campanile and Roman Baroque church body of St George's West Church (now Charlotte Chapel / former Free St George’s, designed by David Bryce 1867–69; tower completed by Sir Robert Rowand Anderson 1879–81) on Shandwick Place/Stafford Street. The tower features a four-stage ashlar construction with pilasters, clock faces at the pedimented stage, open round-arched belfry, Corinthian orders, corner stone urns, and a verdigris copper pyramidal spire.
- **Center-Left Background:** The looming, precipitous basalt crag and massive fortress complex of Edinburgh Castle (Half Moon Battery, Palace Block, Crown Square) perched on its volcanic pedestal.
- **Midground Flanks (C):** Continuous 4-storey residential ashlar tenement facades with regular vertical bays of timber-framed six-over-six and two-over-two double-hung sash windows, rectified gravity-plumb vertical party walls, and uniform cornice lines.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Ashlar & Rubble Stone:** Fine-tooled Craigleith blonde/grey ashlar blocks on church facades and tenement frontages, transitioning to coursed random rubble on rear elevations and party walls.
- **Roofs:** Scottish Ballachulish and Welsh heather-grey slate tiling laid in diminishing courses, with dressed lead hip rolls, lead ridging, and cast-iron valley gutters.
- **Tower Spire:** Oxidized rolled copper sheath exhibiting pale green/cyan verdigris patina.
- **Weathering & Pathology:** Decades of temperate maritime climate exposure and historic Scottish coal smoke ("Auld Reekie") resulting in localized carbon crust deposition within protected cornices, undercut mouldings, and belfry niches, contrasted with rain-washed pale sandstone facets; subtle damp-induced lichen and algae growth along lower plinths and stone parapets.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Sparse, mature urban temperate foliage interspersed in private gardens and rear courtyards between tenement plots:
- *Platanus x acerifolia* (London plane), *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), *Fraxinus excelsior* (European ash), and occasional *Tilia x europaea* (Common lime) with dense, broadleaf dark green summer canopies visible between stone chimney clusters and street openings.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient visual noise has been strictly removed: zero vehicles, zero pedestrians, zero traffic signage, zero temporary construction screens, and zero trash bins. Pure architectural and geophysical permanence is maintained across the entire field of view: stone chimneys, cast-iron rain pipes, parapet copings, stone urns, belfry balustrades, and slate roofs.
