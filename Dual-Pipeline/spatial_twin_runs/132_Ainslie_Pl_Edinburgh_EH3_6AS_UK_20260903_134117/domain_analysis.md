# Spatial Domain Analysis: 13/2 Ainslie Pl, Edinburgh EH3 6AS, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
* **Bedrock & Stratigraphy:** Underlain by Lower Carboniferous sedimentary formations of the Gullane Formation (Strathclyde Group) within the Midland Valley of Scotland, blanketed by dense Quaternary glacial till and boulder clay.
* **Quarry Lithics:** Structural masonry is Lower Carboniferous Craigleith Sandstone (fine-grained, silica-cemented quartz arenite of exceptional structural density) with secondary Binny and Hailes stone insertions. 
* **Mortar & Groundwater:** Traditional lime-sand mortar matrix (calcium hydroxide slaked lime blended with graded river sand) laid in thin, flush-dressed joints. Subterranean hydrological gradient drains northwestward down the geological ridge toward the Water of Leith gorge.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
* **Typology & Order:** Regency / Late Georgian Neoclassical sweeping concave palace-block terrace, designed in 1822 by architect James Gillespie Graham as part of the Moray Estate.
* **Massing & Storey Hierarchy:** 3 storeys over a sunken basement area with attic level set behind a continuous stone blocking course; continuous ashlar platts oversail the basement lightwells to provide threshold access.
* **Façade Articulation:** Principal floor features continuous V-jointed channelled rustication; first and second floors articulated in smooth polished ashlar demarcated by a continuous string course, moulded second-floor cill course, and full classical entablature with heavy eaves cornice.
* **Fenestration:** Uniform vertical fenestration grid of 6-over-6 and 2-over-2 double-hung timber sash-and-case windows recessed in unadorned ashlar reveals.
* **Planar Rectification:** Facade geometry regularized from photogrammetric noise to plumb vertical planes forming an exact concentric curve; horizontal band courses, entablatures, and parapets straightened to crisp architectural profiles.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
* **Primary Elevations:** Fine-tooled, ashlar-cut Craigleith sandstone blocks with tight lime mortar bedding.
* **Civil & Structural Metalwork:** Wrought-iron spearhead railings and cast-iron urn finials bounding the continuous basement lightwells; cast-iron fanlight frames and over-door lamp irons.
* **Weathering Pathology:** Localized historical coal-smoke gypsum crusts and dark carbon patination beneath projecting cornices, dentils, and sub-cill zones; micro-algal biological greening localized to sandstone damp courses above street level.
* **Paving Fabric:** Caithness flagstone footways bordered by dressed Scottish whinstone/granite kerbstones flanking a smooth macadam carriageway.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
* **Species Identification:** Central circus garden populated by mature *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore maple), *Tilia × europaea* (Common lime), and *Ulmus glabra* (Wych elm).
* **Phenology (September 3):** Fully expanded, dense late-summer crowns with deep dark green foliage; subtle earliest-stage crown-margin chlorosis, localized leaf-tip desiccations, and ripening winged samara clusters; completely intact understory and clipped perimeter turf.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient urban noise eradicated: complete absence of vehicles, parked automobiles, pedestrians, wheelie bins, construction barriers, and modern temporary street signage. All permanent architectural elements, boundary ironwork, and botanical specimens remain intact.
