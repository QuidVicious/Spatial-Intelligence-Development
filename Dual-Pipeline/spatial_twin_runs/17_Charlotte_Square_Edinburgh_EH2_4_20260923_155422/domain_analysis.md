# Spatial Domain Analysis: 17 Charlotte Square, Edinburgh EH2 4DJ, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean strata comprise Carboniferous sedimentary bedrock of the Strathclyde and Clackmannan Groups, overlain by dense glacial till. Primary structural lithic is fine-grained, pale buff-grey Craigleith quartzose sandstone, supplemented by Hailes sandstone at sub-grade footings. Historic bedding uses traditional non-hydraulic hot-mixed lime mortar with graded river sand aggregate.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Neoclassical Georgian palatial unified terrace designed by Robert Adam and executed by Robert Reid. Dominant central terminus features West Register House (former St George’s Church) comprising a rusticated ground floor, giant-order tetrastyle portico with unfluted columns, pedimented entablature, and a multi-tiered circular drum supporting a hemispherical dome. Flanking symmetrical three-storey-and-attic townhouse ranges display channelled rustication at ground level, smooth ashlar upper storeys, moulded string courses, modillioned cornices, balustraded parapets, and regular vertical fenestration bays fitted with 6-over-6 timber sash-and-case frames.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Facades: Craigleith buff-grey ashlar sandstone with droved margins and rusticated channels, bearing historical urban coal-smoke patination and localized rain-wash leaching.
- Dome: Standing-seam oxidised sheet copper with pale verdigris patina over timber substructure.
- Roofing: Scottish Ballachulish grey-black slate with lead flashings and dressed sandstone ridge caps.
- Ground & Boundaries: Caithness flagstone pedestrian walkways, whinstone/granite street kerbs, fine-grain asphalt carriageway with thermoplastic road markings, and wrought-iron spearhead perimeter railings on dressed plinths.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Mature urban trees of *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore) and *Tilia cordata* (Small-leaved Lime) situated along the perimeter garden edge. Late September phenology displays fully developed, dense canopies exhibiting early chlorosis, transitioning from deep olive green to mottled pale ochre and dull yellow, with minimal defoliation.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All photogrammetry artifacts, distorted mesh volumes representing transient parked vehicles, and street-level debris are removed. Ground planes are rectified into continuous tarmac carriageways, clean granite kerblines, and planar flagstone walkways.
