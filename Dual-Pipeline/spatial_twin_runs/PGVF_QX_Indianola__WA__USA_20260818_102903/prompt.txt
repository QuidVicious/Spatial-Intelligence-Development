A prominent 2-storey weathered cedar residence rising to 7.8m elevation perches atop the precipitous sandy bluff, flanked by neighboring 2-storey coastal dwellings embedded in deep green Pacific Northwest woodland. A broad, low-angle telephoto view from the dark blue-gray ripples of Puget Sound captures the soaring, pale cream-tan escarpment of the Indianola feeder bluffs, where exposed Pleistocene glacial sand, gravelly outwash, and silt striations drop sheerly down to a narrow intertidal strip of wet pebbles and sun-bleached driftwood. Patches of hardy sword ferns, salal, and young alders cling to vertical erosion gullies and slump scars along the cliff face. Above the cliff lip, the residences exhibit expansive floor-to-ceiling glass fenestration, wrap-around timber viewing decks, and dark charcoal pitched rooflines half-sheltered beneath towering mature Douglas-firs and western redcedars. Re-synthesized under clear midday summer light (~5600K), the blazing sun rakes across the textured grain of the chalky cliff face, casting crisp, sharp diagonal shadows beneath projecting trees and under-eaves, while deep crystalline reflections ripple over the cold marine surface in the foreground.
