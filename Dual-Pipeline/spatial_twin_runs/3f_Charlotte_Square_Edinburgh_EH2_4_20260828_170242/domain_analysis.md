# Spatial Domain Analysis: 3f Charlotte Square, Edinburgh EH2 4HQ, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- Bedrock: Carboniferous sedimentary strata of the Strathclyde Group / Gullane Formation, characterized by interbedded sandstones, siltstones, and mudstones underlying the Edinburgh basin.
- Primary Lithics: Pale cream-to-buff, fine-to-medium grained Carboniferous quartz arenite sandstone quarried locally from the Craigleith Quarry (high silica content, extremely durable, carbonaceous flecking) with secondary rubble stone from Hailes and Ravelston quarries for interior structural backing.
- Mortar & Foundations: Traditional non-hydraulic to weakly hydraulic hot-mixed lime mortars with sharp river sand aggregates; continuous ashlar strip footings resting upon stiff glacial till over consolidated sandstone bedrock.
- Hydrogeology: Sloping subterranean terrain shedding groundwater northwest towards the Water of Leith gorge, managed via Georgian subterranean stone vaults, area wells, and gravitational perimeter drainage channels.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- Typology: Georgian Neo-Classical monumental duodecagonal circus (Moray Place, master planned 1822 by James Gillespie Graham for the 10th Earl of Moray).
- Massing & Storey Heights: Symmetrical palace-fronted blocks comprising sunken basements within railed dry areas, rusticated ground floors, piano nobile first floors, second floors, and attic storeys beneath pitched mansard and hipped roofs punctuated by stone dormers and substantial transverse chimney stacks.
- Rectification & Geometry: Orthogonal vertical line plumbed to absolute gravity; duodecagonal curved plan segmented into planar, ashlar-faced pavilions with engaged Greek Doric / Tuscan pilastrades and central pedimented porticos. Fenestration arranged in rigorous vertical grids: 6-over-6 and 8-over-8 timber double-hung sash-and-case windows with fine astragals.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Facades: Precision ashlar Craigleith sandstone dressed with fine droved and polished finishes, channelled V-joint horizontal rustication on principal floors, smooth ashlar on upper storeys, moulded string courses, and deep mutule eaves cornices.
- Roofing & Flashings: Diminishing courses of dark grey Ballachulish / Easdale Scottish roofing slate secured with copper nails, dressed lead valleys, ridges, and gutters, coped stone skews, and buff sandstone chimney cans.
- Metalwork: Wrought and cast-iron spearhead boundary railings, overthrow lantern arches, anthemion and palmette iron balconettes on first-floor windows.
- Weathering & Pathology: Historical coal soot encrustations preserved in recessed stone joints and undersides of cornices, localized gypsum crusting, natural ferrous oxidation veining, and micro-vegetation along shaded basement drop areas.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- Botanical Composition: Urban canopy of mature broadleaf specimens within the central private pleasure garden (Moray Place Gardens), including *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm), *Tilia × europaea* (Common Lime), and *Fraxinus excelsior* (European Ash), bordered by *Ilex aquifolium* (Holly) perimeter hedgerows.
- Seasonal Phenology (November): Late-autumn dormancy; broadleaf canopies mostly defoliated revealing intricate, bare dark-timber branch architectures, with sparse clusters of desiccated yellow-brown and bronze senescent foliage remaining on sheltered inner limbs and a blanket of fallen leaf litter over maintained turf.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All modern transient clutter, parked and moving motor vehicles, traffic markings, temporary signage, pedestrians, wheeled bins, and surface photogrammetry artifacts are entirely excised. Permanent civil infrastructure—whinstone kerbs, caithness flagstone pavements, cast-iron railings, stone gatepiers, and vintage cast-iron lamp posts—is fully preserved.
