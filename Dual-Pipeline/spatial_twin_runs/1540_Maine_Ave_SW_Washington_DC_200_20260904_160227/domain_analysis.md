# Spatial Domain Analysis: 1540 Maine Ave SW, Washington, DC 20004, USA

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- Bedrock: Potomac Formation saprolite over crystalline schist and gneiss of the Piedmont Province, with deep alluvial silt, gravel, and clay fill across the reclaimed Potomac flats.
- Monument Substructure: Massive unreinforced bluestone gneiss rubble foundation underpinned by concrete slab and underpinning buttresses extending to bedrock.
- Lithic Provenance: 
  * Washington Monument shaft: Lower 152 feet composed of Cockeysville/Texas Maryland calcitic marble; upper 400+ feet composed of Lee, Massachusetts dolomitic marble and Cockeysville marble backing blocks, backed by Maine granite ashlar and New England gneiss masonry.
  * Federal Triangle & surrounding civic structures: Indiana Bedford calcitic limestone ashlar facades, Deer Isle granite plinths, and red terracotta clay tile roofs.
  * Circular plaza: Thermal-finished crystalline granite paving stones and honed Pennsylvania bluestone perimeter bands.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- Washington Monument: Free-standing, hollow Egyptian-revival masonry obelisk, precisely 555 feet 5⅛ inches in height with a 10:1 height-to-base ratio; four battered planar stone faces tapering to a 55-foot-tall pyramidal capstone (pyramidion) topped with cast aluminum apex. True plumb vertical load-bearing lines.
- Base Plaza: Concentric circular granite and bluestone plaza ringed by 50 stainless steel/aluminum flagpoles set into granite bollards; security screening facility integrated at the base in matching honed stone and structural glass.
- Urban Fabric (Background/Periphery):
  * National Museum of African American History and Culture (NMAAHC): Inverted three-tiered stepped ziggurat (corona) clad in ornate cast-aluminum lattice panels with bronze-toned PVDF finish, resting on a glazed transparent ground-level pavilion.
  * Federal Triangle Complex (Herbert C. Hoover / Commerce, Ronald Reagan Building, EPA / Ariel Rios, IRS): Monumental Neoclassical and Beaux-Arts federal administrative blocks; multi-story rusticated ashlar bases, continuous colossal Doric and Ionic colonnades, pedimented porticos, repetitive multi-lite double-hung timber/bronze fenestration grids, and pitched terracotta Spanish tile hipped roofs with classical balustrades.
  * Ground Geometries: Monument grounds contoured into a subtle concentric knoll surrounded by curvilinear asphalt and decomposed-granite pedestrian pathway ribbons.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Obelisk Masonry: Finely dressed calcitic and dolomitic marble ashlar in alternating course heights, showing a distinct horizontal color demarcation line at the 152-foot mark (transition from weathered pale cream Maryland marble to lighter Massachusetts marble). Fine-jointed hydraulic lime and Portland mortar.
- Surrounding Pavings: Bituminous asphalt walkways edged with granite curbing; crushed aggregate gravel trails; concentric rings of split-face and flame-finished gray granite pavers at the monument base.
- Federal Facades: Uniform light buff Indiana limestone ashlar with minimal tooling marks, cast-bronze window trim, and unglazed vitreous red-clay roof tiles.
- NMAAHC Envelope: Micro-textured bronze-finished cast aluminum lattice mesh filigree over dark structural glazing.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- Date-Grounded Phenology (September): Fully expanded late-summer canopy volumes displaying mature deep olive and forest greens, with early faint chlorosis/yellowing on select branch tips; zero leaf drop.
- Botanical Taxa:
  * Overstory Groves & Mall Borders: American Elm (*Ulmus americana*), Pin Oak (*Quercus palustris*), White Oak (*Quercus alba*), and American Sycamore (*Platanus occidentalis*).
  * Perimeter Plantings: Red Maple (*Acer rubrum*), London Planetree (*Platanus × acerifolia*), and Sweetgum (*Liquidambar styraciflua*).
  * Turf Cover: Dense, high-durability sod composition of Kentucky Bluegrass (*Poa pratensis*) and Tall Fescue (*Festuca arundinacea*), clipped to uniform height across the undulating knoll.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
- Complete elimination of all dynamic road vehicles, tour buses, transient traffic, pedestrians, crowd lines, temporary fencing, and maintenance equipment.
- All structural features (curbs, fixed flagpole rings, permanent paved paths, masonry benches, fixed bronze bollards) retained in crisp, static architectural clarity.
