# Spatial Domain Analysis: 145 Princes St., Edinburgh EH2 4BL, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- Bedrock: Subterranean foundation situated along the northern flank of the Castle Rock crag-and-tail volcanic formation, underpinned by Early Carboniferous sedimentary rock formations (Gullane Formation and Wardie Shales).
- Masonry Lithics: Primarily Carboniferous pale buff-to-grey quartz-rich Craigleith sandstone and Hailes sandstone ashlar blocks, with later nineteenth-century infills utilizing buff sandstone and Red Permian/Triassic Sandstone details.
- Mortar Chemistry: Historical non-hydraulic and feebly hydraulic slaked lime mortar matrices with coarse river-sand aggregate, showing historic localized lime repointing.
- Hydrology & Subterranean Drainage: Southern gradient falls toward the historic infilled hollow of the former Nor Loch (now West Princes Street Gardens), managed via subterranean municipal stone culverts.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- Typology: Late Georgian to Victorian commercial terraces, Edwardian Baroque/Renaissance Revival department store conversions (notably 145 Princes Street, the former Binns/House of Fraser building, now the Johnnie Walker Experience), terminating westward toward the West End junction, Shandwick Place, and the distant triple spires of St Mary's Episcopal Cathedral.
- Vertical Rectification: All load-bearing facades plumbed to strict 90-degree gravity vertical; bulging photogrammetric distortions and warped window jambs rectified to razor-straight ashlar courses and planar vertical reveals.
- Storey Heights & Massing: 4-to-6 storey continuous commercial and domestic elevations with rusticated ashlar ground-floor plinths, classicized architraves, pronounced pedimented dormers, and mansard slate rooflines interrupted by heavy chimney stacks and stone balustrades.
- Distant Focal Points: Square campanile tower of St Cuthbert's Parish Church / St John's Church flanking the green edge of the gardens to the southwest, and the dominant Gothic revival spires of St Mary's Cathedral rising in the background.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Facades: Fine-grained, tooled Craigleith ashlar masonry exhibiting authentic atmospheric carbon soot patina settled into stone relief carvings, cornices, and stringcourses.
- Roofing: Scottish and Welsh blue-grey roofing slates, lead flashings, copper ridge rolls displaying pale verdigris oxidation, and cast-iron rainwater downpipes.
- Ground-Floor Fenestration: Modern bronze-framed and plate-glass storefronts integrated cleanly into the historic ashlar street frontage, free of warped mesh artifacts.
- Roadway & Infrastructure: Dual-track embedded tramway steel rails flush with dark asphalt carriageway; Caithness flagstone and modern granite slab pedestrian sidewalks with tight, straight granite curbstones.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- Urban & Park Foliage: Mature broadleaf deciduous trees along the southern edge flanking West Princes Street Gardens and churchyards, comprising Sycamore (*Acer pseudoplatanus*), Wych Elm (*Ulmus glabra*), European Lime (*Tilia x europaea*), and English Oak (*Quercus robur*).
- Botanical Clarity: Dense organic canopy structures with individualized leaf clusters, organic branching, and natural bark texture, fully decoupled from adjacent stone parapets and retaining walls.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
- Total exclusion of all moving and parked vehicles (buses, tram cars, maintenance vans, private vehicles).
- Total removal of pedestrians, transient crowds, construction barriers, temporary street banners, plastic trash bins, and paper street litter.
- Absolute preservation of permanent civil fixtures: steel catenary tram lines and poles, fixed cast-iron streetlamp posts, traffic signal masts, bus stop glass shelters, and stone boundary walls.
