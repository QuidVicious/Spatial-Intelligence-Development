# Spatial Domain Analysis: 26 London St, Edinburgh EH3 6NA, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean foundation rests upon the Lower Carboniferous (Dinantian) sedimentary succession of the Strathclyde Group, comprising interbedded siltstones, mudstones, and thick quartzitic sandstones overlain by dense glacial till. Primary structural masonry consists of quarried Craigleith sandstone (pale grey to buff quartz arenite with siliceous and calcareous cementation, known for high structural compressive strength) with subsidiary rubble backing of Hailes sandstone. Bedding joints are laid in traditional non-hydraulic to weakly hydraulic slaked lime mortar (calcium hydroxide with local river sand aggregate). Subsurface drainage is managed via gravel-backfilled perimeter lightwells and cut-stone subterranean vaults beneath the pedestrian footways.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Uniform early 19th-century Georgian New Town residential terrace typologies laid out on a strictly symmetrical cross-axial grid. Massing comprises mirrored northern and southern terraces: three storeys above ground over continuous sunken basement areas, topped with pitched slate attic levels. Facade geometry is rectified to true gravity vertical: ground floors feature deeply channelled horizontal ashlar rustication with flat-arched voussoir lintels; upper first and second storeys feature smooth-dressed polished ashlar with continuous projecting stringcourses and an overarching moulded eaves cornice capped by a solid ashlar blocking course. Fenestration is organized on a rigorous, unyielding grid of recessed timber 6-over-6 and 12-pane sash-and-case windows with thin glazing bars. Symmetrical ridge-line and wallhead chimney stacks with coped cornices support regular arrays of cylindrical buff and terracotta chimney pots. Roofs are rectilinear pitched gables featuring slate-hung dormers with lead cheek flashing. Stone access platts (flying bridges) span the basement wells to entrance doors crowned with geometric and semicircular fanlights.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Facades: Craigleith pale buff-grey ashlar sandstone, horizontal droved margin profiling on ground-level rustication, polished fine-ashlar dressing on piano nobile and upper floors.
- Roofs: Grey-black Scottish Ballachulish and Welsh roofing slates laid in diminishing courses, lead valleys, zinc flashings, and cast-iron rainwater gutters/downpipes.
- Openings & Railings: Painted timber sash frames; wrought and cast-iron spearhead perimeter railings and urn finials mounted on dressed sandstone dwarf boundary walls.
- Weathering & Pathology: Historic industrial particulate crusts (calcium sulfate/soot) concentrated beneath projecting cornices, sills, and protected stringcourses; subtle iron-oxide oxidation banding across upper ashlar tiers; damp-induced green algal patina and moisture ingress along low-level basement plinths and lightwell revetments.
- Ground Plane: Caithness flagstone paving slabs, finely squared whinstone kerbstones, and dark granite road setts.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
The western terminus of London Street opens axially toward Drummond Place Gardens, dominated by mature temperate broadleaf canopy cover. Predominant species include *Ulmus glabra* (Wych elm), *Platanus × acerifolia* (London plane), *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), and *Tilia cordata* (Small-leaved lime). Botanical phenology corresponds to late-summer maturation: deep green, high-density leaf canopies forming an unbroken organic foliage wall across the street axis, showing fully expanded mature leaves with localized inner-canopy shade chlorosis and absence of defoliation.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All photogrammetry mesh noise, warped polygonal vehicles, modern parked cars, vans, wheelie bins, plastic street barriers, temporary signage, and pedestrian figures are completely eliminated. The carriageway, pavements, basement lightwells, and iron railings are preserved in a pristine, static architectural survey state.
