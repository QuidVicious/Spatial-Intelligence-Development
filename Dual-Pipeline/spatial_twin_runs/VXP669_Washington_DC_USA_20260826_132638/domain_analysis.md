# Spatial Domain Analysis: VXP6+69 Washington, DC, USA

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The subterranean stratigraphy beneath the National Mall and Monument Grounds consists of Quaternary river terrace alluvium and saturated tidal silts overlying deeply buried crystalline metamorphic basement (Piedmont Province schist and gneiss). The massive 555-foot Washington Monument rests upon a reinforced concrete and Potomac bluestone gneiss underpinning. 
* **Primary Lithics (The Monument):** An unreinforced stone masonry obelisk comprising three distinct historical quarry layers:
  1. Base (0–152 ft): Coarse crystalline calcitic/dolomitic marble from the Texas quarry in Baltimore County, Maryland.
  2. Transitional belt (~152–160 ft): Distinct brownish-banded fine marble from Lee/Sheffield, Berkshire County, Massachusetts.
  3. Upper shaft & pyramidion (160–555 ft): Slightly grayer dolomitic marble quarried in Cockeysville, Baltimore County, Maryland, containing fine phlogopite mica traces.
  * The structural backing consists of Devonian Mount Waldo coarse-grained biotite granite from Maine.
* **Background Urban Fabric:** The Federal Triangle and Constitution Avenue monumental row are faced predominantly with Mississippian Bedford Indiana oolitic limestone, anchored with steel and reinforced concrete framing, capped with fired terracotta pantile roofs.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
* **Center (Washington Monument):** Pure Egyptian-revival monumental obelisk measuring 554 ft 7 in (169.05 m) in height, with a 55 ft base tapering smoothly to a 34 ft pyramidion base capped by an apex pyramidion. Photogrammetry-induced geometric wobble is rectified: all four corner edges are plumbed to absolute straight linear convergence, with smooth planar ashlar joints. Surrounding the circular marble plaza is a formal ring of 50 vertical stainless steel flagpoles.
* **Flanking Right / Mid-Ground (NMAAHC):** The National Museum of African American History and Culture—a 5-storey tiered inverted corona structure enveloped in a bronze-colored architectural lattice/scrim made of cast aluminum panels over dark vision glazing.
* **Background (Federal Triangle & Constitution Avenue North Facades):** 
  - Herbert C. Hoover Building (Department of Commerce): 7-storey Neoclassical/Beaux-Arts palazzo massing with massive Doric/Ionic colonnades, deep window reveals, and low-pitch hipped red terracotta tile roofs.
  - Ronald Reagan Building & International Trade Center: 8-storey modern limestone Neoclassical volume with crisp unadorned cornice lines and stepped pavilions.
  - Department of the Interior / GSA buildings and distant monumental domes along Constitution and Pennsylvania Avenues.
  * All vertical facades across the frame are plumbed perpendicular to gravity, planar wall surfaces flattened, and rooflines straightened.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
* **Washington Monument:** Differentiated tri-tone marble courses. Lower third displays weathered calcitic white-cream stone; distinct darker weathered horizontal band at the 152–160 ft line; upper two-thirds shows subtle cool dove-gray dolomitic marble. Natural micro-climate patina includes mild atmospheric sulfate alteration, clean rainwater vertical wash lines on exposed planes, and soft graying in sheltered corner joints.
* **Federal Triangle Enfilade:** Indiana limestone with natural pale buff-gray matte finish, showing subtle carbon and atmospheric darkening along deep cornice undercuts and architraves. Roofs are clad in authentic variegated terra-cotta clay barrel tiles showing natural mineral weathering.
* **National Museum of African American History and Culture:** Rich bronze-finished micro-textured metal corona with deep warm copper-brown undertones, framing recessed low-iron dark architectural glass.
* **Ground Infrastructure:** Circular plaza paving of smooth light gray granite flags, bordered by compacted tan-buff gravel walking paths and asphalt circulation roadways.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
The grounds are dominated by the manicured turfgrass of the Washington Monument Grounds (*Poa pratensis* / *Festuca arundinacea*). Disambiguated mature deciduous urban tree canopies include:
* American Elm (*Ulmus americana*) lining Constitution Avenue and the Mall paths.
* London Plane Trees (*Platanus × acerifolia*) and Pin Oaks (*Quercus palustris*) around the periphery of the knoll.
* Red Maple (*Acer rubrum*) and Littleleaf Linden (*Tilia cordata*) clusters framing the surrounding park spaces.
* Dense organic foliage with realistic layered branching, serrated green leaves, and natural leaf-shadow canopies completely free of polygonal artifacts.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient vehicular traffic on Constitution Avenue, 15th Street, and 17th Street has been entirely purged. All pedestrian tourists, crowd barricades, temporary event tents, portable restrooms, and utility bins have been removed. Preserved infrastructure includes the circular ring of fixed flagpoles, permanent stone curbs, historical cast-iron lampposts, granite bollards, and paved gravel pathways.
