## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The subterranean substrate consists of Lower Carboniferous sedimentary formations of the Strathclyde and Inverclyde Groups, primarily Gullane and Wardie siltstones and shales interspersed with intrusive volcanic basalt sills characteristic of the Midland Valley basin. The monumental centerpiece (St Mary's Episcopal Cathedral) and the surrounding New Town residential terraces are predominantly constructed from indigenous quartzose Carboniferous sandstones, notably sourced from the historic Craigleith and Hailes quarries. Craigleith sandstone, a pale grey-to-buff, silica-cemented quartz arenite of exceptional structural density, forms the primary fine ashlar masonry and intricate Gothic tracery. Flanking residential tenements utilize laminated Hailes sandstone and Cullalo sandstone, bedded in traditional hydraulic non-hydraulic lime and sand mortar. Subsurface hydrology is governed by glacial till (boulder clay) overlying sloping bedrock draining southward toward the Water of Leith basin.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
A prominent high-angle oblique elevation facing East (92.9° heading) capturing the Victorian Gothic masterpiece of St Mary's Episcopal Cathedral (designed by Sir George Gilbert Scott, 1874–1879) framed against the wider Edinburgh West End.
- **Centerpiece (St Mary's Cathedral):** Cruciform plan featuring a monumental central crossing tower terminating in a 90-meter (295 ft) octagonal stone spire, flanked at the western facade by dual matching twin spires (the Mary and Barbara spires, added 1913–1917). Planar geometry includes flying buttresses, lancet window fenestration, deeply recessed arched portals, high clerestory bays, a steeply pitched nave roof clad in Scottish Ballachulish slate, and an eastern apse.
- **Foreground & Flanks (Residential Terraces):** Victorian and Late Georgian New Town residential tenements (4 to 5 storeys, including raised basements and attic mansards/dormers). Rectangular, rhythmic ashlar facades characterized by repetitive vertical sash window bays, canted bays, ashlar string courses, prominent parapets, and dense arrays of symmetrical chimney stacks crowned with terracotta cylindrical pots.
- **Planar Rectification:** All distorted photogrammetric polygons along the stone towers, spires, and tenement facades are rectified to strict vertical plumb lines. Horizontal floor stringcourses, eaves, and ridgelines are restored to sharp, unwarped geometric planes.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Cathedral Facades:** Finely dressed Craigleith and Dunmore sandstone ashlar with variable natural graining, exhibiting 150-year post-industrial carbon deposition ("Auld Reekie" soot patina) concentrated in sheltered under-crofts, spandrel recesses, and deep mouldings, contrasted against natural wind-and-rain-scoured pale grey sandstone ribs.
- **Residential Ensembles:** Buff, honey, and charcoal-weathered sandstone ashlar on front elevations; coarser coursed rubble masonry with lime mortar pointing on rear elevations and returns. Micro-crusts of dark gypsum and carbon staining under projecting cornices, water runoff streaks beneath stone window cills, and subtle efflorescence along lower plinth courses.
- **Roofs & Details:** Deep blue-grey Welsh and Scottish Ballachulish slates with lead ridge rolls, zinc valley gutters, cast-iron rainwater downpipes, and patinated black wrought-iron area boundary railings.

Foliage is rendered as organic leafy canopies with natural botanical branching and vein detail, completely disambiguated from adjacent masonry.
