# Spatial Domain Analysis: 13/2 Ainslie Pl, Edinburgh EH3 6AS, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- **Subterranean Bedrock**: Upper Carboniferous sedimentary bedrock sequence (Gullane Formation / Strathclyde Group) comprising layered sandstone, siltstone, and mudstone, overlain by dense Devensian glacial till and perched clay deposits.
- **Lithic Extraction**: Facade masonry composed entirely of blonde-to-buff Craigleith sandstone (Lower Carboniferous quartz-arenite, silica-cemented, high crushing strength). Subterranean foundation and lightwell retaining walls built from mixed Carboniferous freestone and basalt rubble.
- **Mortar Chemistry**: Slaked high-calcium non-hydraulic to feebly hydraulic hot-mixed lime mortar with sharp quartz sand and crushed sandstone aggregate.
- **Hydrology & Drainage**: Topography slopes gently northward toward the Water of Leith basin; street engineered with subterranean vaulted cellars under the flagstone footway and perimeter sunken lightwells for foundation perimeter drainage.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- **Typology & Era**: Neoclassical / Greek Revival double-crescent urban palace-fronted terrace, designed by James Gillespie Graham in 1822 as part of the Moray Estate (Moray Feu).
- **Vertical Hierarchy & Massing**: 3 principal storeys over a continuous sunken basement lightwell, topped with an attic/mansard storey behind continuous stone parapets and deep classical cornices. Advanced end and central pavilion bays articulated by colossal Giant Order Ionic pilasters spanning the first and second storeys.
- **Fenestration**: Strict vertical grid alignment; ground floor with flat-arched and round-arched openings within channelled masonry; first-floor piano nobile featuring tall timber sash-and-case windows (typically 6-over-6 glazing pattern) with delicate cast-iron anthemion/palmette window guards; upper storeys with square 6-over-6 or 3-over-3 timber sash configurations.
- **Planar Rectification**: All building lines rectified to true gravity vertical; curved crescent radius resolved into smooth polygonal facets of perfectly flat ashlar ashlar wall-planes; chimney stacks squared with distinct terracotta chimney cans.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Ground Floor**: Droved and V-jointed channelled rusticated Craigleith ashlar, featuring pronounced horizontal striations and radial voussoirs over entrance fanlights.
- **Upper Facades**: Polished blonde Craigleith sandstone ashlar blocks with fine-dressed lime joints; projecting continuous stringcourses, architraves, and classical moulded eaves cornices.
- **Roofing**: Scotch slate / Ballachulish grey-black slate shingles nailed to timber sarking boards on pitched and mansard profiles; lead valley flashings and roll ridges.
- **Ironwork & Civil Elements**: Cast-iron spearhead railings and urn-finial lamp brackets bordering sunken lightwells; Caithness stone paving slabs on sidewalks; whinstone/dolerite and granite sett-paved crescent carriageway with straight granite kerbstones.
- **Weathering & Pathology**: Superficial particulate carbon-crust deposits and soot patina under sheltered soffits, friezes, and window lintels; clean, rain-washed exterior stone surfaces with localized damp green algae on basement lightwell flagstones.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- **Botanical Taxa**: Ainslie Place central private garden enclosure containing mature *Platanus × acerifolia* (London plane), *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore maple), and *Tilia cordata* (Small-leaved lime), anchored by manicured perimeter lawn and dense understory shrubbery (*Ilex aquifolium*, *Taxus baccata*).
