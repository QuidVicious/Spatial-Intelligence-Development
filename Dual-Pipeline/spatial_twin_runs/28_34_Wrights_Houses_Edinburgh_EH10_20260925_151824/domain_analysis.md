# Spatial Domain Analysis: 28 & 34 Wright's Houses, Edinburgh EH10 4HR, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Bedrock comprises Carboniferous sedimentary strata of the Inverclyde and Strathclyde Groups (sandstones, siltstones, and mudstones), punctuated by the distant volcanic plug of Castle Rock (olivine basalt / dolerite crag). Primary building stone consists of blonde and buff Carboniferous sandstones quarried from the Craigleith, Hailes, and Binnie formations. Mortar systems across Victorian masonry feature traditional slaked non-hydraulic and feebly hydraulic lime mortars blended with coarse river sand, contrasted with rigid Portland cement mortars across mid-to-late 20th-century infill blocks. Subterranean hydrological dynamics are defined by gently sloping glacial till over bedrock, draining northward toward the historic Nor Loch basin.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Barclay Viewforth Church (Barclay Bruntsfield Church, 1864, architect F.T. Pilkington): High Victorian / Franco-Venetian Gothic ecclesiastical structure featuring an asymmetrical massing, a towering 76-metre octagonal spire with decorative lucarnes and corner pinnacles, complex steeply pitched slate roofscapes, faceted gables, and heavy stone tracery. Wright's Houses and adjacent terrace frontages comprise 4-to-5-storey Scottish tenement blocks alongside late-20th-century flatted infill; detailing includes crow-stepped and steep gables, corbelled corner features, canted bay windows, and timber sash-and-case fenestration patterns (1-over-1 and 2-over-2 configurations). Distant profile includes the crenellated and curtain-wall massing of Edinburgh Castle atop its sheer basalt precipice. All vertical wall planes rectified to true vertical plumb lines.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Facades: Blonde Craigleith sandstone ashlar dressings, snecked buff Hailes sandstone rubble masonry, rusticated quoins; adjacent modern infill displaying precast concrete lintels, brick slips, and reconstituted stone render panels.
- Roofing: Scottish Ballachulish and Welsh blue-grey roofing slates, lead valleys, zinc ridges, cast-iron gutters, and downpipes.
- Ground Plane: Bruntsfield Links open public turf, compacted crushed gravel and bituminous asphalt park pathways, whinstone and granite kerbing, Caithness stone flagstones along perimeter pavement interfaces.
- Weathering / Patina: Historical industrial coal-smoke carbon encrustations across deep-relief sandstone mouldings, run-off leaching stains, localized microalgal patina on moisture-retaining base courses.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Urban parkland canopy composed of mature *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm), *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), and *Tilia × europaea* (Common Lime). Mature specimens feature heavy, fluted, dark-barked single trunks dividing into broad, spreading, multi-tiered structural crown architectures, with distinct vertical branching scaffolds anchored into deep turf verges along pathway borders.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient vehicles, parked cars, traffic elements, pedestrians, waste bins, temporary ground signage, and street clutter are completely suppressed. Fixed municipal cast-iron railings, sandstone boundary walls, and static civil park infrastructure remain intact.
