# Spatial Domain Analysis: 29 London St, Edinburgh EH3 6LY, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- **Bedrock Stratigraphy**: Lower Carboniferous (Tournaisian-Visean) sedimentary succession of the Inverclyde Group and Gullane Formation, comprising layered quartz-arenite sandstones, siltstones, and bituminous shales, capped by dense Quaternary glaciogenic lodgement till.
- **Lithic Extraction**: Primary structural masonry sourced from local Lothian Carboniferous quarries, predominantly fine-grained blonde Craigleith and Hailes quartzose sandstone characterized by high silica content and planar bedding.
- **Mortar & Binding**: Traditional hot-mixed non-hydraulic to weakly hydraulic lime mortar formulated with calcined local Carboniferous limestone and sharp river aggregate, ensuring vapour permeability.
- **Hydrogeology & Subterranean Dynamics**: North-sloping post-glacial drumlin topography directing natural perched groundwater toward the Water of Leith basin; intersected at depth beneath Drummond Place by the vaulted 1847 Scotland Street subterranean railway tunnel.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- **Typology & Provenance**: Edinburgh Second New Town late Georgian Neoclassical residential terraces and terminal crescent pavilions, laid out under the 1802 masterplan by Robert Reid and William Sibbald, completed c. 1807–1824 with facade revisions by Thomas Brown.
- **Massing & Storey Heights**: Unified repetitive street wall; 3 storeys over raised basement lightwells, crowned by continuous friezes, mutule cornices, blocking courses, and recessed slate-hung piended attic dormers.
- **Planar Rectification & Fenestration**: Corrected to rigid plumb verticals and continuous horizontal datum lines; channelled ashlar rustication at ground floor; symmetrical regular fenestration grids holding recessed 12-pane (6-over-6) and 8-over-8 timber double-hung sash-and-case windows.
- **Roofscape Geometry**: Symmetrical dual-pitched timber roofs clad in regional Scottish and Welsh slates, punctuated by broached ashlar wallhead and party-wall chimneystacks capped with circular terracotta chimney cans.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Facade Envelope**: Polished blonde Craigleith sandstone ashlar on principal elevations, droved ashlar basements, and coursed squared sandstone rubble with lime pointing on secondary returns.
- **Metalwork & Hardscape**: Cast-iron spearhead and anthemion-tipped boundary railings mounted on tooled sandstone plinths; Caithness flagstone pavements and whinstone (dolerite) roadway setts with carved sandstone kerbstones.
- **Climate Pathology**: Historic Carboniferous coal-smoke sulfate crusts concentrated beneath horizontal string courses and projecting cornices; vertical grey-black rain-wash run-off tracks; damp-induced bryophyte and chlorophyte micro-greening along subterranean basement lightwell flagstones.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- **Botanical Taxa (Drummond Place Gardens)**: Dominant mature urban broadleaf canopy composed of *Ulmus glabra* (wych elm), *Acer pseudoplatanus* (sycamore), *Tilia × europaea* (common lime), *Fagus sylvatica* (European beech), and *Platanus × acerifolia* (London plane).
- **Date-Grounded Phenology (November 11)**: Mid-November late-autumn senescence; 75–90% canopy defoliation exposing complex architectural ramification and dark wet bark; residual desiccated ochre, russet, and dull yellow-brown leaves concentrated within lower crown branch bifurcations; damp fallen leaf litter layering underlying lawn turf.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering

Retained static historic civil infrastructure: cast-iron lampposts, iron boundary railings, stone retaining copes, and granite roadway sett surfaces.
