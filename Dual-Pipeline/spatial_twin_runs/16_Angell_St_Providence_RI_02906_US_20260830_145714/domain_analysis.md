# Spatial Domain Analysis: 16 Angell St, Providence, RI 02906, USA

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The subterranean foundation is situated on the western terraced flank of College Hill within the Pennsylvanian-age Narragansett Basin. Bedrock comprises folded, carbonaceous meta-sedimentary lithologies of the Rhode Island Formation, characterized by dark-gray to black graphitic phyllite, interbedded quartzose sandstone, siltstone, and localized pebble conglomerate. Structural foundations bear directly on competent sedimentary strata and glacial lodgment till. Masonry across the foreground and midground incorporates regionally quarried fine-grained Westerly granite (Westerly, RI) for ashlar foundation plinths and retaining walls, coupled with dense, pressed New England red common clay brick bound in historic lime-sand and early Portland cementitious mortars. Groundwater hydrology is governed by steep westward sub-surface runoff toward the Providence River basin.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
The spatial hierarchy is anchored in the foreground by the 1885 Dr. George W. Carr Mansion (Carr Haus, designed by Edward I. Nickerson), presenting an asymmetrical 3.5-storey Queen Anne / Chateauesque volume. Massing features a four-storey cylindrical corner turret terminating in a conical spire, steeply pitched slate cross-gables with flared eaves, projecting bay windows, half-timbered upper gables, and towering multi-flue brick chimneys with corbelled caps. Adjoining midground structures comprise late 19th- to mid-20th-century Rhode Island School of Design (RISD) academic and studio blocks (including the 1893 Romanesque/Venetian Revival Waterman Building massing and studio workshops), defined by rectilinear multi-storey red brick masonry envelopes, standing-seam copper clerestory lanterns, saw-tooth studio skylights, and a prominent freestanding cylindrical brick industrial chimney stack. Planar lines are strictly plumbed vertical, rectifying photogrammetric geometric drift into crisp masonry parapets, aligned fenestration grids, and planar roof junctions.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Foreground Historic Mansion: Dressed Westerly granite plinth, red brick running bond to lower levels, upper-level half-timbering with tinted stucco infill panels, dark unfading Vermont gray-black slate roof shingles, verdigris copper flashing, and painted multi-pane timber casements and double-hung sash frames.
- Institutional RISD Studio Complex: Deep red wire-cut and pressed New England brick laid in common and English bond patterns, planar cast-stone belt courses, oxidised verdigris copper standing-seam mansard roofs, factory-profile multi-lite steel sash fenestration grids, corrugated metal mechanical penthouses, and painted primary-color industrial ductwork/ventilation apparatus in the upper service interstitial court.
- Background Skyline: Smooth limestone ashlar, precast architectural concrete panels, and dark curtain-wall structural glazing.
- Weathering & Pathology: Localized atmospheric carbon soot encrustation along leeward brick surfaces and chimney stacks, biological verdigris oxidation on copper flashings and roof monitors, rain-wash reveals across upper stone water-tables, and micro-efflorescence along mortar repointing lines.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Street-level canopy anchors consist of mature urban broadleaf trees:
- *Gleditsia triacanthos* var. *inermis* (Thornless Honeylocust): Compound pinnate foliage forming an open, spreading crown along sidewalk easements.
- *Ulmus americana* (American Elm / Princeton cultivar): Vase-shaped branching habit with dense, fully expanded dark green foliage.
- *Acer rubrum* (Red Maple): Full, dense late-summer crowns showing peak canopy density with mature, deep green leaf morphology and zero defoliation.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient urban entities—pedestrians, moving and parked vehicles, street traffic, municipal refuse containers, construction staging, and temporary signage—are completely excised. Static civil fixtures are preserved: saw-cut granite curbs, patterned brick sidewalk paving, asphalt road surfaces, painted continental crosswalk markings, fixed cast-iron bollards, and permanent streetlamp poles.
