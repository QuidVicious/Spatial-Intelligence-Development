# Spatial Domain Analysis: West End, Atholl Cres, Edinburgh EH3 8HA, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- Subterranean Bedrock: Underlain by the Carboniferous Strathclyde and Inverclyde Group sandstones, siltstones, and basalts characteristic of the Edinburgh volcanic complex.
- Indigenous Lithics: Predominantly polished fine-grained pale honey-buff and grey Craigleith and Hailes sandstones (Calciferous Sandstone Series) dominating the ashlar masonry and coursed rubble tenements.
- Mortar Chemistry: Traditional non-hydraulic and feebly hydraulic slaked lime-sand mortars with fine grit aggregate, deeply recessed along restored joints.
- Drainage & Topography: Elevated terrace sloping gently northeast toward the Firth of Forth basin, with artificial subsurface drainage networks established during the 18th- and 19th-century New Town expansions.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- Core Focal Monument: St George's West Church (Charlotte Chapel, 58 Shandwick Place) featuring a Roman Baroque body (designed by David Bryce, 1866–1869) integrated with a 4-stage Venetian campanile/belfry terminating in a pale copper-clad pyramidal spire (designed by Sir Robert Rowand Anderson, 1879–1881, inspired by San Giorgio Maggiore).
- Skyline Secondary Anchor (Background Left): The Neoclassical dome of West Register House (formerly St George's Church, Charlotte Square, by Robert Reid, 1811–1814), featuring a tall peristyle drum supporting a ribbed copper dome and lantern cupola.
- Urban Tenement Blocks: 3- to 4-storey plus attic Classical and Victorian tenements with timber double-hung sash-and-case fenestration, rusticated ashlar ground-floor arcades, pronounced moulded string courses, dentilled eaves, vertical chimney stacks with terracotta chimney pots, and zinc/slate mansard roofs with dormer windows.
- Planar Rectification: All vertical load-bearing facades, corner pilasters, and campanile shafts plumbed perpendicular to the horizon; warped mesh artifacts eliminated from slate roofs, parapets, and cornices.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Facades: Fine-dressed Craigleith ashlar masonry exhibiting authentic atmospheric carbon patination, rain-wash striations, and biological micro-crusts within stone recesses.
- Spire & Dome Cladding: Heavily patinated verdigris copper sheeting on the Shandwick Place spire and the Charlotte Square dome.
- Roof Finishes: Dark grey Ballachulish and Scottish slate tiles with lead-flashed ridges, zinc valley gutters, and cast-iron rainwater downpipes.
- Ground Planes: Smooth asphalt carriage roadway with painted thermoplastic lane markings and granite setts/kerbs along sidewalks.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- Urban Canopy: Mature broadleaf deciduous trees (principally *Acer pseudoplatanus* / Sycamore, *Tilia x europaea* / Common Lime, and *Ulmus glabra* / Wych Elm) emerging from courtyard pockets and Charlotte Square gardens to the rear/east, rendered with dense, authentic leafy branching and natural organic canopies rather than solid polygonal volume.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
- Decluttering Confirmation: Complete removal of all moving and parked automobiles, vans, buses, bicycles, pedestrians, waste containers, wheelie bins, temporary traffic cones, and ephemeral paper posters. Permanent municipal street infrastructure (cast-iron street lamp standards, traffic signals, street sign posts, and granite kerbs) remains intact.
