High-angle 35mm documentary photography looking down at a prominent 4-storey Georgian sandstone residential crescent rising to 18.5m elevation along Moray Place in Edinburgh, complemented by lower 2-storey terraced rear extensions of 6.8m height stepping down the steep northern slope. The camera angles downward from an altitude of 177.5 meters looking south-southeast at 170.2° with a 32.8° field of view, capturing the sweeping concave rear curve of the ashlar masonry facades. Surfaces display authentic, weathered grey-buff Craigleith sandstone marked by deep vertical drainage soot stains, crisply plumbed 12-pane timber sash windows, protruding rear canted stone bays, leaded dormers, and grey slate mansard roofs studded with heavy ashlar chimney stacks and terracotta pots. Immediately abutting the deep terraced rear gardens and boundary walls to the right, a dense, steep-sided river valley canopy of mature beech, sycamore, and wych elm trees cascades down toward the Water of Leith gorge. Crisp, cool Edinburgh afternoon daylight (5600K) casts gentle raking shadows across the masonry facades, highlighting the structural relief of stone copings, slate pitches, and lush green garden lawns. Shot on Kodak Portra 400 with a 50mm architectural tilt-shift lens, sharp corner-to-corner focus, authentic atmospheric micro-haze, natural grain, and impeccably rectified vertical geometry.
