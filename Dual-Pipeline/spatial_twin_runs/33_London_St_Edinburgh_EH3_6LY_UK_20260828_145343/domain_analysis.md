# Spatial Domain Analysis: 33 London St, Edinburgh EH3 6LY, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean sequence comprised of Carboniferous sedimentary bedrock (Strathclyde Group sandstone, siltstone, and shale) overlain by dense Devensian lodgement till. Superstructure masonry is predominantly pale buff-to-grey Craigleith quartz-arenite sandstone, characterized by high silica cementation and exceptional compressive strength, with secondary Hailes micaceous laminated sandstone in rear elevations and foundation courses. Ashlar blocks are laid strictly on their natural quarry bed using historic non-hydraulic calcitic slaked lime mortar blended with sharp, well-graded pit sand. Subterranean moisture is managed through sunken front basement lightwells, stone drop arches, and vaulted coal cellars underpinning the flagstone pedestrian footways.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Edinburgh Second New Town classical Georgian urban fabric (London Street terminating into Drummond Place). Symmetrical palace-fronted terraced tenements and townhouses spanning 3 storeys over raised rusticated basements, topped with attic dormers. Planar facades rectified to absolute vertical gravity lines: V-jointed rusticated ashlar ground storeys, polished smooth ashlar upper storeys, continuous molded stringcourses, and dentiled/mutule eaves cornices with parapet blocking courses. Fenestration follows a strict vertical and horizontal grid with 12-pane (6-over-6) timber double-hung sash-and-case windows set into deep ashlar stone reveals with hand-tooled margins. Symmetrical dual-pitched timber roof structures clad in graded metamorphic slates, punctuated by substantial transverse ashlar chimney stacks crowned with cylindrical terracotta chimney cans. Sunken basement wells are bridged by monolithic droved ashlar platts and enclosed with cast-iron spearhead railings.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Primary Facades: Polished and droved Craigleith ashlar sandstone bearing atmospheric particulate weathering, including localized carbon-smoke encrustations under projecting cornices and rain-washed honey-buff surfaces on exposed projections.
- Roofs: Dark grey-blue Scottish West Highland and Welsh Penrhyn slates laid in diminishing courses, with dressed lead flashings, valleys, and dormer cladding.
- Civil Paving: Fine-grained Caithness flagstone pedestrian sidewalks, Whinstone (quartz-dolerite) kerbing, and dressed granite sett carriageway surfaces.
- Metalwork: Wrought and cast iron railings, balustrades, bracketed fanlight lanterns, and cast rainwater downpipes finished in matte black.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Drummond Place Gardens internal parkland enclave dominated by mature canopy trees: *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), *Fagus sylvatica* (European Beech), *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm), and *Tilia × europaea* (Common Lime). Phenological state for November 11: Advanced late-autumn defoliation (~85% leaf drop), revealing dense dark skeletal branching structures, intricate crown ramifications, and lingering clusters of desiccated ochre, russet, and dull yellow marcescent leaves. Understorey features trimmed turf covered in moist brown leaf litter and clipped *Taxus baccata* and *Ilex aquifolium* evergreen perimeter shrubs.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
Complete purge of all transient elements: zero motor vehicles, zero parked cars, zero pedestrians, zero modern street signage, wheelie bins, or surface trash. All permanent architectural and civil elements—granite setts, Caithness flags, iron railings, kerbstones, and mature botanical structures—are retained in pristine, geometrically plumbed static alignment.
