# Spatial Domain Analysis: 16 Great Stuart St, Edinburgh EH3 7TN, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- **Bedrock Stratigraphy**: Carboniferous sedimentary succession (Gullane Formation), comprising sandstone, siltstone, and mudstone strata underlying the Edinburgh New Town plateau, mantled by stiff lodgement till.
- **Lithic Masonry**: Fine-to-medium grained quartzose blonde Craigleith and Hailes sandstone; non-hydraulic to weakly hydraulic lime mortar with graded river sand aggregate in tight 3–5mm ashlar bedding joints.
- **Groundwater & Drainage**: Naturally sloping bedrock ridge shedding subterranean water northwest towards the Water of Leith basin; flagged subterranean area vaults with gravity-fed drainage channels.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- **Typology & Provenance**: Early 19th-century Regency/Late Georgian neoclassical residential terraces and elliptical circus (Lord Moray Estate, designed by James Gillespie Graham, constructed c. 1822–1831).
- **Massing & Elevation**: 3 storeys over rusticated/channelled basements with sunken area lightwells; attic storeys with slate-clad box and piended dormers.
- **Fenestration & Detailing**: Regular vertical grid of 6-over-6 and 12-pane painted timber sash-and-case windows; piano nobile first-floor window bays fitted with cast-iron anthemion and palmette balconettes/window guards; continuous moulded string courses, projecting eaves cornice, and plain ashlar blocking course.
- **Planar Rectification**: Photogrammetric surface collapse rectified to absolute plumb verticals; terrace planes rectified into continuous rectilinear (left) and sweeping concave elliptical (centre-distance) ashlar facades.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Masonry Dressings**: Polished Craigleith sandstone ashlar upper storeys; droved margins and channelled rustication at ground floor; droved ashlar coped chimney stacks with cylindrical terracotta chimney cans.
- **Civil & Structural Elements**: Scottish and Welsh grey-blue roofing slate with lead roll-ridge flashings; Caithness stone flag pavements and basement lightwell surfacing; dark asphalt carriageway edged with squared granite kerbs.
- **Ferrous Elements**: Black painted cast-iron spearhead and fleur-de-lis perimeter railings mounted on raised sandstone plinth kerbs.
- **Weathering & Pathology**: Residual historical coal-smoke carbon crusting in sheltered cornice undercuts and architraves; localized biological patination and micro-algae on damp lower sandstone courses; clean rain-washed sandstone facades across exposed upper ashlar.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- **Botanical Identifications**: *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), *Tilia cordata* (Small-leaved Lime), *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm), and *Fagus sylvatica* (European Beech) situated within the enclosed Ainslie Place Gardens.
- **Phenology (September 3)**: Late-summer to early-autumn transition; dense, high-volume canopies retaining primary chlorophyll green, with incipient apical chlorosis and marginal dull yellow/ochre tipping across upper branch peripheries; foliage fully intact without major abscission.
