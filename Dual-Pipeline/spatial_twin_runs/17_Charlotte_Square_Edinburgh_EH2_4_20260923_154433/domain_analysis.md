# Spatial Domain Analysis: 17 Charlotte Square, Edinburgh EH2 4DJ, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean foundation consists of Early Carboniferous sedimentary bedrock interbedded with volcanic basalt and dolerite intrusions of the Midland Valley basin. Superstructure lithics are predominantly local Craigleith and Hailes sandstone, characterized by fine-to-medium grained quartz arenite bound with silica and minor iron oxides, yielding high compressive strength and buff-grey coloration. Historic bedding joints utilize hot-mixed non-hydraulic slaked lime and coarse volcanic sand mortars. Subsurface drainage directs into modified urban culverts overlying glacial till.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Central civic focal point is West Register House (formerly St George's Church, 1811–1814, designed by Robert Reid after Robert Adam), featuring a monumental Neoclassical temple front with an Ionic portico, dentiled pediment, elevated cylindrical drum surrounded by paired Corinthian column pairs, and a ribbed hemispherical dome terminating in an octagonal lantern cupola. Flanking wings comprise continuous three-storey plus attic and rusticated basement Georgian palace-front terraced townhouses (Adam New Town scheme) characterized by rigorous vertical bay alignments, 6-over-6 timber sash-and-case fenestration, rusticated ground-floor masonry, projecting continuous stringcourses, and pitched roofs clad in Scottish slate with heavy ashlar chimneystacks. In the garden foreground stands the 1876 Prince Albert Memorial, featuring an equestrian bronze figure atop a multi-tiered polished granite pedestal with secondary sculptural bronze groupings at the corners.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- West Register House: Ashlar Craigleith sandstone with localized historic coal-smoke soot encrustations in unwashed relief recesses; ribbed dome clad in oxidized sheet copper exhibiting pale verdigris patina; cast bronze lantern finials.
- Georgian Terraces: Droved and polished Craigleith ashlar, rusticated sandstone ground courses, timber window frames with white lead-equivalent gloss paint, natural grey-black roofing slates, lead flashing along parapets and valleys.
- Monument: Dark patinated cast bronze equestrian figure and relief plaques set upon a pink-red Peterhead granite ashlar pedestal with carved plinth moldings.
- Ground Plane: Level expanses of manicured amenity turf (*Lolium perenne* and *Poa pratensis*), edge-trimmed against gravel/paved garden pathways.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Mature urban parkland specimens dispersed across the garden perimeter: *Platanus × acerifolia* (London plane), *Acer pseudoplatanus* (sycamore), and *Tilia × europaea* (common lime). Late September phenology (2026-09-23): Dense canopies showing early autumn senescence, leaf margins exhibiting pale chlorosis, yellow-ochre tipping on lime and sycamore branches, minimal canopy thinning, and sparse early leaf scatter on turf beneath drip lines.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient visual clutter, vehicular traffic along the perimeter roadway, pedestrians, modern barricades, and photogrammetric mesh distortion artifacts have been completely excised. Only permanent geological, botanical, and architectural fabric remains.
