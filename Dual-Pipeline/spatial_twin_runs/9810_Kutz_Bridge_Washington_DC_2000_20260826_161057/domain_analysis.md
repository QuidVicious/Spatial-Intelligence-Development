# Spatial Domain Analysis: 9810 Kutz Bridge, Washington, DC 20004, USA

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The subterranean bedrock beneath the National Mall and Monument Grounds sits upon the Atlantic Coastal Plain margin overlying the Piedmont crystalline basement (metamorphic gneiss and schist). The immediate surface stratum is low-lying Potomac alluvial silt, sand, and gravel, fortified beneath the Washington Monument by a massive late 19th-century concrete underpinning and granite buttress foundation. 

Lithic composition is distinctly multi-phase:
- **Washington Monument:** The lower 152-foot (46 m) tier is built with white fine-grained Texas/Cockeysville marble from Baltimore County, Maryland, bonded with natural hydraulic cement. At the 152-foot mark, a distinct horizontal line transitions through a thin band of Massachusetts Sheffield marble into the upper two-thirds (398 ft) faced with Cockeysville/Beaver Dam marble, backed with New England granite ashlar and Seneca red sandstone masonry.
- **Federal Triangle & Surrounding Civic Fabric (Herbert C. Hoover Building, EPA / Clinton Building, IRS):** Heavy ashlar claddings of Mississippian Salem Oolitic Limestone (Indiana Limestone) resting over dark Deer Isle / Cold Spring crystalline granite plinths.
- **National Museum of African American History and Culture (NMAAHC):** Corona structure engineered with architectural bronze-tinted cast aluminum latticework panels mounted on a structural steel frame.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- **Centerpiece (Washington Monument):** A pure 555 ft 5⅛ in (169.29 m) hollow Egyptian-style obelisk tapering at 1:48, capped with a 55-foot pyramidal apex (pyramidion) terminating in a cast aluminum capstone. Vertical plumb lines are absolute, terminating at a circular granite plaza surrounded by concentric flagstone rings and 56 commemorative flagpoles.
- **Left/Mid-Ground North (Federal Triangle Complex):** Monumental Neoclassical and Beaux-Arts civic office blocks (Herbert C. Hoover Building / Department of Commerce, Ronald Reagan Building, EPA/Ariel Rios buildings). 6 to 8 habitable storeys (30m–38m cornice height) defined by colossal Tuscan and Ionic colonnades, deeply recessed fenestration bays, vermiculated rusticated bases, and hipped roofs clad in red semi-cylindrical terra-cotta mission barrel tiles.
- **Mid-Ground East/Right (National Mall Axis):** Open planar turf panels (The Mall) flanked by Beaux-Arts neoclassical museum buildings (National Museum of Natural History with green copper dome, National Gallery of Art West Building with Tennessee pink marble massing), leading toward the US Capitol dome in the far eastern vanishing point.
- **Rectification:** Vertical lines along all stone colonnades, obelisk corner ribs, and building corners are plumbed straight to true gravity vertical; curved walking loops and elliptical road rings conform to precise, planar landscape grades.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Obelisk Facade:** Differentiated marble courses showing a distinct tonal and textural shift at the 152 ft mark—the lower tier has a warm, slightly darker grey-tan historic calcitic patina with softened mortar lines, while the upper tier is lighter off-white to creamy beige with subtle vertical rain-wash tracks and minor atmospheric particulate soiling in the recessed stone joints.
- **Federal Triangle Buildings:** Neutral warm buff Indiana limestone showing subtle gypsum crusting under deep cornices and architraves, weathered copper flashing with pale green verdigris run-off, and low-sheen multi-pane bronze/steel casement windows.
- **Ground Surfaces:** Crushed aggregate gravel walkways, granite curb borders, light grey split-face granite pavers on the central monument plinth, and fine-graded mowed turf.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- **Dominant Species:** Mature American Elms (*Ulmus americana*) lining the perimeter of the National Mall panels in formal double-row allees; London Plane Trees (*Platanus occidentalis × acerifolia*), Pin Oaks (*Quercus palustris*), Willow Oaks (*Quercus phellos*), and American Sycamores scattered along Constitution Avenue and the monument knoll grounds.
- **Canopy Structure:** Fully foliated, dense green summer/early autumn canopies with organic, multi-tiered branching structures, clear understories, and soft leafy shadows cast upon the grass turf.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient visual noise has been strictly removed: zero vehicles on Constitution Avenue and 15th Street NW, zero pedestrian figures on the monument plaza or walkways, zero temporary security barriers, dumpsters, or trash receptacles. Permanent civil infrastructure—cast-iron lampposts, formal granite retaining curbs, perimeter flagpoles, fixed park benches, and paved circulation pathways—is immaculately preserved.
