# Spatial Domain Analysis: 12 Charlotte Square, Edinburgh EH2 4ET, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- Bedrock: Carboniferous sedimentary strata of the Strathclyde Group (Gullane Formation and Calders Formation sandstones, siltstones, and oil-shales), interspersed with quartz-dolerite intrusions and underlying glacial till (boulder clay).
- Lithic Provenance: Fine- to medium-grained Craigleith carboniferous quartzose sandstone (pale honey-buff to grey-ochre, high-silica matrix) ashlar blocks and Hailes sandstone rubblestone foundations.
- Mortar Chemistry: Traditional non-hydraulic to weakly hydraulic slaked lime (quicklime) mortar with sharp river sand aggregate, lime pointing with historical coal-dust ash adulteration in re-pointing phases.
- Hydrology & Subterranean Drainage: Sloping glacial drumlin terrain shedding groundwater northward toward the Water of Leith basin, mitigated by subsurface stone vaulting under pavements and subterranean cellar drainage voids.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- Primary Structure (Center): St George’s Church (West Register House), designed by Robert Reid (1811–1814) adapting Robert Adam's masterplan. Neoclassical Greek-cross/centralized plan featuring a monumental tetrastyle Ionic portico with pediment, rusticated basement/plinth, corner ashlar pavilions with round-headed blind niches and balustrades, rising to an octagonal drum, circular colonnaded peristyle drum, and a copper-clad ribbed dome crowned by a gilded stone lantern and cross.
- Flanking Terraced Townhouses: Robert Adam’s A-listed Georgian New Town terrace compositions (Charlotte Square West elevation). Strict symmetrical 3-storey plus attic mansard and sunken basement/area lightwells. Regular 3-bay modules, rusticated ground floors with segmental-arched or square-headed openings, v-jointed channelled masonry, smooth ashlar upper storeys with molded string courses, dentiled cornices, and blocking courses.
- Secondary/Rear Infill: 20th-century institutional extensions along rear lanes featuring flat bituminous roofs, horizontal ribbon glazing, and precast concrete spandrel panels, abutting traditional 2- to 3-storey mews buildings with pitched slate roofs and rubble masonry.
- Fenestration & Planar Rectification: 6-over-6 and 12-over-12 double-hung timber sash-and-case windows set in deep stone reveals; semicircular fanlights over panelled timber entrance doors. All vertical ashlar walls rectified to plumb vertical axes; roof planes flattened along authentic ridge and mansard hips.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Primary Facades: Droved and polished Craigleith sandstone ashlar, V-profile horizontal rustication at ground tier, fine dressed ashlar upper tiers; extensive urban coal-smoke gypsum encrustation (black carbon crusts on sheltered cornices, soffits, and entablatures) balanced by wind-scoured, rain-washed blonde reveals on exposed friezes.
- Dome & Roofing: Standing-seam copper sheet on the hemispherical dome exhibiting dense, stable pale-green verdigris (basic copper carbonate/sulphate patina) over timber carcase; gilded finial/lantern; Ballachulish blue-grey slates laid in diminishing courses on mansard and pitched roofs; rolled lead sheet valleys, ridges, hips, and flat dormer caps.
- Ground Plane & Boundary Enclosures: Paved walkways in riven Caithness stone flagstones and pale granite kerbs; carriageway surfaced in bituminous asphalt over historic whinstone (dolerite) setts; wrought- and cast-iron spearhead railings, urn finials, and lamp brackets treated in matte black protective bitumen paint.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- Deciduous Canopy (Edge of Charlotte Square Garden): Mature *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore) and *Tilia × europaea* (Common Lime) specimens at square periphery.
- Phenological State (December 22): Full winter dormancy/complete defoliation; completely bare architectural branch silhouettes, exposed ramified crown twigs, grey-brown rugose bark with patches of dormant green corticolous lichens (*Lecanora*, *Xanthoria*). Zero persistent foliage.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All motorized transport (buses, delivery vans, passenger cars), transient street furnishings, temporary traffic cones, wheelie bins, and human figures are entirely expunged. Retained static civic infrastructure comprises permanent basalt kerbs, Caithness flagstone pavements, cast-iron area railings, and fixed street lamps.
