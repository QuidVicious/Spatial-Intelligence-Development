# Spatial Domain Analysis: 9 Ainslie Pl, Edinburgh EH3 6AT, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean foundation situated on the Lower Carboniferous Gullane Formation (Strathclyde Group), characterized by interbedded sandstones, siltstones, and mudstones underlain by glacial till deposits. Superstructure masonry comprises fine-grained, silica-cemented pale buff-grey Craigleith and Hailes sandstone ashlar. Mortar chemistry consists of traditional non-hydraulic to weakly hydraulic slaked lime (*calcium hydroxide*) blended with graded quartz river sand and crushed sandstone aggregates, facilitating vapor permeability. Sub-grade moisture is managed via excavated dry area lightwells lined with coursed rubble retaining walls.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Category A listed Regency neoclassical concave circus terrace designed by James Gillespie Graham (1822) forming the Moray Estate crescent. Massing follows a 3-storey plus attic and submerged basement configuration, flanked by advanced 4-storey terminal pavilions. Planar alignment rectifies bowed photogrammetry into plumb vertical load-bearing facades:
- Ground floor (principal floor): Deep V-jointed rusticated sandstone ashlar with round-arched/lintelled door openings and recessed rectangular window apertures.
- Piano nobile and upper storeys: Polished smooth ashlar punctuated by molded continuous stringcourses, cill courses, projecting window architraves with decorative cornices at the first floor, and an eaves entablature crowned by a solid ashlar blocking course parapet.
- Fenestration: Regular grid of timber-framed sash-and-case windows (predominantly 6-over-6 and 2-over-2 configurations) painted off-white.
- Entrances: Six-panel timber doors set within ashlar surrounds, surmounted by geometric fanlights, accessed across sandstone bridging platts oversailing the basement area lightwells.
- Roof: Mansard and pitched rafters clad in traditional Scottish/Welsh slate tiles with dressed ashlar chimney stacks and cylindrical terracotta pots.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Primary facade: Craigleith quartzitic sandstone ashlar, displaying authentic urban sulfur-dioxide gypsum crusts in sheltered cornices and light surface micro-abrasions from North Sea maritime exposure.
- Area railings and ironwork: Wrought and cast iron spearhead balustrades, urn finials, and integral overthrow lantern brackets finished in semi-gloss black enamel.
- Pavements & ground plane: Caithness flagstone footpaths with squared whinstone/granite kerbstones flanking a dressed whinstone sett carriageway.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Ainslie Place central pleasure gardens feature mature deciduous urban parkland canopy anchored by *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), *Tilia × europaea* (Common Lime), and *Fagus sylvatica* (European Beech). Early September phenology manifests as full, dense, dark green crown foliage exhibiting nascent edge chlorosis and minor wind-frayed leaf margins, without significant leaf drop. Low-profile manicured turf and perimeter hedging (*Taxus baccata*) line the inner boundary behind the perimeter iron railing.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient visual noise—including modern motor vehicles, pedestrians, waste bins, temporary street signs, and photogrammetric distortion artifacts—has been eliminated. Fixed historical street infrastructure (cast-iron lampposts, stone kerbing, boundary railings) is retained in an undisturbed architectural state.
