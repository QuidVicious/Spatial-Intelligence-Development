# Spatial Domain Analysis: 13 E Basin Dr SW, Washington, DC 20004, USA

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The Thomas Jefferson Memorial is situated atop engineered hydraulic fill placed during the late 19th-century dredging of the Potomac River Flats. Beneath the artificial fill lie Quaternary alluvium deposits and the Cretaceous Potomac Formation (unconsolidated silts, sands, and gravels) resting over Piedmont crystalline schist and gneiss bedrock. Due to high groundwater saturation and tidal fluctuations from the adjacent Tidal Basin, the structural superstructure is supported by 637 steel-cased concrete cylinders driven down to solid bedrock. The exterior stepped terraces and foundation bulkheads are constructed of fine-grained Georgia granite, while the main colonnade, portico, and stepped dome exterior are carved from Vermont Imperial Danby white marble. The interior chamber integrates an Indiana limestone dome interior, Tennessee pink marble flooring, and an axial black Minnesota granite pedestal.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Designed in the Neoclassical / Classical Revival style by John Russell Pope (completed by Eggers & Higgins in 1943), the monument is modeled after the Roman Pantheon. It features a circular open-air rotunda enclosed by a colonnade of 26 fluted Ionic columns, fronted to the north by an octastyle portico with an additional 12 Ionic columns supporting a triangular sculptured pediment. The structure is capped by a shallow, stepped circular marble dome. All vertical column shafts, wall faces, and terrace retaining walls are rectified to absolute plumb vertical, correcting photogrammetric mesh shearing. The perimeter tiered plinths, granite stairways, and surrounding curved retaining parapets are straightened into pure geometric radii and planes.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Colonnade, Portico & Exterior Dome: Vermont Imperial Danby marble displaying natural calcitic veining, micro-crystalline luster, and subtle grey atmospheric weathering concentrated along drip courses, cornices, and fluted column recesses.
- Stepped Terraces & Retaining Walls: Axed Georgia granite retaining blocks exhibiting light grey salt-and-pepper crystalline speckling with tight hydraulic mortar joints.
- Paved Plazas & Walkways: Exposed-aggregate concrete and scored washed-gravel asphalt paths with low-sheen weathered surfaces, bordered by dressed granite curbing.
- Tidal Basin Seawall: Dressed coping stone blocks and rough-hewn seawall masonry bordering the dark, calm brackish water.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
The monument is encircled by mature urban parkland and riparian flora:
- Canopy Trees: Dense stands of Japanese flowering cherries (*Prunus serrulata* / *Prunus × yedoensis*) lining the shoreline path, complemented by American elms (*Ulmus americana*), willow oaks (*Quercus phellos*), and London planetrees (*Platanus occidentalis* / *acerifolia*) with distinct leafy branching structure and organic canopies.
- Shrubbery & Lawn: Manicured Kentucky bluegrass (*Poa pratensis*) turf on the surrounding berms, bordered by low, dense evergreen boxwood hedges (*Buxus sempervirens*) and yew ground covers along the concentric stone retaining walls.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All temporary construction barriers, orange plastic fencing, metal scaffolding, service vehicles, parked/moving passenger cars, tourist crowds, individual pedestrians, and transient debris have been entirely removed. Permanent civil infrastructure—including fixed radial asphalt walkways, stone balustrades, cast-iron bollards, bronze architectural doors, and period cast-iron streetlamps—remains pristine and grounded.
