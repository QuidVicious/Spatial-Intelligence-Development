# Spatial Domain Analysis: 6 Rothesay Pl, Edinburgh EH3 7SL, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The site occupies the incised gorge of the Water of Leith, founded upon Lower Carboniferous sedimentary bedrock of the Strathclyde Group (sandstone, siltstone, and mudstone formations interspersed with volcanic dolerite sills). Historic structural masonry comprises local Carboniferous Craigleith and Hailes pale buff-to-grey quartzose sandstone ashlar alongside imported Permian red sandstone (Corsehill / Dumfriesshire origin) for decorative relief and lintels. Bedding mortars consist of historic non-hydraulic and feebly hydraulic lime-sand compositions, transitioning to Portland-cement-modified mortars on mid-to-late 20th-century peripheral works. Subterranean moisture dynamics are heavily dictated by the alluvial river corridor and steep valley-wall groundwater seepage.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
A dense, multi-era village enclave characterized by steep vertical topography and complex roofscapes:
- **Historic Core (Well Court Complex, 1883–1886, Sydney Mitchell):** 3- to 4-storey Arts and Crafts / Scottish Baronial tenement blocks featuring prominent crow-stepped and steep-pitched gables, conical-capped stair turrets, a clock tower with pyramidal timber fleche, multiple pitched-roof dormers with timber-framed casement fenestration, and timber sash-and-case configurations (predominantly 6-over-6 and 8-over-8 patterns).
- **Valley-Floor & Flank Infill Developments (Late 20th Century):** 2- to 4-storey terraced and staggered residential blocks exhibiting continuous pitched rooflines, gabled wallhead projections, regularly spaced timber and UPVC casement windows, and integrated ground-level garage bays.
- **Perimeter Upper Tenements:** 4-storey classic Victorian Scottish tenements with vertical timber sash-and-case windows, vertical chimney stacks with clay pots, and slate mansard attic storeys.
- **Geometric Rectification:** All wall planes rectified to true gravitational vertical; dormer cheeks, gable ridges, roof valleys, and chimney alignments straightened into crisp, coplanar linear geometry.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Well Court & Historic Fabric:** Dressed red Permian sandstone coursed ashlar and squared rubble, sections of traditional pale-cream lime harling (roughcast), Scotch slate on conical turrets and dormer pitches, and natural handmade dark-red/autumn-brown clay plain roof tiles. Heavy carbon-crust soot patination concentrated beneath string courses and window sills, with localized bio-patination (moss, chlorophyta) along wet valley gutters.
- **Modern Flank Residences:** Textured pale buff/ochre mineral render over blockwork, cast stone lintels, and preformed profiled red-brown clay pantiles and concrete interlocking tiles.
- **Upper Perimeter Fabric:** Fine-grained pale blonde Craigleith sandstone ashlar with weathered grey mineral wash and dark bituminous coatings on chimney flaunching.
- **Ground Surfacing:** Whinstone and granite sett paving, dressed sandstone retaining parapets, and tarmacadam access courts.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Mature riparian and valley-slope woodland belt dominated by *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), *Fraxinus excelsior* (European Ash), *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm), *Salix fragilis* (Crack Willow), and *Alnus glutinosa* (Common Alder). Canopy state for early September corresponds to full, mature late-summer foliar volume, displaying deep-green hues transitioning to subtle early chlorosis and muted yellowing on peripheral upper crown foliage, with dense understory vegetation lining the steep embankment.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient vehicles, parked cars, pedestrians, wheeled bins, traffic cones, and temporary utility signage have been completely excised. Fixed civil infrastructure—including stone boundary walls, cast-iron railings, stone stairs, and permanent lampposts—is fully retained and geometrically rectified.
