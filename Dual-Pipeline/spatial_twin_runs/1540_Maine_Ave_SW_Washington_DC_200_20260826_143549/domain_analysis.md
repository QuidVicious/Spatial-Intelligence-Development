# Spatial Domain Analysis: 1540 Maine Ave SW, Washington, DC 20004, USA

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The Washington Monument grounds occupy the transition zone between the Piedmont Province and the Atlantic Coastal Plain, situated atop Quaternary alluvium, river terrace gravels, and clays of the Potomac Formation resting on deep crystalline Precambrian-to-Paleozoic metamorphic bedrock (schist and gneiss). Due to original settlement issues, the obelisk rests upon an engineered underpinning of bluestone gneiss aggregate and mass concrete installed by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers.

The obelisk’s exterior ashlar comprises three distinct lithological strata:
1. **Lower 152 feet (Phase I, 1848–1854):** Coarse-grained calcitic marble quarried from the Texas Quarry in Baltimore County, Maryland.
2. **Intermediate beltline:** Four courses of fine-grained Early Ordovician Stockbridge/Sheffield calcitic marble from Lee/Sheffield, Berkshire County, Massachusetts.
3. **Upper 390+ feet (Phase II, 1879–1884):** Fine-grained dolomitic Cockeysville Marble containing phlogopite mica from Cockeysville, Maryland, backed with Devonian biotite granite from Mount Waldo, Maine.

The surrounding plaza incorporates dense, fine-grained grey granites and *Mesabi Black* gabbroic/noritic granite paving. Subterranean drainage dynamics are managed through perimeter deep-well storm collection discharging toward the Tidal Basin and Potomac River.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- **Centerpiece (Washington Monument):** Pure unreinforced stone masonry obelisk designed by Robert Mills, executed and structurally refined to a height of 554 ft 7 11/32 in (169.05 m) with a 10:1 height-to-base proportion. The shaft tapers continuously to a 55-foot pyramidal apex (pyramidion) capped with an aluminum point. Vertical load-bearing lines are plumb, and corners maintain crisp 90-degree planar convergence.
- **Plaza & Hardscape:** Symmetrical concentric circular granite security barrier walls and pedestrian aprons encompassing 56 ringed steel flagpoles, intersected by curved, geometric decomposed granite/gravel pedestrian pathways cutting through manicured lawn turf.
- **Right Flank (National Museum of African American History and Culture):** Designed by Freelon Adjaye Bond / SmithGroup, massed as a 5-storey above-grade, 3-tiered inverted step pyramid ("Corona") inspired by the Yoruban Caryatid, cantilevering outward at a 17-degree angle that matches the monument's pyramidion. 
- **North / Northeast Perimeter (Federal Triangle / National Museum of American History):** Strict monumental Neoclassical and Modernist civic massing, defined by multi-storey colonnaded limestone volumes, crisp horizontal parapets, and recessed rooftop mechanical enclosures.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Washington Monument:** Dressed ashlar marble showing subtle two-tone color shift at the 152-foot level (warmer, slightly weathered Texas marble below; brighter, cooler-grey Cockeysville marble above). Weathering consists of Mid-Atlantic humid subtropical patina: localized rainwater dissolution lines along vertical mortar joints, micro-pitting, minor carbon deposition beneath horizontal ashlar relief, and subtle oxidation of interior iron/pyrite traces.
- **NMAAHC:** 3,600 cast-aluminum filigree screens finished in a multi-layered warm metallic bronze coating, suspended over dark curtain-wall structural glazing and reinforced concrete cores. Patina reflects uniform matte bronze warmth with diffuse skylight reflectivity.
- **Civil Hardscape:** Thermal-finished gray granite curbings, honed *Mesabi Black* granite benches/copings, brushed steel security bollards, and compressed crushed ochre aggregate pathways.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- **Canopy Trees:** Mature stands of American elm (*Ulmus americana*), London plane (*Platanus occidentalis × orientalis*), northern red oak (*Quercus rubra*), white oak (*Quercus alba*), and sweetgum (*Liquidambar styraciflua*) framing the perimeter of the Monument grounds and flanking Constitution Avenue. Canopies are fully foliage-dense, featuring natural branching structures, deciduous leaf texture, and organic root flare zones.
- **Ground Cover:** Uniformly graded, aerated Kentucky bluegrass (*Poa pratensis*) and tall fescue (*Festuca arundinacea*) turfgrass marked by crisp radial mowing lines, devoid of photogrammetric mesh distortion.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All temporary elements, motor traffic, tour buses, transient service vehicles, mobile vendors, dumpsters, temporary fencing, and pedestrians are fully purged. Permanent municipal and institutional infrastructure—including anchored flagpoles, granite ring-walls, security bollards, fixed bronze interpretive plaques, lamp posts, and granite-bordered aggregate pathways—remains structurally intact and pristine.
