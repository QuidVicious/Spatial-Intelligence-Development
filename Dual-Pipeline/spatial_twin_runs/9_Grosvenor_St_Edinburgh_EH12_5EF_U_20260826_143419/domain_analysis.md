# Spatial Domain Analysis: 9 Grosvenor St, Edinburgh EH12 5EF, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The subterranean terrain rests upon the Lower Carboniferous sedimentary formations of the Strathclyde Group (primarily Gullane Formation sandstones and silty mudstones), overlaid with firm glacial till. The primary structural fabric of the central monument—St Mary’s Episcopal Cathedral—is constructed from hard, fine-to-medium-grained Carboniferous sandstone quarried locally and regionally from Dunmore, Craigleith, and Hailes sandstone beds, with colonnettes and decorative load-bearing shafts utilizing pink Shap granite from Cumbria and polished dark igneous stones. The surrounding Georgian and Victorian West End residential terraces are built from buff-blonde to honey-toned Craigleith and Binny sandstone ashlar. Mortar systems consist of historic non-hydraulic and feebly hydraulic lime-sand mixes, promoting natural moisture breathability across high-density ashlar coursing. Subterranean moisture drains naturally toward the Water of Leith basin to the north.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- **Centerpiece (St Mary's Episcopal Cathedral):** High Victorian Gothic Revival (Early Pointed style) designed by Sir George Gilbert Scott (consecrated 1879; western spires completed 1917). Cruciform plan with a nave, lean-to side aisles, clerestory, transepts, and an apsidal choir. Features three massive stone spires—a soaring central crossing spire (90 meters tall) and twin western towers topped with octagonal broach spires ("Barbara" and "Mary"). Planar rectification enforces rigid vertical plumb lines on all buttresses, crocketed pinnacles, lancet fenestration, rose windows, and spire apexes.
- **Flanking and Foreground Fabric:** Unified Late Georgian to Early Victorian 3- to 4-storey residential tenement and townhouse terraces (along Palmerston Place, Manor Place, and Grosvenor Street). Symmetrical repetitive bays with rusticated ground-floor basements with sunken areas (lightwells), ashlar piano nobile storeys, molded continuous string courses, cornices, slate mansard/hipped roof planes, ashlar chimneystacks with ceramic chimney pots, and timber sash-and-case windows rectified to true horizontal and vertical planes. In the foreground right, the domed neoclassical lantern pavilions of adjacent institutional/residential corners stand geometrically plumb.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Cathedral Masonry & Roof:** Tooled and squared Carboniferous sandstone ashlar, displaying authentic 19th-century atmospheric carbon sooting deep within carved portal archivolts, under-eaves, and leeward clerestory tracery. Raking faces and exposed spires show localized rain-wash leaching and softened ashlar bedding planes. Roofs are clad in natural Welsh and Scottish West Highland dark blue-gray slates with lead ridge rolls and cast-iron/lead downpipes.
- **Terrace Housing Fabric:** Blonde-to-buff Edinburgh sandstone ashlar with subtle olive and gray tonal shifts. Lower basement retaining walls and rustication show typical maritime-temperate moisture patina, sub-aerial green micro-algae at shaded basecourses, and historic soot deposition along window architraves. Roofs feature dark slate tiling, lead flashing, and stone dormers.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
The cathedral grounds and perimeter residential gardens feature mature deciduous urban trees. Identified species include mature London plane trees (*Platanus x acerifolia*), European sycamores (*Acer pseudoplatanus*), English oaks (*Quercus robur*), and copper beeches (*Fagus sylvatica* f. *purpurea*), rendered with full, organic, branching leafy canopies and fine seasonal foliage, disambiguated entirely from adjacent stone walls. The cathedral lawns consist of dense, deep-green perennial ryegrass (*Lolium perenne*) and fescue turf bordered by low manicured shrubbery.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
The urban ground plane and street corridors (Palmerston Place, Grosvenor Street, Melville Street axis) are entirely stripped of all transient elements: zero moving or parked automobiles, zero buses, zero pedestrians, zero waste bins/dumpsters, and zero roadworks barriers. Preserved permanent civil infrastructure includes clean caithness stone flagstone pavements, hand-dressed whinstone/granite street curbs, tarmac road surfaces with crisp painted civil lane markings, cast-iron spearhead boundary railings along basement lightwells, and historic Victorian cast-iron streetlamps.
