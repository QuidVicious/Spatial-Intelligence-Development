# Spatial Domain Analysis: Waverley Steps, 1 Princes St., Edinburgh EH2 2EQ, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- Subterranean Bedrock: Early Carboniferous sedimentary succession of the Strathclyde Group (sandstones, siltstones, and bituminous shales) overlain by stiff Devensian glacial till.
- Lithics: Historic facades constructed from local Carboniferous buff-to-pale grey quartzitic Craigleith and Hailes sandstone; contemporary redevelopments incorporate matching buff sandstone rainscreen panels alongside Portland-stone-toned reconstituted architectural stone.
- Mortar Chemistry: Traditional structures constructed with slaked lime and sharp sand mortars with pozzolanic additives; modern structural frameworks secured with Portland cement bedding and engineered elastomeric sealant joints.
- Hydrogeology: Subsurface gradient drains southward toward the Waverley Valley / former Nor' Loch basin.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- Typology: Dense historic New Town commercial block featuring a juxtaposition of 19th-century Victorian Venetian Gothic warehouse forms, mid-20th-century monumental Neo-Classical commercial palazzo massing, and contemporary 21st-century adaptive reuse insertions.
- Facades & Massing:
  * Central Sector (The Mint Building & West Register Street redevelopment): Multi-storey rectilinear volume framed in structured buff stone piers with deeply recessed fenestration grids, topped by a set-back multi-level structural glass and zinc-clad penthouse pavilion. Integrates the restored lower multi-bay mansard and Venetian Gothic arched fenestration.
  * Background Right (The Edinburgh Grand / 42 St Andrew Square): 6-storey monumental Neo-Classical palazzo in channelled ashlar with stepped attic storey, heavy projecting stringcourses, and regular punched rectangular window grid.
  * Far Background: St Andrew Square framing the vertical fluted shaft of the Melville Monument.
  * Foreground & Left: Sloping roofscapes of Princes Street properties showing planar lead-sheathed flat decks, dormered slate mansards, brick chimney stacks, and skylight apertures.
- Planar Rectification: All photogrammetry distortion removed; vertical load-bearing lines plumbed to true gravity vertical; planar flat roofs and rectilinear curtain walls straightened.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Primary Masonry: Fine-grained, droved, and polished Craigleith buff sandstone ashlar; rusticated lower tiers; moulded cornices; precast architectural concrete lintels.
- Glazing & Framing: Floor-to-ceiling high-performance structural solar glass set within anthracite-finished extruded aluminum mullions; multi-pane timber sash-and-case windows on historic levels.
- Roofing: Blue-grey Scottish and Welsh roof slates; rolled sheet lead hip valleys, flashing, and zinc standing-seam capping on penthouse plant enclosures.
- Environmental Weathering: Historic Carboniferous sandstone displaying localized coal-smoke soot accumulation within shaded moldings and sheltered friezes; rain-washed stone highlights on exposed cornices; clean matte modern stone panels.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- Urban Canopy: St Andrew Square municipal park visible in the background.
- Tree Taxa: *Platanus × acerifolia* (London plane), *Acer pseudoplatanus* (sycamore), and *Tilia cordata* (small-leaved lime).
- Phenology: Fully expanded, dense, dark green late-summer canopies forming a continuous rounded botanical boundary behind the stone skyline.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
- Total elimination of all vehicles, buses, tram carriages, moving and stationary pedestrians, temporary signage, roadworks barriers, dumpsters, and street clutter. Static civil elements (stone kerbs, cast-iron parapets, permanent roof infrastructure) fully maintained.
