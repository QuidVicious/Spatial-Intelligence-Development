# Spatial Domain Analysis: 40 Charlotte Square, Edinburgh EH2 4HQ, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean foundation composed of Carboniferous sedimentary sequences (Gullane Formation sandstones and shales) overlying volcanic dolerite sills and basaltic bedrock of the Edinburgh central volcanic complex. Superstructure masonry comprises fine-grained, silica-cemented pale buff-grey Craigleith sandstone ashlar and Hailes/Ravelston sandstone rubble backing, bonded with non-hydraulic lime and sharp sand mortar. The monument plinth incorporates polished grey and pink Peterhead granite blocks.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Neoclassical civic and Georgian residential square ensemble:
- Central background: West Register House (former St George's Church, Robert Reid, 1811–1814) featuring a monumental Greek-cross central block, tetrastyle portico, square clock-tower attic, cylindrical peristyle drum with Corinthian pilasters, and a massive copper-clad ribbed dome terminating in an octagonal stone lantern and gilded cross.
- Flanking wings: Robert Adam-designed neoclassical terraced townhouses (3 storeys over rusticated basements with attic dormers), channelled rustication to ground floor, polished ashlar upper floors, ionic pilastered end/centre bays, 6-over-6 and 12-pane timber sash-and-case fenestration, and pitched Scottish slate roofs with regular stone ashlar chimney stacks.
- Foreground: Prince Albert Memorial (Sir John Steell, 1876), stepped stepped granite pedestal surmounted by a bronze equestrian statue flanked by bronze corner sculptural groupings. All facade planes and vertical colonnade lines plumbed to true gravity vertical.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- West Register House: Tooled and polished Craigleith sandstone ashlar, oxidised verdigris copper dome plating, lead flashings, and cast iron rainwater goods.
- Townhouse Terraces: Craigleith and Binny carboniferous buff sandstone ashlar, Ballachulish grey slate roof tiling, cast iron window guards and railings, timber sash frames.
- Monument: Cast bronze with stable green-brown cuprite/patina; polished rusticated granite plinth.
- Ground: Well-maintained perennial rye and meadow fescue turf; asphalt/tarmac roadway with whinstone/granite kerbstones visible along the perimeter perimeter.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Mature urban parkland specimens within Charlotte Square Gardens: *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), *Ulmus glabra* (Wych elm), and *Tilia cordata* (Small-leaved lime). Late September phenology: dense broadleaf canopies exhibiting early autumn chlorosis (mottled yellowing and pale olive-brown margins) with localized crown thinning and early leaf senescence.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient road vehicles, parked cars along Charlotte Square perimeter, pedestrians, cyclists, trash cans, and temporary barriers have been completely removed. Retained: permanent turf lawns, fixed bronze/stone monuments, tree canopies, stone kerbs, and architectural enclosures.
