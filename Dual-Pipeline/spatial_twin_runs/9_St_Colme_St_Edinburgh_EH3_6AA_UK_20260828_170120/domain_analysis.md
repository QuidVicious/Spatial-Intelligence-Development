# Spatial Domain Analysis: 9 St Colme St, Edinburgh EH3 6AA, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- Bedrock Stratigraphy: Lower Carboniferous (Tournaisian–Visean) sedimentary succession of the Inverclyde and Strathclyde Groups, predominantly calciferous sandstone measures interbedded with siltstone and mudstone, overlying Devonian Old Red Sandstone, bounded by the Calton fault block system.
- Primary Lithics: Upper Lower Carboniferous quartz arenite sandstone sourced directly from local quarries—predominantly Craigleith Quarry (pale buff to silver-grey siliceous sandstone), complemented by Hailes Quarry (argillaceous laminated sandstone) and Binny Sandstone for ashlar dressings.
- Aggregate & Paving: Devonian olivine-basalt and quartz-dolerite (Whin) kerbs and setts; Devonian Caithness flagstone and Carboniferous flagstone pedestrian pavements.
- Mortar Chemistry: Traditional hydraulic and non-hydraulic lime mortars composed of hot-mixed quicklime, local coarse river sand, and crushed sandstone fines, exhibiting natural carbonation with high vapour permeability.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- Typology: Georgian / Regency Neoclassical terraced townhouses (Albyn Place / St Colme Street, Moray Estate / New Town expansion) and monumental civic ecclesiastical architecture (West Register House / Former St George’s Church, designed by Robert Reid after Robert Adam, 1811–1814).
- Planar Rectification: All vertical facade planes rectified to absolute 90° plumb lines; photogrammetric mesh faceting eliminated across continuous rusticated basements, string courses, modillion cornices, and roof ridgelines.
- West Register House: Colossal square-plan neo-classical base with tetrastyle Ionic portico in antis, stepped stylobate, engaged corner pavilions, massive cylindrical peristyle drum with peristyle colonnade and alternating niches/oculi, surmounted by a ribbed hemispherical dome and gilded cupola lantern.
- Terraced Facades: 3-storey plus basement and attic terraced townhouses; channelled rusticated ground floors with voussoired segmental window arches; smooth dressed ashlar piano nobile with molded architraves and pediments; 6-over-6 and 2-over-2 timber double-hung sash-and-case fenestration grids; continuous blocking courses and parapets; Scottish slate pitched roofs punctured by periodic dormers and ashlar chimney stacks with terracotta pots.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Facade Envelopes: Droved and polished Craigleith sandstone ashlar ashlar blocks laid in regular ashlar courses; rusticated channelled sandstone ground floors; pick-dressed basement retaining walls.
- Dome & Roofing: Ribbed copper sheet roofing with fully developed verdigris (hydrated copper carbonate) patina on the St George's dome; Ballachulish and West Highland dark grey metamorphic roofing slates across terraced mansards and pitched gables; cast lead flashing, valleys, and ridge caps.
- Metalwork: Wrought- and cast-iron spearhead railings, decorative urn finials, overthrow lamp arches, and basement lightwell balustrades painted in matte historic dark oil paint.
- Micro-Climate Weathering: Differential surface patina with localized historical coal-smoke soot encrustations in sheltered friezes, rain-washed white/buff quartz arenite reveals on windward elevations, and minor biological micro-algal colonization along sandstone plinths and cornice projections.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- Urban Woodland Typology: Mixed cool-temperate urban estate trees within Queen Street Gardens and Charlotte Square enclosures.
- Species Identification: Dominance of *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm), *Tilia × europaea* (Common Lime), and occasional *Aesculus hippocastanum* (Horse Chestnut).
- Phenology (Mid-November / Epoch 2026-11-11): Late autumn transitional state; 80–90% canopy defoliation across mature deciduous specimens; intricate bare woody branch architectures exposed; residual sparse leaves exhibiting advanced senescent chlorosis, dull ochre-brown coloration, and curled margins clinging to terminal twigs; damp ground plane covered by fallen leaf litter.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
- Complete elimination of all dynamic elements: zero moving or stationary automobiles, buses, delivery vans, pedestrians, cyclists, temporary roadworks, plastic garbage receptacles, or transient urban debris.
- Retention of static civil fabric: plumb iron lampposts, continuous sandstone kerbstones, Caithness flagstone footways, cast-iron perimeter railings, stone boundary walls, and authentic asphalt carriage surfaces.
