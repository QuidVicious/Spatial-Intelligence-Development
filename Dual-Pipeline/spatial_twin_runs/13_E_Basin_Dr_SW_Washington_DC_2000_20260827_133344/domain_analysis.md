# Spatial Domain Analysis: 13 E Basin Dr SW, Washington, DC 20004, USA

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The subterranean profile beneath the southern shore of the Tidal Basin consists of anthropogenic hydraulic silt and gravel infill over late Quaternary Potomac Formation alluvium. Deep crystalline metamorphic bedrock (Piedmont schist/gneiss) lies tens of meters below. The memorial foundation rests on heavy reinforced concrete caissons sunk through the mud into solid bedrock to counteract structural settlement. Above grade, the monument's exterior peristyle, stylobate, steps, and drum are constructed from fine-grained white Imperial Danby marble quarried from Dorset Mountain in Vermont (Ordovician Shelburne Formation). The shallow stepped dome is faced in Middle Mississippian Indiana oolitic limestone (Salem Formation). The circular interior colonnade features Georgia white marble, with floor and substructure elements anchored by pink granite from Salisbury, North Carolina. Non-staining Portland cement mortar with lime binder fills the stone masonry joints.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Designed by John Russell Pope, Daniel P. Higgins, and Otto R. Eggers in the Neoclassical / Beaux-Arts Pantheon-inspired typology, completed in 1943. The primary structure comprises a monumental circular peristyle colonnade of 26 fluted Ionic columns supporting an unadorned frieze and a stepped circular attic topped by a shallow hemispherical dome. A pedimented portico opens north toward the National Mall, framed by a broad cascading flight of granite and marble steps leading down to the water's edge. The circular drum features rhythmic vertical openings between columns into an open-air central rotunda. Behind the water body across the axis rises the vertical obelisk of the Washington Monument (Robert Mills, 1884), perfectly plumbed to true gravity vertical against the distant planar geometry of Federal Triangle office complexes.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Primary Colonnade & Drum: Imperial Danby white marble displaying faint, authentic grey linear calcitic veining, softened by subtle atmospheric carbonate patination and localized moisture runoff staining along column bases and lower cornices.
- Dome: Finely dressed Indiana limestone blocks with mild surface pore weathering and natural mineral oxidation.
- Base & Embankment: Pink Salisbury granite retaining walls and kerbstones, along with dressed granite steps showing faint water-level mineral crusting.
- Surrounding Grounds: Asymmetrical semicircular asphalt access roads, bordered by cut stone curbing, brick-paved plazas, and manicured green turf lawns.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
The memorial is surrounded by dense groves of mature Yoshino cherry trees (*Prunus × yedoensis*) and Kwanzan cherries (*Prunus serrulata*) exhibiting full, textured summer canopies of deep green foliage and dark gnarled bark. Flanking the lawns and pathways are mature native hardwoods including American elm (*Ulmus americana*), willow oak (*Quercus phellos*), sweetgum (*Liquidambar styraciflua*), and red maple (*Acer rubrum*). The background shoreline across the Tidal Basin is densely populated with broadleaf deciduous tree cover.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All temporary construction fencing, pedestrian crowds, tour groups, passenger vehicles, tour buses, temporary maintenance bins, and transient street-level equipment have been entirely stripped from the roadways, plazas, and perimeter pathways. Fixed permanent infrastructure—including dressed granite seawalls, stone balustrades, cast-iron park bollards, and permanent park lamps—remains intact.
