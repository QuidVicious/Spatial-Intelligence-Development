# Spatial Domain Analysis: 301 14th St SW, Washington, DC 20228, USA

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- **Bedrock Lithology & Subterranean Stratigraphy**: The site straddles the boundary of the Atlantic Coastal Plain and the Piedmont physiographic province. Underneath the surficial fill sits a succession of Pleistocene river terrace deposits (silty/sandy clays and gravels), underlain by the Cretaceous Potomac Formation (dense clays and compact sands), resting on deeply weathered crystalline Precambrian/Early Cambrian metamorphic bedrock (Wissahickon Schist and Sykesville Formation gneiss).
- **Masonry Lithics & Indigenous Stone Sourcing**:
  - *Washington Monument*: Tripartite marble cladding comprising Texas (Cockeysville, Baltimore County, Maryland) calcitic marble for the lower 152 feet (1848–1854); a thin transitional band of Sheffield, Massachusetts dolomitic marble; and upper elevation Beaver Dam (Cockeysville, Maryland) marble (1879–1884). Backed with granite rubble from the Little Falls Quarry on the Potomac and blue gneiss rubble.
  - *Federal Triangle & Smithsonian Flanking Blocks*: Primary cladding consists of Salem Formation oolitic limestone (Indiana Limestone), Deer Isle / Stony Creek pink and gray granites at the plinths, and buff-hued architectural cast stone.
- **Mortar Chemistry & Subterranean Drainage**: High-density historic hydraulic lime-sand mortar in the lower monument core, transitioned to Portland-pozzolan hydraulic cements in the late-19th-century underpinning and upper shaft; modern subterranean retaining walls feature continuous slurry cutoffs and deep pile stabilization to resist high water-table hydrostatic pressure from the Potomac tidal basin.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- **Typologies & Massing**:
  - *Center Axis*: Egyptian Revival monumental obelisk (Washington Monument), towering at a verified civil height of 555 feet 5⅛ inches (169.29 m), with a 15-foot-square base plaza surrounded by a circular granite terrace and geometric serpentine pedestrian rings.
  - *Northeast Quadrant (Center-Right)*: Contemporary Neo-African / Deconstructivist Corona (Smithsonian National Museum of African American History and Culture - NMAAHC), designed by Freelon Adjaye Bond/SmithGroup, featuring a 5-storey inverted three-tiered stepped corona over an expansive sunken plinth with a southern cantilevered entry "Porch".
  - *East Flank (Right)*: Mid-Century Formalist/Modernist institutional block (National Museum of American History, Kenneth E. Behring Center, McKim Mead & White, 1964), configured as a 5-storey rectangular pavilion with recessed central bay and stripped classical marble/limestone pilasters.
  - *North Quadrant (Top Background)*: Classical Revival / Beaux-Arts Federal Triangle civic megastructures (Department of Commerce / Herbert C. Hoover Building and Internal Revenue Service), displaying monumental colonnades, rusticated bases, and classical pedimented terra cotta tile hipped roofs.
- **Planar Rectification & Anti-Warp Protocol**: The central obelisk is rectified to absolute gravitational vertical with crisp, razor-sharp tapering planar facets converging to its pyramidion cap. All rectilinear parapets, flat gravel-and-PV museum roofs, columned porticos, and horizontal floor plates of the flanking Federal Triangle and Smithsonian complexes are plumbed, eliminating any photogrammetric melt, jagged edges, or polygon distortion.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Washington Monument**: Dressed fine-grained white-to-light-gray crystalline marble ashlar blocks showing distinct horizontal colour differentiation (the distinct 152-foot construction hiatus line). Surfaces exhibit subtle natural atmospheric micro-patina, gentle gypsum wash on sheltered corner joints, and clean wind-swept stone facets.
- **NMAAHC (Corona Facade)**: 3,600 cast-aluminum architectural filigree panels with a warm, multi-layered bronze lacquer finish referencing historical Afro-American decorative ironwork; high-performance structural low-iron glass curtain wall recessed behind the metal lattice; dark gray zinc roof flashing and rooftop photovoltaic arrays.
- **Museum of American History & Federal Triangle**: Honed Indiana limestone ashlar wall panels, flame-finished Lake Placid granite plinths, copper-lined gutters with faint verdigris oxidation, and historic unglazed red-orange Spanish terra-cotta clay roof tiles with light atmospheric carbon accumulation in deeply recessed cornices.
- **Pavements & Surrounds**: Crushed buff pea-gravel decomposed granite pedestrian pathways, smooth-dressed gray granite curbs and plaza flags, and asphalt roadways with crisp thermoplastic painted markings.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- **Mature Deciduous & Coniferous Canopy**: Disambiguated organic tree groves, cleanly differentiated from masonry and civil surfaces.
- **Botanical Identifiers**:
  - American Elm (*Ulmus americana*) lining the primary formal allées with high arching vase-shaped canopies.
  - London Plane (*Platanus occidentalis × orientalis*) and American Sycamore along roadway margins, displaying mottled olive-and-cream exfoliating bark.
  - Willow Oak (*Quercus phellos*), Pin Oak (*Quercus palustris*), and Red Maple (*Acer rubrum*) populating the rolling lawn clusters.
  - Manicured turfgrass matrix comprising Kentucky Bluegrass (*Poa pratensis*) and Tall Fescue (*Festuca arundinacea*) exhibiting distinct circular and radial mown striping patterns.
