## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The subterranean geology of Edinburgh's West End is founded upon the Lower Carboniferous Gullane Formation and Strathclyde Group sedimentary strata, dominated by silica-cemented, fine-to-medium grained quartz arenite sandstones interleaved with siltstones and mudstones. The prominent architectural masonry throughout the view is cut from historic regional quarries—principally pale buff-to-grey Craigleith sandstone, noted for its high quartz density (over 95% silica) and remarkable resistance to chemical degradation, with secondary elements utilizing softer blonde sandstone from Hailes and Dunfermline beds. The deep mortar matrix across the Victorian and Edwardian facades consists of traditional non-hydraulic to weakly hydraulic slaked quicklime and sharp crushed river sand, formulated to allow vapor permeability and prevent frost-induced moisture entrapment over centuries of Scottish maritime freeze-thaw cycles.
