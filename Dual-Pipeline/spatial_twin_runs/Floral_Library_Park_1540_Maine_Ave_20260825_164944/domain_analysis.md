# Spatial Domain Analysis: Floral Library Park, 1540 Maine Ave SW, Washington, DC 20004, USA

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The Washington Monument grounds occupy the transition zone between the Atlantic Coastal Plain sedimentary wedges and the crystalline bedrock of the Piedmont Province. Bedrock beneath this section of the National Mall consists of late Proterozoic to early Paleozoic metamorphic rock (Kensington Tonalite and Potomac Formation saprolites), overlaid by alluvial silt, clay, and historic artificial fill from Potomac River tidal flat reclamations. 

The primary lithic material of the central 555-foot obelisk features three distinct stratifications of ashlar facing:
1. **Lower Phase (Base to ~150 ft):** Calcitic white marble from the Cockeysville Formation, quarried at Texas, Baltimore County, Maryland.
2. **Intermediate Beltline (approx. 4 courses at 150–160 ft):** Ordovician Stockbridge Marble from Sheffield/Lee, Berkshire County, Massachusetts, exhibiting distinct brown/grey dolomitic banding and darker oxidation.
3. **Upper Phase (160 ft to 555 ft apex):** Finer-grained Cockeysville dolomitic metalimestone quarried near Cockeysville, Maryland.
The inner masonry core is comprised of crystalline blue gneiss and Maine Dix Island granite bonded with hydraulic lime and early Portland cement mortars. Subterranean drainage around the obelisk knoll is heavily engineered via deep retaining stabilization piles, aggregate drainage layers, and perimeter sump routing to mitigate Potomac tidal groundwater intrusion.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- **A. Foreground & Circular Apron (1540 Maine Ave SW / Monument Grounds):** A gently sloping turf knoll traversed by curvilinear, fine-aggregate crushed-granite pathways and asphalt service loops. The base of the obelisk is circumscribed by a broad, laser-level paved plaza flanked by 56 static stainless-steel flagpoles arranged in an exact circle.
- **B. Center Structure (Washington Monument):** A 555-foot-5-inch (169.29 m) neo-Egyptian geometric stone obelisk terminating in an unornamented pyramidion capstone with a 17.2° slope. Vertical edges plumb directly to true gravity vertical with strict planar rectitude, devoid of photogrammetric mesh skewing or warped seams.
- **C. Midground & Background Right Structures (Federal Triangle & National Mall):**
  - *National Museum of African American History and Culture (NMAAHC):* A 5-story above-grade inverted stepped ziggurat ("Corona") featuring a three-tiered trapezoidal massing leaning at a ~17° outward angle, matching the obelisk capstone angle.
  - *Federal Triangle / Department of Commerce / Ronald Reagan Building:* Massive 6- to 7-story classical revival and Beaux-Arts limestone colonnades, rusticated bases, and mansard roofs covered in terracotta Spanish-S clay tiles.
  - *Old Post Office Pavilion (Right Background):* Romanesque Revival granite and limestone clock tower rising to a dominant conical spire.
- **D. Left Background:** Classical colonnades and white Indiana limestone/marble facades of the White House complex, Executive Office buildings, and downtown DC commercial grid.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Washington Monument:** Differentiated two-tone natural patina across the stone envelope. The lower 150 ft exhibits subtle biological patina, atmospheric carbon wash in the hairline mortar joints, and cream-buff mineralization; the distinct Sheffield beltline is visually evident; the upper two-thirds display a colder, crystalline, pale-grey dolomitic limestone tone. Fine seismic repairs and lime-grout repointing from 2011 are subtly visible upon close inspection.
- **NMAAHC:** 3,600 cast-aluminum filigree screens finished in a multi-layered, deep bronze-brown metallic PVDF coating mimicking historic African American decorative wrought-iron lattice, layered over dark low-iron curtain-wall glazing.
- **Federal Triangle Complexes:** Honed Indiana Bedford limestone ashlar blocks displaying gentle grey gypsum crusting in sheltered cornices and pediments, topped by aged kiln-fired red clay roof tiles.
- **Ground Surfaces:** Stabilized decomposed granite pedestrian paths, manicured fescue-bluegrass turf, and dressed granite curbs.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
The surrounding National Mall and Monument knoll are populated by mature deciduous and mixed broadleaf specimens:
- *Platanus × acerifolia* (London Plane) and *Quercus phellos* (Willow Oak) along outer concentric path rings.
- *Ulmus americana* (American Elm) groves lining the Mall boulevards, featuring arching vase-shaped canopies.
- *Prunus serrulata* (Yoshino Cherry) specimens bordering the southern and southwestern aprons.
All trees are structurally distinct with intricate, organic branch architecture and lush, leafy summer canopies completely resolved from geometric ground noise.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient visual noise is strictly expunged: zero motor vehicles on Maine Avenue and Constitution Avenue, zero transit buses, zero pedestrians on pathways or the plaza deck, zero temporary crowd barriers, and zero construction dumpsters. Permanent fixed urban fabric—granite curbs, recessed flush ground-recessed spotlights, bronze historical markers, cast-iron bollards, and circular flagpoles—is fully preserved in its static, archival state.
