# Spatial Domain Analysis: 9 Belgrave Cres, Edinburgh EH4 3AH, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
* **Bedrock Stratigraphy:** Carboniferous Lower Oil-Shale Sandstone formation overlain by glacial till.
* **Lithics & Mortar:** Fine-grained buff Craigleith sandstone and pale carbonaceous Hailes sandstone ashlar; hot-mixed slaked non-hydraulic lime mortar with sharp river sand aggregate.
* **Hydrology & Drainage:** Sloping topography toward the Water of Leith river basin; subterranean natural spring seepage managed by subsurface stone culverts.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
* **Residential Facades (Foreground/Left):** 3-to-4 storey Victorian/Late Georgian residential townhouse terrace. Dressed ashlar masonry, projecting multi-storey canted window bays, horizontal moulded stringcourses, rusticated base, plumb vertical structural lines, timber sash-and-case fenestration grids.
* **Ecclesiastical Massing (Center Ground):** Gothic Revival church edifice featuring a square ashlar tower with corner spires and crocketed pinnacles, pointed-arch lancet windows with stone tracery, stepped lateral buttresses, and a steeply pitched Scottish slate gable roof.
* **Perimeter Infrastructure:** Low coursed rubble sandstone boundary walls capped with dressed stone copings and wrought-iron spearhead railings.
* **Street Realm:** Asphalt roadway lined with dressed whinstone kerbstones and Caithness stone slab pedestrian footways.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
* **Masonry:** Tool-dressed and droved pale buff/ochre Scottish sandstone with regional coal-smoke encrustation in sheltered reveals, rain-wash scouring across projecting stringcourses, and localized damp biological greening on base courses.
* **Roofing & Ironwork:** Grey Ballachulish slate roof tiles with lead flashings, ridges, and valleys; black bitumen-painted cast-iron rainwater downpipes, hoppers, and boundary railings.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
* **Species Identification:** *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), *Fraxinus excelsior* (European Ash), and *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm).
* **Phenology (September 24):** Dense late-season canopies showing early autumn senescence; marginal foliar chlorosis, patchy yellowing, desiccated leaf margins, and minor early leaf fall along road margins.

All photogrammetry mesh artifacts, distorted polygon rubble, parked cars, transient vehicles, pedestrians, and street clutter are fully purged. Static architectural, lithic, and botanical elements are cleanly delineated.
