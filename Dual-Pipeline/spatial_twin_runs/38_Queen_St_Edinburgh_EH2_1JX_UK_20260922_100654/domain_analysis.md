# Spatial Domain Analysis: 38 Queen St, Edinburgh EH2 1JX, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean foundation composed of Carboniferous sedimentary stratigraphy (sandstones, siltstones, and oil-shales of the Strathclyde Group) overlying volcanic basaltic intrusions. Structural lithics predominantly Craigleith sandstone—a dense, pale-buff to cream quartz arenite characterized by high silica content and carbonaceous laminations—utilized for polished ashlar street elevations. Secondary and rear elevations utilize coursed Hailes and Craigmillar sandstone rubble. Mortar systems comprise traditional hot-mixed feebly hydraulic lime slaked with coarse, sharp quartz river aggregate. Free-draining northern slope topography descending toward the Firth of Forth basin.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Classic Georgian New Town residential tenement and terraced townhouse blocks (c. 1790–1820). Massing comprises 3- to 4-storey elevations over sunken lightwell basements, topped by pitched attic storeys. Fenestration adheres to strict classical Georgian proportion: regular grids of 12-pane (6-over-6) and 4-pane timber sash-and-case windows set within plain raised ashlar architraves, stone lintels, and projecting cills. Roofscape features dual-pitch roofs punctuated by timber-framed, slate-hung pedimented and canted dormer windows. Gable and party walls terminate in heavy squared sandstone chimney stacks with moulded coping stones supporting arrays of cylindrical terracotta chimney cans. Basement wells accessed via stone platt bridges spanning to timber panelled doors under geometric fanlights. Structural verticals rectified to true perpendicular load-bearing axes.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Principal Facades: Fine-droved and polished Craigleith sandstone ashlar blocks with flush lime pointing.
- Gables and Chimneys: Coursed squared sandstone rubble with lime pinning and dressed ashlar quoins.
- Roof Surfacing: Scottish Ballachulish and Welsh blue-grey slate tiles, lead sheet flashing at valleys, hips, and dormer cheeks.
- Civil Ironwork: Wrought and cast-iron spearhead railings, urn finials, and lamp brackets anchored into sandstone plinth copings with molten lead.
- Ground Paving: riven Caithness flagstone paving slabs, dressed blue-grey whinstone (dolerite) kerbs, and continuous mastic asphalt road surfaces.
- Pathology & Weathering: Localized historic coal-smoke carbon crusting in sheltered cornice recesses and under window cills; rain-washed pale reveals on exposed upper ashlar courses; minor biological greening along damp basement plinths.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Bordering urban woodland canopy (*Queen Street Gardens*): mature *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore) and *Tilia × europaea* (Common Lime). Canopy state reflects late-June phenology: fully expanded, dense deep-green leaf volume with complete crown closure and heavy foliar density; zero chlorosis or defoliation.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient visual elements are systematically removed: zero motor vehicles, zero moving or parked traffic, zero pedestrians, zero temporary municipal signage, zero waste bins. Street surfaces, stone kerbs, iron railings, lampposts, and static architectural fabric are completely rectified and retained.
