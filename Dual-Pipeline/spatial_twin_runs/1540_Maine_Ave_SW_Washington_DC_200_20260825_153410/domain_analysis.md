# Spatial Domain Analysis: 1540 Maine Ave SW, Washington, DC 20004, USA

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- Bedrock Lithology: Underlying the National Mall is late Precambrian to early Paleozoic Piedmont crystalline basement (schists and gneisses), overlain by unconsolidated Atlantic Coastal Plain Quaternary alluvial silt, sand, and clay terraces of the Potomac River floodplain.
- Monument Substructure: Gneiss and bluestone rubble core underpinned by a deep concrete sub-foundation and slab installed by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers (1876–1884) to arrest subsidence in the soft alluvium.
- Indigenous Lithics: The primary obelisk is faced with three distinct white-to-buff calcitic/dolomitic marbles:
  1. Base to 152 ft (46.3 m): White dolomitic Cockeysville Marble quarried from Texas, Baltimore County, Maryland.
  2. Intermediate band (approx. 4 courses): Stockbridge Marble from Sheffield/Lee, Berkshire County, Massachusetts, exhibiting a distinct darker, brownish-grey tint.
  3. Upper 390 ft (118.9 m) to pyramidion: Cockeysville dolomitic marble from the Beaver Dam quarry in Cockeysville, Maryland.
- Surrounding Hardscape: Plinth ring paved with light grey, fine-grained Barre granite pavers bordered by Maryland granite curbs. Walkways composed of compacted decomposed granite and crushed orange-ochre gravel over stabilized road base.
- Subterranean Drainage: Extensive subterranean drainage channels, French drains, and perimeter sumps managing high regional Potomac water table conditions.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- Obelisk (Washington Monument - Robert Mills / Thomas Lincoln Casey, completed 1884):
  - Height: 554 ft 7 11/32 in (169.05 m).
  - Form: Pure unadorned monumental hollow stone masonry obelisk tapering upward at a continuous subtle draft to an apex capped by a steep 17-degree marble pyramidion topped with a 100-ounce cast aluminum point.
  - Plinth: Concentric circular paved viewing terrace surrounded by a ring of 56 polished stainless steel flagpoles.
- Upper Center-Right Structure (National Museum of African American History and Culture - Freelon Adjaye Bond / SmithGroup, opened 2016):
  - 5 storeys above grade (approx. 85 ft / 25.9 m height).
  - Three-tiered inverted pyramidal corona wrapped around a central glass volume, inspired by the Yoruban Caryatid capital.
  - Deep flat roof housing high-efficiency photovoltaic arrays, linear skylights, and mechanical penthouses behind continuous parapets.
- Lower Right Institutional Pavilions & Wings:
  - 4-to-5 storey historic red brick masonry wings with slate hip roofs, dormers, stone belt courses, and classical limestone pedimented porticos.
- Planar Rectification: True plumb vertical lines enforced on the obelisk shaft and all structural cores; the 3D-photogrammetry noise across the glass-and-mesh museum corona is fully resolved into rigid, laser-straight geometric tiers of laser-cut bronze latticework.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Washington Monument Shaft:
  - Lower third: Tight-jointed, hand-chiseled Cockeysville marble showing 170+ years of subtle Atlantic humid-temperate weathering, mild atmospheric carbon wash in sheltered joints, and biological micro-staining.
  - Distinct horizontal color-break visible at the 152-foot level, transitioning through four subtle brown-gray Massachusetts courses into the slightly cooler, lighter Maryland marble of the upper shaft.
  - Fine historic lime-based mortar joints, perfectly plumb and flush.
- NMAAHC Facade:
  - 3,600 cast-aluminum architectural screen panels finished in warm, multi-layered iridescent bronze-colored fluoropolymer PVDF coating over structural steel subframes, backed by high-performance low-emissivity structural glazing.
- Surrounding Pavements & Hardscape:
  - Fine-grain Barre granite pavers on the circular monument apron.
  - Packed ochre crushed-gravel radial pathways intersecting dark asphalt and concrete access roads with granite curbs.
- Weathering / Patina:
  - Subtropical-temperate wash lines along the northern and western faces of the obelisk; subtle mineral efflorescence on ground-level retaining walls; dry summer wear on unpaved pathway verges.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- Surrounding Turf: Cultivated, aerated tall fescue (Festuca arundinacea) and Kentucky bluegrass (Poa pratensis) turfgrass maintained in sweeping circular radial cuts.
- Surrounding Arboreal Canopy:
  - Mature American Elm (Ulmus americana) lining the formal Mall allées to the east and northwest.
  - Willow Oaks (Quercus phellos), Pin Oaks (Quercus palustris), and White Oaks (Quercus alba) forming lush, dense, irregular woodland clusters across the Monument grounds.
  - Sweetgum (Liquidambar styraciflua) and American Sycamore (Platanus occidentalis) scattered along peripheral drainage swales and lawn margins.
  - Well-defined branch scaffolding, individual leaf clusters, deep summer chlorophyll-rich green tones, and zero polygonized or stony mesh distortions.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
- Transient Decluttering: Complete removal of all moving and parked automobiles, tourist buses, delivery vans, pedestrians, tour groups, queue barriers, mobile barricades, and temporary event tents.
- Permanent Civil Infrastructure Retained: Fixed circular stone curbs, asphalt road beds with permanent thermoplastic crosswalk markings, cast-iron streetlamps, fixed granite retaining structures, security bollards, and the circle of 56 stainless steel flagpoles around the monument base.
