# Spatial Domain Analysis: West End, Atholl Cres, Edinburgh EH3 8HA, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- Bedrock Lithology: Devonian and Lower Carboniferous sandstone and siltstone (Inverclyde Group), underlain by volcanic bedrock of the Midland Valley basin.
- Indigenous Masonry Lithics: Built primarily of Carboniferous Craigleith and Hailes sandstones—dense, quartzose, pale honey-to-greyish buff building stones typical of Edinburgh's New Town and West End developments.
- Mortar Chemistry: Traditional hot-mixed hydraulic lime (calcium oxide) and sharp pit-sand mortar with crushed aggregate, allowing breathability across thick stone load-bearing skins.
- Hydrogeology & Drainage: Glacially sculpted terrain with boulder clay (till) overburden; natural subterranean surface water runoff gradients sloping north towards the Water of Leith and east along Shandwick Place.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- Typological Matrix: High Victorian / Roman Baroque ecclesiastical architecture integrated into a late Georgian and Victorian civic streetscape.
- Key Anchor Landmark (St George's West / Charlotte Chapel, 58 Shandwick Place): Designed by David Bryce (1866–1869) with its soaring 56-meter Venetian campanile tower completed by Sir Robert Rowand Anderson (1879–1881), modeled after the campanile of San Giorgio Maggiore.
- Massing & Storey Heights:
  * Church: 4-stage campanile tower springing from the south-west corner featuring a vermiculated ashlar base, pedimented clock stages, Corinthian pilastered orders, an open balustraded loggia, an octagonal belfry, and a verdigris copper pyramidal spire.
  * Surrounding Tenements & Commercial Terraces: 4-to-5-storey classical Georgian and Victorian blocks with rusticated basements, piano nobile timber sash fenestration (rectified 12-pane and 2-over-2 configurations), continuous ashlar plat bands, and canted dormered mansard roofs with Welsh slate finishes.
- Distant Backdrop: The prominent dome of West Register House (Robert Reid, Charlotte Square) terminating the vista to the northeast.
- Planar Rectification: All vertical masonry lines, campanile pilasters, church nave elevations, chimney stacks, and tenement facade planes strictly rectified to true gravitational plumb, eliminating photogrammetric mesh skewing and warped cornice lines.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Primary Facades: Polished ashlar sandstone, rusticated course bases, vermiculated block detailing, and droved sandstone ashlar.
- Roof Coverings: Dark blue-grey Scottish/Welsh slate tiles with lead-dressed valleys, ridge rolls, and zinc/lead flashings.
- Spire & Domes: Oxidized verdigris sheet copper on the campanile spire and the distant Charlotte Square dome.
- Environmental Weathering & Patina: Authentic Scottish maritime climate patina, including localized soot staining (historical coal smoke legacy) in sheltered ashlar recesses, microscopic green algae colonisation along damp north-facing drip courses, and selective surface desquamation and calcium sulfate crusting along cornice underhangs.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- Urban & Park Vegetation: Dispersed mature deciduous street and garden trees in adjacent West End plots and rear gardens.
- Botanical Species: Mature European Sycamore (*Acer pseudoplatanus*), Wych Elm (*Ulmus glabra*), London Plane (*Platanus × acerifolia*), and common Lime (*Tilia x europaea*), rendered with distinct leafy branch architecture and organic crown volumes, fully separated from stone facades.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
- All transient vehicular traffic (trams, buses, cars), moving pedestrians, road barriers, temporary signage, dumpsters, and ephemeral debris are completely removed.
- Permanent civic fixtures—cast iron railings, stone kerbstones, historic streetlamp standards, road surfaces, tram track rails, and stone paving—are fully preserved.
