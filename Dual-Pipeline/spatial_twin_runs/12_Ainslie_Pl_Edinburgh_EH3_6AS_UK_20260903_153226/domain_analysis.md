# Spatial Domain Analysis: 12 Ainslie Pl, Edinburgh EH3 6AS, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The subterranean stratum rests upon Lower Carboniferous sedimentary bedrock of the Strathclyde Group (sandstones, siltstones, and mudstones), overlain by dense Devensian glacial till and boulder clay with natural groundwater migration towards the Water of Leith ravine. Primary structural masonry consists of Craigleith sandstone—a pale buff-grey, silica-cemented quartz arenite renowned for low porosity and immense load strength—paired with Hailes sandstone for subterranean backing. Subterranean basement wells and foundation platts are laid in hydraulic and lime-sand mortars incorporating local volcanic aggregate.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Designed in 1822 by James Gillespie Graham as part of the Moray Estate masterplan, Ainslie Place is a unified, Grade A-listed neoclassical curved crescent block. Massing comprises 3 full storeys over an excavated basement area, topped with an attic and slate roof behind a continuous ashlar parapet and blocking course. The ground floor features crisp, channelled V-jointed rusticated ashlar with round-arched entry doorways and radial glazed fanlights. Upper floors display smooth polished ashlar with continuous projecting string and cill courses, terminal pavilions framed by colossal-order pilasters, and a disciplined grid of 6-over-6 and 2-over-2 timber sash-and-case windows. Droved sandstone steps oversail the basement wells to access principal doors.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Principal Facades**: Craigleith sandstone ashlar dressed in fine polished and droved tooling; V-joint rustication at ground level; lime-pointed ashlar joints.
- **Roofs & Chimneys**: Scotch Ballachulish grey slate pitched and mansard roofs with lead rolled ridges, lead-lined valley gutters, and dressed ashlar chimney stacks with terracotta cylindrical pots.
- **Metalwork & Joinery**: Cast- and wrought-iron spearhead and anthemion perimeter railings bordering basement lightwells; painted panelled timber storm doors.
- **Patina & Weathering**: Historic coal-smoke carbon encrustation within shaded moulding friezes and window embrasures, selective rain-wash scouring along projecting cornice drip-edges, and basal micro-algal patination on lower north-facing basement retaining blocks.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
The central communal oval garden contains mature broadleaf urban specimens:
- **Species Composition**: *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), *Tilia × europaea* (Common Lime), *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm), and *Betula pendula* (Silver Birch).
- **Phenology (September 3)**: Full, heavy late-summer canopy volume showing early signs of apical senescence, subtle leaf-margin chlorosis on lime and birch canopies, deep olive-green maturity across sycamore foliage, and manicured perennial turf understorey bounded by shrub borders.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
Complete elimination of all transient vehicles, parked cars, moving traffic, pedestrians, refuse bins, and temporary municipal signboards. Photogrammetry mesh anomalies are planarized into plumb vertical ashlar facades, straight horizontal cornices, and authentic granite/whinstone kerbs with stone flagstone pavements.
