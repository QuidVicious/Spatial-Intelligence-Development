# Spatial Domain Analysis: 1500 Maine Ave SW, Washington, DC 20024, USA

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean geology comprises Pleistocene alluvial gravels, silts, and clays of the Atlantic Coastal Plain unconformably overlying Piedmont crystalline metamorphic bedrock (metagraywacke and mica schist of the Wissahickon Formation). Foundation underpinning beneath the monument utilizes a massive mass-concrete sub-footing collar encasing original blue gneiss and granitic rubble. Primary monumental masonry features fine-grained calcite and dolomite marble from Cockeysville, Maryland (lower 46 meters) transitioning above the 1854 construction hiatus line to white crystalline marble from Sheffield/Lee, Massachusetts and upper-tier Beaver Dam marble, backed with coursed Maine granite blocks. Surrounding civic architecture incorporates Salem Limestone (Indiana Oolitic limestone), Aquia Creek sandstone, and Georgia granite curbstones. Mortar systems vary from hydraulic lime-sand mixes at lower tiers to Portland cement-lime mortar throughout late-nineteenth-century upper courses.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
The central obelisk is a hollow unreinforced load-bearing masonry shaft rising 169.3 meters, tapering with a 1:55 batter to a 55-degree pyramidion capped with a cast aluminium apex. Planar load lines align vertically to true gravity without structural deflection. The adjacent museum building features an inverted three-tiered ziggurat corona clad in ornamental cast-aluminium lattice screens inspired by Yoruba Yoruban caryatid metalwork, surrounding a structurally glazed inner curtain wall envelope. Surrounding federal complexes represent monumental Beaux-Arts, Neoclassical, and French Second Empire typologies exhibiting strict horizontal entablatures, rusticated ground-floor arcades, colossal fluted Doric and Ionic colonnades, rhythmic rectangular 6-over-6 fenestration grids, and pitched hipped mansards with red Spanish barrel terra-cotta tiles.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
Obelisk facade displays distinct differential lithic color striations marking the 1854–1879 construction pause, showing localized atmospheric calcium-sulfate dissolution, microcrystalline gypsum crusting in sheltered relief joints, and pointing mortar repointing lines. Civic institutional facades display uniform buff Indiana oolitic limestone ashlar panels with sharp chamfered joints, rusticated granite plinths, and bronze-framed glazing assemblies. Corona screen exhibits micro-textured bronze-toned fluoropolymer coating over cast aluminium panels. Monument base apron comprises radial slabs of thermal-finished grey granite pavers bounded by circular granite curbing and fixed stainless steel bollards. Pedestrian circulation surfaces consist of stabilized compacted decomposed granite and crushed bluestone fines bordered by flush precast concrete and stone edging.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Vegetation consists of mature urban parkland plantings dominated by *Ulmus americana* (American Elm), *Quercus phellos* (Willow Oak), *Quercus palustris* (Pin Oak), *Platanus occidentalis* (American Sycamore), and *Liriodendron tulipifera* (Tulip Poplar). In early September, canopies maintain full, dense late-summer leaf volume with deep green pigmentation; occasional localized chlorotic yellowing and edge necrosis appear on exposed upper crown leaves, with zero branch defoliation. Broad continuous lawns consist of deeply rooted, closely mown *Festuca arundinacea* (Tall Fescue) and *Poa pratensis* (Kentucky Bluegrass) turf displaying localized foot-traffic compaction along unpaved paths.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient vehicular traffic, moving pedestrians, temporary queue stanchions, maintenance machinery, tour buses, and street waste receptacles have been entirely removed. Permanent street furniture, static bronze flagpoles, granite curbs, and fixed lighting standards remain intact.
