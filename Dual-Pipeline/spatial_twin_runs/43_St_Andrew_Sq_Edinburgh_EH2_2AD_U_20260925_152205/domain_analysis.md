# Spatial Domain Analysis: 43 St Andrew Sq, Edinburgh EH2 2AD, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean strata comprise Carboniferous sedimentary rock formations of the Strathclyde Group (sandstone, siltstone, and mudstone) overlain by dense glacial till (boulder clay). Primary load-bearing masonry consists of Lower Carboniferous Craigleith and Hailes sandstone (dense, silica-cemented pale grey-to-buff quartzose freestone) and Binny sandstone for monumental structures. Bedding mortar utilizes traditional non-hydraulic to weakly hydraulic lime blended with sharp pit sand. Subsurface hydrology drains southward towards the historic Nor Loch basin/Princes Street Gardens trough.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Spatial layout defined by Edinburgh New Town James Craig grid (St Andrew Square).
- Center-left midground: Melville Monument (1821–1827, William Burn/Robert Forrest), 42-metre fluted Roman Doric column on a high square rusticated pedestal, surmounted by a heroic stone statue and stepped drum.
- Left foreground: Late Georgian/Early Victorian Classical corner palazzo facade; channelled rusticated ashlar ground floor, upper floors delineated by string courses, molded architraves, 6-over-6 and 2-over-2 timber sash-and-case windows, terminating in a dentilled parapet cornice.
- Right mid-to-background: Mid-to-late 20th-century commercial blocks; repetitive vertical precast concrete fins and structural mullions interspersed with recessed aluminium-framed curtain-wall glazing.
- Far right background: Scott Monument (1844, George Meikle Kemp); high Victorian Gothic steeple with open-crocketed pinnacles, flying buttresses, and tiered micro-architecture.
- Midground park: Elliptical garden perimeter bounded by cast-iron railings set into dressed stone dwarf copings.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Historic Facades & Monument: Dressed Craigleith ashlar masonry displaying localized atmospheric carbon crusting, coal-smoke soot banding on sheltered under-cornices, and rain-washed quartz reveals.
- Modern Commercial Buildings: Precast aggregate concrete panels, anodized architectural aluminium framing, tinted structural glass panes.
- Scott Monument: Binny sandstone exhibiting characteristic dark bituminous patina and oxidized mineral veining.
- Ground Plane: Rolled bituminous asphalt carriageway with painted white/yellow thermoplastic road markings, fine-picked Scottish granite kerbstones, and Caithness flagstone pedestrian paving.
- Garden Perimeter: Wrought- and cast-iron vertical palisade spear-top railings mounted with lead caulking into tooled sandstone copestones.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Urban garden enclosure populated by mature broadleaf trees:
- *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore maple) and *Platanus × acerifolia* (London plane); massive upright trunks with peeling, mottled bark plates, strong scaffolding limb structure branching into broad, hemispherical spreading canopies measuring 14–20 metres in height.
- *Tilia × europaea* (Common lime); upright oval crown structure, dense fine-twig epicormic growth around lower trunk bases.
- Understory: Clipped mixed ornamental shrub beds and manicured perennial ryegrass (*Lolium perenne*) turf lawns.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient vehicles, buses, passenger cars, delivery vans, pedestrians, temporary traffic cones, construction hoarding, roadworks signage, and surface litter are completely excised. Structural masonry, fixed cast-iron streetlamps, permanent kerbs, and static trees remain intact.
