# Spatial Domain Analysis: West End, Atholl Cres, Edinburgh EH3 8HA, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The subterranean foundation rests upon the Lower Carboniferous Gullane and Wardie Formations of the Strathclyde Group, comprising interbedded sandstones, siltstones, and bituminous shales overlain by dense glacial till. The dominant structural masonry across the foreground and midground is composed of pale buff-to-grey Carboniferous quartz arenite sandstone, historically sourced from the nearby Craigleith and Hailes quarries. Bedding planes are set in lime-based mortars composed of calcined Scottish limestone aggregate and sharp river sand. Ground water drains northward following the topographic fall toward the Water of Leith basin.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
The scene captures the urban corridor of Shandwick Place looking northeast along the West End toward Charlotte Square and George Street. The prominent midground focal point is St George's West Church (Charlotte Chapel), featuring an Italian Romanesque/Venetian-inspired design originally conceived by David Bryce (1866–1869) with an imposing 56-metre Venetian campanile campanile tower and spire added by Sir Robert Rowand Anderson (1881–1882), terminating in an octagonal belfry, copper-clad spire, and clock faces. Surrounding the church are 4-to-5-storey classical Georgian and Victorian tenement blocks and commercial terraces featuring rusticated ground-floor arcades, ashlar upper facades, continuous stringcourses, classical architraves, canted bay windows, and steep slate mansard roofs punctuated by gabled and segmental pedimented dormers. In the background left, the neoclassical green copper dome of West Register House (formerly St George's Church, Charlotte Square, by Robert Reid) rises above the skyline. All vertical corner quoins, pilasters, and campanile shaft edges are strictly plumbed perpendicular to the horizontal ground plane.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Facades: Craigleith fine-to-medium grained quartz-rich sandstone in tight-jointed ashlar blocks, polished dressings, and rock-faced rustication. Weathering manifests as authentic historical industrial soot deposition (patina) along sheltered cornices, localized granular spalling along exposed stringcourses, and run-off staining under stone sills.
- Spires and Domes: Verdigris-oxidized copper sheet and lead flashing on the campanile spire and the distant Charlotte Square dome.
- Roofscapes: Scottish and Welsh blue-grey roofing slate in regular diminishing courses, with dressed sandstone chimney stacks and terracotta chimney pots.
- Fenestration: Timber-framed sash and case windows (six-over-six and two-over-two configurations) painted in heritage white and muted earth tones, set into deep reveals.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Along peripheral courtyard pockets and rear property lines are mature deciduous urban trees, including *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm), and *Betula pendula* (Silver Birch), exhibiting branching structures and full green foliage.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All moving and parked motor vehicles, public buses, pedestrians, waste bins, temporary signage, and transient street clutter have been completely evacuated from the asphalt roadway, tramline corridors, and flagstone pavements, leaving an unobstructed civil landscape.
