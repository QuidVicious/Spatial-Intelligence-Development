Looking directly north-northwest down the steep axial descent of Frederick Street from an elevation of 121.7 meters, the frame is flanked symmetrically by continuous four-storey Georgian ashlar tenements rising 15.5 meters to slate mansard roofs with rectilinear dormers and massive stone chimney stacks, perfectly framing the monumental 49.4-meter neoclassical square clock tower of William Henry Playfair’s St Stephen's Church terminating the urban vista in the center valley against the distant blue waters of the Firth of Forth and the Fife hills. Captured on a Mamiya 7 medium format rangefinder with an ultra-sharp 45mm wide-angle lens on Kodak Ektar 100 film, the photograph reveals crisp planar droved Craigleith sandstone facades, multi-pane white timber sash-and-case windows, cast-iron basement lightwell railings, and authentic historic carbon soot patina under classical string courses and eaves cornices; mature deciduous elm and sycamore canopies cluster naturally around the lower Stockbridge descent; the entire street canyon is completely devoid of motor vehicles, traffic cones, parked cars, pedestrians, and transient street clutter, illuminated under clean 5600K northern daylight that casts razor-sharp geometric shadows across the downward-sloping asphalt roadway and broad sandstone flagstone pavements.
