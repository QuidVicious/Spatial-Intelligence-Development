# Spatial Domain Analysis: 200 Michigan Ave NE, Washington, DC 20017, USA

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The site sits upon the Atlantic Seaboard Fall Line transition zone, anchored over crystalline metamorphic bedrock of the Piedmont Province (specifically Kensington Tonalite and Wissahickon schist formations) overlain by Atlantic Coastal Plain marine and terrace gravels, sandy loams, and dense silty clays. 
- Primary Architectural Lithics: The Basilica of the National Shrine of the Immaculate Conception is engineered as a traditional load-bearing unreinforced masonry structure without modern structural steel framework. Its superstructure incorporates over 11.5 million structural clay bricks (common and face brick) faced with ashlar Indiana Bedford oolitic limestone, fine-grained Roman travertine, and polished crystalline granite at the sub-base plinth. 
- Surrounding Campus Fabric: Collegiate Romanesque and Late Gothic Revival structures across the Catholic University of America grounds employ Port Deposit granite, Potomac bluestone rubble, Indiana limestone quoins/dressings, and deep red vitrified shale brick. 
- Subterranean Dynamics & Mortar: Heavy hydraulic lime and high-calcium Portland cement mortars bind thick multi-wythe load-bearing brick cores. Topographic grade slopes toward the Anacostia River basin, directing subterranean moisture and runoff southward.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
A high oblique aerial perspective looking northeast across the Brookland campus, centered on the monumental Romanesque-Byzantine Revival Basilica of the National Shrine of the Immaculate Conception.
- Basilica Superstructure: Massive Latin-cross plan massing with thick battered walls, heavy stepped buttresses, deeply recessed round-arched portals, and a grand south entrance portico. The central crossing is surmounted by a 108-foot diameter ribbed saucer dome (the Great Dome/Trinity Dome) resting on an arcaded drum ringed with arched clerestory fenestration. The attached southwest Romanesque campanile (Knight's Tower) rises 329 feet (100 m) across ten distinct vertical tiers, transitioning through arcaded belfries, louvered openings, and a stepped pyramidal roof cap. Roofs over the nave and transepts consist of gabled planes clad in barrel-profile red terra-cotta clay tiles.
- Surrounding Campus Context: To the west and north stand multi-storey collegiate halls (e.g., Father O'Connell Hall, Gibbons Hall, Caldwell Hall) exhibiting Collegiate Gothic and Romanesque typologies with slate/clay tile mansard roofs, copper-flashed dormers, and ashlar masonry facades.
- Planar & Geometric Rectification: Photogrammetric mesh warping is fully corrected. Campanile vertical lines are plumbed to absolute 90-degree axes; drum balustrades, cornice projections, nave parapets, arched stone voussoirs, and flat limestone plinth terraces are rectified into razor-sharp, planar lines.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Primary Dome: Glazed polychrome ceramic mosaic tiles in vivid royal blue, gold, turquoise, and white forming geometric Marian stars and religious motifs, crowned with a gilded bronze finial cross.
- Facades & Porticos: Sawn-finish Indiana limestone ashlar blocks displaying subtle sub-millimeter tooling marks, limestone belt courses, and deep round-arched limestone archivolts. 
- Roofscapes: Interlocking Spanish/mission red clay barrel tiles over the gabled nave roofs, showing natural micro-lichen colonization along northern aspects and mortar-capped ridge capstones.
- Campus Secondary Masonry: Variegated coursed ashlar granite, red-brown clay brickwork laid in common bond with weathered raked joints, and lead-coated copper gutters displaying subtle mineral oxidation and verde-gris run-off along drip edges.
- Plazas & Curbs: Honed granite steps, poured aggregate concrete slabs with expansion joints, and dressed granite curbs bounding curvilinear asphalt roadways.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
A continuous urban forest canopy typical of the Mid-Atlantic Washington D.C. urban canopy:
- Identified Species: Mature Quercus alba (White Oak), Quercus phellos (Willow Oak), Acer rubrum (Red Maple), Liriodendron tulipifera (Tulip Poplar), Platanus occidentalis (American Sycamore), and clusters of flowering Prunus serrulata (Yoshino Cherry) along campus borders.
- Disambiguation: Photogrammetry foliage noise is resolved into dense, organic leafy crowns with visible branching structures, cast understory depth, and neatly manicured turfgrass lawns with crisp mulch rings surrounding mature street and courtyard trees.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient visual noise is completely eliminated: zero motor vehicles (all parking lots, drop-off circles, and Michigan Avenue NE road lanes are clear of moving or parked cars), zero pedestrians or tourists on the south plaza and sidewalks, zero dumpsters, zero temporary traffic cones, and zero construction apparatus. Permanent static infrastructure—including cast-iron lamp posts, paved pedestrian walkways, road striping, stone curbs, and landscaped traffic roundabouts—is pristinely preserved.
