# Spatial Domain Analysis: 17 Charlotte Square, Edinburgh EH2 4DJ, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Bedrock comprises Carboniferous sedimentary strata of the Lothian Oil-Shale Group, interleaved with basaltic volcanic sills. Superficial deposits consist of dense Quaternary glacial till. Building fabric utilizes indigenous pale-buff to blonde Craigleith and Hailes sandstones, bedded in non-hydraulic lime and crushed stone mortars. Ground drainage follows engineered historic subterranean storm sewers along the ridge profile.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Neoclassical Edinburgh New Town palatial urban block designed by Robert Adam and executed by Robert Reid. Central visual anchor features West Register House with its monumental portico, colossal attached columns, and elevated copper-clad dome drum. Flanking symmetrical three-storey and attic terraced townhouses over rusticated basements. Planar facades rectified to strict plumb verticals; fenestration follows regular grids of timber sash-and-case windows (six-over-six and twelve-over-twelve configurations) with continuous stringcourses and projecting eaves cornices.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
* Facades: Fine-grained Craigleith sandstone ashlar with droved margin dressings; heavily rusticated basement and ground levels; localized coal-smoke particulate encrustation across protected friezes and undercut moldings.
* Roofs: Scottish and Westmorland blue-grey slate roofs with lead flashings, stone skews, and robust ashlar chimney stacks.
* Central Dome: Lead roll-jointed base supporting ribbed sheet-copper dome showing advanced verdigris patina.
* Civil Groundwork: Dark asphalt roadway bordered by granite setts and squared Caithness flagstone pedestrian footpaths; boundary defined by cast-iron spearhead railings set into dressed sandstone plinths.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Perimeter of Charlotte Square Gardens populated by mature *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), *Tilia × europaea* (Common Lime), and *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm). Late-September phenology displays dense, mature canopies exhibiting early autumn senescence: partial chlorophyll loss, mottled yellowing leaf margins, and scattered crown thinning without major defoliation.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All photogrammetry distortion hulls, transient vehicles, temporary road cones, waste bins, and pedestrians completely expunged. Preserved elements: structural stone masonry, iron perimeter palisades, stone kerbstones, and mature garden flora.
