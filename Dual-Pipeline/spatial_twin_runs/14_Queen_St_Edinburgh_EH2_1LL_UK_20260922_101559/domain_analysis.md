# Spatial Domain Analysis: 14 Queen St, Edinburgh EH2 1LL, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean strata comprise Carboniferous sedimentary sandstones and siltstones of the Gullane Formation, underlain by glacial till and volcanic quartz-dolerite intrusions (Castle Rock crag). Primary masonry lithology consists of local pale-buff Craigleith and Hailes sandstone (calciferous quartz-arenite), bound with historic hydraulic lime mortar.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Linear Georgian classical terrace row along Queen Street (James Craig New Town plan). Structures feature 3- to 4-storey residential blocks over sunken basement lightwells, accessed via bridging plinths. Vertical load-bearing ashlar envelopes rectified to strict gravity alignment. Pitched rooflines with dormered mansards, prominent masonry chimney stacks with terracotta pots, and regular fenestration grids of 6-over-6 and 12-over-12 timber sash-and-case windows.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
Tooled and droved Craigleith sandstone ashlar facades exhibiting coal-smoke patina, sulfur encrustation, and selective biological greening at plinths. Ballachulish blue-grey roofing slate, lead ridge flashing, and cast-iron downpipes. Cast-iron spearhead lightwell railings and stone boundary steps. Road surface of rolled asphalt over historic basalt/whinstone setts, edged with dressed granite kerbstones.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Queen Street Gardens woodland flank containing mature *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm), *Fagus sylvatica* (Common Beech), and *Tilia × europaea* (Common Lime). Late December phenology: complete winter defoliation, bare dark timber canopies, intricate branch silhouettes, and dormant understory turf.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All vehicular traffic, parked cars, pedestrians, temporary traffic signs, waste receptacles, and modern street clutter completely excised. Permanent historical cast-iron streetlamps, lightwell railings, and stone kerbs preserved.
