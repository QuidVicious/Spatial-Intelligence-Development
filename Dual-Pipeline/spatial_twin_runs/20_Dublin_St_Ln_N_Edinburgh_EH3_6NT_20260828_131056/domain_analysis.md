# Spatial Domain Analysis: 20 Dublin St Ln N, Edinburgh EH3 6NT, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- Bedrock: Carboniferous sedimentary sequences of the Strathclyde Group (Gullane Formation), interbedded sandstones, siltstones, and bituminous mudstones, overlain by dense Quaternary lodgement till.
- Lithic Fabric: Primary dimensional masonry extracted from the historic Craigleith Quarry (pale blonde/buff quartz-arenite sandstone) with complementary secondary coursing from Hailes Quarry (grey laminated sandstone).
- Mortar Chemistry: Traditional hot-mixed non-hydraulic lime mortar formulated from calcined limestone and coarse, sharp pit-sand aggregate, flush and neatly ribbon-pointed.
- Hydrology & Subterranean Dynamics: Glacial till subsoil sloping north toward the Water of Leith basin; engineered subterranean interceptor cellars and lightwell dry areas providing gravity drainage away from foundation footings.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- Typology: Regency/late Georgian neoclassical residential circus and curved terrace quadrants (Drummond Place, Reid & Sibbald Second New Town plan, c. 1802–1823).
- Massing & Verticality: 3-storey plus attic and sunken basement configurations. Plumb vertical load-bearing facades with fully planar ashlar frontages and rectilinear courtyard returns.
- Ground Storey: Horizontal channelled ashlar rustication over sunken basement lightwells bridged by monolithic droved sandstone access platts.
- Upper Storeys: Polished ashlar field with continuous moulded first-floor cill bands, projecting moulded eaves cornices, and plain ashlar blocking parapets.
- Fenestration: Symmetrical grids of 6-over-6 and 2-over-2 double-hung timber sash-and-case windows recessed in droved stone reveals; arched timber fanlights above six-panel timber entrance doors.
- Roofscape: Multi-pitched hipped roofs clad in Scottish Ballachulish blue-grey slate in diminishing courses; massive dressed ashlar chimney stacks with aligned cylindrical terracotta flue terminals and lead ridge flashings.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Primary Facades: Polished and droved Craigleith blonde sandstone ashlar; rusticated ashlar basement retainers; Caithness stone flag paths and granite/whinstone kerbs.
- Carriage Surface: Traditional Scottish whinstone (quartz-dolerite) rectangular setts laid in concentric radiating arcs.
- Boundary Infrastructure: Wrought-iron and cast-iron spearhead railings with urn finials mounted on dressed sandstone dwarf copings.
- Weathering Pathology: Localized historical coal-smoke gypsum encrustations under protected cornice projections, rain-washed clean buff sandstone facades, minor biogenic green algal patina on lower basement retaining walls, and authentic oxidised iron-run streaks at railing embedment pockets.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- Botanical Composition: Drummond Place Gardens central park enclave dominated by mature specimens:
  - *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm)
  - *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore Maple)
  - *Platanus × acerifolia* (London Plane)
  - *Tilia × europaea* (Common Lime)
  - Understory layer of *Taxus baccata* (English Yew), *Ilex aquifolium* (European Holly), and manicured fine fescue/ryegrass lawn (*Festuca rubra* / *Lolium perenne*).
- Phenology: Full mid-to-late summer foliage; broad, dense, multi-tiered deciduous canopies with deep green pigment saturation, heavy branch framing, and mature trunk architectures.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
- Total purge of all moving and parked motor vehicles, delivery vans, passenger cars, wheelie bins, plastic street furniture, and human foot traffic.
- Complete retention of permanent civic fabric: continuous whinstone sett road paving, stone curbs, Caithness flagstone sidewalks, cast-iron streetlamps, and historic garden enclosure railings.
