# Spatial Domain Analysis: 15 Coates Cres, Edinburgh EH3 7AF, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The subterranean geology of Edinburgh’s West End sits atop the Carboniferous sedimentary sequence of the Strathclyde and Inverclyde Groups, featuring interbedded siltstones, mudstones, and dense quartz-arenite sandstones underlain by deep glacial till and basaltic intrusions. The primary masonry of the surrounding Georgian and Victorian urban fabric is composed of Carboniferous sandstones, predominantly blonde-to-buff Craigleith sandstone and pale golden-brown Hailes sandstone, extracted from historic local quarries. St Mary's Episcopal Cathedral is constructed from a mixture of durable buff-grey Carboniferous sandstone and Dunmore/Hailes stone set in traditional non-hydraulic to feebly hydraulic hot-mixed lime mortars (slaked quicklime and coarse river sand). Subterranean hydrology drains northward toward the Water of Leith basin, with permeable stone foundation footings protected by subterranean damp-proof courses and gravity-fed ashlar plinth drainage lines.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- **Foreground / Street Level (Lower Third):** Multi-storey Victorian and late-Georgian residential tenement blocks and stone terraces (4 to 5 storeys plus attic mansards and basements). Planar vertical facades are plumbed true to gravity, rectifying all photogrammetry mesh warping. Straight-cut string courses, projecting moulded cornices, rusticated ground-floor ashlar, and uniform arrays of 2-over-2 and 1-over-1 timber sash-and-case windows set into recessed stone architraves. Roofscapes feature regular rhythmic arrays of stone-built chimney stacks with terracotta chimney pots, pitched slate gable roofs, and planar lead-dressed dormers.
- **Center Structures (Midground to Sky):** The imposing silhouette of St Mary's Episcopal Cathedral (designed in Victorian Early Gothic Revival by Sir George Gilbert Scott). The central square tower rises through multi-tier louvred belfry stages and corner pinnacles to support an octagonal stone spire reaching 90 meters (295 ft) into the sky. Symmetrical nave, transept gables, high-pitched lead and slate roofs, flying buttresses, lancet window groupings, and a massive circular wheel/rose window in the transept facade.
- **Left and Right Flanking Structures:** To the left (west front), the twin western spires ("Barbara" and "Mary") rise with matching octagonal stone profiles. Flanking to the far right is the polygonal pyramidal-roofed Chapter House and contiguous Victorian ashlar terraces stretching into the rolling topography of western Edinburgh.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Cathedral Facades:** Finely tooled buff-to-honey Carboniferous ashlar sandstone with darker mottling and historical carbon deposition in the sheltered undercuts of tracery, crockets, and arch moldings. Scottish Dalradian and Westmorland green/grey roofing slates along the pitched nave and choir roofs, with weathered lead ridges, copper flashings exhibiting faint verdigris oxidation, and carved stone pinnacles.
- **Residential Tenements:** Hand-dressed sandstone ashlar and coursed squared rubble with fine lime mortar pointing. Weathering pathology reflects maritime-temperate Scottish rainfall and historic industrial soot: selective carbon crusting beneath horizontal window sills, subtle rainwater run-off wash tracks along exposed western exposures, and softened edge-chamfers on sandstone lintels.
- **Roof & Fenestration:** Dark grey slate tiles with distinct overlapping grain, lead-lined valleys, cast-iron rainwater downpipes with black bitumen paint, and timber window frames painted in heritage off-white and charcoal tones.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Disambiguated from masonry meshes, the urban foliage consists of mature deciduous canopy trees rooted in communal rear gardens, churchyard grounds, and private crescents:
- *Platanus x acerifolia* (London Plane) and *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), displaying full, dense crowns of broad, lobed green leaves.
- Scattered *Betula pendula* (Silver Birch) and *Fraxinus excelsior* (Ash) with organic, branching woody limb structures, casting natural soft leafy shadow patterns against courtyard stone pavements.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient dynamic clutter, pedestrians, moving or parked motor vehicles, waste containers, wheelie bins, and temporary street construction signs are eliminated. Retained static civic infrastructure includes dressed whinstone and granite kerbstones, Caithness flagstone pavements, cast-iron spear-topped railings set into dwarf stone walls, and fixed Victorian cast-iron streetlamp posts.
