# Spatial Domain Analysis: 14 Atholl Cres, Edinburgh EH3 8HA, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The underlying bedrock belongs to the Carboniferous Gullane and Wardie Formations of the Strathclyde Group, comprising interbedded sedimentary sandstone, siltstone, and mudstone sequences overlain by compacted Devensian glacial till. Structural masonry across the terraces is almost exclusively sourced from historical local quarries, dominated by Carboniferous Craigleith and Hailes sandstones—dense, quartz-arenite lithologies noted for low porosity, silty grain matrix, and pale buff to ash-grey hue. Subterranean foundation walls interface with natural sloping drainage towards the Water of Leith basin to the north. Structural mortar consists of traditional non-hydraulic and feebly hydraulic slaked lime (calcium hydroxide) mixed with well-graded local river sand, maintaining vapour-permeable interstitial jointing across all load-bearing ashlar courses.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
The scene captures the western gateway to Edinburgh's Georgian New Town along Shandwick Place (A8 corridor), flanked symmetrically by Thomas Bonnar’s Neo-Classical curved crescents: Atholl Crescent (southwest) and Coates Crescent (northeast), designed circa 1825. 
- **Atholl & Coates Crescents**: Continuous curved 3-storey plus basement and attic mansard/dormer residential palazzo blocks. Rectilinear, laser-straight floor plates with giant-order shallow Ionic pilasters anchoring the centre and terminal pavilions. Drop-front basement lightwells with perimeter wrought-iron spear-headed railings. Pitched timber roofs clad in Scotch/Welsh slate with repeating ridge-line ashlar chimney stacks and terracotta pots. Windows are strictly timber-framed 12-pane (six-over-six) sash-and-case fenestration.
- **St George's West Church (Shandwick Place)**: Designed by David Bryce (1866–1869), featuring a dominant 4-stage campanile-style clock tower (approx. 50 metres tall) with a rusticated stone base, pedimented clock tier, open columned belfry, and an octagonal pyramidal copper-clad spire.
- **Distant Landmarks**: To the northeast, the prominent drum and ribbed green copper dome of Robert Reid's West Register House (former St George's Church, Charlotte Square) terminates the lateral vista, with distant views across the Firth of Forth to the Fife coastline. All wobbly photogrammetric mesh artifacts are rectified into true vertical gravity planes, sharp pediments, and plumb street corners.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Ashlar & Rubble**: Premium fine-tooled Craigleith and Hailes sandstone ashlar on principal public facades; coarse squared coursed sandstone rubble on secondary flanks and rear chimney gables. 
- **Roofing & Spire Cladding**: Blue-grey Scottish/Welsh roofing slate; weathered sea-green oxidised copper verdigris on St George's West spire and West Register House dome. 
- **Metals & Surfacing**: Wrought-iron railings and street furniture finished in matte black bitumen; asphalt road surfacing cut with double-track steel tramway rails embedded flush within the central carriageway.
- **Environmental Patina**: Authentic North Atlantic maritime weathering: differential atmospheric carbon soot staining embedded in sandstone pores ("Auld Reekie" historic crusting in sheltered entablatures and friezes), sub-vertical water-run streaks below projecting cornices, and localized micro-lichen colonization along northern ashlar faces.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Atholl Crescent Gardens and Coates Crescent Gardens feature mature urban broadleaf parkland landscaping. Identified species include mature European Lime (*Tilia x europaea*), London Plane (*Platanus x acerifolia*), Common Ash (*Fraxinus excelsior*), and Sycamore (*Acer pseudoplatanus*). Foliage is represented with dense, organic green leafy canopies, individual leaf cluster textures, and branching hardwood trunks, completely disambiguated from adjacent masonry.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All motor vehicles (cars, buses, delivery vans), trams, moving traffic, pedestrians, transient commercial sidewalk signs, dumpsters, waste bins, and temporary street clutter are entirely removed. The urban streetscape is preserved strictly as clean, static civil architecture: intact granite curbs, stone pedestrian flags, permanent cast-iron lampposts, embedded tram tracks, and historic cast-iron boundary railings.
