# Spatial Domain Analysis: 4 Glenfinlas St, Edinburgh EH3 6AQ, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- Bedrock Lithology: Subterranean foundation situated on Lower Carboniferous sedimentary formations of the Strathclyde Group (sandstones, siltstones, and mudstones), punctuated by regional volcanic intrusions (dolerite sills).
- Indigenous Masonry Lithics: Polished Craigleith and Hailes cream-to-honey fine-grained quartzose sandstone ashlar, characterized by high silica content, hard calciferous cementation, and horizontal bedding planes.
- Mortar Chemistry: Traditional non-hydraulic to weakly hydraulic slaked lime mortar blended with sharp local sand, exhibiting clean, fine-tooled ashlar joints.
- Subterranean Drainage Dynamics: Natural south/southwest slope runoff across the New Town ridge, drained through underlying permeable till layers above impermeable bedrock strata.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- Typology: Category A listed Neoclassical monumental civic/ecclesiastical architecture (West Register House, formerly St George’s Church, 1811–1814, designed by Robert Reid after Robert Adam), flanked by continuous Georgian residential and commercial ashlar terraces.
- Structural Massing & Storey Counts:
  - Central Monument: Symmetrical square-plan pavilion block rising 5 levels in massing, anchored by a prominent central copper-clad ribbed dome mounted on a massive rusticated base and tall peristyle circular drum (height approximately 49 m / 160 ft to the summit) crowned with an open lantern cupola and gilded finial.
  - Flanking New Town Terraces: 3 to 4 storeys over raised basements with rusticated ground floors, smooth ashlar upper levels, dentiled cornices, and panelled parapets.
- Roof & Fenestration Geometry:
  - Slender ribbed hemispherical dome surrounded by an attic drum balustrade.
  - Symmetrical timber-sash multi-pane fenestration (6-over-6 and 8-over-8 patterns), round-arched recesses, rectangular bays, and classical pediments along the roofline.
- Planar Vertical Rectification: Complete anti-warp alignment rectifying all vertical drum colonnades, pavilion corners, stringcourses, and horizontal entablatures to true planar plumb.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Primary Facades: Polished cream Craigleith sandstone ashlar blocks with fine-tooled draughted margins.
- Dome & Roofing: Ribbed standing-seam sheet copper on the dome, weathered to an authentic pale verdigris and sea-green patina with localized oxidized bronze accents; Scottish slate pitched roofs on surrounding terrace blocks.
- Weathering & Environmental Patina: Maritime temperate microclimate exposure exhibiting historical atmospheric carbon staining in sheltered under-cornice niches, delicate surface friability, and water-wash runoffs across stringcourses.
- Secondary Elements: Dark cast iron perimeter railings, wrought iron parapet fixtures, and lead flashing along roof valleys.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- Canopy & Tree Species: Mature broadleaf urban trees in the Charlotte Square gardens, including European Elm (*Ulmus glabra*), Sycamore (*Acer pseudoplatanus*), and English Oak (*Quercus robur*).
- Botanical State: Rich green, leafy organic branching canopies with deeply textured bark, fully disambiguated from adjacent masonry.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
- Total elimination of all vehicles, parked cars, moving traffic, pedestrians, temporary barriers, municipal garbage bins, and commercial delivery clutter.
- Pure preservation of historic civil infrastructure: continuous dressed whinstone and sandstone kerbs, cast iron street railings, flagstone pavements, and permanent lamp standards.
