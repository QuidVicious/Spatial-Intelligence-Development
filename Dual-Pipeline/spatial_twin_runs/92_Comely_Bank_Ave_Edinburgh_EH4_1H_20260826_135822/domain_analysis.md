# Spatial Domain Analysis: 92 Comely Bank Ave, Edinburgh EH4 1HE, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The terrain sits upon the Lower Carboniferous Gullane and Wardie Formations of the Strathclyde Group, comprising interbedded sedimentary sandstone, siltstone, and mudstone overlain by Devensian glacial till. Above ground, the primary load-bearing material is indigenous blonde and honey-toned Scottish sandstone predominantly quarried from nearby Craigleith and Hailes quarries. The masonry is bonded with historic, vapor-permeable non-hydraulic slaked lime and coarse river sand mortars. Subterranean moisture is governed by gentle northern descent towards the Water of Leith basin, with subterranean stone perimeter gutters and basement lightwells managing local runoff.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
The scene features late-Victorian and Edwardian perimeter tenements designed primarily under the late 19th-century masterplans of Sir James Steel (1894–1910s), interspersed with earlier 19th-century classical Georgian-influenced block edges.
- **Centre Structures (Foreground Block):** Four-storey Scottish baronial/classical tenement blocks with projecting ashlar bay windows (canted and square bays), prominent stone stringcourses, massive shared chimney stacks with clay chimney pots (cans), and steeped gable ends with coping stones.
- **Flanking Terraces (Left & Right Backgrounds):** Continuous 4-storey residential tenement facades featuring repetitive vertical rhythms of double-hung sash-and-case timber windows, rusticated basement/ground plinths, and Mansard/slate pitched roofs punctuated with ashlar dormers and sky lanterns.
- **Geometry Rectification:** All vertical facades plumbed to strict perpendicular axes, eliminating photogrammetric mesh slouch. Bay alignments, floor-level stone entablatures, parapet lines, and lead roof ridges are fully planarized.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Ashlar & Rubble Sandstone:** Hewn Craigleith ashlar dressing on principal street elevations; course-squared and snecked sandstone rubble on secondary facades and chimney return walls.
- **Roofing:** Scottish Ballachulish and Welsh slate roof coverings, finished with dressed lead valley flashings, zinc ridge caps, and cast-iron rainwater downpipes with decorative hopper heads.
- **Micro-Climate Weathering & Patina:** Temperate maritime Scottish atmospheric weathering; sheltered sandstone friezes and window lintels show historic industrial carbon soot stratification and gypsum crusting, while rain-exposed upper stonework displays washed blonde tones with faint vertical rainwater drip staining and subtle green micro-moss colonization in damp mortar joints.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- **Enclosed Urban Green Space (Right/Center Rear):** Bowling greens and communal rear drying greens (back-greens) featuring mown *Lolium perenne* (perennial ryegrass) turf bordered by low privet (*Ligustrum ovalifolium*) hedges.
- **Canopy Trees:** Dispersed mature *Acer pseudoplatanus* (sycamore), *Ulmus glabra* (Wych elm), and *Tilia x europaea* (common lime) displaying fully articulated, dense organic green leaf canopies, disambiguated from adjacent masonry.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All temporary and moving objects have been stripped from the scene: zero automobiles (no parked cars along Comely Bank Avenue or East Fettes Avenue), zero pedestrians, zero municipal refuse wheelie bins, and zero loose street debris. Intact permanent civil elements remain: Caithness flagstone pavements, hand-cut whinstone kerbstones, historic cast-iron street lamps, and black cast-iron perimeter boundary railings.
