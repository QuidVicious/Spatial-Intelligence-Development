# Spatial Domain Analysis: 26 London St, Edinburgh EH3 6NA, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean geology comprises Lower Carboniferous (Tournaisian–Viséan) sedimentary sequences of the Gullane Formation and Strathclyde Group, consisting of interbedded quartzose sandstones, siltstones, carbonaceous mudstones, and underlying intrusive basaltic sill topography. Structural masonry is dominated by fine-grained, pale buff-to-grey quartz-arenite sandstone quarried locally from the Craigleith Quarry (Edinburgh) and Cullalo Quarry (Fife), laid in thick load-bearing ashlar blocks. Sub-street level retaining walls and basement area lightwells utilize coursed, laminated sandstone from Hailes Quarry bedded with hydraulic lime and sharp sand mortar. Natural drainage slopes gently west-northwest toward the Water of Leith drainage basin.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Linear urban corridor designed by Thomas Bonnar and William Sibbald (c. 1819–1823) framing the eastern approach to Drummond Place. The street wall consists of continuous four-storey-and-basement neoclassical Georgian residential tenements and townhouses. Basements sit within sunken area lightwells behind cast-iron perimeter spearhead railings. The principal ground floors exhibit horizontally channelled (rusticated) Craigleith ashlar, divided from upper storeys by molded stone string courses and platbands. First and second storeys feature smooth droved ashlar with vertically aligned fenestration grids housing regular timber sash-and-case windows (predominantly six-over-six and two-over-two patterns) with stone cills and architrave surrounds. Rooflines terminate in continuous molded eaves cornices and blocking courses, backed by pitched timber-framed roofs clad in Ballachulish slate, punctuated by lead-cheeked dormers and regularly spaced stone chimney stacks carrying cylindrical terracotta pots.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Primary Facades: Polished and broached Craigleith quartzitic sandstone ashlar, displaying variable authentic surface pathology including historic coal-smoke carbon crusting, localized granular spalling along bedding planes, and micro-vegetation along base plinths.
- Basements & Rear Returns: Hammer-dressed squared rubble masonry with lime pointing.
- Roofing: Dark blue-grey Scottish Ballachulish slate tiles, rolled lead flashings, ridges, valley gutters, and lead-dressed timber dormers.
- Metalwork: Wrought and cast iron railings, basement overthrow arches, and anthemia-patterned balconettes painted in bitumen black.
- Carriageway & Footways: Dressed whinstone (quartz-dolerite) kerbs, Caithness flagstone paving slabs along pavements, and grooved whin/granite sett channel gutters beneath tarmacadam road surface.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
The western visual terminus terminates at Drummond Place Gardens. Mature urban woodland canopy is dominated by *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), *Fraxinus excelsior* (European Ash), *Tilia × europaea* (Common Lime), and *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm). In late August, the canopy exhibits full, dense, dark-green foliar volume, with deep broad crowns forming an unbroken organic curtain wall across the street vista, showing early minor chlorosis on outer crown tips.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient visual elements are fully eliminated: complete removal of parked vehicles, delivery vans, wheelie bins, communal recycling units, temporary construction barriers, and pedestrian traffic. Fixed civil assets—including cast-iron Victorian streetlamps, flagstone sidewalks, stone kerbstones, and continuous boundary railings—are strictly maintained in precise geometric alignment.
