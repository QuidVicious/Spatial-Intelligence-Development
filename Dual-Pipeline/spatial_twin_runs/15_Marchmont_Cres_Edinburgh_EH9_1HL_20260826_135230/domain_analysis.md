# Spatial Domain Analysis: 15 Marchmont Cres, Edinburgh EH9 1HL, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- **Subterranean Bedrock**: The site rests on the Lower Carboniferous Gullane Formation (part of the Strathclyde Group), characterized by interbedded siltstones, mudstones, and cyclic sandstone sequences overlain by glacial till.
- **Indigenous Masonry Lithics**: The primary building envelope consists of Carboniferous blonde-to-buff sandstones, predominantly extracted from the Hailes and Craigleith quarries, with occasional fine-grained red sandstone accents from Dumfriesshire quarries (e.g., Locharbriggs) typical of late Victorian Scots Baronial tenement construction.
- **Mortar Chemistry**: Structural masonry utilizes non-hydraulic to feebly hydraulic regional Scottish lime mortars (slaked quicklime paired with local quartz-rich aggregate), displaying traditional flush and slightly recessed lime-pointed joints.
- **Subterranean Drainage**: Natural southward subsurface drainage gradients slope from the volcanic Arthur's Seat and Burgh Muir toward the historic former Boroughmuir basin, accommodated by deep perimeter stone drains and sub-pavement stone-lined vaults.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- **Typology & Era**: High Victorian / Scots Baronial 4-to-5 storey flatted tenement blocks (developed c. 1878–1888 under the Warrender Estate feu charters).
- **Massing & Structural Geometry**: Continuous vertical perimeter blocks flanking the intersection of Marchmont Crescent and Roseneath Street. Features canted multi-storey oriel bay projections, steep crow-stepped gables, conical-roofed corner turrets (bartizans), and heavy oversailing corbel tables at the upper storeys.
- **Roofscape**: Complex multi-gabled roofs clad in Scottish Ballachulish slate (grey-black, heavy texture) and Westmorland green slate, interspersed with lead-clad flat roof plateaus, zinc-flashed skylights, decorative stone skews, and massive ashlar chimney stacks crowned with terracotta cylindrical pots.
- **Fenestration**: Regular rhythmic bays of two-over-two and single-pane timber sliding sash windows set in deeply rebated stone reveals with chamfered polished ashlar surrounds.
- **Planar Rectification**: All building lines are strictly plumbed to gravity vertical; all cantilevered oriels, string courses, eaves lines, and timber shopfront fascia lintels at street level are rectified into true planar geometries, eliminating all photogrammetric warping.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Masonry Treatment**: Squared, coursed, and snecked blonde sandstone ashlar with fine-axed and droved face dressing. Quoins, window architraves, lintels, and corbel bands are finished in smooth-polished ashlar.
- **Environmental Weathering & Patina**: Characteristic Edinburgh temperate urban weathering—historic coal-smoke carbon encrustation (gypsum-carbon soot crusts) concentrated in sheltered under-eaves, window hoods, and under-corbels, contrasted with pale, rain-washed water runs across exposed upper ashlar faces. Lower plinths display mild rising damp salt crystallization and microscopic green algae staining near ground splashes.
- **Joinery & Metalwork**: Dark painted timber sash frames, solid panelled storm doors, wrought-iron cast rainwater hoppers and downpipes with lead flashings, and cast-iron perimeter area railings anchored with lead into stone cope walls.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- **Urban Flora**: Mature specimen broadleaf street and garden trees, primarily London Planes (*Platanus x acerifolia*) and Sycamores (*Acer pseudoplatanus*), displaying dense, multi-lobed deciduous foliage canopies and mottled exfoliating bark.
- **Understory Vegetation**: Well-established domestic tenement back-green privet hedging (*Ligustrum ovalifolium*), English ivy (*Hedera helix*) creeping on lower rear boundary rubblestone walls, and small ornamental rowan (*Sorbus aucuparia*) saplings.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
- **Exclusions**: Absolute removal of all parked motor vehicles, driving cars, transient pedestrians, wheelie bins, communal dumpsters, temporary signage, roadwork barriers, and litter.
- **Preserved Civil Infrastructure**: Intact granite kerbstones, Caithness flagstone and modern asphalt pavements, traditional cast-iron heritage street lighting columns, fixed zebra-crossing Belisha beacons, permanent painted road layout markings, and cast-iron utility access covers.
