# Spatial Domain Analysis: Forth House, Edinburgh EH1 2BW, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- **Subterranean Bedrock**: The site rests on the Lower Carboniferous sedimentary sequence of the Strathclyde Group (Gullane and Wardie Formations), characterized by interbedded siltstones, mudstones, and thick quartz-arenite sandstone units formed within ancient river deltas, overlying deep volcanic basalt sills characteristic of the Lothian basin.
- **Indigenous Masonry Lithics**: The primary historic structures are built from local Scottish Carboniferous sandstones, predominantly pale blond-to-buff **Craigleith sandstone** (exceptionally durable, dense quartz-arenite) and secondary **Hailes sandstone** (distinctive fine-grained laminated grey-brown stone). Contrast masonry features red clay facing bricks and red Locharbriggs/Dumfriesshire Permo-Triassic sandstone accents on post-Victorian infills.
- **Mortar & Foundations**: Traditional non-hydraulic to feebly hydraulic hot-mixed lime mortars (slaked quicklime with sharp silica pit sand aggregate) used throughout the 19th-century ashlar joints and rubble core backings.
- **Hydrogeology & Drainage**: Highly permeable glacial till overlying sandstone fissures directs subsurface runoff toward the Water of Leith basin to the north.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- **Centerpiece Structure (Mother 4 Grounding)**: The prominent ecclesiastical campanile is **Charlotte Chapel / former St George’s West Church** (58 Shandwick Place, architects David Bryce, 1866–1869, with the Venetian Renaissance campanile by Sir Robert Rowand Anderson, 1879–1881).
  - *Massing*: 56-meter (184 ft) free-standing style 4-stage campanile tower terminating in an open columnar loggia belfry topped by an octagonal copper-clad pyramidal spire.
  - *Rectification*: Plumb vertical lines of the campanile to exact 90° true gravity alignment. Straighten the segmented classical cornices, pilastered pediments, round-headed Gibbsian windows, and the giant-order Corinthian corner pilasters of the church hall.
- **Flanking Fabric & Urban Grid**:
  - *Left (West End / Shandwick Place)*: 4-to-5 storey Victorian and late-Georgian classical tenements, with horizontal ashlar string courses, rusticated ground floors, continuous molded eaves, and timber-framed 12-pane sash-and-case windows.
  - *Right (Stafford Street & Melville Street corridors)*: Regular 3-and-4 storey Georgian New Town row facades with regular chimney stacks, lead flashing, slate-pitched hipped roofs, and canted/pedimented dormers.
  - *Foreground Structure*: 4-storey commercial tenement with flat-lead/felt roof coping, red brick party walls, and blonde Craigleith ashlar dressing.
  - *Distant Skyline*: The gentle roll of the Forth Valley and the volcanic Corstorphine Hill ridge forming the northwest horizon.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Facade Facings**:
  - Church & Tower: Monolithic ashlar ash-grey to honey-buff Craigleith sandstone blocks with fine-tooled draughted margins and rusticated baseline courses.
  - Belfry & Spire: Heavily oxidised copper sheet cladding displaying a vivid pale verdigris / seafoam-green patina (copper carbonate) on the upper pyramid and octagonal cupola.
  - Domestic Tenements: Mixed buff Craigleith and grey Hailes ashlar, with rubble sandstone to the rear courtyards, punctuated by dark Scottish Ballachulish slate roofs.
  - Foreground Tenement: Orange-red Scottish kiln-fired clay bricks on the upper floor return with dressed stone lintels and sills.
- **Environmental Weathering & Patina (Temperate Oceanic Microclimate)**:
  - Atmospheric legacy: Historic carbon-soot deposition and dark gypsum crusting concentrated in sheltered under-cornice recesses, window reveals, and pediment moldings.
  - Hydrological weathering: Linear vertical rainwater wash tracks along exposed western ashlar elevations; fine lichen colonies and green moss accrual on shaded northern roof valleys, chimney haunchings, and parapet lead troughs.
  - No pristine or sterile finishes; authentic centuries-old tactile masonry abrasion, softened corner arrises, and historic tuck-pointed lime repairs.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
