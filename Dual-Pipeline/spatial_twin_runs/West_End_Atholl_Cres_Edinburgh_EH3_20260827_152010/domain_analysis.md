# Spatial Domain Analysis: West End, Atholl Cres, Edinburgh EH3 8HA, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- **Subterranean Bedrock**: Underlain by Lower Carboniferous sedimentary strata of the Strathclyde Group (sandstone, siltstone, and mudstone formations) mantled by glacial till (boulder clay).
- **Local Stone & Lithics**: 
  - Primary structural masonry consists of blonde/honey-buff Carboniferous sandstones predominantly sourced from historic local quarries (Craigleith and Hailes sandstones), featuring fine-to-medium grain silica quartz matrix.
  - Kerbs, street sets, and base footings utilize hard intrusive volcanic basalt/dolerite and Whin stone.
- **Mortar Chemistry**: Historic non-hydraulic and feebly hydraulic lime-sand mortar (`Ca(OH)2` + coarse river sand/crushed sandstone aggregate), exhibiting historic re-pointing with non-expansive breathable lime joints.
- **Drainage & Groundwater**: Subsurface flow gravitates north-westward toward the Water of Leith basin through deep, permeable sandy till layers beneath paved road prisms.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- **Typologies & Prominent Structures**:
  - **St George's West Church (Charlotte Chapel)**: Roman Baroque/Venetian campanile revival church designed by David Bryce (1866–1869), terminating in a soaring 56-meter freestanding-style Italianate campanile bell tower added by Sir Robert Rowand Anderson (1879–1881). Features a classical pedimented lower facade, giant order Corinthian pilasters, rusticated base, clock tier, arched belfry with louvres, and a copper-clad pyramidal spire cap.
  - **West End Georgian & Victorian Tenement Terraces (Atholl Crescent & Shandwick Place)**: 3-to-4-storey plus attic and raised basement Georgian/Neoclassical continuous terrace facades (Thomas Bonnar, c. 1825). Symmetrical ashlar frontages with channelled ground-floor rustication, projecting belt courses, continuous cill bands, modillion cornices, and slate-clad mansard rooflines with pitched lead-flashed dormers.
  - **Distant Focal Point**: West Register House (Robert Reid / Robert Adam design, 1811–1814) featuring its monumental peristyle drum and verdigris copper-clad dome on Charlotte Square.
- **Planar Rectification**: All photogrammetry polygonal melt and leaning lines are plumbed to true 90-degree vertical planes. Facade lines, entablatures, parapet balustrades, stringcourses, and street tramway grids are rectified to true planar linearity.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Masonry & Facades**: Fine ashlar sandstone blockwork with tooled margin drafts and droved centers, rusticated ground floors, and rubblestone flank walls. 
- **Roofing & Detailing**: Scottish West Highland and Welsh blue-grey roofing slates in diminishing courses, rolled lead valley gutters, cast-iron rainwater downpipes, and zinc/lead ridge capping.
- **Spire & Dome Cladding**: Natural green verdigris oxidized sheet copper cladding on the church spire and West Register House dome.
- **Weathering Pathology & Patina**: Subdued sooty carbon crusts lodged in deep architectural undercuts and entablatures from historic coal smoke ("Auld Reekie" legacy), natural damp-line efflorescence, micro-lichen colonization along north-facing stone cornices, and slight biological staining on lower plinths.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy

- **Botanical Identifiers**: Mature European Sycamore (*Acer pseudoplatanus*), Wych Elm (*Ulmus glabra*), and European Ash (*Fraxinus excelsior*) situated within adjacent garden plots and Crescent enclosures, displaying natural branch scaffolding and detailed multi-tonal green leafy canopies.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
