# Spatial Domain Analysis: 30 Frederick St, Edinburgh EH2 2JR, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The subterranean foundation consists of Lower Carboniferous sedimentary sandstone, siltstone, and mudstone of the Strathclyde Group, punctuated by glacial till and hard quartz-dolerite / basalt intrusions characteristic of the Edinburgh volcanic ridge. Primary structural lithics comprise pale buff-to-grey Craigleith and Hailes sandstones (medium-grained quartzose arenite with siliceous and calcareous cementing matrices) laid with traditional non-hydraulic and semi-hydraulic slaked lime mortars. Low-lying northern subterranean drainage falls steadily towards the Water of Leith basin and the Firth of Forth graben.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
The scene captures the intersection of George Street and Frederick Street within James Craig’s Georgian First New Town plan, looking north. 
- Right Fore-to-Midground (Northeast Corner): 4-storey plus attic commercial/tenement block (80 George Street / 24–26 Frederick Street); ashlar sandstone construction, rusticated ground floor with segmental-headed glazed apertures, upper floors defined by classical architraves, moulded string courses, entablatures, and stone dormer gables set into a steep Scottish slate roof.
- Left Midground (Northwest Corner & Frederick Street Terraces): 3- to 4-storey late 18th-century Georgian ashlar townhouses stepping downhill; strict planar facades, regular 3-bay modules per tenement, rectilinear fenestration with recessed timber sash-and-case window frames (6-over-6 and 2-over-2 patterns), robust stone chimney stacks with ceramic flue terminals along party parapets.
- Center Intersection: Category A-listed bronze statue of William Pitt the Younger (Francis Chantrey, 1831) mounted on a tapered, polished Peterhead granite and sandstone plinth seated upon a circular stone sett traffic island.
- Distant Vista: Axial termination descending to Queen Street Gardens and the distant low skyline of Stockbridge and the Firth of Forth. All mesh warping is rectified into plumb vertical elevations and crisp horizontal parapets.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Facades: Craigleith and Hailes pale buff ashlar sandstone, displaying variable historic coal-smoke carbon encrustation on upper entablatures and unwashed reveals, contrasting with rain-leached pale ashlar spandrels.
- Roofs: Grey-black Scottish Ballachulish and West Highland roofing slates laid in diminishing courses, lead-flashed ridges, valleys, and dormer cheeks.
- Fenestration: Painted white and dark olive timber sash-and-case joinery with float glass glazing.
- Ground Plane: Bituminous asphalt roadway with thermoplastic zebra crossing markings, fine-dressed granite kerbstones, whinstone gutter courses, and rounded river-pebble / granite setts circling the monument base.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Distant urban canopy within Queen Street Gardens and northern street corridors comprises mature *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm), *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), and *Tilia × europaea* (Common Lime). Canopy architecture is dense, heavy, and fully expanded, displaying deep green late-summer leaf mass with trace chlorophyll depletion on outer crown margins.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient visual noise has been eliminated: zero moving or parked vehicles, zero pedestrians, zero temporary signage, zero commercial waste bins. Fixed civil infrastructure is preserved: cast-iron lamp standards, historic basement well railings, stone kerbing, and the monument plinth.
