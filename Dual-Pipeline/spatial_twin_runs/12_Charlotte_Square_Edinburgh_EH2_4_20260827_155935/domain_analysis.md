# Spatial Domain Analysis: 12 Charlotte Square, Edinburgh EH2 4DJ, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The terrain sits within the Edinburgh Midland Valley basin, underlain by Early Carboniferous sedimentary formations (Gullane Formation) comprising interbedded sandstones, siltstones, and mudstones with proximate dolerite volcanic intrusions (Whin Sill suite). The structural masonry is predominantly pale cream-to-buff quartzose Craigleith and Hailes sandstone, known for its fine-grained siliceous matrix, high compressive strength, and durability. Mortar systems throughout the historic fabric consist of traditional hot-mixed non-hydraulic slaked lime and coarse river sand aggregates, facilitating vapor permeability across the massive ashlar retaining walls and structural load-bearing envelopes. Subterranean drainage routes naturally slope southward and westward toward the Water of Leith catchment basin.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
A neoclassical municipal and civic ensemble centered on West Register House (originally St George's Church, designed by Robert Reid, 1811–1814, after Robert Adam's 1791 conceptual scheme) and the surrounding Georgian terraces of Charlotte Square and Hope Street. 
- **West Register House**: A monumental central church/record office terminating the western axis. Features a prominent square-plan central mass with flanking corner pavilions, stepped ashlar plinths, and a commanding high cylindrical peristyle drum supporting a ribbed copper dome topped with a tempietto lantern cupola and cross.
- **Terraced Townhouses**: 3- to 4-storey residential Georgian townhouses over raised rusticated basements with cantilevered ashlar drop areas and parapeted rooflines. Planar facades are strictly vertical, featuring disciplined rectilinear rhythms of timber 6-over-6 and 12-pane sash-and-case fenestration, molded entablatures, rusticated ground floors, projecting ashlar cornices, and pitched roofs clad in Scottish and Welsh slates with regular stone chimney stacks and clay pots.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Ashlar Facades**: Hand-dressed, smooth-tooled Craigleith pale sandstone ashlar blocks with fine-line lime mortar joints. Stone displays historic urban atmospheric sulfation crusts, dark coal-smoke carbon patina within sheltered lintels, drip moldings, and balustrade recesses, balanced by natural salt-weathered pale buff highlights on windward faces.
- **Dome & Metalwork**: Ribbed standing-seam copper cladding exhibiting a fully developed, pale verdigris-green and turquoise malachite patina. Wrought and cast iron perimeter railings with lead-caulked sockets set into dressed stone copings, finished in matte black.
- **Roofing**: Scottish grey-blue fissile slate roof planes punctuated by lead flashings, copper roll-top ridges, and dressed stone chimney stacks with terracotta cylindrical flues.
- **Pavement**: Sawn granite kerbstones flanking traditional whinstone (basalt) and granite setts across roadway gutters, with smooth Yorkstone and sandstone flagstone pedestrian paths.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Bordering the corner of the Charlotte Square central garden enclosure to the left foreground:
- Mature broadleaf specimens including European Sycamore (*Acer pseudoplatanus*), Wych Elm (*Ulmus glabra*), and English Oak (*Quercus robur*).
- Foliage presents dense, dark green leafy canopies with complex branch architecture, fissured bark textures, and lush, manicured turf lawn groundcover beneath the perimeter tree line.

The scene is resolved to pure, static architectural and environmental form with permanent infrastructure (granite kerbing, historic cast-iron streetlamps, fixed perimeter railings) fully preserved.
