A 35mm eye-level documentary photograph taken from the water’s edge at Indianola, Washington, framing a prominent 2-storey timber-framed blufftop residence rising to 7.8 meters atop the towering glacial cliffline. The sheer 25-meter-high sedimentary sea bluffs, composed of stratified sand, compact gravel, and beige silt, show realistic shear fissures and erosion scars, interspersed with patches of low seaside shrubs and hanging roots. Along the high crest of the escarpment, a dense, tall forest canopy of 35-meter Douglas firs, Western red cedars, and peeling red-barked Pacific madrones overlooks the shoreline. Below, the intertidal beach is covered with damp dark pebbles, smooth grey glacial cobbles, and washed-up driftwood logs lying above the waterline. The low golden hour sun (approx. 3300K) casts a deep amber, raking light across the vertical face of the bluff and tree tops, casting long shadows across the beach while the Puget Sound water reflects subtle warm glints over deep navy and olive tones. Fine film grain, natural depth of field, sharp structural details on the blufftop home, crisp textures on the glacial cliff, and realistic maritime Pacific Northwest atmosphere.
