# Spatial Domain Analysis: 12 Charlotte Square, Edinburgh EH2 4ET, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The subterranean stratigraphy consists of Carboniferous sedimentary bedrock of the Strathclyde Group (Gullane Formation sandstone and siltstone) mantled by stiff lodgement till. Structural lithics are dominated by pale cream-to-buff Craigleith sandstone—a dense, highly siliceous Carboniferous quartz-arenite—interspersed with Hailes and Cullalo sandstone for rear courses and basements. Structural joints are pointed with traditional natural hydraulic lime (NHL 2/3.5) and sharp quartz sand. The Prince Albert Memorial sits on a monumental tiered plinth of igneous Peterhead pink granite. The undulating topography slopes gently north-northwest toward the Water of Leith, facilitating natural subsurface gravity drainage across glacial drift.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
A formal Georgian urban composition terminating the western vista of George Street. In the immediate center stands the Prince Albert Memorial (1876, Sir John Steell), featuring a monumental bronze equestrian figure atop a multi-tiered polished granite plinth with projecting stepped pedestals bearing subsidiary bronze figure groups. The architectural backdrop is dominated by West Register House (formerly St George's Church, 1811–1814, Robert Adam / Robert Reid), defined by a monumental Neoclassical front with a colossal Greek-order portico, projecting entablature, and an elevated circular colonnaded peristyle drum supporting a ribbed hemispherical copper cupola crowned by a stone lantern and finial. Flanking the central civic core are symmetrical Georgian terraced wings (Charlotte Square west facade), unified as palatial pavilions: three principal storeys over a rusticated basement and attic with pitched dormered roofs, rigorous 6-over-6 timber sash-and-case fenestration grids, modillioned cornices, and planar ashlar frontages.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- West Register House & Facades: Fine-droved and polished Craigleith sandstone ashlar; V-jointed channel rustication across ground-level courses; smooth ashlar on upper storeys. Coal-smoke carbon crusting in sheltered undercut profiles, rain-washed stone reveals, and localized biological greening near plinths.
- Dome & Roofing: Sheet copper cladding over the hemispherical dome exhibiting advanced verdigris (hydrated copper carbonate) oxidation; Scottish/Welsh slate roof tiles on adjacent townhouses with lead roll-top ridges and flashings.
- Monument: Cast bronze with dark oxidized brown-black patina and verdigris run-off stains over fine-grained, dressed and polished Peterhead pink-red granite blocks.
- Ground Plane: Level expanse of mown turf (*Lolium perenne* and *Poa pratensis*) with compacted crushed gravel garden pathways.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Parkland garden trees inside the square comprise mature deciduous specimens of *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm), and *Tilia × europaea* (Common Lime). Grounded in late-December winter phenology, all canopies exhibit complete defoliation, revealing intricate bare branch networks, dark wet bark, dormant terminal buds, and exposed skeletal silhouettes.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient vehicles, delivery vans, pedestrians, modern road cones, bins, and temporary street clutter have been fully expunged. Static cast-iron perimeter boundary railings, dressed stone kerbstones, and fixed historic civil fixtures remain intact.
