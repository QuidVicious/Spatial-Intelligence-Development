## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- **Bedrock & Subterranean Matrix:** The subterranean stratum beneath Edinburgh's West End is rooted in Carboniferous sedimentary sequences of the Lower Oil-Shale Group (Gullane Formation), characterized by interbedded quartzose sandstones, siltstones, and mudstones with deep glacial till drift. 
- **Indigenous Masonry Lithics:**
  - *Cathedral Complex:* St Mary's Episcopal Cathedral is constructed predominantly from pale buff to grey, fine-to-medium grained Carboniferous quartz arenite sandstone, primarily quarried from historic regional sources including the revered Craigleith and Binny quarries. Spire pinnacles, voussoirs, and traceried ashlar blocks feature dense siliceous sandstone.
  - *Surrounding West End Tenements:* Four-to-five storey residential blocks are built of coursed ashlar and snecked/squared rubble Hailes and Craigleith sandstone, featuring subtle horizontal bedding laminae and varied honey-buff to cool silver-grey tones.

- **Hydrogeology & Subterranean Drainage:** Moderate natural infiltration through sandstone fissures and glacial drift, draining north towards the Water of Leith basin.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- **Primary Monument (Center & Midground):** *St Mary's Episcopal Cathedral* (Category A Listed, designed by Sir George Gilbert Scott, consecrated 1879). High Victorian Gothic Revival style with an expansive Latin cross cruciform layout, aisled nave, transepts, and a soaring 90-meter (295 ft) central crossing spire fluted with multiple tiers of gabled louvers and lancet openings, flanked by two towering western spires (completed 1917). 
- **Planar Rectification & Geometric Orthogonality:**
  - Flying buttresses, clerestory walls, vertical corner buttresses, and the trio of stone spires are strictly plumbed to true gravity vertical.
  - Rectified steep dual-pitch nave roofs, crossing ridges, and dormered valley slopes to crisp, straight geometric lines.
  - Octagonal chapter house to the northeast, punctuated by tall lancet windows and a steeply pointed stone roof.
- **Flanking & Foreground Civil Fabric:**
  - Classical 19th-century Georgian/Victorian West End residential tenements (4 storeys over raised basements with mansard/attic dormers).
  - Distinctive tall stone chimney stacks (coped ashlar with terracotta chimney pots), horizontal stringcourses, rusticated ground floors, and disciplined timber-framed sash fenestration.
  - Clean horizontal roof parapets and straight dormer gables.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Masonry & Cladding:** 
  - Ashlar Craigleith and Hailes sandstone on tenement facades, showing fine tool markings (droved and stugged finishes) and authentic non-sandblasted industrial-era carbon deposition.
  - Cathedral walls of massive squared ashlar sandstone, exhibiting subtle tonal variations between the 1870s nave/central spire and the early 20th-century west spires.
- **Roofing Materials:** Scotch slate and dark Westmorland/Welsh blue-grey roofing slates laid in diminishing courses, paired with lead-rolled ridge caps, hips, flashings, and cast-iron rainwater goods (gutters and downpipes).
- **Climate-Adaptive Environmental Weathering & Patina:** 
  - *Historic Post-Industrial / Cool Temperate UK Weathering:* Prominent historic atmospheric carbon crusting and dark soot wash patterns concentrated in sheltered arch reveals, under cornice dripstones, and within the deep relief of the Gothic carved capitals and gargoyles.
  - Clean vertical rainwater runoff streaks along exposed ashlar wall planes.
  - Subtle micro-algal and lichen growth in damp shaded north-facing corners and lower foundation plinths.
