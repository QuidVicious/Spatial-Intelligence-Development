A documentary photograph on 35mm film captures the iconic Neoclassical mineral well rotunda standing as a singular 1-storey open circular temple rising 8.5 meters above the Water of Leith gorge floor. From an elevated, eye-level perspective looking south-southwest down the wooded ravine (heading 194.8°, pitch -9.2°), the historic 1789 Craigleith blonde ashlar sandstone rotunda occupies the immediate midground, its ten fluted Doric columns perfectly plumb, supporting an entablature and a shallow leaded dome crowned with a carved stone pineapple. Inside the colonnade, the marble statue of Hygieia stands quietly over the natural spring vault. To the right, the Water of Leith flows steadily over rounded pebbles and dark riverbed basalt, lined by a low, weathered sandstone retaining wall topped with stone copings and an adjacent beaten gravel walkway. The steep gorge embankments rise steeply on both sides, clad in dense lush foliage of towering sycamore, mature beech, and weeping willow branches trailing toward the water's surface. Soft, clear morning light filters down through the leafy canopy, casting dappled raking highlights across the weathered sandstone grain and gentle cool reflections upon the rippling river current, rendered with natural analog depth of field, fine film grain, and rich tactile material fidelity.
