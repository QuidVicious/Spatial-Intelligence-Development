# Spatial Domain Analysis: St John's Church, Princes St., Edinburgh EH2 4BJ, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean base composed of Lower Carboniferous sedimentary sandstone and volcanic strata draped in dense glacial till. Primary architectural lithics comprise local pale buff-to-grey Craigleith and Hailes Carboniferous sandstones, finely dressed into ashlar masonry. Basal storeys feature polished Peterhead granite and dark igneous plinths. Historic masonry bound with lime-based mortar; road surfaces underlain by crushed basalt aggregate and reinforced concrete sub-bases.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Rectified urban intersection at the convergence of Princes Street, Lothian Road, and Shandwick Place. 
- Right frontage: Continuous 4-to-6-storey commercial streetscape. Features early-20th-century Edwardian classical ashlar commercial blocks with giant order fluted pilasters, heavy modillion cornices, balustraded parapets, and attic dormers; adjacent modern infill displaying vertical steel-and-glass curtain walling.
- Left frontage: 4-storey New Town classical corner block with rusticated ashlar ground floor, regular fenestration grids with timber 2-over-2 and 6-over-6 sash-and-case windows, molded architraves, continuous string courses, and a hipped Scottish slate roof punctuated by dressed ashlar chimney stacks with ceramic pots.
- Distance skyline: Slender vertical spires and Italianate campaniles rendered in tooled sandstone masonry.
- Ground plane: Planar asphalt thoroughfare embedded with twin grooved steel tram rails, painted cross-hatched yellow junction markings, edged by straight granite kerbstones and broad Caithness flagstone pedestrian pavements.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Facades: Craigleith sandstone ashlar showing selective carbon-crust soot patina in protected recesses and rain-washed pale relief; dark-toned granite shopfront surrounds; modern clear float glass panels with charcoal-anodized aluminum mullions.
- Roofs: Grey-blue Ballachulish and Scotch roofing slates, lead flashing aprons, zinc valley gutters, and oxidized green copper cladding on attic dormers and distant spires.
- Ground: Bituminous asphalt, cold-applied thermoplastic road markings, rolled steel rail profiles, fine-grained grey granite kerbs, and split Caithness stone paving flags.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Date-grounded phenology (September 9, late summer transitioning to early autumn): Distant incidental street trees (*Acer pseudoplatanus* and *Tilia cordata*) retain full, dense canopy volumes in deep forest green, exhibiting initial localized margin chlorosis and dry foliage edges consistent with late-season urban conditions.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient vehicles, tram units, pedestrians, temporary barriers, traffic cones, and floating photogrammetric mesh slivers are completely excised. Structural civil assets, fixed tracks, road markings, and municipal paving remain fully planar and plumb.
