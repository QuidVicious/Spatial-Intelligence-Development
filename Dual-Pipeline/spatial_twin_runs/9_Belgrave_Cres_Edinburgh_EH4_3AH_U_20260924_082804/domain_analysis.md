# Spatial Domain Analysis: 9 Belgrave Cres, Edinburgh EH4 3AH, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean foundation composed of Lower Carboniferous sandstones and siltstones over basaltic volcanic bedrock. Superstructure lithics dominated by local Craigleith and Hailes pale buff-to-grey quartz-arenite sandstone. Bedding laid with historic non-hydraulic lime mortar and fine quartz sand aggregate.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Left: Victorian residential terrace townhouse; three storeys over basement, planar ashlar facade, projecting canted three-light bay windows, molded stone cornices, and rectangular sash-and-case fenestration.
Center: Gothic Revival church featuring a square crenellated bell tower with corner pinnacles, stepped stone buttresses, pitched slate nave roof, and pointed-arch lancet windows with stone tracery.
Ground: Planarized asphalt roadway bounded by dressed whinstone kerbs, sandstone retaining plinth wall, and garden perimeter railings.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Townhouse: Fine-grained Craigleith sandstone ashlar with droved margins; localized coal-smoke atmospheric crusting in recesses and water-washed lintel reveals; painted timber window joinery.
- Church: Squared rock-faced sandstone rubble with droved ashlar dressings, weathered sandstone stringcourses, and dark Scottish slate roof tiles with lead flashings.
- Roadway & Walkway: Bituminous asphalt carriageway, Caithness flagstone paving, and grey granite kerbstones.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Matures stands along boundary: *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore) and *Tilia × europaea* (Common Lime). Late September phenology displaying full dense canopy with peripheral chlorosis, early ochre-yellow leaf edge discoloration, and initial leaf fall revealing structural limb branching.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All photogrammetric vehicle mesh artifacts, transient street items, temporary barriers, and pedestrian approximations are eliminated. Permanent civil infrastructure—stone boundary walls, kerbs, and mature trees—are preserved.
