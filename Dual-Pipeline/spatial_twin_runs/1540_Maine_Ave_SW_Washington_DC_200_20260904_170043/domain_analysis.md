# Spatial Domain Analysis: 1540 Maine Ave SW, Washington, DC 20004, USA

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The terrain occupies the transitional terrace between the Atlantic Coastal Plain and the crystalline Piedmont formation, underlain by alluvial silt, sand, and gravel deposits over deep metamorphic schist and gneiss bedrock. The central obelisk sits upon a massive concrete underpinning footing extending to dense gravel strata. Primary masonry lithics include fine-grained white Cockeysville calcitic marble (Baltimore County, MD) on the lower 46 meters and upper shaft, interrupted by a distinct stratum of Lee dolomite marble (Berkshire County, MA) backed with coarse grey Vinalhaven granite (Maine). Surrounding civic structures utilize Salem Formation Indiana oolitic limestone, Aquia Creek freestone sandstone, and Georgia red terra cotta clay roofing tiles. Crushed decomposed bluestone and granite aggregate form the pedestrian circulatory paths.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
The central monument is a 169.3-meter load-bearing stone masonry obelisk tapering at a 1:48 slope, terminating in a 16.8-meter pyramidal capstone capped with cast aluminum. The base is enveloped by a circular paved granite plaza ringed by 56 vertical stainless steel flagpoles. To the northeast, the National Museum of African American History and Culture features a contemporary 5-story tripartite inverse-tier corona structure enveloped in ornamental bronze-finished architectural latticework over a curtain-wall glass core. The northern Federal Triangle perimeter consists of monumental Beaux-Arts and Classical Revival government edifices (Commerce, Reagan, EPA buildings) spanning 6 to 8 storeys, defined by continuous rusticated stone podiums, giant-order Ionic and Doric colonnades, projecting pedimented porticos, and hipped roofs clad in red clay barrel tiles.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Washington Monument: Lower third in cream-white Cockeysville marble; distinct horizontal chromatic shift at the 46-meter line to greyish-white Lee marble, returning to Cockeysville marble above; jointed with lime-pozzolan mortar; ashlar dressed finish with minor atmospheric particulate buildup along bedding planes.
- Museum Corona: Triple-tiered corona wrapped in cast-aluminum panels coated in bronze architectural fluoropolymer lacquer, backed by low-iron insulated glass units.
- Federal Triangle Complex: Smooth-sawn and bush-hammered Indiana limestone facades, cast-bronze window spandrels, rusticated Deer Island granite basement levels, and unglazed terracotta roof tiles with weathered lead flashing.
- Ground Plane: Manicured Kentucky bluegrass (*Poa pratensis*) and tall fescue turf across the undulating knoll; compacted buff-tan decomposed granite walking pathways; fine-grained granite curb edges.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Vegetation reflects late-summer Mid-Atlantic phenology (September 4). Canopies are dense, heavy, and fully expanded:
- American Elm (*Ulmus americana*): Formal alignments bordering adjacent avenues, featuring vase-shaped canopies of deep olive-green foliage displaying faint localized late-summer leaf scorch.
- Willow Oak (*Quercus phellos*) and Pin Oak (*Quercus palustris*): Broad, conical canopies with narrow, dark green, glossy leaves.
- London Planetree (*Platanus × acerifolia*): Dense globose crowns with mottling exfoliating bark along primary boles.
- Ground cover: Uniform, high-density green turfgrass showing subtle heat-stress variations along footpath margins.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All moving and stationary vehicular traffic, tour buses, temporary barricades, security bollards, pedestrian crowds, ground maintenance equipment, and mobile signage have been completely excised from roadways, pathways, and the central monument plaza.
