# Spatial Domain Analysis: 2436 Rayburn House Office Bldg, Washington, DC 20004, USA

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The site rests on the Atlantic Fall Zone transition, underlain by Potomac Formation Cretaceous sediments consisting of unconsolidated fluvial sands, clays, and gravels over crystalline Piedmont metamorphic basement rock (mica schists and gneisses). Structural foundations bear directly on consolidated gravel strata and deep historical masonry footings. 

Masonry lithology reflects historic mid-Atlantic quarries:
- **Central Core:** Aquia Creek Cretaceous quartz-rich sandstone from Stafford County, Virginia, heavily painted and sealed.
- **House and Senate Wings:** Crystalline Lee, Massachusetts dolomitic marble and Cockeysville/Beaver Dam marble from Baltimore County, Maryland for exterior colonnades.
- **West Front Substructures & Terrace:** Cut Vermont marble and Mount Airy (North Carolina) biotite granite stylobates and steps.
- **Mortar Systems:** Historic lime-sand mortar formulations on older wings, retrofitted with non-hydraulic hydraulic lime re-pointing; subterranean drainage utilizes integrated gravity channels to prevent hydrostatic head buildup beneath the East Front Plaza and Capitol Visitor Center substructure.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- **Typology:** Neoclassical civic monumentalism (Greek Revival and Roman Corinthian orders) designed progressively by William Thornton, Benjamin Henry Latrobe, Charles Bulfinch, Thomas U. Walter, and Frederick Law Olmsted (landscape and terraces).
- **Massing & Vertical Dimensions:** Symmetrical tripartite composition. Central cast-iron double-shell dome culminates in the Statue of Freedom at 88 meters (288 ft) total height. Flanking 3-to-4 habitable-storey Senate (North) and House (South) wings with pedimented porticos and Corinthian colonnades.
- **Roof Geometry:** Weathered verdigris standing-seam sheet copper roofs over low-pitch gable structural steel framing; cast-iron central dome and low flanking rotunda domes.
- **Planar Rectification:** Strict nadir orthogonal alignment; all cornice returns, balustrade parapets, and East Plaza granite pavers rectified to exact planar axes. The subterranean Capitol Visitor Center roof slab creates a level, geometrically paved pedestrian plaza punctuated by flush skylights and dual rectangular sunken courtyards with granite fountain coping.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Dome:** Cast iron finished in multi-layered off-white protective elastomer/alkyd coatings, exhibiting subtle directional solar exposure and micro-scale dirt retention along sheltered concave fluting.
- **Roofs:** Heavy patinated sheet copper displaying brilliant oxidized verdigris (cupric carbonate) turquoise tones, with darker mineral weathering lines along standing seams and valley flashings.
- **Facades:** Crisp, light-colored Lee dolomitic marble and painted Aquia sandstone showing soft differential atmospheric patina, fine calcite crystallization, and mild urban humidity wash along drip courses.
- **Plaza Paving:** Multi-toned pink, gray, and buff cut granite modular pavers laid in an orthogonal grid with tight saw-cut joints; glass skylights set in dark bronze-anodized metal framing.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Part of the Level III accredited U.S. Capitol Grounds Arboretum designed by Frederick Law Olmsted:
- **Canopy Trees:** Mature *Ulmus americana* (American Elm), *Quercus alba* (White Oak), *Quercus palustris* (Pin Oak), *Liriodendron tulipifera* (Tulip Poplar), *Ginkgo biloba*, and *Fagus grandifolia* (American Beech) forming dense, organic leafy canopies with natural crown branching and varied summer chlorophyllic tones.
- **Turf & Understory:** Manicured cool-season turfgrass (*Festuca arundinacea* and *Poa pratensis*) framed by broad-curved gravel/crushed stone pedestrian paths, English ivy groundcovers, and boxwood perimeter hedges along terrace bases.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient mobile elements—including moving vehicles, parked staff/security cars on East Drive and Constitution/Independence access loops, delivery vans, pedestrians, temporary queue stanchions, barricades, and waste dumpsters—are completely stripped. Preserved static elements include historic bronze lampposts, granite bollards, curb lines, permanent stone balustrades, and iron tree grates.
