# Spatial Domain Analysis: VXM7+CJ Washington, DC, USA

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The subterranean floor beneath the National Mall occupies the Atlantic Coastal Plain margin where unconsolidated Quaternary and Holocene alluvial silt, clay, and gravel overlie crystalline Piedmont metamorphic bedrock (Piedmont gneiss and schist). Due to historic proximity to the Potomac River and Tiber Creek floodplains, artificial fills and massive engineered foundations (including the 1880s underpinning of the Washington Monument using mass concrete sub-bases) stabilize the terrain. Indigenous lithics include:
- **Washington Monument:** Faced with dolomitic white marble from the Beaver Dam / Texas quarry in Cockeysville, Maryland (lower 46m / 152ft), transitional courses of Stockbridge marble from Lee, Massachusetts, and upper Cockeysville marble, backed by Maine granite and Seneca red sandstone.
- **Federal Triangle Buildings:** Extensive cladding of dense calcarenitic Salem Limestone (Indiana oolitic limestone) set over structural steel and concrete frames.
- **Smithsonian Complex:** Triassic iron-rich arkosic red sandstone from the Poolesville Member quarried at Seneca, Maryland (Smithsonian Castle), paired with deep red fired clay structural bricks and polychrome pressed brickwork.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- **Centerpiece (Washington Monument):** Pure neoclassical Egyptian obelisk form, measuring 169.05 meters (554.6 feet) in height, tapering uniformly to an apex pyramidion capped in cast aluminum. Grounded on a paved circular plaza ringed with fifty flagpoles. Planar alignment rectified to true gravity vertical.
- **Flanking Northeast / East (Federal Triangle):** Monumental Beaux-Arts, Neoclassical, and French Renaissance revival federal office complexes (including the Herbert C. Hoover Building / Department of Commerce, Ronald Reagan Building, and Clinton Federal Building). Consistent massing spanning 6 to 8 stories (approx. 30–40 meters), punctuated by massive Corinthian and Doric colonnades, rusticated bases, hipped roofscapes, and red clay Spanish barrel-tile roofs.
- **East-Center (National Museum of African American History and Culture - NMAAHC):** Contemporary inverted three-tiered corona stepped pyramid wrapped in an ornate bronze-tinted cast aluminum lattice filigree over a glazed curtain-wall base.
- **Southeast / Foreground Flanks:** Victorian Romanesque Revival and polychromatic brick pavilions (Smithsonian Arts and Industries Building and Castle complex) featuring turrets, gabled pavilions, and clerestory dormers.
- **Urban Topography & Pathways:** Curved pedestrian walkways and grand elliptical axes (The Ellipse / President's Park South to the north, Constitution Avenue corridor, and circular monument grounds).

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Obelisk Cladding:** Distinct horizontal material demarcation (the "tide mark" at 46m / 152ft) where the lower weathered, slightly darker, fine-veined Cockeysville marble meets the subtler gray-toned upper courses. Weathered by seasonal Mid-Atlantic humidity, rainwater wash paths along planar ashlar joints, and minor micro-biological patination in shaded recesses.
- **Civic Palaces (Federal Triangle):** Hone-finished buff-gray Indiana limestone showing subtle gypsum crusting under deep carved cornices, crisp rusticated channel incisions, and terracotta-red curved clay roof tiles weathered with soft atmospheric oxidation.
- **NMAAHC:** Matte dark bronze, corrosion-resistant coated aluminum openwork mesh with precise geometric apertures, catching metallic raking highlights against deep internal voids.
- **Ground Pavements & Plazas:** Fine aggregate compacted gravel pathways, granite curbs, and smooth light-gray granite flagstones surrounding the obelisk circle.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- **Mature Urban Canopy:** American elm (*Ulmus americana*) lining the mall corridors in formal linear allees, American sycamore (*Platanus occidentalis*), London plane trees (*Platanus × acerifolia*), willow oaks (*Quercus phellos*), pin oaks (*Quercus palustris*), and Eastern redcedar (*Juniperus virginiana*).
- **Turf & Vegetation:** Maintained Kentucky bluegrass and fescue blend lawn expanses showing seasonal micro-variations, clover clusters, and aeration striping across the broad greens of the Monument grounds and the Ellipse.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient visual noise has been strictly excised:
- Zero pedestrians, zero tourists, and zero moving/parked motor vehicles on Constitution Avenue, 14th Street, 15th Street, or surrounding parkways.
- Zero dumpsters, temporary tour buses, queue stanchions, or transient waste bins.
- Retained permanent infrastructure: Flagpole circle, fixed cast-iron streetlamps, permanent directional granite bollards, stone curbs, pedestrian asphalt/gravel walkways, and mature botanical plantings.
