# Spatial Domain Analysis: 43 St Andrew Sq, Edinburgh EH2 2AD, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean foundation consists of the Lower Carboniferous Strathclyde and Inverclyde Group sedimentary strata (interbedded sandstone, siltstone, and mudstone) intersected by the volcanic basalt neck of Castle Rock to the south. Primary masonry lithology is Carboniferous quartz arenite sandstone, historically sourced from Craigleith, Hailes, and Binny quarries. Surfacing lithics include Scottish whinstone (dolerite/basalt) and Peterhead granite setts, edged with Caithness flagstone kerbstones. Mortar chemistry across historic envelopes is traditional non-hydraulic to feebly hydraulic hot-mixed lime and graded quartz sand.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Central focal structure is the Melville Monument: a 42.7-metre fluted sandstone Roman Doric column elevated on a tiered ashlar pedestal and square plinth, surmounted by a standing stone statue. 
St Andrew Square perimeter comprises a continuous terrace of rectified four- to six-storey late Georgian, Victorian, and Edwardian commercial palazzi interspersed with late 20th and early 21st-century commercial infill:
- Historic blocks: rusticated ground storeys, regular fenestration grids with 6-over-6 and 2-over-2 timber sash-and-case windows, projecting entablatures, modillioned cornices, classical pediments, and mansard slate roofs with dressed ashlar dormers.
- Contemporary infill: rectilinear multi-storey curtain wall assemblies composed of structural steel mullions, bronze-anodized louvres, flat planar high-performance glass panes, and polished reconstituted stone rainscreen cladding panels.
- Park structure: single-storey circular pavilion featuring curved structural glass walls and a low-pitch zinc-sheathed standing-seam roof.
- Background ridge: the crag-and-tail silhouette of Castle Rock supporting Edinburgh Castle's rubble-built curtain walls, crenellated parapets, and the neo-Gothic spire of The Hub.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Monument & Historic Terraces: fine-grained droved and polished Craigleith sandstone ashlar; cast-iron railings; lead flashings; Scotch and Welsh slate pitched roofs.
- Contemporary Interventions: clear float glass, extruded architectural aluminium, zinc sheet roofing, precast concrete and polished stone rainscreen.
- Ground Plane: manicured perennial turf, bound gravel aggregate pedestrian paths, porphyry and granite kerbing, bituminous carriageways with embedded grooved steel tram rails.
- Pathology & Weathering: selective coal-smoke atmospheric crusts across ashlar friezes and column fluting recesses, contrasted with rain-washed quartz sand faces on exposed planar elevations.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Urban public garden square populated by mature broadleaf trees:
- *Platanus × acerifolia* (London plane): high-branching columnar trunks with exfoliating bark plates, wide-spreading hemispherical crown architecture reaching 18–22 metres in height.
- *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore): single stout boles dividing into dense, rounded, multi-stem structural canopies.
- *Tilia cordata* (Small-leaved lime): upright trunks supporting narrow-to-ovate canopy frameworks positioned along the perimeter path alignments.
- Ground cover: high-density *Poa pratensis* and *Lolium perenne* civic turf encircled by low stone kerbs and perimeter cast-iron balustrades.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All moving and parked motor vehicles, public transit buses, trams, pedestrians, temporary traffic signs, waste receptacles, construction scaffolding, and commercial vinyl building wraps are fully purged. Fixed civil street furniture, cast-iron lamp columns, steel rails, paved traffic islands, and permanent architectural envelopes remain intact.
