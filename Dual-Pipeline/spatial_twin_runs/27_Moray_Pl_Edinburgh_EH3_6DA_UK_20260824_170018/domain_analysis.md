# Spatial Domain Analysis: 27 Moray Pl, Edinburgh EH3 6DA, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The subterranean terrain consists of Early Carboniferous sedimentary bedrock (Gullane Formation / Strathclyde Group) interbedded with sandstone, siltstone, and mudstone strata falling steeply toward the incised Dean Gorge and the Water of Leith. The superstructures are constructed predominantly of indigenous pale-buff to blonde Carboniferous sandstone quarried from the historic Craigleith and Hailes quarries of Edinburgh. Masonry elements are bound with traditional non-hydraulic to weakly hydraulic lime mortar (slaked quicklime with sharp silica sand aggregate), allowing vapor permeability through the thick mass-masonry perimeter walls. Subterranean hydrology is defined by natural subterranean runoff descending the northern slope of the New Town ridge, retained by stepped rubble-stone revetments and terrace foundations.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
A grand Georgian neoclassical sweep designed by James Gillespie Graham (1822–1825) forming the rear curved elevation of the Category A listed Moray Place palatial polygon. The massing comprises 4 principal storeys above substantial basement levels built on falling ground, culminating in continuous pitched roofs clad in Scottish and West Highland slates. The rear elevation features planar, plumb sandstone coursing, regularly spaced timber 12-pane (6-over-6) and 8-pane double-hung sash windows, projecting chimney stacks topped with clay cylindrical chimney pots, and stepped individual rear plot outshoots/terraces descending into private gardens. Photogrammetric angular planar collapse is rectified: all vertical party walls and window reveals stand true gravity-vertical (90.0°), while roof ridge lines and stone eaves gutters form unified continuous curves.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Masonry:** Hewn Craigleith buff-grey sandstone ashlar and squared coursed sandstone rubble on rear elevations.
- **Roofing:** Natural dark grey-black West Highland slate tiles laid in diminishing courses, with lead flashings, ridges, valleys, and coped stone skews.
- **Fenestration & Joinery:** Painted off-white historic timber sash frames and structural mullions.
- **Micro-Climate Weathering/Patina:** Characteristic maritime-temperate Scottish weathering with deep carbon/soot crusting in sheltered cornices, lintels, and downpipe reveals from historical coal combustion, vertical rainwater washing trails across blonde sandstone faces, and biological green micro-algae growth near the lower damp basement levels and retaining boundary walls.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
The foreground and middle-ground steep ravine gardens (Bank Gardens descending toward the Dean Valley) are densely populated with mature temperate deciduous and riparian trees:
- *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore / Scottish plane)
- *Quercus petraea* (Sessile oak)
- *Fagus sylvatica* (Common beech)
- *Fraxinus excelsior* (European ash)
- Understorey: *Hedera helix* (English ivy climbing boundary walls), native ferns (*Dryopteris filix-mas*), and manicured private lawn turf. Photogrammetric faceted mesh leaves are fully disambiguated into organic foliage with distinct leaf clusters, branching twigs, and natural translucent green canopies.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All temporary and transient urban noise is omitted: zero pedestrians, zero motor vehicles, zero refuse containers, and zero modern garden clutter. Preserved static civil fabric includes original 19th-century coursed stone garden boundary walls, cast-iron spearhead railings, stone retaining copings, and permanent slate outbuilding structures.
