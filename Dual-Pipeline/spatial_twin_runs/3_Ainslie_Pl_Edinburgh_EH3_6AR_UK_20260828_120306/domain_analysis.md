# Spatial Domain Analysis: 3 Ainslie Pl, Edinburgh EH3 6AR, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean formation comprises Lower Carboniferous (Tournaisian–Viséan) sedimentary strata of the Strathclyde Group, dominated by interbedded siltstones, sandstones, and volcanic basaltic intrusions characteristic of the Lothian basin. Principal structural masonry is pale blonde-to-grey quartz-arenite Craigleith sandstone, supplemented by carbonaceous Hailes sandstone, bonded with non-hydraulic to weakly hydraulic slaked lime mortar and fine river sand aggregates. Subsurface hydrology slopes northwestward toward the Water of Leith incised gorge, governed by historical French drainage channels beneath cobbled area wells and cellar vaults.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Neoclassical Greek Revival / Regency urban palace-fronted crescent terraces designed by architect James Gillespie Graham (1822–1828) for the Moray Estate. Uniform four-storey elevation profile comprising sunken basement service areas with flagstone bridging, rusticated ashlar ground storeys, two polished ashlar principal storeys, and low-profile slate mansard attic storeys behind stone blocking courses. Facades articulate strict tripartite vertical bay rhythms, channelled ashlar rustication on base courses, molded platbands, classical stringcourses, and dentiled eaves cornices. Symmetrical multi-flue rectangular ashlar chimney stacks with terracotta cylindrical pots crest slate roof ridges. Fenestration is rectified to rigid plumb lines, featuring regular 6-over-6 and 2-over-2 double-hung timber sash-and-case windows set into molded architraves.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Primary Facades: Dressed blonde Craigleith ashlar, fine-tooled droved margins, and channelled rustication, showing authentic atmospheric coal-smoke gypsum crusting in protected moldings, soot patina, and rain-scoured upper freestone surfaces.
- Basement & Retaining Structures: Squared rubble masonry, droved sandstone revetments, cast-iron spear-headed railings with decorative urn finials, and York/Caithness stone flags in the sunken areas.
- Roofing: Dark grey Ballachulish and Welsh slate laid in diminishing courses, lead valleys, zinc flashings, and cast-iron rainwater downpipes with decorative hopper heads.
- Roadways & Kerbs: Basalt/whinstone roadway surfaces, tooled granite kerbstones, and York stone perimeter flagstone pavements.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Ainslie Place Gardens contains mature broadleaf urban canopy specimens:
- *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore) and *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm), exhibiting massive multi-stem crown spreads with fully developed late-summer deep green foliage, dense branch matrices, and minor leaf-tier chlorosis.
- *Tilia × europaea* (Common Lime) and *Betula pendula* (Silver Birch), displaying upright form, dense leaf distribution, and defined radial root flares anchoring within maintained perennial turf and curved gravel pedestrian circuits.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient vehicles, parked cars, commercial vans, pedestrians, temporary traffic cones, wheelie bins, and photogrammetry surface artifacts are completely removed. Static infrastructure is fully restored: continuous perimeter cast-iron palisade railings, cast-iron fluted streetlamp posts, tooled kerbing, and intact garden boundary masonry.
