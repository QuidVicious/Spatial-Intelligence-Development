# Spatial Domain Analysis: 6 St Andrew Sq, Edinburgh EH2 2BD, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean foundation seated on the Carboniferous sedimentary sequence of the Midland Valley, interrupted by Early Carboniferous volcanic plugs and sills (olivine basalt and dolerite intrusions of Castle Rock and Calton Hill). Primary structural lithic: pale grey to buff Carboniferous Craigleith and Hailes sandstone (quartz-rich, silica-cemented, high crushing strength), interspersed with Binny and Cullalo sandstones. Base courses and road paving composed of dark grey microgabbro/dolerite whinstone and Peterhead/Aberdeenshire granite setts. Mortar matrix throughout historic masonry consists of traditional non-hydraulic to feebly hydraulic hot-mixed lime mortars with coarse quartz and river-gravel aggregates; modern infill structures seated on reinforced Portland cement concrete pile foundations into stiff glacial till.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- Melville Monument: 46-metre-tall Roman Doric fluted freestone column raised on an elevated rusticated pedestal and stepped square plinth, surmounted by a stepped capital, drum pedestal, and colossal carved stone statue.
- St Andrew Square Gardens: Concentric elliptical plan with radiating gravel circulation paths, low stone retaining kerbs, and a low-profile glass-and-steel circular pavilion structure with a radial ribbed conical roof.
- Perimeter Square Fabric: Heterogeneous multi-era street wall. West and East flanks feature 4-to-6-storey Georgian and Victorian Classical palazzo blocks with rusticated basements, ashlar piano nobiles, molded architraves, pedimented window headers, balustraded parapets, and slate mansard roofs with pedimented dormers. Interspersed with late-20th and early-21st-century commercial infill: multi-storey planar curtain-wall structures with expressed vertical aluminum fins, regular grid fenestration, recessed glazed double-height loggias, and flat membrane roofs.
- Urban Horizon: Rising south-facing slope of the Old Town ridge featuring high-density 6-to-10-storey historic rubble and ashlar tenements with crow-stepped gables, punctuated by slender Gothic Revival stone spires (including the Tolbooth Kirk spire), terminating at the western precipice of Castle Rock crowned by the ashlar ramparts and fortified curtain walls of Edinburgh Castle.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Historic Facades: Fine-grained Craigleith and Hailes ashlar sandstone, displaying horizontal tooling, diamond-stippled rustication, and smooth-rubbed dressings. Environmental patina consists of differential carbonaceous soot crusts in sheltered friezes/moldings, contrasted with rain-washed, pale buff-grey exposed ashlar faces.
- Modern Structures: Anodised extruded aluminium mullions, structural silicone-glazed float glass curtain walls, precast pale limestone-aggregate concrete panels, and dark zinc parapet copings.
- Roofing: Scottish West Highland and Welsh Penrhyn dark blue-grey roofing slates in diminishing courses, lead roll-top ridges, zinc flashing, and cast-iron rainwater downpipes.
- Ground Plane: Mown perennial turf panels bounded by crushed golden gravel paths; perimeter roadways surfaced in dense asphalt with embedded steel twin-groove tram tracks, bordered by fine-picked granite kerbstones and riven Caithness stone flag pavements.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Urban parkland canopy composed of mature broadleaf specimens:
- *Tilia cordata* (Small-leaved Lime): Upright, dense pyramidal to rounded crown structures with extensive multi-tiered twiggy branching, supported on deeply fissured, dark grey single trunks.
- *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore): Spreading, domed canopies with heavy horizontal scaffold limbs, scaly grey-brown bark plates.
- *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm): Vase-shaped branching architecture with high arching upper scaffolds and rough, furrowed bark.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All vehicular traffic, moving trams, pedestrians, street-level vendor installations, waste bins, construction wraps, and temporary signage are fully removed. Fixed structural civil infrastructure—tram rails, overhead traction poles, cast-iron streetlamps, stone kerbs, and fixed iron perimeter railings—is completely retained.
