# Spatial Domain Analysis: 1400 Independence Ave SW, Washington, DC 20250, USA

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean strata consist of Atlantic Coastal Plain unconsolidated sedimentary deposits (gravel, sand, silt, and clay of the Potomac Formation) overlying crystalline metamorphic basement rocks (Piedmont Province Potomac terrane mica schist and gneiss). Historic fill and alluvial muds from the Potomac River paleochannel underlie the knoll, reinforced by an engineered mass-concrete underpinning slab. Primary lithics comprise Cockeysville calcitic marble (Symington quarry, Baltimore County, Maryland) at the lower 46 meters, a narrow transitional belt of Lee marble (Berkshire County, Massachusetts), and Cockeysville marble (Beaver Dam quarry, Maryland) extending through the pyramidal apex. Structural backing consists of mortared gneiss rubble and coarse-grained biotite granite blocks. Paved surfaces feature thermal-finished Rockville white granite flags, bluestone curbing, and compacted decomposed granite pathways.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
The central obelisk rises 169.29 meters with a 10:1 height-to-base ratio, tapering from a square footprint of 16.79 meters per side to a 10.5-meter square shaft top, capped by a 16.8-meter pyramidal apex with eight interior observation ports. The surrounding concentric plaza features low granite security walls, circular paved aprons, and radiating curvilinear pedestrian walkways. The adjacent cultural pavilion structure comprises an inverted three-tier ziggurat corona clad in cast-aluminum filigree screens over a glazed base volume with a zinc standing-seam penthouse. Surrounding civic buildings feature stripped Neoclassical and Beaux-Arts limestone colonnades, multi-pane steel fenestration grids, and flat built-up roofs with parapets. All vertical surfaces plumb to true gravity axes, rectifying photogrammetric geometric drift.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Obelisk: Finely dressed ashlar Cockeysville calcitic marble with lime-pozzolan pointing, displaying a distinct horizontal color-phase demarcation line at 46 meters; weathered surface shows microcrystalline gypsum crusting in sheltered recesses and rain-washed crystalline reveals.
- Corona Pavilion: 3,600 porous cast-aluminum lattice panels coated in multi-layer bronze fluoropolymer finish, structural steel armature, ultra-clear low-iron curtain-wall glazing, and polished absolute black granite water-table courses.
- Surrounding Neoclassical Facades: Indiana Bedford oolitic limestone ashlar, Georgia white Cherokee marble friezes, rusticated granite stylobates, copper-clad mansards with green basic copper carbonate patination, and bronze spandrel panels.
- Grounds: Crushed tan stone aggregate paths, saw-cut gray granite bollards, stainless steel flag standards, and cast-iron civic luminaires.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Vegetation reflects late-summer phenology on September 4. Canopy composition features mature *Ulmus americana* (American elm) forming dense vaulted canopies with deep green foliage showing localized chlorosis along leaf margins; *Quercus palustris* (pin oak) and *Quercus alba* (white oak) with fully expanded, dark green coriaceous leaves; *Prunus serrulata* (Yoshino cherry) groupings showing late-season foliar fatigue and subtle foliage thinning; *Acer rubrum* (red maple) with incipient anthocyanin accumulation on terminal leaf sprays; and *Platanus × acerifolia* (London planetree) displaying peeling cream-olive bark plates beneath mature lobed leaves.
