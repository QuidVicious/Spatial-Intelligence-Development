# Spatial Domain Analysis: 45 N Washington St, Boston, MA 02114, USA

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
* **Bedrock & Stratigraphy:** Boston Basin tectonic depression underlain by Neoproterozoic Cambridge Argillite (metamorphosed laminated mudstone/pelite) mantled by Pleistocene Boston Blue Clay (glaciomarine silty clay) and 19th-century anthropogenic tidal fill from the former Mill Pond drainage and reclamation.
* **Lithics & Sourcing:** Glacial clay-extruded red brick (fired from local Mystic and Merrimack River valley clays); Quincy and Chelmsford biotite granite foundation footings, water tables, and ground-level storefront lintels; red Medina/Longmeadow arkosic sandstone lintels and belt courses.
* **Hydrology & Mortar Chemistry:** Historic non-hydraulic calcitic lime-and-sand mortar (1:3 lime-to-quartz sand ratio) across 19th-century masonry, transition to Type N Portland-lime mortars on 20th-century repointing; high subterranean water table governed by proximity to the Charles River and Boston Inner Harbor.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
* **Typological Matrix:** 
  * *45–51 North Washington Street (Center Corner):* 5-storey Federal/Greek Revival transitional commercial-residential masonry block; planar rectangular massing with flat parapet roof, regular punched fenestration grid of rectangular 2-over-2 and 1-over-1 double-hung timber and aluminum-clad sashes, projecting steel commercial billboard framework affixed to roof deck.
  * *53–55 North Washington Street (Left Midground):* 4-storey commercial block with slate-shingled mansard roof and timber pedimented dormers.
  * *77–81 North Washington Street (Far Left Anchor):* 10-storey early-20th-century commercial daylight factory/warehouse loft; steel-framed with deep masonry piers, multi-bay Chicago-style broad industrial window spans, and brick-painted historic ghost signage ("Scotch 'n Sirloin") on the blind lateral exposure.
  * *North End Residential Fabric (Right Background):* 4-to-5-storey late-19th-century brick tenements; cast-iron external fire escape ladders, pronounced corbelled brick cornices, stone sills, and flat built-up bitumen roofs.
* **Planar Rectification:** Strict gravity-plumbed vertical wall planes; elimination of photogrammetric mesh distortion; crisp orthogonal alignment across street curbs, parapets, sill courses, and roof edges.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
* **Per-Structure Material Discrimination:**
  * *Corner & Tenement Blocks:* Unpainted and painted oxidized waterstruck red brick in common and running bonds; fine-grained bush-hammered granite base courses; cast-iron fire escapes with black protective enamel; smooth-dressed sandstone headers and lintels.
  * *Mid-Rise Industrial Warehouse (77 N Washington):* Deep wire-cut brown-red brick, concrete spandrels, painted commercial lettering on weathered exposed brickwork.
  * *Civil Infrastructure:* Milled Chelmsford granite curb stones, bituminous asphalt road decking, thermoplastic white and red-dyed transit bus lane surface coatings, cast-iron utility access covers, steel tubular traffic light cantilever mast arms.
* **Weathering & Pathology:** Atmospheric sulfur and particulate soiling concentrated beneath stone sills and projecting brick stringcourses; rain-wash clean paths along vertical mortar joints; localized rising damp and efflorescence along lower masonry base courses.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
* **Taxonomy:** *Gleditsia triacanthos* var. *inermis* (Thornless Honeylocust) and *Tilia cordata* (Littleleaf Linden) situated in sidewalk tree pits.
* **Canopy Phenology (Late August / August 30):** Full mature canopy density; dark green pinnately compound foliage showing subtle late-summer moisture stress; mature seed pods developing on honeylocust branches; complete foliage retention prior to autumn abscission.
