# Spatial Domain Analysis: 23 Dublin St Ln N, Edinburgh EH3 6NT, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean geology comprises Lower Carboniferous (Mississippian) sedimentary strata of the Strathclyde Group (Gullane and West Lothian Oil-Shale Formations), overlying basaltic and doleritic volcanic intrusions. Superficially blanketed by dense Devensian glacial till (boulder clay). Primary structural lithics derive from local historic quarries: high-durability Craigleith sandstone (pale buff to silver-grey quartz-arenite bonded by siliceous-calcareous matrix) and fine-bedded Hailes sandstone. Pervasive bedding utilizes traditional non-hydraulic to weakly hydraulic slaked lime mortar (*Calx*) tempered with well-graded river sand. Groundwater table is maintained via subterranean natural gravity slopes falling northward toward the Water of Leith basin.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Georgian Second New Town urban composition arranged around a geometric residential circus/square (Drummond Place). Continuous classical terrace blocks rising 3 storeys plus raised basement and attic dormers over rusticated/channelled ground floors. Strict neoclassical rhythm characterized by:
- Gravity-rectified vertical wall planes and continuous horizontal ashlar string courses, plat bands, and moulded eaves cornices.
- Regular repetitive fenestration grids comprising 6-over-6 and 12-over-12 double-hung timber sash-and-case windows set in cleanly dressed, margin-drafted embrasures.
- Symmetrical entrance bays featuring round-arched doorways with fanlights, rusticated voussoirs, and polished ashlar doorpieces.
- Pitched rooflines clad in Scotch slate / Ballachulish slate with lead flashings, punctuated by heavy rectangular ashlar chimney stacks with clay flues/pots.
- Lower perimeter protected by sunken basement areas with stone boundary retaining walls and wrought/cast-iron spear-head railings.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Primary Facades: Fine-grained Craigleith and Hailes pale buff ashlar masonry, planar-dressed, with rusticated ashlar channel coursing at ground tier. 
- Side Elevations & Rear Returns: Squared, snecked, and coursed rubble sandstone masonry.
- Roofscapes: Uniform grey Ballachulish metamorphic slate laid in diminishing courses, lead ridge roll caps, lead valleys, and cast-iron rainwater downpipes.
- Weathering / Patina: Localized historic hydrocarbon/coal-smoke soot staining (*Auld Reekie* sulfur encrustation) on sheltered cornices, granular surface rain-washing on exposed ashlar bands, minor biological lichen/green algae growth along damp basement retaining walls.
- Ground Planes: Caithness and York stone flagstone pedestrian pavements with whinstone/granite setts lining the perimeter roadway carriage routes.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Enclosed communal parkland (Drummond Place Gardens) dominated by mature temperate broadleaf trees:
- *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm), *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), *Tilia cordata* (Small-leaved Lime), and *Aesculus hippocastanum* (Horse Chestnut).
- Canopy Phenology (August 28): Climax late-summer vegetative state; heavy, fully expanded, deep-green leaf canopies with slight leaf-tip drying/chlorosis and early seed/samara maturation prior to autumn defoliation.
- Understory: Dense domestic shrub layer including *Taxus baccata* (Yew), *Prunus laurocerasus*, and manicured lawn turf.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient visual clutter is eliminated: zero moving or parked automobiles/vans, zero pedestrians, zero municipal waste containers, zero traffic bollards, and zero temporary signage. Permanent civil infrastructure retained includes perimeter cast-iron balustrades, stone dwarf walls, whinstone kerbs, and Edinburgh-pattern cast-iron streetlamp columns.
