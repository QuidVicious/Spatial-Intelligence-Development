# Spatial Domain Analysis: 15B London St, Edinburgh EH3 6LY, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- Subterranean Bedrock: Carboniferous Lower Oil-Shale formation (sandstone, siltstone, and mudstone sequences) overlain by stiff, overconsolidated Pleistocene glacial till (boulder clay).
- Dimension Stone Lithics: Fine-to-medium-grained Carboniferous Craigleith quartz arenite sandstone (pale buff to light olive-grey siliceous matrix) utilized for polished ashlar and decorative dressings; Hailes and Ravelston sandstone used in backing rubble and basements.
- Pavement Lithics: Quartz-dolerite (Midland Valley Whinstone) setts for carriageways; fissile Devonian Caithness flagstone paving slabs and polished granite kerbstones on footways.
- Mortar Chemistry: Historical hot-mixed slaked non-hydraulic to weakly hydraulic lime mortar ($Ca(OH)_2$) paired with coarse, sharp quartz river sand aggregate.
- Hydrogeology: Subsurface drainage gradients descending northward toward the Water of Leith basin.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- Typology: Edinburgh New Town (Reid and Sibbald Second New Town Plan, c. 1820s) Category A and B listed classical Georgian residential terraces.
- Massing & Storeys: Symmetrical 3-storey plus attic and sunken basement terraces flank the axial approach of London Street, curving into the drum-ended perimeter of Drummond Place.
- Facade Articulation: Channelled/rusticated ground floor ashlar, smooth droved and polished ashlar upper storeys, continuous horizontal platbands/string courses, moulded eaves cornices with blocking courses, and stone chimney stacks crowned with terracotta cylindrical pots.
- Fenestration: Vertical 6-over-6 and 2-over-2 timber double-hung sash-and-case windows set in clean, dressed stone reveals with stone cills.
- Roof Geometry: Pitched slate roofs clad in Scottish Ballachulish slate in diminishing courses, broken by lead-cheeked pitched dormer windows and stone skews.
- Boundaries: Sunken area lightwells enclosed by black painted cast-iron spearhead balustrades with urn-finial newels.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Primary Facade: Polished and droved Craigleith sandstone ashlar showing historic coal-smoke carbon patination and gypsum crusting under projecting cornices, alongside rain-washed buff quartz surfaces.
- Basement/Plinth: V-grooved rusticated sandstone ashlar with tool-dressed droved margins.
- Roofing: Blue-grey West Highland / Ballachulish metamorphic slate with hand-dressed edges, lead ridging, and zinc flashings.
- Metalwork: Wrought and cast iron decorative railings and historic lamp standards with matte black enamel protective finishes.
- Ground Surfaces: Dark grey basaltic whinstone road setts flanked by planar Caithness stone flags and profiled granite kerb edges.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- Drummond Place Enclosure Canopy: Dense mature urban canopy comprising *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm), *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), *Fagus sylvatica* (Common and Copper Beech), *Tilia × europaea* (Common Lime), and *Aesculus hippocastanum* (Horse Chestnut).
- Phenology (Late Summer / August): Dense, fully expanded deep-green mature canopies displaying high foliar density, continuous interlocking crowns, and subtle localized leaf chlorosis on lower branch tiers prior to autumnal senescence.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
- Complete elimination of all transient vehicles (parked vans, cars, traffic), wheelie bins, plastic street furniture, and pedestrian figures.
- Static retention of permanent civil fabric: stone paved setts, Caithness flagstones, stone kerbs, cast-iron area railings, and fixed street lamp columns.
