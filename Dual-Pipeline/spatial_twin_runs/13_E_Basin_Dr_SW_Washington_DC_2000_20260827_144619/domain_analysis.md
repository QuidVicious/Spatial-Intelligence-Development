# Spatial Domain Analysis: 13 E Basin Dr SW, Washington, DC 20004, USA

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The Thomas Jefferson Memorial rests on engineered reclaimed alluvial fill and dredge material overlying the crystalline bedrock of the Atlantic Fall Zone (predominantly Precambrian-to-Early Paleozoic metamorphic Sykesville Formation schist and gneiss). Due to deep estuarine sediments and variable unconsolidated fill adjacent to the Potomac River and the man-made Tidal Basin, the central monument is anchored by concrete-filled steel caissons and grade beams driven up to 120 feet down into solid bedrock. 

The primary exterior superstructure is built of dense, fine-grained Vermont Imperial Danby marble. The lower terrace, retaining perimeter, and primary plaza steps are assembled from coursed blocks of domestic granite and gray metamorphic stones, set with historical lime-portland mortar formulations engineered to resist high seasonal water tables and brackish ground moisture intrusion.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Designed by John Russell Pope (completed by Eggers & Higgins, dedicated 1943), the structure is a masterwork of Classical Revival/Neoclassical architecture modeled after the Roman Pantheon. 
- **Rotunda & Colonnade:** A circular peripteral colonnade composed of 26 fluted Ionic columns supporting a clean entablature and stepped circular attic base.
- **Dome:** A true hemispherical exterior dome constructed with stepped concentric rings culminating in a smooth marble capstone.
- **Portico:** An open pedimented Ionic portico facing north toward the Tidal Basin and the National Mall axis, framed with deep marble entablatures and unglazed openings leading into the open-air rotunda.
- **Planar Rectification:** All 26 perimeter columns, portico shafts, and stylobate walls are rectified to absolute plumb vertical, correcting photogrammetric raster distortions along the curved entablature, step tiers, and circular perimeter terraces.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Exterior Envelope:** Vermont Imperial Danby marble panels and fluted column drums.
- **Attic & Dome:** Concentric stepped marble courses with non-uniform mineral inclusions, fine micro-veining, and subtle calcite dissolution patina characteristic of eight decades of exposure to Mid-Atlantic atmospheric moisture and rainfall.
- **Plaza & Terraces:** Granite paving slabs, rusticated retaining granite blocks, and dressed stone copings showing natural iron-toning, localized efflorescence along step joints, and historic tidal water-wash patination near the seawall fringe.
- **Background Urban Fabric:** The distant National Mall perimeter features Indiana limestone and buff masonry office blocks of the Federal Triangle, with the obelisk of the Washington Monument formed of Maryland marble, Massachusetts granite, and Maine stones.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
The memorial is embedded within the cultivated historic parkland of West Potomac Park and the Tidal Basin:
- **Canopy Trees:** Specimen Yoshino Cherry trees (*Prunus × yedoensis*) and Kwanzan Cherry trees (*Prunus serrulata*) forming dense, spreading, multi-branched crowns along the shoreline.
- **Urban Forest & Overstory:** Mature American Elms (*Ulmus americana*), Red Oaks (*Quercus rubra*), Willow Oaks (*Quercus phellos*), and Sweetgums (*Liquidambar styraciflua*) with rich, organic, segmented deciduous canopies.
- **Groundcover:** Manicured *Poa pratensis* (Kentucky bluegrass) turf lawns, low evergreen shrubs (*Taxus*, *Ilex glabra*), and perimeter hedge beds bordering radial flagstone paths.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All temporary construction fences, site trailers, portable barriers, motor vehicles on adjacent loop drives, maintenance equipment, tourist clusters, and transient municipal clutter visible in photogrammetry tiles have been entirely purged. The monument, permanent curbstones, stone seawall, balustrades, cast-iron lampposts, manicured lawns, and mature trees remain completely clear, pristine, and static.
