# Spatial Domain Analysis: VXP5+5X Washington, DC, USA

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- **Subterranean Foundation**: Sited within the Atlantic Fall Line transition zone over alluvial and terrace deposits of the Potomac Formation, underlain at depth by Precambrian-to-Early Paleozoic metamorphic crystalline bedrock (Piedmont Province schists, bluestone gneiss, and Sykesville Formation meta-greywacke/granite gneiss).
- **Monument Masonry & Lithics**:
  - *Lower Shaft (up to ~152 ft / 46.3 m)*: Coarse-grained, high-calcite white marble quarried from Texas, Maryland.
  - *Mid-to-Upper Shaft & Pyramidion*: Fine-grained dolomitic white marble quarried from Beaver Dam / Cockeysville, Maryland, with intermittent courses of Massachusetts Sheffield marble.
  - *Sub-foundation & Inner Core*: Massive unreinforced Sykesville metamorphic gneiss, Potomac River bluestone, and Maine granite infill blocks bound by hydraulic lime and early Portland cements.
- **Urban Facade Lithics (Midground/Background)**: Salem/Bedford oolitic limestone from Indiana across the Federal Triangle complexes (Herbert C. Hoover Building, Ronald Reagan Building, EPA/Ariel Rios buildings), backed with structural brick and steel framing; low-iron granite curbstones along thoroughfares.
- **Hydrology**: High water table associated with the reclaimed marshlands of the historic Tiber Creek and Potomac tidal flats, controlled via integrated sub-surface drainage sumps and deep perimeter curtain walls.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- **The Washington Monument (Center)**:
  - Free-standing Egyptian-revival monumental obelisk measuring 554 ft 7+11/32 in (169.046 m) in absolute height, tapering smoothly upward to a pyramidal apex (pyramidion) capped with cast aluminum.
  - Rectified strictly to true plumb vertical; planarized 4-sided geometry without photogrammetric faceted polygon warp or surface mesh distortion.
  - Sits centered atop an elliptical graded earthen knoll surrounded by a circular granite-paved plaza and fifty evenly spaced perimeter flagpoles.
- **Federal Triangle & Downtown DC Fabric (Flanking/Background)**:
  - *Federal Triangle (Right/Mid-ground)*: Unified monumental Neoclassical and Beaux-Arts administrative massing. Consistent 6-to-7 storey street walls (~80–110 ft / 24–33 m streetwall height) with deep-set rusticated arcades, colonnaded loggias, continuous dentiled classical entablatures, and pitched hipped roofs surfaced in variegated terracotta barrel tiles.
  - *Downtown Core (Far Left/Upper Background)*: Strictly height-regulated (1910 Height of Buildings Act, 90–130 ft max) commercial and institutional mid-rises with flat parapets, mechanical penthouses, and punched-opening fenestration.
  - *Planar Alignment*: All rectilinear facades, fenestration bands, roof cornices, and street axes (Constitution Ave, 14th/15th/17th Streets) are fully plumbed and horizontally trued to planar architectural geometry.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Washington Monument Facade**:
  - Distinct horizontal color demarcation line at the 152-foot tier marking the 1854–1879 construction hiatus: lower section exhibits deeper atmospheric gray-tan oxidation and mineral patination, while the upper section presents lighter, tighter-grained dolomitic marble.
  - Subtle vertical wash lines, microscopic atmospheric gypsum deposition under shadow lines, and micro-fissure mortar repointing lines across ashlar joints.
- **Federal Triangle Buildings**:
  - Indiana Bedford limestone ashlar with soft matte warm-buff coloration, accented by historic carbon wash in sheltered window reveals and cornice soffits.
  - Roofs clad in unglazed terra-cotta Spanish and Mission s-tiles showing slight natural tonal variance from burnt ochre to deep terracotta red.
- **Ground Surfaces**:
  - Decomposed granite gravel and stabilized earth pedestrian walkways (warm tan-ochre); brushed gray granite plaza curbs; smooth asphalt road surfaces on surrounding perimeter parkways without transient debris.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- **Canopy Disambiguation**: Trees and vegetative masses are strictly differentiated from surrounding masonry and turf:
  - Mature *Quercus phellos* (Willow Oak) and *Quercus alba* (White Oak) forming dense, leafy horizontal crowns with distinct trunk-and-branch architecture along the edges of the Mall grounds.
  - Stately *Ulmus americana* (American Elm) lines bordering Constitution Avenue and peripheral parkways with characteristic high vase-shaped canopies.
  - Interspersed groupings of *Prunus serrulata* (Yoshino Cherry) and *Tilia cordata* (Littleleaf Linden) with individual multi-layered foliage clumps.
- **Groundcover**: Manicured, continuous cool-season turfgrass (*Festuca arundinacea* / Tall Fescue and Kentucky Bluegrass blend) across the Washington Monument knoll, exhibiting subtle mowing striations and natural topographical contouring.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
- Total elimination of all moving and parked automobiles, tour buses, delivery vans, construction vehicles, dumpsters, temporary chain-link fencing, pedestrian barricades, temporary event tents, and human figures.
- Absolute retention of permanent civil fabric: monolithic stone curbs, concrete-embedded bronze and granite boundary markers, cast-iron classical streetlamps, directional ground lanterns, and perimeter steel flagpoles around the monument base.
