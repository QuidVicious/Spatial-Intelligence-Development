# Spatial Domain Analysis: 12 Glenfinlas St, Edinburgh EH3 6AQ, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- **Bedrock Lithology & Stratigraphy:** Underlain by Carboniferous sedimentary successions of the Strathclyde Group (sandstones, siltstones, and mudstones) interleaved with localized volcanic dolerite/basalt intrusions of the Edinburgh Volcanic Complex.
- **Masonry Lithics:** Primary construction executed in indigenous Scottish Carboniferous Lower Oil-Shale Group cream-to-blonde sandstones, predominantly fine-grained quartzitic Craigleith and Hailes sandstone, alongside secondary Binny freestone.
- **Mortar Chemistry:** Traditional non-hydraulic and feebly hydraulic lime-sand bedding and pointing mortars, mixed with coarse river sand aggregate.
- **Subterranean Drainage Dynamics:** Natural westward-sloping terraced topography shedding surface runoff toward the Water of Leith basin, regulated by subterranean flagstone drainage vaults beneath street basements.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- **Typologies & Design:** Neoclassical civic and ecclesiastical architecture (Robert Reid, 1811–1814, adapting Robert Adam's master plan) centered around West Register House (former St George's Church), terminating the western axis of Charlotte Square. Flanked by symmetrical 3-to-4 storey classical Georgian residential terraces.
- **Massing & Storey Counts:** Central monumental structure features a Greek cross plan on a square base, 5 internal floor plates, an Ionic portico, square corner pavilions with attic storeys, and a 160 ft (49 m) high peristyle drum surmounted by a ribbed dome and lantern cupola. Surrounding residential blocks maintain 3 principal storeys over sunken basement lightwells with rusticated ground floors.
- **Roof Geometry:** Heavy stepped lead and slate hip roofs, segmented lead rolls, copper-clad central dome with standing seams, and robust stone ashlar chimneystacks with ceramic chimney cans.
- **Fenestration & Planar Rectification:** Strict vertical wall alignments, plumb classical columns, crisp stone string courses, rectangular timber sash-and-case windows (six-over-six and twelve-over-twelve), Venetian and Diocletian upper apertures, and circular oculi within pavilion panels.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Facades & Envelopes:** Polished ashlar cream sandstone blocks, channel-rusticated basement and ground floors, smooth-faced upper storeys, carved stone cornices, and fluted peristyle pilasters.
- **Roofing & Dome:** Ribbed sheet copper displaying an aged verdigris/oxidized green patina on the dome, dark Scottish Ballachulish slate on pitched roofs, and weathered sheet lead on hips, valleys, and portico copings.
- **Weathering & Environmental Patina:** Micro-climate marine moisture and historical atmospheric coal smoke deposit streaks (gypsum crusting and black carbon patina in sheltered recesses), granular surface exfoliation on exposed sandstone cornices, and light algal staining along stone plinths and water runoffs.
- **Ironwork:** Wrought and cast iron spearhead railings guarding the sunken lightwells, painted black with slight base oxidation.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- **Urban Canopy & Botanical Species:** Mature deciduous parkland specimens inside Charlotte Square Gardens and street borders, including Common Lime (*Tilia x europaea*), European Beech (*Fagus sylvatica*), Sycamore (*Acer pseudoplatanus*), and English Oak (*Quercus robur*).
- **Foliage Characteristics:** Full, dense leafy canopies with natural botanical branch bifurcations, distinct crown volumes, and organic separation from adjacent stone parapets and paving.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
- **Exclusions:** Total removal of all transient motor vehicles (vans, cars, buses), moving or static pedestrians, plastic wheelie bins, dumpsters, road construction cones, temporary barricades, and ephemeral signage.
- **Retained Civil Fixtures:** Permanent dressed granite curbs, stone gutter courses, asphalt carriage surfaces, historic cast iron lampposts, flagstone perimeter sidewalks, and iron boundary railings.
