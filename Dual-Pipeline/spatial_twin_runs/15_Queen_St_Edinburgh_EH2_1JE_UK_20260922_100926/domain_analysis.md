# Spatial Domain Analysis: 15 Queen St, Edinburgh EH2 1JE, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean strata comprise Carboniferous sedimentary bedrock of the Inverclyde and Strathclyde Groups, overlain by glacial till. Primary lithic building material is pale-grey to buff Carboniferous Craigleith and Hailes sandstone, bonded with traditional hydraulic lime mortar.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Continuous Georgian neoclassical terraced tenements and townhouses (circa 1780–1820). Linear facade planes feature 3 to 4 storeys over sunken basement lightwells bridged by stone plinths. Vertical load-bearing walls rectified to plumb axes. Pitched roofs clad in Scottish slate with regular gabled and canted dormer projections, flanked by shared masonry chimney stacks with terracotta pots. Regular fenestration grid of timber-framed sash-and-case windows (primarily 6-over-6 and 2-over-2 patterns).

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Facades: Tooled, polished, and rusticated Craigleith ashlar sandstone displaying historic carbon soot patination, biocide weathering, and water run-off staining.
- Roofing: Dark grey Ballachulish slate, lead valley gutters, zinc flashing, and dressed stone skews.
- Ironwork: Cast-iron spearhead railings and basement drop balustrades.
- Ground: Mastic asphalt roadway with painted junction markings, granite kerbstones, and Yorkstone/Caithness stone slab pedestrian sidewalks.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Mature urban tree boundary along Queen Street Gardens: *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), *Tilia × europaea* (Common Lime), and *Ulmus procera* (English Elm). Canopy state reflects late September phenology, exhibiting dense crown volumes with early autumnal chlorosis and localized yellow-green foliage transitions.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient vehicles, moving traffic, parked cars, pedestrians, temporary signage, and street waste are completely excised. Structural civil fixtures, iron railings, streetlamps, and stone retaining kerbs are preserved.
