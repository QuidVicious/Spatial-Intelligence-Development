# Spatial Domain Analysis: 35A London St, Edinburgh EH3 6LY, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- Bedrock: Carboniferous sedimentary strata of the Strathclyde and Inverclyde Groups, primarily Gullane Formation sandstone and siltstone, overlain by dense Devensian glacial till and boulder clay.
- Building Lithics: High-quartz blonde and buff Craigleith and Hailes sandstones; medium-to-fine-grained carboniferous arenite exhibiting high compressive strength and low permeability. Paving composed of split Caithness flagstone footways and Scottish whinstone (quartz-dolerite) / Mountsorrel granite kerbstones and carriageway setts.
- Mortar & Binding: Traditional non-hydraulic to weakly hydraulic slaked lime mortar (calcium hydroxide) mixed with sharp local river sand and crushed crushed sandstone aggregate.
- Hydrology & Subterranean Drainage: Gradual westward subterranean hydraulic gradient descending toward the Water of Leith basin; engineered subterranean barrel-vaulted cellars beneath pedestrian pavements with dedicated sandstone drainage gullies.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- Typology: Edinburgh New Town Northern Extension (Reid & Sibbald masterplan, c. 1802–1825) late Georgian / Regency neoclassical residential terraces and tenements.
- Massing & Storeys: Uniform 3-storey to 4-storey plus attic and sunken basement continuous street walls terminating at Drummond Place circus.
- Facade Organization: Strictly rectified planar ashlar facades; channelled rustication at ground level, droved ashlar at basement lightwells, and smooth-polished ashlar across upper piano nobile and attic levels; defined continuous horizontal belt courses, moulded stringcourses, and heavy eaves cornices.
- Roofscape: Double-pitch slate roofs with lead ridges and flashing; symmetrical transverse stone chimney stacks surmounted by cylindrical terracotta chimney pots; dressed stone parapets and skew copes.
- Fenestration: Rectilinear grid of vertical timber sash-and-case windows (predominantly 6-over-6 and 2-over-2 glazing bar configurations), deep ashlar reveals, moulded architraves, and cast-iron drop-down safety grilles/railing enclosures.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Primary Facades: Hewn Craigleith/Hailes buff-yellow sandstone ashlar blocks with fine-jointed lime pointing; localized industrial coal-smoke carbon crusting, soot encrustation across cornices, and selective rain-wash channeling revealing bright silica substrates.
- Basements & Rear Elevations: Stugged and coursed rubble sandstone masonry, dressed quoins, cast-iron area railings with spear-head finials, and painted timber front doors with glazed fanlights.
- Roof Coverings: Ballachulish and Welsh grey-blue slates laid in diminishing courses; cast lead gutters, zinc valleys, and cast-iron rainwater downpipes fixed to masonry reveals.
- Street Furniture & Pavements: Caithness sandstone slab walkways, dressed granite kerbs, cast-iron fluted gas-pattern street lamps, and cast-iron pavement cellar plates.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- Botanical Classification: Urban parkland canopy in Drummond Place Gardens dominated by *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore maple), *Ulmus glabra* (Wych elm), *Fagus sylvatica* (Common beech), *Platanus × acerifolia* (London plane), and *Tilia × europaea* (Common lime).
- Phenology (November 11): Advanced late-autumn defoliation state (approximately 75–85% leaf loss); exposed dendritic branch architecture, sparse clinging russet, dull amber, and bronze foliage across inner crowns; dense understory carpets of fallen decaying leaf litter across lawn turf.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All vehicular traffic, parallel parked automobiles, transient municipal bins, commercial signs, and pedestrians are fully purged. Paved roadways, Caithness flagstone footways, cast-iron railings, stone staircases, fixed heritage lamp columns, and static architectural fabric remain preserved in uncontaminated static alignment.
