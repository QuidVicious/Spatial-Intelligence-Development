# Spatial Domain Analysis: 4 Ainslie Pl, Edinburgh EH3 6AR, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The subterranean geology consists of the Lower Carboniferous Gullane and Wardie Shales of the Strathclyde Group, overlain by dense, poorly drained glacial lodgement till perched above the deep gorge of the Water of Leith. The primary structural masonry is premium pale grey-to-buff Craigleith sandstone (quartz-arenite with siliceous and calcareous matrix), celebrated for its low porosity and structural compressive strength. Lime mortar joints consist of hot-mixed calcitic non-hydraulic lime and river sand aggregate. Bedrock gradient and natural groundwater drain northwestward toward the Water of Leith via sub-pavement subterranean cellar drainage systems.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Designed by James Gillespie Graham (Moray Feu scheme, 1822–1830), the structure is a Grade A listed neoclassical curved terrace/oval crescent. 
- **Typology & Storeys:** 3-storey, attic, and raised basement residential palace block with advanced multi-bay terminal and central pavilions. 
- **Planar Rectification & Load Bearing:** Facades follow a geometrically true concave arc with plumb vertical ashlar lines. Rusticated ground floors feature V-jointed channel courses. Floor plates, continuous cill courses, entablatures, and coped blocking courses are rigorously level.
- **Roof Geometry:** Sharp, dual-pitched Scottish M-roof profiles and hipped pavilion projections clad in authentic Welsh and West Highland (Easdale/Ballachulish) grey-black slates, punctured by lead-flashed skylights, conical cupolas, and rectilinear ashlar chimney stacks with terracotta cylindrical flues.
- **Fenestration:** Timber sliding sash and case windows (principally 12-pane configurations) set into deep rectangular reveals with continuous stone architraves and classical pedimented/corniced headers at the piano nobile.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Ashlar Facades:** Finely droved and polished Craigleith sandstone ashlar showing two centuries of authentic post-industrial Scottish climatic patina: dark carboniferous soot deposition sheltered beneath projecting cornices, water-wash channels across outer stone faces, and subtle mineral oxidation.
- **Roofs & Chimneys:** Diminishing courses of metamorphic grey roofing slate, rolled sheet-lead valleys, ridging, and zinc flashings weathered to a matte chalky grey. Broached ashlar chimneys with salt-efflorescence and sooted clay chimney pots.
- **Basements & Streetworks:** Whinstone and dark basalt sett (cobblestone) pavement surfaces with granite kerbing; hand-forged black painted cast-iron spearhead railings and basement drop-areas (areas oversailing flagged stone platts).

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
To the rear and along the central crescent garden, mature deciduous canopy trees characteristic of the Edinburgh urban landscape: mature Common Beech (*Fagus sylvatica*), Sycamore (*Acer pseudoplatanus*), Wych Elm (*Ulmus glabra*), and Lime trees (*Tilia cordata*). Tree canopies are fully disambiguated from built structures, exhibiting organic, branching leaf mass with textured bark, dappled foliage, and underlying turf borders.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All modern transient clutter—including all parked and moving cars, tire tracks, road signs, dumpsters, pedestrians, and temporary artifacts—has been completely purged. The scene retains only fixed, historical civic elements: hand-laid whinstone street setts, tooled sandstone curbs, wrought/cast-iron railings, cast-iron lamp standards with glass lanterns, and stone entrance bridge platts.
