# Spatial Domain Analysis: 2436 Rayburn House Office Bldg, Washington, DC 20004, USA

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The site sits upon Jenkins Hill (Capitol Hill), an elevated plateau near the Atlantic Seaboard Fall Line transition between the crystalline bedrock of the Piedmont Province (schists and gneisses of the Potomac Terrane) and unconsolidated Cretaceous-Tertiary sedimentary strata (sand, gravel, and silt clay lenses of the Potomac Group). Subterranean drainage flows westward towards the historic Tiber Creek canal and the Potomac River basin. 

The primary structural lithics include:
1. **Original Central Core:** Lower Cretaceous Aquia Creek sandstone (arkosic freestone from Government Island, Stafford County, Virginia), characterized by high quartz-feldspar content and porous matrix.
2. **Extensions & Wings (House & Senate):** High-density, fine-grained white Lee marble (Berkshire County, Massachusetts) on the exterior elevations, supported by monolithic fluted columns of coarse crystalline Cockeysville marble (Baltimore County, Maryland).
3. **East Front Extension (1958–1962):** Georgia Cherokee white crystalline marble.
4. **Olmsted Terraces (West Front):** Dover marble and granite foundations.
5. **Plaza Hardscape:** Thermal-finished Rockville and Chelmsford granites paired with quartzite aggregate pavers. Mortar systems transition from historical slaked quicklime-sand mixes to contemporary breathable non-hydraulic lime and ASTM C270 Type N preservation mortars.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- **Typology:** Neoclassical / American Palladian monumental civic architecture with Beaux-Arts urbanist landscape integration designed by Frederick Law Olmsted.
- **Center Mass:** Symmetrical tripartite composition. Central rotunda surmounted by Thomas U. Walter's double-shelled cast-iron dome (completed 1866) terminating in the Tholos lantern and Thomas Crawford's bronze *Statue of Freedom*, rising 88 meters (288 ft) above the grade line.
- **Wings:** Two major flanking 3-storey axial wings—the Senate Wing (North/left in vertical axis) and the House of Representatives Wing (South/right in vertical axis)—featuring Corinthian colonnaded porticos, deep-set pedimented fenestration, denticulated modillion cornices, and flat low-pitch copper roofs.
- **West Front (Left of Frame):** Olmsted marble terraces with dual sweeping semicircular grand stairways and fountain basins stepping down toward the National Mall.
- **East Front Plaza (Right of Frame):** Expansive planar paved esplanade concealing the subterranean 3-level, 54,000 m² Capitol Visitor Center (CVC), punctuated by two flush rectangular glass skylights and perimeter ventilation lantern grilles.
- **Flanking Monuments:** Supreme Court (northeast / top right) in Corinthian Classical white Vermont Imperial Danby marble; Library of Congress Thomas Jefferson Building (southeast / bottom right) in Italian Renaissance granite.
- **Planar Rectification:** All vertical colonnades, pilasters, balustrades, and dome drums are plumbed strictly to gravity vertical; all rooftop parapets, balustrades, and terrace retaining walls are rectified into sharp horizontal planes.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Dome & Tholos:** Cast and wrought iron with multi-coat lead-free elastomeric mineral paint in uniform off-white/monumental pearl, displaying subtle micro-shading in the fluting, pilaster capitals, and coffered ribs.
- **Roof Plates:** Standing-seam rolled copper sheets across the House, Senate, and connecting corridors, displaying advanced natural verdigris patina (cupric carbonate / brochantite) in vibrant turquoise-green, with darker weathering along standing drip edges and flashing.
- **Façade Stone:** Lee and Georgia marble ashlar blocks showing sharp, plumb-jointed coursing; faint historic atmospheric gypsum wash along recessed undersides of entablatures; pristine water-shedding surfaces on projecting cornices.
- **Grounds Hardscape:** Crushed tan pea-gravel pedestrian paths bordered by dressed granite curbing; asphalt perimeter roadways; charcoal-hued honed granite bollards and security barriers.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
The grounds form the historic Capitol Arboretum (Olmsted landscape plan):
- **Canopy Trees:** Mature American Elms (*Ulmus americana*), Northern Red Oaks (*Quercus rubra*), White Oaks (*Quercus alba*), London Plane Trees (*Platanus × acerifolia*), and Ginkgo Biloba (*Ginkgo biloba*).
- **Understory & Specimen Flora:** Saucer Magnolias (*Magnolia soulangeana*), American Hollies (*Ilex opaca*), Flowering Dogwoods (*Cornus florida*), and dense taxus/boxwood hedge borders framing formal lawns.
- **Turfgrass:** High-traffic sports/monumental turf blend of Kentucky Bluegrass (*Poa pratensis*) and Perennial Ryegrass (*Lolium perenne*), exhibiting manicured mowed stripes and deep emerald-to-olive hydration variations.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All non-permanent transient elements have been systematically eliminated from the capture:
- **Vehicles:** Zero tour buses, staff sedans, police cruisers, maintenance vans, or service utility vehicles on the plaza, perimeter drives, or Constitution/Independence avenues.
- **Pedestrians:** Zero tourists, security personnel, or foot traffic across the East Front Plaza, Olmsted terraces, or pedestrian walkways.
- **Temporary Clutter:** Zero temporary barricades, trash receptacles, construction dumpsters, traffic cones, or signage stands.
- **Preserved Civil Infrastructure:** Fixed cast-iron historic lampposts, recessed security bollard arrays, stone retaining balustrades, bronze plaza flagpoles, and integrated subterranean CVC skylight assemblies remain intact.
