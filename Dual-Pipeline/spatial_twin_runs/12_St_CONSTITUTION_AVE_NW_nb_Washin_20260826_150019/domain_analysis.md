# Spatial Domain Analysis: 12 St & CONSTITUTION AVE NW nb, Washington, DC 20004, USA

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The subterranean substrate beneath this sector of the National Mall sits along the Atlantic Coastal Plain-Piedmont Fall Line boundary, underlain by Quaternary alluvial silt, sand, gravel, and saturated historical marshlands shaped by the buried paleo-channel of Tiber Creek. Bedrock at depth consists of late Proterozoic to early Paleozoic crystalline schist and Potomac terrane gneiss. 
- **Washington Monument:** Load-bearing masonry comprises an exterior of calcitic Cockeysville/Texas Maryland marble and Massachusetts Lee marble, backed by blue gneiss rubble and massive granite blocks set in hydraulic lime mortar and Portland cement additions. The distinctive tonal shift at the 152-foot datum marks the 1854–1879 quarry change from Symington quarry marble to Beaver Dam/Cockeysville marble.
- **Hardscape & Plinths:** Mesabi Black granite curbings, step units, and retaining seat walls; compacted stabilized gravel and decomposed granite pedestrian paths across the Monument knoll. Subterranean drainage relies on active dewatering sumps and historic groundwater management systems.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- **Washington Monument (Center):** Egyptian-revival/neoclassical obelisk rising to 555 feet 5⅛ inches (169.3 m). Strict plumb vertical massing tapering from 15-foot thick walls at the base to 18 inches beneath the pyramidal aluminum-capped apex. Fifty-six bronze-mounted perimeter flagpoles encircle the granite-edged circular plaza at the summit of the earthwork knoll.
- **National Museum of African American History and Culture (Foreground Right):** Three-tiered inverted trapezoidal corona envelope inspired by West African Yoruban sculpture capitals. Supported internally on four massive reinforced concrete cores, featuring an asymmetrical projecting ground-level steel porch canopy along the southern facade and an expansive flat roof arrayed with solar photovoltaic panels and high-albedo gravel ballast.
- **Surrounding Monumental Grid & Flanking Structures:**
  - *Left Foreground:* Department of Agriculture / Smithsonian Castle perimeter greenery.
  - *Right Mid-ground:* Federal Triangle neoclassical government offices (Department of Commerce / Herbert C. Hoover Building) featuring limestone trabeated pilasters, red clay barrel-tile mansard profiles, and deep punched rectangular windows.
  - *Distance / Axis:* Axial vista extending west along the Reflecting Pool toward the Lincoln Memorial (Doric peristyle in Colorado Yule marble), crossing the Arlington Memorial Bridge over the Potomac River toward the Virginia ridgelines.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Monument Ashlar:** Crisp, fine-grained white Maryland marble at the base lower 150 feet, transitioning into a slightly darker, grayish-tan weathered marble upper shaft. Natural ambient weathering shows faint vertical rainwater streaking, micro-scale gypsum deposition in recessed mortar seams, and mineral oxidation consistent with mid-Atlantic temperate humidity.
- **NMAAHC Facade:** 3,600 intricate filigree panels of cast aluminum coated in a rich, multi-layered warm bronze/copper-tinted PVDF finish, backed by low-iron architectural curtain wall glazing. Dark non-reflective glass and matte-honed Mesabi black granite base course.
- **Asphalt & Pavement:** Non-reflective dark gray bitumen roadways (Constitution Avenue NW, 14th Street NW, 15th Street NW) with clean thermoset white lane striping, unweathered granite curbs, and porous beige decomposed granite radial walkway ribbons.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Lush mid-Atlantic parkland ecology across the National Mall grounds and West Potomac Park:
- Mature double-avenue plantings of American Elm (*Ulmus americana*) lining the Mall greensward.
- Canopy groves of London Plane (*Platanus occidentalis × acerifolia*), Pin Oak (*Quercus palustris*), Willow Oak (*Quercus phellos*), and Sweetgum (*Liquidambar styraciflua*) surrounding the Washington Monument grounds.
- Yoshino Cherry (*Prunus × yedoensis*) encircling the Tidal Basin visible to the south.
- Well-maintained, uniform fescue-bluegrass turf lawns displaying clean mechanical mowing striations.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient visual noise has been eliminated:
- Zero pedestrians, tourists, or foot traffic across the monument grounds, walkways, and museum plaza.
- Zero passenger cars, tour buses, transit shuttles, or maintenance vehicles on Constitution Ave NW, 14th St NW, 15th St NW, or perimeter access loops.
- Removal of all temporary event fencing, sandwich boards, dumpsters, barricades, and plastic waste bins.
- Complete preservation of static civil fixtures: bronze flagpoles, granite curbs, permanent bronze interpretive markers, and historic cast-iron street lighting standards.
