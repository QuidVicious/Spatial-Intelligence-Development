# Spatial Domain Analysis: 9 Ainslie Pl, Edinburgh EH3 6AT, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- **Bedrock Stratigraphy:** Carboniferous sedimentary strata of the Strathclyde Group (Gullane Formation), characterized by interbedded sandstones, siltstones, and carbonaceous mudstones overlying regional volcanic intrusions.
- **Lithic Masonry:** Primary construction in Lower Carboniferous Craigleith Sandstone—a tightly silica-cemented, pale cream-to-buff quartz arenite renowned for compressive strength; secondary foundation/plinth courses in Hailes Sandstone.
- **Mortar Chemistry:** Traditional hot-mixed lime mortar utilizing non-hydraulic local calcined limestone aggregate and sharp river sand, allowing high vapor permeability through tight ashlar joints.
- **Subterranean Hydrology:** Well-drained glacial lodgement till over sloping terrain descending northwest toward the Water of Leith drainage basin.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- **Typology & Provenance:** Neoclassical Georgian residential circus block, designed by architect James Gillespie Graham (1822) as part of the Moray Estate development.
- **Storeys & Massing:** Symmetrical curved palace-front terrace consisting of a sunken basement, rusticated principal ground floor, piano nobile first floor, second floor, and an attic/blocking course storey. Advanced terminal and central pavilions break the curved facade plane.
- **Vertical & Planar Alignment:** Rectified vertical load-bearing lines plumbed to true gravity vertical. Planar ashlar walls flattened from 3D scan distortions; horizontal datums restored along continuous stringcourses, sill bands, and projecting classical entablatures.
- **Fenestration & Detailing:** Regular grid of timber sash-and-case windows (predominantly 6-over-6 and 12-pane configurations) recessed within dressed stone reveals. Piano nobile windows feature moulded architraves with projecting cornices. Cast-iron spearhead balustrades enclose sunken area wells crossed by oversailing ashlar entrance platts. Pitched slate roofs with dressed ashlar chimneystacks and cylindrical clay chimney pots.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Masonry Treatment:** Polished Craigleith ashlar with fine-tooled ashlar finishes on upper floors; deep V-jointed channelled rustication across the principal floor level.
- **Roofing & Envelope:** Scottish Ballachulish and Welsh Penrhyn dark blue-grey slate tiles laid in diminishing courses, lead valleys, zinc/lead flashing, cast-iron rainwater goods with rectangular hopper heads.
- **Environmental Pathology:** Stratified historical carbon and sulfur encrustation (gypsum black crusts) in sheltered lintel recesses and cornice undersides; surface rain-wash leaching across exposed ashlar faces; minor iron oxide staining and basal biological damp patina along plinth courses.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- **Botanical Identity:** Mature urban garden specimens within Ainslie Place Gardens featuring *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), *Tilia × europaea* (Common Lime), and *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm).
- **Date-Grounded Phenology (September 3):** Full late-summer canopy volume with early transitional phenology; mature deep-green broadleaf foliage exhibiting incipient margins of chlorotic yellowing on upper exposed crowns; hanging clusters of dry samaras/keys; dense, manicured understory turf with perennial border plantings.
