# Spatial Domain Analysis: 1501 Maine Ave SW, Washington, DC 20024, USA

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean substrate situated along the Atlantic Seaboard Fall Zone transition between Piedmont crystalline metamorphic basement (Potomac Formation crystalline schist and gneiss) and unconsolidated Atlantic Coastal Plain Quaternary alluvial deposits (interbedded gravels, sands, silts, and soft plastic Potomac clays). Structural load is anchored by a massive reinforced concrete sub-foundation underpinning system (installed 1879–1880) resting upon compact alluvial gravel and clay strata. Lithic assembly of the central obelisk comprises lower-tier coarse-grained calcite marble from Beaver Dam Quarry (Cockeysville, Maryland) with Potomac gneiss rubblestone backing, transitioning above the 152-foot (46 m) construction hiatus to fine-grained dolomitic marble from Sheffield, Massachusetts, and upper courses of Cockeysville marble backed by biotite granite coursing from Maine and Massachusetts. Surrounding regional government edifices utilize Mississippian Salem calcarenite (Indiana limestone) over Milford pink granite plinths. Mortar chemistry shifts from early slaked lime-sand mortars at the lower shaft to high-strength unslaked hydraulic cement and Portland cement mortar in the upper tiers.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Central landmark: hollow masonry Egyptian revival obelisk rising 555 feet 5⅛ inches (169.29 m) from a 55-foot (16.8 m) square base with a 1:48 shaft taper, terminating in an 8.9-meter-tall pyramidion (1:2 proportion) crowned with a cast aluminum apex. Plumb lines strictly rectified to true gravity vertical; planar ashlar courses aligned horizontally. The circular plaza deck features an inner perimeter of 56 polished stainless steel flagpoles set in concentric granite paving bands. Northeast quadrant: National Museum of African American History and Culture (NMAAHC), a modern inverted tripartite ziggurat structure featuring three tiers of upward-flaring corona canopies suspended over an inverted pyramid structural steel diagrid with glazed ground-level curtain wall. East/Northeast perimeter: Federal Triangle complex (Herbert C. Hoover Building / U.S. Department of Commerce), an 8-storey Neoclassical/Beaux-Arts civic mega-structure featuring rusticated base arcades, Doric colonnades along continuous 1,000-foot facades, deeply recessed multi-pane double-hung fenestration grids, and red terracotta Spanish-barrel roof pantiles punctuated by classical pedimented dormers. North axis: symmetrical perimeter paths circling the southern verge of The Ellipse.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Washington Monument: Fine-dressed planar ashlar marble blocks with tight ⅛-inch (3 mm) raked joints; distinct color variation visible at 152-foot line (warmer cream-tan weathered lower Cockeysville marble vs. lighter gray-white upper Sheffield/Beaver Dam marble); cast aluminum apex cap; concentric rings of split-face and honed gray granite pavers at the monument base.
- NMAAHC: 3,600 bronze-finished cast-aluminum filigree fretwork lattice panels with porous historic African American ironwork patterning; low-iron clear structural glazing; dark granite site walls and poured concrete foundation bulkheads.
- Federal Triangle (Hoover Building): Buff-to-light-gray Indiana oolitic limestone ashlar with horizontal rustication grooves at the ground tier; fluted monumental limestone columns; unglazed high-fired terracotta roof tiles; bronze spandrel panels and patinated bronze entrance grills.
- Ground Plane: Decomposed red-tan granite gravel pedestrian pathways intersecting manicured Kentucky bluegrass and tall fescue turf; poured asphalt roadways flanked by straight-edge sawn granite kerbstones.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Late-summer temperate urban parkland canopy (September 4 phenology):
- *Ulmus americana* (American elm): Mature specimens lining flanking avenues and mall borders with expansive vase-shaped canopies; deep forest-green foliage with localized early-margin chlorosis.
- *Quercus phellos* (Willow oak) and *Quercus palustris* (Pin oak): Dense, rounded to conical crowns with dark green, glossy, mature late-season foliage.
- *Platanus × acerifolia* (London plane) and *Liriodendron tulipifera* (Tulip tree): Broad spreading canopies with multi-lobed leaves showing early autumn stiffness and patchy pale bronzing.
- *Prunus × yedoensis* (Yoshino cherry): Rounded spreading sub-canopy groves exhibiting full, dark green mature leaves prior to autumn senescence.
Turf zones exhibit deep green irrigated turfgrass with dry pedestrian trail compression paths.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient visual noise is expunged: zero vehicular traffic, zero tour buses, zero pedestrians, zero crowd queues, zero security barricades, zero trash receptacles, and zero temporary construction sheds. Permanent civic fixtures (granite kerbs, bronze boundary markers, iron perimeter bollards, fixed streetlamps, and flagpole rings) remain fully intact and planar-rectified.
