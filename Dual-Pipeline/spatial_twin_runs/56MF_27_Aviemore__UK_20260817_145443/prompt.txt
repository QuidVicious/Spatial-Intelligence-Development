High-altitude cinematic 35mm film still of a traditional Scottish Highland steading and cottage complex near Aviemore in the Spey Valley. The camera looks down at a -29° oblique pitch from 360 meters altitude, framed toward heading 232.9° southwest across rolling pastureland. The primary longhouse cottage features crisp, plumb, whitewashed harled rubble stone walls, pitched dark Ballachulish slate roofing, and distinctive pitched wallhead dormers with stone chimneys. Adjacent agrarian barns show weathered corrugated metal roof panels and timber siding. 

A compacted dirt and crushed gravel track curves past the front forecourt, cutting through naturalized green pasture turf and rough moorland grass over ancient Cairngorm glacial till geology. The scene is illuminated by crisp, pure Highland afternoon daylight with realistic soft-edged directional shadows cast toward the northeast, fully stripped of low-resolution photogrammetric blur and geometric melt. Sharp architectural lineation, natural highland atmosphere, realistic ground texture depth, captured on Kodachrome 64, Leica Summilux 50mm f/1.4 lens, natural color grading, hyper-detailed environmental realism.
