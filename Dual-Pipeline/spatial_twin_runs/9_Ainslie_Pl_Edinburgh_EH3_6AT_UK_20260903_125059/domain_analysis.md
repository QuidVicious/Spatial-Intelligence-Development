# Spatial Domain Analysis: 9 Ainslie Pl, Edinburgh EH3 6AT, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The subterranean geology of Edinburgh’s New Town rests upon Lower Carboniferous sedimentary bedrock of the Strathclyde Group (Gullane and Wardie Formations), characterised by alternating siltstones, mudstones, and thick-bedded quartz-arenite sandstones. The superstructures are constructed primarily of pale buff-grey Carboniferous Craigleith sandstone, supplemented by laminated Hailes sandstone. Subterranean basement lightwells and foundation retaining walls are bedded in traditional hot-mixed non-hydraulic to weakly hydraulic slaked lime mortar (calcium hydroxide with local river sand aggregate). Subsurface drainage directs groundwater runoff downslope towards the Water of Leith via natural glacial till and engineered subterranean brick vaulted cellars.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
A classical Georgian neoclassical palace-fronted crescent designed by architect James Gillespie Graham in 1822. The sweeping curved terrace (Nos 1–15 Ainslie Place) comprises a symmetrical composition of 3 storeys over a rusticated principal floor and sunken basement, crowned by an attic storey and slate-clad mansard/piend roofs with ashlar chimney stacks. The primary facade features a prominent 9-bay advanced central pavilion flanked by 15-bay linking terraces and 4-bay terminal pavilions. Fenestration follows a strict Georgian grid of timber sash-and-case windows (predominantly 6-over-6 on lower floors and 3-over-3 at the attic level) recessed within crisp stone reveals. All mesh-warped verticals are rectified to 90° gravity plumb; the faceted curvature of the street frontage is smoothed to an authentic continuous octagonal circus arc.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Principal Facades:** Fine-grained, droved, and ashlar-polished Craigleith quartz-arenite sandstone with sharp arrises; heavy V-jointed rustication across the ground floor course.
- **Mouldings & Entablature:** Continuous projecting stone plat bands, moulded sill courses, running dentilled cornice, and solid ashlar blocking course.
- **Basement & Area Steps:** Polished sandstone flying steps (entrance platts) oversailing the open basement lightwells, guarded by cast-iron lance-headed spear balustrades and urn finials.
- **Weathering & Pathology:** Incipient particulate soot encrustation in sheltered soffits and cornice drip lines, micro-biological green algae colonisation at stone dampcourses, and rain-washed honey-grey exfoliation on exposed planar ashlar blocks.
- **Streetscape:** Curving whinstone (basalt) kerbs and modern tarmacadam road surfaces abutting historic Yorkstone and sandstone flagstone footways.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
The central communal pleasure grounds (Ainslie Place Gardens) feature mature broadleaf deciduous specimens:
- *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore maple) and *Tilia × europaea* (Common lime): Full, dense late-summer canopies displaying dark forest green foliage with early autumn margin chlorosis, dense branch massing, and lichen-mottled bark.
- *Ulmus glabra* (Wych elm) and *Prunus spinosa* understory: Full leafy crown structure consistent with early September phenology, maintaining canopy density prior to autumn leaf drop.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All photogrammetry mesh noise, transient street clutter, traffic furniture, garbage bins, pedestrians, and parked or moving motor vehicles have been completely purged. The spatial plane preserves only the permanent architectural masonry, cast-iron railings, sandstone steps, stone kerbs, and static botanical elements of the central gardens.
