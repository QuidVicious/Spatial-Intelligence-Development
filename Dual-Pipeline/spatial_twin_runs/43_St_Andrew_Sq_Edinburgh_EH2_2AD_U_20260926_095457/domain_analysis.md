# Spatial Domain Analysis: 43 St Andrew Sq, Edinburgh EH2 2AD, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Bedrock consists of Lower Carboniferous sedimentary sequences (Inverclyde and Strathclyde Group sandstones, siltstones, and mudstones) intruded by the volcanic dolerite and basalt sills forming the Arthur's Seat volcanic complex and Salisbury Crags visible in the background. Primary historic masonry lithic is fine-grained, silica-cemented Craigleith Carboniferous quartz-arenite sandstone, complemented by Binny and Hailes sandstones. Bedding mortar is traditional slaked lime and hydraulic lime with quartz aggregate. Subterranean hydrological movement drains eastward over dense glacial till toward the Firth of Forth basin.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Centerpiece is the Melville Monument (1821–1827): a 42-metre fluted Roman Doric freestone column elevated on a high stepped square pedestal with heavy rusticated base masonry, terminated with a stone drum and colossal standing statue. The surrounding urban fabric of St Andrew Square presents 4- to 6-storey continuous perimeter frontages. Eastern and southern perimeter encompasses:
- Georgian Neo-Classical and Victorian Renaissance Revival palazzi: channelled rusticated ground floors, piano nobile fenestration with moulded architraves and triangular pediments, giant order Corinthian and Ionic pilastrades, entablatures, balustraded parapets, and dormered slate mansards.
- Dundas House (1774): recessed Neo-Palladian mansion with pedimented ashlar central core and Corinthian pilaster order.
- Late-20th and 21st-century modern commercial infill: multi-tier structural glass curtain walling, planar post-and-beam grids, vertical sun-shading fins, and recessed penthouse levels.
- Distant background skyline features the Scots Baronial clock tower of the Balmoral Hotel and the ashlar cylindrical tower of the Nelson Monument atop Calton Hill.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Historic facades & monument: Fine-ashlar Craigleith sandstone exhibiting historic coal-smoke carbon crust patination in sheltered architectural recesses and crisp rain-washed sandstone on exposed faces; lead and copper sheet flashings.
- Modern buildings: Dark bronze-anodized aluminium mullions, low-emissivity glass panels, polished grey granite cladding, and precast concrete composite panels.
- Roofs: Pitched Scotch slate and Welsh slate with lead rolls, zinc ridges, and flat mastic-asphalt roofs with concealed plant enclosures.
- Ground: Rolled asphalt carriageways, Scottish granite kerbstones, Caithness flagstone paving slabs, crushed bound-gravel aggregate paths, and cast-iron perimeter railings.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Civic parkland landscape contains mature urban trees:
- *Platanus × acerifolia* (London Plane): Large spreading structure, thick flaking-bark trunk, wide hemispherical canopy volume.
- *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore): Dense, multi-tiered rounded canopy form on stout, upright-branching trunks.
- *Tilia cordata* (Small-leaved Lime): Upright oval to columnar branch architecture with basal epicormic growth.
- Distant natural vegetation: Basaltic upland turf, gorse (*Ulex europaeus*), and low scrub communities flanking the volcanic crags.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient objects, including buses, cars, commercial delivery vehicles, traffic cones, pedestrians, temporary barriers, and movable bins, are completely eliminated. Fixed municipal hardware retained: cast-iron fluted streetlamps, traffic signal poles, granite boundary bollards, perimeter railings, and permanent asphalt road striping.
