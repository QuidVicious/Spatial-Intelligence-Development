# Spatial Domain Analysis: Constitution Ave at 12th St, Washington, DC 20004, USA

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The subterranean geology of the National Mall sits along the Atlantic Coastal Plain margin over unconsolidated Holocene alluvial silts, clays, and gravels laid down by the ancestral Potomac River and the historic, culverted Tiber Creek floodplain. Bedrock beneath the deep sediments comprises crystalline metamorphic Piedmont schists and gneisses.
- **Washington Monument:** Load-bearing core of coarse blue gneiss, faced with calcitic/dolomitic white crystalline marble: lower 150 feet (Phase 1) quarried from Texas, Maryland; transitional courses from Sheffield, Massachusetts; upper 400+ feet (Phase 2) sourced from the Beaver Dam quarry in Cockeysville, Maryland. Mortar: historic hydraulic lime, lead shims, and high-strength natural Portland cement.
- **Smithsonian Castle (Renwick Building):** Clad in fine-grained, arkosic Seneca Red Sandstone (Poolesville Member of the Triassic Manassas Formation, Culpeper Basin), bonded with traditional lime-sand mortar.
- **National Museum of African American History and Culture (NMAAHC):** Cast-in-place reinforced concrete subterranean bathtub basement seated on driven steel pilings through high-water-table alluvium, hardscaped with flamed and honed Minnesota Mesabi Black granite paving and curbing.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- **Centerpiece / Distal Center:** The Washington Monument stands as a 555-foot, 5-inch (169.3 m) Egyptian-style unreinforced stone masonry obelisk with a distinct 17-degree pyramidal capstone (pyramidion). Beyond it across the Reflecting Pool axis sits the Neoclassical Lincoln Memorial with its Doric peristyle, the World War II Memorial plaza, and the Arlington Memorial Bridge spanning the Potomac River.
- **Center-Right (Foreground/Midground):** The National Museum of African American History and Culture (NMAAHC)—a 5-storey above-grade, inverted tiered corona inspired by traditional Yoruban caryatid column capitals. The triple-tiered cantilevered volumes flare upward at 17-degree angles, wrapped in an intricate bronze-toned lattice screen. Low-slope roof surface with integrated PV solar panels and mechanical penthouses.
- **Left Flank:** The National Museum of American History (Modernist/Classical hybrid monumental box clad in Indiana limestone) and the Smithsonian Institution Castle (Norman/Gothic Revival with asymmetrical crenellated towers, turrets, and rose windows).
- **Planar Rectification:** Strict plumb-line vertical alignment applied to the obelisk shaft and the NMAAHC facade envelopes. All horizontal gravel pathways, road grades (Constitution Avenue, 14th Street), and lawn terraces rectified to true gravity planes.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Washington Monument:** Triple-strata marble facade exhibiting distinct differential atmospheric patina—the lower 150 feet shows a warmer, aged cream-tan tone, transitioning across a defined demarcation line to the cooler, light-grayish white of the upper Cockeysville marble. Subtle rain-wash tracks, micro-crystalline calcite weathering, and historical carbon runoff in sheltered joints.
- **NMAAHC:** 3,600 cast-aluminum panels with a warm architectural multi-layer bronze coating, exhibiting subtle directional sheen variations and minor urban dust accumulation in the lower filigree recesses. Clear low-iron structural curtain-wall glazing recessed behind the lattice envelope.
- **Smithsonian Castle:** Deep brownish-red ferruginous sandstone with pronounced historic weathering, soft surface exfoliation, and softened relief carving.
- **National Mall Hardscape:** Crushed buff-colored decomposed granite/gravel pedestrian walks, charcoal-gray asphalt road beds with crisp white thermoplastic road markings, honed gray granite curbs, and perimeter stainless-steel bollards.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- **National Mall Canopy:** Dense, mature urban groves comprising American Elms (*Ulmus americana*), London Plane trees (*Platanus × acerifolia*), Pin Oaks (*Quercus palustris*), Willow Oaks (*Quercus phellos*), and Red Maples (*Acer rubrum*).
- **Turf & Understory:** Manicured cool-season turfgrass (Tall Fescue *Festuca arundinacea* and Kentucky Bluegrass *Poa pratensis*) showing faint linear mowing striping, flanked by formal manicured boxwood hedges (*Buxus sempervirens*) around building foundations and native ornamental sedge plantings.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient passenger cars, tour buses, delivery vans, road traffic, moving/parked vehicles, pedestrians, tourists, temporary stanchions, trash dumpsters, and ephemeral maintenance clutter have been systematically cleared. Fixed municipal streetlamps, permanent stone curbs, traffic light masts, cast-iron signposts, perimeter security bollards, and manicured turf edges remain fully intact and pristine.
