# Spatial Domain Analysis: 12 Randolph Cres, Edinburgh EH3 7TT, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The deep incised gorge of the Water of Leith is carved into the Carboniferous sedimentary strata of the Lower Oil-Shale Group (specifically Granton Sandstone and Wardie Shales), heavily shaped by post-glacial meltwater erosion. The civil and bridge structures are founded directly on competent sandstone bedrock along the gorge floor and flanks. Primary construction lithics consist of blonde-to-buff, carboniferous Craigleith sandstone ashlar—renowned for its high quartz density and exceptional durability—supplemented by Hailes sandstone for structural backing. Mortar beds utilize traditional Scottish non-hydraulic to feebly hydraulic lime formulations with coarse river aggregates, facilitating breathability across deep load-bearing joints. Subterranean moisture dynamics are dictated by groundwater seepages along fault planes and quartz-dolerite dykes within the gorge walls.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- **Foreground / Central Span:** Dean Bridge (engineered by Thomas Telford, 1829–1831), a Category A listed high-level masonry road bridge carrying the A90. Features four towering semi-circular arches (each spanning 27.4m, rising 32.3m above the riverbed) integrated with projecting outer segmental arches on hollow ashlar piers that support cantilevered pedestrian walkways. Raised stone parapets (retrofitted 1912) cap the rectilinear deck.
- **Center-Left Background:** Holy Trinity Episcopal Church (Gothic Revival, 1838, John Henderson), exhibiting a multi-tiered square bell tower with pinnacles, crocketed spires, flying buttresses, lancet fenestration, and steep slate pitched gables.
- **Left & Right Flanking Background:** Georgian New Town / Moray Estate residential terraces (Randolph Crescent, Randolph Cliff, and Dean Terrace), presenting unified 3- to 4-storey Classical palace-front facades with rusticated basements, piano nobile sash windows, entablatures, continuous ashlar band courses, and Scottish slate mansard roofs punctuated by ashlar chimney stacks.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Dean Bridge:** Hand-tooled Craigleith buff-gray sandstone ashlar with fine-axed and droved finishes. Weathering exhibits deep industrial soot/carbon crusts accumulated along sheltered pier reveals and soffits, softened outer ashlar edges, and run-off rainwater washing striations across the vertical spandrel planes.
- **Holy Trinity Church & Surrounding Terraces:** Yellow-gray sandstone ashlar facades, Scottish West Highland dark gray roofing slates, cast-iron parapet rails and balustrades with black bitumen coatings, and timber multi-pane double-hung sash windows painted in off-white lead-oil. Lower bridge piers in the gorge display localized green bryophyte (moss and micro-algae) colonies fed by the micro-climate humidity of the river valley.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
The Dean Gorge and Dean Gardens beneath the bridge form a dense, mature riparian urban forest. Canopies are dominated by broadleaf deciduous species:
- *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore / Scottish Plane)
- *Fagus sylvatica* (European Beech)
- *Fraxinus excelsior* (European Ash)
- *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm)
- Understorey comprising *Hedera helix* (English Ivy) clambering over natural rock outcrops and lower retaining revetments, riverbank ferns, and wild garlic (*Allium ursinum*) along the Water of Leith riverbed.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All ephemeral modern street clutter—pedestrians, moving traffic, parked automobiles, temporary barriers, municipal road signs, dumpsters, and transient refuse—has been completely removed. Retained permanent civil infrastructure includes the continuous granite bridge curbs, authentic stone ashlar parapets, historic cast-iron gas-style streetlamp standards, boundary masonry retaining walls, and established mature gorge forestry.
