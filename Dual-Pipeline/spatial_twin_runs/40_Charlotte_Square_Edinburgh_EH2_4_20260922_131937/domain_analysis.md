# Spatial Domain Analysis: 40 Charlotte Square, Edinburgh EH2 4HQ, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- Subterranean Bedrock: Dinantian (Lower Carboniferous) sandstone and siltstone sedimentary strata of the Gullane and Wardie Formations, overlain by dense glacial till.
- Lithic Fabric: Primary structural ashlar quarried from local Craigleith Sandstone (fine-to-medium grained, silica-cemented, quartz-rich pale grey-buff freestone). Monument pedestal comprised of polished Peterhead red granite and subsidiary sandstone blocks.
- Mortar Chemistry: Historical non-hydraulic and feebly hydraulic lime-sand bedding mortar with high calcium lime putty matrix.
- Hydrology & Subsurface Drainage: Gentle westward fall across the Old/New Town glacial plateau toward the Water of Leith catchment, drained via sub-turf agricultural tile drains and perimeter stone culverts.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- Central Focal Massing (West Register House / St George's Church): Neoclassical civic-ecclesiastical composition designed by Robert Reid (1811–1814). Symmetrical frontispiece featuring a monumental tetra-style portico with Ionic columns, pedimented entablature, rusticated ground level, and an octagonal/circular colonnaded drum carrying a prominent ribbed hemispherical dome capped with an open lantern and gilded cross.
- Flanking Terraces (West side of Charlotte Square): Symmetrical Georgian terraced townhouses (designed to Robert Adam's comprehensive 1791 scheme). Three principal storeys above sunken basement lightwells, regular rhythm of 3-bay modular facades, rusticated ground floors, band courses, and timber sash-and-case fenestration (6-over-6 and 12-pane configurations). Continuous eaves cornice beneath pitched slate roofs punctuated by pedimented attic dormers and ashlar chimney stacks.
- Central Monument (Prince Albert Memorial): Sited symmetrically on the garden axis; Sir John Steell equestrian monument (1876) consisting of a tiered stepped plinth, rectangular polished granite pedestal with allegorical bronze bas-relief panels, corner figural groupings, and a heroic-scale bronze equestrian statue.
- Planar Rectification: Plumb vertical terrace frontages, planarized rusticated ashlar facades, aligned horizontal stringcourses and gutter lines, rectified circular drum geometry beneath the true hemispherical dome.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Monument: Verdigris-oxidized cast bronze equestrian figure and relief groupings on a polished Peterhead pink-red granite base.
- West Register House Facade: Droved and polished Craigleith sandstone ashlar with variable soot-wash carbon deposition along sheltered friezes, rusticated jointing, and rain-washed cornices.
- Cupola & Dome: Standing-seam weathered copper cladding exhibiting uniform pale verdigris-green patination; ashlar drum and decorative urns.
- Residential Pavilions: Pale blonde-to-grey dressed ashlar sandstone facades, cast-iron basement railings, dark Scottish slate roof coverings, lead flashing and hips, painted timber window frames.
- Ground Surface: Uniformly graded manicured turf lawn with damp temperate root thatch.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- Garden Canopy Fabric: Mature urban parkland broadleaves situated within Charlotte Square Gardens.
- Identified Species: *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), *Tilia × europaea* (Common Lime), *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm), and *Fagus sylvatica* (European Beech).
- Date Phenology (September 22 - Early Autumn Transition): Full, dense late-season canopies showing initial senescence; foliage displaying olive-greens with marginal leaf chlorosis, intermittent ochre and pale yellow edge discoloration, intact canopy volume prior to major autumn leaf abscission.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
- Total elimination of all transient cars, delivery vans, road traffic, and temporary pedestrian infrastructure along Charlotte Square roadway.
- Preservation of static historic civil elements: Perimeter iron railings, stone kerbing, lamp standards, mature trees, and monument base.
