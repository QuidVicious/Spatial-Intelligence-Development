# Spatial Domain Analysis: 14 Dublin St Ln N, Edinburgh EH3 6NT, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- Bedrock: Carboniferous sedimentary sequences of the Midland Valley basin, predominantly Lower Carboniferous (Tournaisian-Viséan) sandstones, shales, and mudstones with dolerite intrusions.
- Lithics & Quarries: Fine-to-medium grained quartzose blonde/buff sandstone from Craigleith and Hailes quarries; secondary grey-brown sandstone rubble on rear and flanking elevations; Caithness flagstone paving slabs; Scottish whinstone and granite setts lining the perimeter roadway.
- Mortar Chemistry: Traditional non-hydraulic to weakly hydraulic hot-mixed slaked lime mortar (calcium carbonate matrices) with sharp pit-sand aggregate, devoid of modern Portland cements.
- Hydrogeology & Drainage: Glacial till overburden sloping gently northwards towards the Water of Leith basin, controlled via sub-pavement vaulted cellars, sunken dry-area retaining lightwells, and stone culverts.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- Typology: Edinburgh Second New Town neoclassical residential square / circus (planned 1802 by Robert Reid and William Sibbald, completed c. 1823 by Thomas Bonnar).
- Structural Hierarchy & Massing: Multi-bay continuous terraces comprising 3-storey to 4-storey residential blocks with sunken basement lightwells and hipped attic dormer levels. Terminal pavilions articulated with engaged Ionic and panelled pilasters flanking upper bays.
- Fenestration & Openings: Strict vertical alignment; ground floor set in round-arched recessed ashlar panels with semicircular and rectangular radial timber fanlights over panelled timber entrance doors; upper floors featuring uniform 12-pane (6-over-6) timber sash-and-case windows; projecting ashlar continuous string courses and moulded eaves cornices.
- Roof Geometry & Chimneys: Scottish grey-slate M-profile and pitched roofs behind blocking courses; heavy broached ashlar wallhead and ridge stacks with tall cylindrical terracotta flue cans.
- Planar Rectification: Complete plumb rectification of photogrammetric mesh distortion; facades straightened to true vertical planes; horizontal parapets, cornices, and gutter alignments corrected.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Primary Facades: Droved, polished, and rusticated/channelled Craigleith ashlar sandstone blocks laid in regular ashlar coursing with fine butter-joints.
- Ancillary Enclosures & Basements: Squared coursed rubble masonry lining sunken lightwells; cantilevered droved stone platts (access bridges) with cast-iron spearhead railings and urn finials.
- Weathering Pathology: Localized carbonaceous crusting (historical coal-smoke patination) concentrated beneath projecting sills, friezes, and cornices; minor sulfate salt migration and damp run-off wash lines on exposed buff stone courses.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- Botanical Classification: Drummond Place Gardens central communal canopy. Dominant specimen trees: *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm), *Tilia × europaea* (Common Lime), *Aesculus hippocastanum* (Horse Chestnut), and *Fagus sylvatica* (Copper/European Beech).
- Understory & Enclosure: Perimeter *Ligustrum vulgare* (Privet), *Ilex aquifolium* (Holly), and mature rhododendron shrubberies contained behind boundary cast-iron railings.
- Canopy Phenology: Dense late-summer/early-autumn full crown volume, deep chlorophyll saturation showing initial localized tip chlorosis (early ochre/amber tinting) on deciduous margins; distinct dendritic scaffold limb branching through organic voids.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
- Total elimination of all vehicular traffic, parked passenger cars along the curved perimeter roads, wheelie bins, construction barriers, temporary directional signage, and modern transient clutter.
- Complete preservation of permanent civil and urban infrastructure: whinstone sett street surfaces, granite kerb stones, Caithness flagstone footpaths, historic cast-iron gas-style streetlamp stanchions, and boundary railings.
