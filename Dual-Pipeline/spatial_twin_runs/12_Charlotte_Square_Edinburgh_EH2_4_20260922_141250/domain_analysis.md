# Spatial Domain Analysis: 12 Charlotte Square, Edinburgh EH2 4ET, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean geology comprises Carboniferous sedimentary sequences of the Strathclyde Group (sandstones, siltstones, and oil-shale bedrock) mantled by dense Devensian glacial lodgement till and boulder clay. Architectural lithics are dominated by pale buff and blonde Craigleith and Hailes sandstone (calciferous, fine-grained quartz arenite characterized by silica cementation and exceptional compressive strength). Secondary lithics include Dalbeattie and Peterhead pink biotite granite used for the monumental pedestal base, dressed with fine axed and polished finishes. Historic bedding and pointing utilize semi-hydraulic non-ferrous hot-mixed lime mortar with graded quartz river sand. Subsurface groundwater movement follows the westerly topographic fall toward the Water of Leith basin.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Symmetrical neoclassical civic square composition designed by Robert Adam and Robert Reid. Centerpiece comprises West Register House (former St George's Church): colossal Ionic tetrastyle pedimented portico over a rusticated ashlar basement, surmounted by an unpierced square attic plinth, a peripteral circular Corinthian colonnade drum, and a ribbed hemispherical cupola topped with an Ionic lantern and gilded cross finial. Flanking continuous terraced townhouses (Nos. 19–33) form a unified palace-front elevation: three storeys over rusticated basement floors, channeled ashlar ground levels with segmental-arched openings, piano nobile with architraved and pedimented windows, continuous projecting string courses, dentiled and mutule cornices, and balustraded parapet attics. Fenestration consists of rectilinear timber sash-and-case windows in six-over-six and twelve-pane configurations. Mid-garden civic sculpture is the Prince Albert Memorial: equestrian bronze figure positioned on a multi-tiered polished granite pedestal with four subsidiary bronze sculptural groups at the stepped plinth corners.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- West Register House Facade: Droved, rusticated, and fine-rubbed Craigleith sandstone ashlar; lead and oxidized standing-seam copper sheeting on the hemispherical dome displaying mature verdigris (copper carbonate/malachite patina).
- Terraced Wings: Craigleith sandstone ashlar with localized coal-smoke particulate gypsum crusting under projecting cornices and rain-washed pale sandstone reveals on vertical planes; timber sash frames with white oil-paint finishes; pitched roofs clad in Westmorland and Scottish blue-grey roofing slates with lead ridge rolls and stepped ashlar chimney stacks.
- Monument & Boundary: Cast statuary bronze with olive-brown patination and copper sulfate wash lines; polished Peterhead pink granite pedestal blocks; boundary perimeter of wrought and cast-iron spearhead railings seated on droved sandstone dwarf copings.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Urban parkland deciduous canopy within Charlotte Square Garden:
- *Tilia × europaea* (Common Lime): Mature broad crowns exhibiting late-September phenology with chlorotic leaf margins, early pale-yellow foliar senescence, and partial defoliation.
- *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore): Dense spreading crowns showing dull olive-green leaves marked by autumn tar-spot lesions (*Rhytisma acerinum*) and localized crown-edge browning.
- *Platanus × acerifolia* (London Plane): Broad palmate foliage with olive-to-ochre discoloration at outer twig terminals.
- Ground cover: Dense, fine-textured mown perennial turf (*Lolium perenne*, *Festuca rubra*, *Poa pratensis*) over humic garden loam with moss incursions (*Rhytidiadelphus squarrosus*).

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient vehicular traffic, parked cars, buses, commercial vehicles, cyclists, pedestrians, crowd control barriers, waste bins, temporary street signs, and surface litter are completely excised. Permanent historical fabric—continuous stone kerbstones, Caithness flagstone perimeter pavements, fixed cast-iron lamp columns, and mature root systems—remains strictly preserved.
