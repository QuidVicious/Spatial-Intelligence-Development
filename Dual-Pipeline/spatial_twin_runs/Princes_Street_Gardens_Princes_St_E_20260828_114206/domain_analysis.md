# Spatial Domain Analysis: Princes Street Gardens, Princes St., Edinburgh EH2 2HG, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- Bedrock & Subterranean Foundation: Lower Carboniferous (Tournaisian–Visean) sedimentary strata of the Gullane Formation (calciferous sandstone measures) interspersed with Lower Carboniferous volcanic dolerite sills and basalt plugs (Castle Rock crag-and-tail system).
- Site Lithology: Glacially gouged hollow of the former Nor' Loch filled with deep lacustrine silt, peat, and post-glacial till. Overlying artificial geotechnical embankment: The Mound, composed of over 1.5 million cubic yards of earth, shale, and quarried spoil excavated from New Town foundations between 1781 and 1830.
- Masonry Lithics: Pale grey-to-buff fine-grained quartz-arenite Craigleith Sandstone (Lower Oil-Shale Group) with silica cement; auxiliary red Permo-Triassic sandstones (Locharbriggs/Corsehill) on late-Victorian commercial facades; dark quartz-dolerite setts on historic roadway pavements.
- Mortar Chemistry: Traditional non-hydraulic high-calcium hot-mixed slaked lime (quicklime and river sand) in Georgian/Victorian masonry joints; modern Portland cement grouting in twentieth-century retaining structures.
- Hydrology: Deep subsurface culverting draining the historical Tummel Burn and Nor' Loch basin toward the Firth of Forth.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- Typologies & Stylistic Diversity:
  - Fore/Mid-Ground (The Mound): Greek Revival institutional temples. William Henry Playfair's Royal Scottish Academy (RSA, 1826–1836; Doric octastyle peripteral temple with pedimented portico and seated statue of Queen Victoria) and Scottish National Gallery (1850–1859; flanking Ionic hexastyle colonnades).
  - Princes Street (North Frontage): Linear commercial terrace displaying mixed Neo-Jacobean/Renaissance Revival (Jenners department store, 6 storeys with octagonal corner spires and stepped gables), late Georgian ashlar facades (4 storeys, classical entablatures), and mid-to-late 20th-century commercial infill (4–5 storeys, reinforced concrete post-and-lintel frames, brutalist ribbon windows, anodized aluminium mullions, flat slab roofs).
  - Background Urban Grid: Georgian New Town 3-to-4-storey classical tenements, punctuated by the 168-foot neoclassical spire of St Andrew's and St George's West Church (George Street) and the Melville Monument fluted column in St Andrew Square.
- Planar Rectification & Geometry: Fully plumbed vertical peristyle columns, rectified horizontal architraves, planar roof lanterns/skylights on the RSA, sharpened parapet lines, and rigid orthogonal fenestration grids across Princes Street.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Neoclassical Temples: Dressed ashlar blocks of silica-cemented Craigleith sandstone, fine stugged and polished finishes, carved freestone capitals, lead-capped pediments, and glass roof lantern systems.
- Commercial North Flank:
  - Historic Blocks: Deep red Dumfriesshire/Locharbriggs sandstone ashlar, polished pink Peterhead granite pilasters, cast-iron spandrel reliefs, leaded slate mansard roofs with zinc flashing.
  - Modern Infill: Textured precast aggregate concrete panels, bush-hammered limestone cladding, dark bronze-anodized aluminium window frames, float-glass curtain walls.
- Weathering & Pathology: Variable historic coal-smoke soot encrustations (residual "Auld Reekie" carbon patina) preserved in sheltered stone friezes and column flutings, differential rain-washing on exposed south-facing parapets, biological green algae on low stone retaining balustrades.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- Princes Street Gardens Flora:
  - Canopy Dominants: *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm), *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore Maple), *Fagus sylvatica* (European Beech), *Tilia × europaea* (Common Lime), and *Castanea sativa* (Sweet Chestnut).
  - Sub-canopy/Ornamental: *Betula pendula* (Silver Birch), *Prunus serrulata*, and manicured rhododendron shrubberies.
- Phenology (Late Summer / August): Dense, fully mature, deep-green canopy foliage with slight early chlorosis on sun-exposed sycamore leaf margins; robust, heavy foliage massing obscuring the lower terraced slopes of the Nor' Loch valley.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
- Static civil environment enforced. All moving and stationary vehicular traffic (cars, buses, trams), roadworks, construction hoardings, mobile signage, pedestrians, and temporary municipal street clutter are omitted. Permanent civil fixtures (dressed stone curbs, cast-iron railings, bollards, granite setts, fixed lamp standards) remain intact.
