# Spatial Domain Analysis: 12 Charlotte Square, Edinburgh EH2 4ET, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean geology comprises Lower Carboniferous Gullane Formation sedimentary strata (interbedded sandstone, siltstone, and mudstone) situated over the Edinburgh Volcanic Complex basaltic bedrock. Superstructure masonry is predominantly pale buff, quartz-arenite Craigleith sandstone, renowned for high silica content and structural durability. Mortar chemistry consists of traditional non-hydraulic to weakly hydraulic Scottish lime-sand mortar. The central monument plinth is constructed from dense, fine-grained pink Peterhead granite. Ground substrate consists of compacted glaciofluvial till with loamy urban topsoil supporting structured garden turf and perimeter drainage gravels.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Planar rectified Neoclassical civic and Georgian residential composition:
- Center Axis: West Register House (formerly St George's Parish Church, Robert Reid, 1811–1814). Symmetrical Neoclassical facade featuring a central pedimented hexastyle portico, engaged Corinthian peristyle drum, surmounted by a monumental hemispherical ribbed dome and stone lantern. Planar ashlar walls rectified to strict vertical plumb lines.
- Flanking Terraces: Robert Adam-designed Georgian palace-front townhouses (3 storeys over basement, continuous rusticated ashlar ground floor, smooth-dressed piano nobile with architraved windows, 6-over-6 timber sash-and-case fenestration grids, dentiled cornice, balustraded parapets, and pitched Scots slate roofs with ashlar chimney stacks).
- Foreground Monument: Prince Albert Memorial (Sir John Steell, 1876), comprising a bronze equestrian figure set atop a tiered, profiled Peterhead granite pedestal flanked by subsidiary bronze corner groupings and relief friezes.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- West Register House: Fine-axed Craigleith sandstone ashlar showing historic carbon-soot patina in recessed moldings and rain-washed upper projections. Dome clad in standing-seam sheet copper with mature pale-green verdigris oxidation.
- Flanking Townhouses: Pale grey-buff Craigleith sandstone ashlar, dark grey West Highland/Scots roof slate, wrought and cast-iron balcony railings, white painted timber sash joinery.
- Monument: Dark patinated cast bronze equestrian figure on polished red/pink Peterhead granite plinth blocks.
- Ground Surfaces: Mown perennial turf grass (*Lolium perenne* / *Festuca rubra*) filling the foreground, bounded by rolled crushed whinstone/gravel perimeter walks and cast-iron perimeter palisade railings.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Botanical phenology grounded in late December (winter dormancy):
- Mature urban canopy species: *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), *Tilia × europaea* (Common Lime), and *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm).
- Phenological state: Complete winter defoliation; bare, intricate dark-barked branch silhouettes and structural trunk frameworks exhibiting moisture-darkened bark, void of foliage.
- Turf surface: Dormant, dense, low-cut winter turf layer.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient vehicles, parked cars, traffic elements, pedestrians, temporary barriers, municipal waste containers, and road signs are completely purged. Fixed historic fabric—cast-iron railings, sandstone plinths, paved kerbs, monument stonework, and mature trees—remains fully intact and structurally continuous.
