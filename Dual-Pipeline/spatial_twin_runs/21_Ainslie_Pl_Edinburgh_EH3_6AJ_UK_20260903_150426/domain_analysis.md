# Spatial Domain Analysis: 21 Ainslie Pl, Edinburgh EH3 6AJ, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- Bedrock: Carboniferous sedimentary sequences of the Strathclyde and Inverclyde Groups, primarily cross-bedded fluvial sandstone, siltstone, and grey mudstone under consolidated glacial till.
- Lithic Fabric: Premium-grade pale buff to golden-grey quartzitic Carboniferous sandstone, principally sourced from Craigleith Quarry with secondary contributions from Hailes Quarry.
- Mortar & Foundations: Traditional non-hydraulic to weakly hydraulic hot-mixed slaked lime and coarse river sand mortars, resting on continuous stepped sandstone rubble footings into glacial drift.
- Groundwater & Drainage: Sloping subterranean run-off directed northwest toward the Water of Leith gorge, managed through subsurface flagstone culverts and drained sunken basement lightwells.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- Typology: Late Georgian / Regency Neoclassical palatial crescent terrace, forming the south-western crescent of Ainslie Place (Moray Estate master plan by James Gillespie Graham, c. 1822–1824).
- Massing & Storeys: Monumental 3-storey plus sunken basement and attic storey block with continuous horizontal continuity. Rectilinear vertical plumb lines spanning an elliptical curved street sweep.
- Facade Rectification: Planar vertical rectification applied across the curving frontage. Deeply rusticated and channelled ground-floor ashlar base supporting smooth droved and polished ashlar on upper storeys.
- Fenestration Grid: Semicircular radiating fanlights over panelled timber entrance doors within round-arched recesses on the ground floor; regular grid of 6-over-6 and 2-over-2 double-hung timber sash-and-case windows above; first-floor piano nobile apertures trimmed with moulded architraves, console brackets, and individual cornices.
- Roof & Parapet: Low-pitch Welsh slate hipped and pitched roofs concealed behind continuous moulded entablature, dentilled stone cornice, and blocking parapets punctuated by massive shared ashlar chimney stacks with terracotta pots.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Primary Facade: Dressed Craigleith ashlar sandstone blocks with finely stugged, rusticated ground courses and polished upper courses.
- Metalwork & Railings: Cast-iron spearhead railings, urn finials, and overthrow lamp arches along front basement drop areas; decorative anthemion-pattern cast-iron balconettes at first-floor sills.
- Basement & Pavement: Droved sandstone retaining walls lining deep dry basement wells; Caithness stone flags on public walkways, edged by whinstone and granite kerb setts.
- Weathering & Patina: Historical industrial coal-smoke carbon crusting in sheltered stringcourse recesses; minor calcite leaching at lintel joints; biological patina and moisture-darkened lower plinths.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- Botanical Classification: *Platanus × acerifolia* (London plane) and *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore) dominating the enclosed communal gardens of Ainslie Place.
- Canopy & Phenology (September 3): Fully developed, dense late-summer canopies with heavy foliage volume; deep matte olive-green leaves exhibiting subtle onset of early autumnal edge-chlorosis and scattered yellowing, with intact dense crown profiles.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
- Total elimination of all transient automotive traffic, parked modern vehicles, pedestrians, waste bins, temporary directional signage, and visual photogrammetric mesh artifacts.
- Preserved permanent infrastructure: continuous stone kerbstones, Caithness flag pavements, fixed historic cast-iron gas-style streetlamp posts, perimeter boundary railings, and established parkland perimeter sod.
