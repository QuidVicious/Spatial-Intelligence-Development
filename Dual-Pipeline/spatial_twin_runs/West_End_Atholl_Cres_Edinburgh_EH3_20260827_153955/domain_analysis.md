# Spatial Domain Analysis: West End, Atholl Cres, Edinburgh EH3 8HA, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The subterranean geology of Edinburgh's West End comprises Carboniferous sedimentary bedrock of the Strathclyde Group (sandstones, siltstones, and oil-shales), overlain by glacial till deposits. The primary regional structural masonry consists of blonde Craigleith and Hailes sandstone, characterized by dense quartz-arenite matrices bound with historical non-hydraulic and semi-hydraulic hot-lime mortars. Pavements and carriageways incorporate Scottish whinstone (quartz-dolerite) curbs and setts beneath contemporary asphalt overlays. Subterranean moisture is managed through engineered 19th-century cellar vaults beneath wide stone flag pavements, draining toward the Firth of Forth catchment.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
The urban corridor along Shandwick Place and Atholl Crescent exhibits Scottish Georgian, Neo-Classical, and Victorian Roman Baroque architecture:
- **St George's West Church (Charlotte Chapel)**: Designed by David Bryce (1866–69) with a prominent 56-meter Italianate campanile added by Sir Robert Rowand Anderson (1879–81) inspired by San Giorgio Maggiore in Venice. Features a four-stage tower with vermiculated bands at the base, an octagonal belfry, copper-clad pyramidal spire, and giant-order Corinthian corner pilasters.
- **Atholl Crescent / Shandwick Place Terraces**: 3 to 4-storey plus attic and basement Georgian tenements with rhythmic sash-and-case fenestration (rectified plumb vertical lines), ashlar ground floors, smooth stringcourses, dentilled eaves, rusticated basements with cast-iron drop drop-wells, and pitched slate roofs with dormers and masonry chimney stacks.
- **Distant Landmark**: The Neoclassical copper dome of West Register House (formerly St George's Church, Charlotte Square, by Robert Reid, 1811–1814) is visible in the northeast background.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Ashlar Sandstone**: Weathered buff, honey, and pale-grey Craigleith sandstone facades displaying subtle 19th-century atmospheric coal-smoke carbon crusting, localized salt sub-efflorescence along cornice projections, and crisp ashlar dressings.
- **Roofing**: Scottish Ballachulish grey-blue slates with lead flashing valleys, zinc ridges, and weathered clay chimney pots.
- **Tower Spire**: Weathered verdigris-oxidized copper on the upper campanile spire and dome cupolas.
- **Ground Floor/Joinery**: Painted timber timber-framed shopfront fascias, dark cast-iron area railings with spearhead finials, and large glazed display panes.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Urban dendrological specimens situated in private gardens and rear courtyards include mature broadleaf deciduous trees—predominantly *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm), and *Betula pendula* (Silver Birch)—exhibiting dense, organic leafy canopies with natural branching structures, distinct from adjacent stonework.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient visual elements have been systematically eliminated: zero moving or parked automobiles, zero buses, zero pedestrians, zero rubbish receptacles, zero street bins, and zero temporary vinyl/paper street ads. Permanent civil furniture is preserved: cast-iron street lamps, painted roadway demarcation lines, granite kerbs, and iron railings.
