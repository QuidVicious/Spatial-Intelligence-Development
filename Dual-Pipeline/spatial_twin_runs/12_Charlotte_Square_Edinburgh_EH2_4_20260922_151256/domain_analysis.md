# Spatial Domain Analysis: 12 Charlotte Square, Edinburgh EH2 4ET, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Bedrock foundation comprises Lower Carboniferous sedimentary sequences of the Inverclyde and Strathclyde Groups (interbedded sandstones, siltstones, and mudstones) overlying regional volcanic basaltic dolerite sills. Structural masonry is predominantly Craigleith sandstone—a pale buff, silica-cemented, fine-grained quartz arenite known for low porosity and high compressive strength—supplemented by Hailes sandstone. Pedestal of the central monument rests on dressed blocks of pink Peterhead granite and grey granodiorite. Historic bedding mortars utilize traditional non-hydraulic to feebly-hydraulic hot-mixed lime and well-graded crushed sandstone aggregate. Subterranean moisture is governed by glacial till drift over gently undulating, well-draining urban regolith.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Neoclassical civic ensemble terminating the western axis of the Edinburgh New Town (Robert Adam plan, executed and modified by Robert Reid, completed 1814). Central monumental focus is West Register House (formerly St George's Church): colossal tetrastyle Ionic portico with entablature and triangular pediment, flanked by rusticated vestibule bays, surmounted by a stepped square plinth, a peristyle drum encircled by Corinthian columns, and crowned by a high hemispherical ribbed dome terminating in a lantern cupola. Flanking terraces form a unified three-storey, basement, and attic Georgian palazzo block: rusticated ashlar ground storeys, channeled voussoirs over flat window arches, first-floor piano nobile with continuous molded stringcourses, regular fenestration grids with 6-over-6 timber sash-and-case windows, and pitched rooflines with slated dormers and ashlar chimney stacks. Foreground features the Prince Albert Memorial (Sir John Steell, 1876): multi-tiered stepped granite pedestal supporting a colossal bronze equestrian figure with bronze figurative narrative reliefs and corner plinth groups.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Facades: Buff-grey Craigleith ashlar sandstone with fine droved and polished dressings; rusticated basement and ground floor courses; lime-putty pointed tight ashlar joints.
- Dome & Cupola: Standing-seam sheet copper cladding displaying complete pale green verdigris (copper carbonate/sulfate) oxidation; gilded bronze ball and cross finial.
- Monument: Dark patinated cast bronze with localized turquoise run-off streaking over dressed, polished red Peterhead and grey granite steps and socles.
- Roofs: Grey Scottish and Welsh slate pitched roofs with lead roll ridges, flashings, valleys, and gutters.
- Fenestration & Metalwork: Painted white timber sash-and-case frames; wrought and cast-iron spearhead railings along basement areas; dark-painted cast-iron lamp standards.
- Ground: Well-drained amenity lawn turf interspersed with gravel pathways and Caithness stone flag kerbs.
- Weathering Pathology: Sub-cornice coal-smoke sulfate encrustations, vertical rain-wash tracks on sandstone ashlar, and minor localized granular stone spalling on exposed plinths.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Enclosed square parkland containing mature deciduous canopy trees: *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore), *Tilia × europaea* (Common Lime), and *Ulmus glabra* (Wych Elm). Botanical phenology for late December: 100% leaf senescence and complete winter defoliation, revealing bare, dark, moisture-darkened branch architecture, dense twig tracery, and lichen-crusted bark. Understory comprises dormant perennial turf grasses (*Lolium perenne*, *Festuca rubra*, *Poa pratensis*) with damp winter compaction and bare root-flare perimeters.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient vehicles, traffic infrastructure, parked cars, pedestrians, modern signage, dumpsters, and photogrammetric surface warps are entirely suppressed. Pure static architectural masonry, cast metal, geological bases, and winter botanical framework remain.
