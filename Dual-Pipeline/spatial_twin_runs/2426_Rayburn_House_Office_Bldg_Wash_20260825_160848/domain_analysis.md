# Spatial Domain Analysis: 2426 Rayburn House Office Bldg, Washington, DC 20515, USA

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The site sits upon Capitol Hill (Jenkins Hill) on the geological Atlantic Seaboard Fall Zone transition between the Piedmont metamorphic complex and the Atlantic Coastal Plain. The subterranean substrate consists of unconsolidated sedimentary deposits belonging to the Cretaceous Potomac Formation (interbedded quartzose gravels, sands, silts, and dense plastic clays) over deeply buried crystalline schist/gneiss bedrock. 
The structural masonry fabric of the Capitol complex comprises distinct historical lithic stratifications:
- Central Core (Original 1793–1820s block): Aquia Creek freestone (Cretaceous quartzose sandstone quarried in Stafford County, Virginia) with calcitic/lime-sand mortar beds.
- East Front Colonnade & Central Extension (1958–1962 reproduction): White Cherokee crystalline marble from Tate, Georgia.
- House (foreground) and Senate (background) Wings (1850s Thomas U. Walter expansion): Dolomitic Lee marble quarried in Massachusetts, supported by Cockeysville calcitic marble monolithic fluted columns.
- Plinths and West Sub-structure: Renville Minnesota pink granite and Deer Isle/Quincy granitic basement ashlar courses.
Subterranean hydrologic drainage flows westward down the topographic incline toward the Potomac paleochannel, managed through historic brick barrel vaults and contemporary subterranean storm cistern networks.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- Typology: Neoclassical Civic Architecture (Federalist core integrated with American Renaissance/Beaux-Arts monumental expansions).
- Master Massing & Axial Layout: Symmetrical tripartite E-W plan spanning 751 ft (229 m) along the north-south axis.
- Foreground (Southern Sector): The House of Representatives Wing—a 3-story monumental rectangular mass featuring a colossal two-story Corinthian colonnade over a rusticated ashlar podium basement, capped by a modillioned entablature and an expansive flat/low-hipped seam-metal copper roof with a central copper-clad clerestory/skylight monitor.
- Central Core & Rotunda: A high-drum neoclassical rotunda rising to a monumental cast-iron double dome (designed by Thomas U. Walter, 1855–1866) topped by a peristyle tholos and the bronze *Statue of Freedom* at an absolute peak of 288 ft (88 m) above grade.
- East Front Plaza (Foreground Right to Midground): The expansive, planar East Capitol Plaza, paved in modular granite and red-brown porphyry stone setts, featuring planar underground Capitol Visitor Center (CVC) skylights flush with the plaza plane, rectangular sunken grass panels, and a grand granite staircase leading to the central and wing porticos.
- Rectification: All vertical colonnades, pilasters, tholos peristyle columns, drum ribs, and ashlar corners plumbed to true gravity vertical ($0.0^\circ$). All horizontal entablatures, cornices, balustrades, and copper roof ridges rectified to true planar horizontality, eliminating photogrammetry tilt and polygon artifacts.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- House Wing Facades: Finely dressed Lee dolomitic marble ashlar blocks showing subtle gray veining, crisp relief carvings in Corinthian capitals, and sharp draft margins. 
- East Front Extension: Georgia White Cherokee marble exhibiting bright crystalline reflectivity and clean, uniform pointing.
- Rotunda Dome: Engineered bolted cast-iron architectural shell coated in multiple historic layers of off-white protective acrylic-alkyd elastomeric paint, exhibiting delicate mechanical panel seams and subtle soot/atmospheric runoff tracks along sheltered undercut moldings.
- Low-Pitch Roofs: Standing-seam copper paneling exhibiting mature, non-uniform verdigris oxidation (cupric carbonate patina) ranging from pale turquoise-green on windward exposures to darker oxidized copper oxide in drainage valleys and parapet returns.
- Climate-Adaptive Weathering: Mid-Atlantic humid-temperate patina; faint dark micro-algae striations (*Gloeocapsa magma*) on north-facing stone sheltered under ledges, historical atmospheric carbon washing in deep cornice reveals, and dry mineral efflorescence along the lower granite base courses.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- Surrounding Grounds: Frederick Law Olmsted-designed rolling Capitol Grounds arboretum.
- Species Identified: Mature deciduous and coniferous canopies including American Elms (*Ulmus americana*), Northern Red Oaks (*Quercus rubra*), Willow Oaks (*Quercus phellos*), London Planetrees (*Platanus × acerifolia*), and Sweetgums (*Liquidambar styraciflua*).
- Botanical Structure: Deep, full leafy crowns rendered with distinct branching architecture, individual sub-canopy shadowing, and dense summer green foliage, completely disambiguated from adjacent granite balustrades and retaining walls.
- Lawns: Impeccably manicured cool-season turfgrass (tall fescue/Kentucky bluegrass blend) filling Olmstedian curvilinear perimeter parterres and the crisp sunken rectangles of the East Plaza.
