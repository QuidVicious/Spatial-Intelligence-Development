## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- **Subterranean Bedrock & Foundation Lithology:** The monument rests upon deep artificial terrace fill over late Quaternary Potomac River alluvial silt, clay, and gravel deposits, underlain at depth by the crystalline metamorphic Piedmont basement (Potomac terrane / Sykesville Formation gneiss and schist). 
- **Substructure Stabilization:** Beneath the base lies a massive stepped concrete and blue gneiss rubble underpinning foundation (installed 1879–1880) extending down to solid bearing strata to prevent differential settlement.
- **Superstructure Stone Sources:** 
  1. *Lower 152 feet (Phase 1, 1848–1854):* Coarse-grained dolomitic Cockeysville/Texas white marble from Texas, Baltimore County, Maryland.
  2. *Transition Band (1879):* Four to six courses of calcitic marble from Lee/Sheffield, Berkshire County, Massachusetts.
  3. *Upper Section & Pyramidion (1880–1884):* Fine-grained, magnesium-rich Cockeysville dolomitic marble from Cockeysville, Maryland.
  4. *Interior Core:* Backing masonry of blue Potomac gneiss and Maine/Maryland granite ashlar.
- **Mortar Chemistry:** Hydrated lime and natural cement mortar at the lower levels; high-strength Portland cement and hydraulic lime at upper tiers, with lead and elastomer compression caulking in exposed exterior joints.
- **Hydrology:** High water table tied to the adjacent tidal estuary of the Potomac River and the Tidal Basin; surrounded by a continuous subterranean perimeter drainage system.
