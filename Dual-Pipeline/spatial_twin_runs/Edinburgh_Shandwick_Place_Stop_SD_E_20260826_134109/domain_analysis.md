# Spatial Domain Analysis: Edinburgh Shandwick Place Stop SD, Edinburgh EH2 4RG, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The subterranean bedrock beneath Shandwick Place consists of the Lower Carboniferous Gullane and Wardie Shale Formations (interbedded sandstones, siltstones, and mudstones), overlain by stiff Devensian glacial till. Above ground, the architectural fabric is dominated by Scottish Carboniferous sandstones, predominantly pale honey-to-buff Craigleith and Hailes sandstones, celebrated for high quartz silica content and enduring compressive strength. Mortar chemistry comprises traditional non-hydraulic to weakly hydraulic hot-mixed lime mortars with sharp river sand aggregates, accommodating natural vapor permeability and cyclic subterranean moisture transmission along the Western New Town's sloping gradient.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Looking west-southwest (heading ~250°) along Shandwick Place toward West Maitland Street in Edinburgh's West End:
- **A. Foreground & Street Level (Plumbing & Anti-Warp Rectification):** The roadway is rectilinear and level, embedded with steel Edinburgh Tram tracks set flush into granite sett and asphalt paving. Ground-floor commercial storefronts (formerly fragmented in 3D scan meshes) are reconstituted into crisp, load-bearing trabeated shopfront fascias, pilasters, and plate-glass display windows beneath uniform entablatures.
- **B. Center & Focal Architecture:** The prominent vertical anchor on the north side is the Italianate campanile bell tower of the former St George’s Free Church (now Charlotte Baptist Chapel), designed by David Bryce (1869) with its 56-meter tower completed by Sir Robert Rowand Anderson (1881) inspired by the campanile of San Giorgio Maggiore. Rectified to absolute 90° gravity plumb, it features classical belfry arcades, corniced entablatures, and an open stone loggia crown.
- **C. Flanking Streetfronts:** 
  - *North / Right Flank:* 3-to-4 storey Victorian Free Renaissance and late Georgian ashlar tenements (including John McLachlan’s 1879 design at Nos. 52–56). Regular rhythmic bays, bracketed cill courses, canted upper-floor oriel bays, pedimented attic dormers, cast-iron parapet balustrades, and steep pitched slate roofs with corniced ashlar chimney stacks.
  - *South / Left Flank:* Continuous 3-to-4 storey classical ashlar tenement facades transitioning toward the sweeping arc of Atholl Crescent, featuring disciplined Georgian sash fenestration (twelve-pane and single-pane plate glass timber sashes) and pronounced stringcourses.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Ashlar Sandstone:** Fine-grained buff Craigleith and grey Hailes sandstone ashlar blocks with fine chisel-drafted margins and droved/rubbed face dressings.
- **Micro-Climate Weathering & Patina:** Temperate oceanic maritime climate with historical industrial coal-smoke patina. Deep carbonaceous soot accumulation preserved in under-eaves, window reveals, and pediment recesses, contrasted with natural rain-washed lighter sandstone expanses; granular surface softening and localized gypsum crusting along sheltered moulding arrises.
- **Roofing & Metalwork:** Welsh and Scottish Ballachulish blue-grey slate tiles on timber rafters, lead flashing and valley gutters, cast-iron downpipes, hopper heads, and wrought-iron window guardrails.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Along the peripheries and neighboring crescents (Coates and Atholl Gardens), mature urban broadleaf trees include London Plane (*Platanus x acerifolia*), European Lime (*Tilia x europaea*), and Wych Elm (*Ulmus glabra*). Organic, open branching structures with dense summer-canopy foliage, fully disambiguated from built stonework.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
The urban corridor is entirely decluttered of transient interference: zero vehicles (no cars, buses, or moving trams), zero pedestrians, zero rubbish receptacles, and zero temporary signage. Permanent civil infrastructure—smooth granite kerbstones, steel streetcar tracks flush with the road, cast-iron Victorian street lighting columns, and fixed civic boundary railings—remains immaculately preserved.
