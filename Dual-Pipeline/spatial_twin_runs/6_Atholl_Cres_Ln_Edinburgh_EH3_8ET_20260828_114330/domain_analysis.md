# Spatial Domain Analysis: 6 Atholl Cres Ln, Edinburgh EH3 8ET, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean geology comprises Lower Carboniferous (Dinantian) sedimentary strata of the Gullane and West Lothian Oil-Shale Formations, underlain by basaltic and doleritic volcanic sills. Lithic envelope is dominated by Carboniferous quartz-arenite freestones, predominantly fine-to-medium-grained quartzose Craigleith Sandstone and Hailes Sandstone, interlaid with Binny and Cullalo sandstone units. High silica content (up to 98% quartz in Craigleith) provides exceptional load-bearing durability and compression resistance. Decorative cathedral columns incorporate pink Shap Granite with orthoclase phenocrysts. Mortar matrices are non-hydraulic to feebly hydraulic slaked lime (calcium hydroxide) mixed with sharp river sand and crushed sandstone aggregates, allowing vapour permeability across ashlar joints.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
The urban fabric exhibits a stark juxtaposition between Georgian Neoclassical massing and High Victorian Gothic ecclesiastical monumentality:
- Foreground/Midground: Atholl Crescent and Coates Crescent designed by Thomas Bonnar (1824–1825); regularized 3-storey plus basement and attic/mansard profiles; V-jointed rusticated ground-floor masonry, smooth ashlar upper storeys, continuous stone plat bands, projecting modillioned eaves cornices, blocking courses, and timber 6-over-6 and 12-over-12 double-hung sash-and-case fenestration grids.
- Dominant Midground/Background: St Mary's Episcopal Cathedral designed by Sir George Gilbert Scott (1874–1879; western towers completed 1913–1917); tripartite spire composition dominated by the 90m central crossing spire with lucarnes and octagonal broaches flanked by twin western spires ("Barbara" and "Mary"); Early English / Decorated Gothic vocabulary with stepped buttresses, lancet window groupings, traceried rose windows, and high-pitched timber-trussed roof gables.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Facades: Fine-droved and polished Craigleith blonde/buff sandstone ashlar, contrasting with snecked rubble masonry on secondary elevations and chimneystacks.
- Roofing: Heavy Scottish Ballachulish blue-grey slates and Westmorland green slates laid in diminishing courses; lead flashing on hips, valleys, gutters, and dormer cheeks; copper ridge caps.
- Joinery & Metalwork: Cast-iron spearhead railings with stone plinths along basement areas, anthemion-pattern cast-iron Juliet balconettes, painted timber sash frames.
- Weathering Pathology: Localized gypsum crusting, sub-surface salt crystalization, atmospheric soot staining from historical industrial emissions ("Auld Reekie" patina) concentrated in protected niches, vertical rain-wash tracks on ashlar faces, and biological micro-crusts on slate roofs and sandstone parapets.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Atholl and Coates Crescent communal gardens contain mature broadleaf deciduous canopies:
- Genus & Species: *Platanus × acerifolia* (London plane), *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore maple), *Tilia cordata* (Small-leaved lime), and *Ulmus glabra* (Wych elm).
- Phenology: Mid-to-late summer canopy state characterized by dense, saturated dark-green foliar volume, fully expanded broad lamina with negligible leaf senescence or early chlorosis, and mature branch-frame structure occluding ground-level carriage paths.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All photogrammetric vehicular artefacts, moving traffic on the A8 corridor, tramway rolling stock, transient pedestrians, rubbish bins, commercial signage, and construction hoardings are completely excised. Retained static infrastructure comprises stone kerbing, cast-iron boundary balustrades, granite setts, historic cast-iron gas/electric lamp posts, and permanent masonry boundaries.
