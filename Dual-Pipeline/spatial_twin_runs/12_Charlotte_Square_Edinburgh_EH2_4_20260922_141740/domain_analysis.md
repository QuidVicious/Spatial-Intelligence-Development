# Spatial Domain Analysis: 12 Charlotte Square, Edinburgh EH2 4ET, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean foundation rests on Carboniferous sedimentary bedrock of the Strathclyde Group (sandstone, siltstone, mudstone cycles) overlain by dense glacial till. Primary structural lithic is fine-grained Carboniferous quartz-arenite Craigleith sandstone, characterized by high silica cementation, pale buff-to-grey coloration, and distinct laminar carbonaceous micro-bedding. Pedestal of the central monument composed of coarse-grained pink Peterhead granite with prominent pink potassium feldspar phenocrysts, quartz, and biotite. Mortars consist of traditional slaked non-hydraulic hot-lime and sharp river sand putty with minimal crushed sandstone dust pozzolans. Groundwater dynamics governed by shallow seasonal perched water tables across the central garden hollow.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Central landmark: West Register House (formerly St. George's Church, Robert Reid after Robert Adam, completed 1814), featuring a monumental Neoclassical massing with a tetrastyle Ionic portico supporting an unadorned triangular pediment, flanked by rusticated wings with round-arched niches. Above the central square block rises a stepped circular drum surrounded by an Ionic peristyle colonnade, culminating in a monumental ribbed hemispherical dome with a columned cupola lantern and gilded finial. Flanking background terraces: Charlotte Square Western palatial facade (Robert Adam design), comprising 3-storey-plus-basement and attic dormered Georgian townhouses. Ground storeys feature deep V-jointed horizontal rustication with round-headed entrance doors and fanlights; upper floors exhibit smooth ashlar with giant order Corinthian pilasters framing terminal pavilions, horizontal stringcourses, dentiled and modillioned cornices, and regular grids of 6-over-6 and 12-over-12 double-hung timber sash windows. Center foreground monument: Prince Albert Memorial (Sir John Steell, 1876), consisting of a stepped, multi-tiered classical pedestal with flanking lower plinth wings supporting bronze figurative groups, surmounted by an over-life-size equestrian bronze.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- West Register House: Ashlar Craigleith sandstone with localized historic coal-smoke encrustation in sheltered entablatures and rain-washed pale reveals; oxidized verdigris-green standing-seam copper sheeting over timber ribs on the hemispherical dome; lead flashings; wrought-iron grilles.
- Georgian Terraces: Fine-jointed Craigleith ashlar, Ballachulish blue-grey roofing slate laid to diminishing courses, lead valleys, zinc-clad timber dormers, cast-iron rainwater goods, and painted timber window joinery.
- Central Memorial: High-polish and dressed pink Peterhead granite plinths with dark brown and localized turquoise-green copper carbonate patination on cast bronze statuary and relief plaques.
- Ground Surface: Managed perennial ryegrass (*Lolium perenne*) and red fescue (*Festuca rubra*) lawn over damp humic topsoil, bound by rolled gravel paths and dressed whinstone/granite kerbstones.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Date-grounded phenology (December 22, mid-winter): Deciduous urban tree canopy in full winter dormancy with 100% leaf drop. Canopy composition includes mature Common Lime (*Tilia × europaea*), Sycamore (*Acer pseudoplatanus*), and European Beech (*Fagus sylvatica*), exhibiting dense networks of bare, dark grey-brown ramifications, epicormic twig clusters at trunk bases, and fine leafless silhouette tracery. Turf is cold-stunted, dense, and moss-flecked with minimal winter growth.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient road traffic, parked motor vehicles, pedestrians, temporary barriers, municipal wheelie bins, and movable street clutter are purged. Permanent civil infrastructure—dressed stone kerbs, perimeter cast-iron railings, sandstone plinths, and mature root systems—remains fully resolved.
