# Spatial Domain Analysis: 46 Charlotte Square, Edinburgh EH2 4HQ, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- **Bedrock Stratigraphy:** Lower Carboniferous sedimentary bedrock of the Strathclyde Group (Gullane Formation), comprising interbedded sandstones, siltstones, and carbonaceous mudstones over volcanic tuffs.
- **Lithic Masonry Sources:** Primary building fabric executed in pale blonde-to-buff Craigleith sandstone (calciferous quartz arenite with siliceous cement), with supplementary ashlar from Hailes and Redhall quarries. Plinth of the central Prince Albert Memorial is composed of polished pink/grey Peterhead and Aberdeen granite.
- **Mortar Chemistry:** Traditional non-hydraulic to feebly hydraulic hot-mixed lime mortars with sharp silica sand aggregate, flush-pointed on refined ashlar arrises.
- **Subterranean Hydrology:** Gently sloping glaciofluvial till over glacial drumlin topography, maintaining efficient natural sub-base drainage across the New Town plateau.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- **Central Terminating Landmark (West Register House / St George's Church):** Monumental Neoclassical former church designed by Robert Adam (1791) and executed by Robert Reid (1811–1814). Features a rusticated podium, a tetrastyle Corinthian portico with full entablature and pediment, advancing corner pavilions, and a monumental cylindrical peristyle drum supporting a ribbed hemispherical dome capped with a glazed cupola lantern and gilded finial.
- **Flanking Palace Facades (12–17 Charlotte Square):** Symmetrical 3-storey plus raised basement and attic Neo-Palladian palace block. Channeled V-joint rustication on the ground level, smooth ashlar piano nobile with Ionic giant pilasters terminating the pavilions, molded stringcourses, modillion cornices, and solid blocking courses.
- **Central Monument (Prince Albert Memorial):** Colossal bronze equestrian statue sculpted by Sir John Steell (unveiled 1876) mounted on a stepped, rusticated-and-polished granite pedestal flanked by tiered bronze allegorical groupings.
- **Fenestration & Geometry:** Plumb vertical load-bearing lines with planarized ashlar frontages. Regular grid of timber-framed 12-pane and 15-pane sash-and-case windows set into rectangular and round-arched Gibbs-surround recesses.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Ashlar Facades:** Fine-grained, honed Craigleith sandstone ashlar with clean rain-washed vertical planes and dark atmospheric soot/coal-smoke encrustations concentrated in recessed moldings, column capitals, friezes, and cornices.
- **Cupola & Dome Roofing:** Standing-seam batten-rolled copper cladding exhibiting a uniform, fully developed seafoam-green verdigris patina (basic copper carbonate).
- **Roofing:** Diminishing courses of Scottish Ballachulish / Scotch blue-grey slate with lead flashing, valleys, and dressed ashlar chimney stacks with clay chimney cans.
- **Statuary:** Cast bronze with natural dark brown oxidation and localized verdigris streaking across downward drainage paths.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- **Arboreal Canopy:** Common Lime (*Tilia × europaea*), Sycamore (*Acer pseudoplatanus*), and Horse Chestnut (*Aesculus hippocastanum*) planted within the central Charlotte Square Garden enclosure.
- **Date-Grounded Phenology (Late September):** Early autumn transition; canopies show thinning foliage density, crown margin chlorosis with lime-yellow and pale ochre leaf discoloration, and emerging bare upper branch silhouettes.
- **Ground Cover:** Uniformly mown temperate perennial rye-grass (*Lolium perenne*) lawn with delineated soil circles at tree drip lines.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
- All transient pedestrians, road vehicles, traffic barriers, refuse containers, temporary garden event installations, and photogrammetric surface noise are entirely removed. Fixed historic iron railings, stone plinths, and permanent mature parkland elements remain intact.
