# Spatial Domain Analysis: 8 Ainslie Pl, Edinburgh EH3 6AS, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Bedrock: Mississippian/Carboniferous sedimentary strata of the Strathclyde Group (Gullane Formation), comprising interbedded sandstone, siltstone, and mudstone.
Lithics: Primary structural masonry of fine-to-medium grained pale buff/blonde quartzose Craigleith Sandstone (silica-cemented arenite) for ashlar facades, with dark Hailes sandstone rubble core backing.
Mortar: Historic hot-mixed non-hydraulic to feebly hydraulic lime mortar incorporating sharp crushed quartz sand aggregates.
Hydrology: Gently sloping terrain toward the Water of Leith basin over stiff glaciofluvial boulder clay (till), with engineered subterranean drain channels beneath front lightwells.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Typology: Regency/Late Georgian neoclassical circus crescent (designed by James Gillespie Graham, Moray Estate, c. 1822–1825).
Massing: Four storeys over continuous sunken basement lightwell, arranged in a shallow concave arc.
Order: Colossal attached Greek Ionic pilasters articulating the upper bays of pavilion blocks; continuous molded entablature, projecting dentil cornice, and stone blocking course forming the parapet.
Ground Floor: Channelled ashlar rustication with segmentally arched and round-headed door openings, radial glazed fanlights, and deeply recessed timber entrance doors.
Upper Storeys: Smooth-dressed ashlar piano nobile with projecting continuous sill bands, second storey with molded architraves, and a third attic storey beneath the parapet.
Roof: Pitched slate roofs (West Highland/Ballachulish slate) behind parapets, penetrated by rectangular coursed-ashlar party-wall chimneystacks with terracotta chimney cans.
Fenestration: Planar rectangular openings containing double-hung timber sash-and-case windows in 6-over-6 and 12-pane configurations.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
Facade: Fine-droved and polished buff Craigleith ashlar sandstone.
Basement/Plinth: Droved sandstone retaining walls lining the sunken basement area.
Weathering/Patina: Localized atmospheric coal-smoke crusts concentrated in cornice undercuts, pilaster capitals, and entablature moldings; selective water-wash bleaching on exposed vertical ashlar faces.
Ironwork: Cast and wrought iron spearhead railings with urn finials guarding the basement drops and garden perimeter.
Surfacing: Caithness flagstone pavement, dark whinstone (basalt) kerbs, and asphalt roadway.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Species: Central garden enclosure planted with mature *Acer pseudoplatanus* (Sycamore) and *Tilia × europaea* (Common Lime).
Morphology: Large-stature broadleaf trees with stout, heavily fissured single trunks (0.8–1.2 m DBH) dividing into extensive ascending primary framework boughs and wide, dome-shaped radiating branch canopies.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All photogrammetry geometric mesh noise, transient vehicles, pedestrians, waste bins, and temporary street clutter are excised. Static historic infrastructure—cast-iron railings, sandstone boundary kerbs, and mature trees—is retained and planarly rectified.
