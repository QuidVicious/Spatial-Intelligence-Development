# Spatial Domain Analysis: 15B London St, Edinburgh EH3 6LY, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
- Subterranean Bedrock: Lower Carboniferous sedimentary strata of the Strathclyde Group (Gullane Formation), comprising interbedded sandstones, siltstones, and mudstones overlain by dense glacial till (boulder clay).
- Lithic Provenance & Ashlar: Pale buff to weathered grey Craigleith and Hailes quartzose calciferous sandstone, fine-to-medium grained with subtle carbonaceous laminae. Secondary elements utilize West Lothian / Fife Carboniferous sandstone.
- Dressing & Tooling: Polished fine-jointed ashlar on principal elevations, droved ashlar margins, broached/stugged rustication at basement levels, and coursed squared rubble along secondary/party elevations.
- Mortar Chemistry: Traditional hot-mixed non-hydraulic to feebly hydraulic lime mortars blended with sharp river sand aggregate.
- Hydrogeology: Free-draining terrace sloping northward toward the Water of Leith basin, bisected at depth by the mid-19th-century subterranean Scotland Street railway tunnel beneath Drummond Place.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
- Typology: Edinburgh Second New Town Neo-Classical / Georgian residential tenements and terraced townhouses (designed by Robert Reid and William Sibbald, c. 1808–1823).
- Elevation & Massing: 3-storey and 4-storey elevations over raised rusticated basements, terminated with continuous stone cornices, blocking courses, and pitched rooflines with timber-framed dormers.
- Fenestration Grid: Symmetrical rhythmic fenestration of 6-over-6 and 12-pane timber sash-and-case windows set in planar ashlar reveals, flanked by moulded architraves.
- Planar Rectification: True vertical plumb-line rectification across all facade planes, continuous horizontal band courses, straight parapet copes, and regular party-wall chimney stacks crowned with terracotta pots.
- Ground Transition: Droved stone steps and cantilevered ashlar platts spanning deep sunken area basements, enclosed by wrought and cast-iron spear-head balustrades.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Primary Facades: Polished Craigleith sandstone ashlar with fine butter joints; rusticated V-jointed ashlar basecourses.
- Roofing: Scottish Ballachulish and Welsh Bangor blue-grey roofing slates laid in diminishing courses with dressed lead valley flashings and rolled zinc ridges.
- Metals & Joinery: Painted wrought and cast iron (railings, balcony brackets, boot scrapers, lampposts), painted timber sash-and-case joinery.
- Weathering & Pathology: Historical coal-smoke particulate encrustation (black gypsum crusts) under projecting stringcourses and cornices; differential rain-washing revealing blonde-grey sandstone bodies; localized biological greening (moss and microalgae) along dampened basement revetment walls.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
- Urban Forest Canopy: Drummond Place Gardens enclosed central woodland stand.
- Genus & Species: Dominated by mature Wych Elm (*Ulmus glabra*), Sycamore (*Acer pseudoplatanus*), Common Lime (*Tilia × europaea*), Copper Beech (*Fagus sylvatica* f. *purpurea*), and Horse Chestnut (*Aesculus hippocastanum*).
- Phenology (August / Late Summer): Dense, fully expanded deep-green canopy volume; heavy foliage crowns interlocking across the garden perimeter; subtle early-season foliar dust accumulation and minor localized chlorosis.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
- Complete elimination of all motorized vehicles, parked cars, traffic cones, wheelie bins, and temporary street furniture.
- Strict preservation of authentic static civic infrastructure: whinstone/granite roadway setts, dressed stone kerbs, flagstone footpaths, historic cast-iron streetlamps, and perimeter boundary railings.
