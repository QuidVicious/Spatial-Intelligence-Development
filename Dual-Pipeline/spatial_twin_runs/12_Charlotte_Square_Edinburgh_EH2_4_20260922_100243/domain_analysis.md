# Spatial Domain Analysis: 12 Charlotte Square, Edinburgh EH2 4ET, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
Subterranean foundation seated upon Carboniferous sedimentary strata of the Strathclyde Group (sandstone, siltstone, and mudstone formations overlain by dense glacial till). Superstructure masonry composed predominantly of Carboniferous Craigleith Sandstone—a fine-to-medium-grained, quartz-cemented pale buff-to-grey arenite prized for exceptional compressive strength and weathering resistance. Secondary structural walling and rear elevations employ coursed Hailes Sandstone rubble with a higher mica and clay content. Mortar matrices consist of traditional non-hydraulic to feebly hydraulic lime blended with sharp, poorly sorted river sand aggregate. Subterranean urban drainage channels into historical culverts beneath the bedrock interface.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
Neoclassical civic monumental core (St George's Church / West Register House, designed by Robert Reid after Robert Adam) defined by a central colossal tetrastyle Roman Ionic portico recessed between projecting rusticated outer pavilions with arched niches. Above the attic level rises a high circular drum with a peristyle colonnade supporting a prominent ribbed spherical dome and cupola finial. Flanking the monument are unified Class-A listed Georgian residential and institutional terraces (three storeys over rusticated basements and attic tiers). Facades feature strict vertical alignment: channelled rusticated ground floors, smooth ashlar upper tiers, 6-over-6 and 12-pane timber sash-and-case windows, continuous string courses, moulded entablatures, and balustraded parapets. Roofs are hipped and pitched with lead-lined valleys, flat asphalt pavilion crowns, and coped ashlar party-wall chimney stacks bearing cylindrical terracotta cans.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- Facades: Polished Craigleith ashlar sandstone displaying characteristic localized carbon-crust accumulation in sheltered cornices and clean rain-washed buff-grey surfaces on exposed orthogonal faces.
- Dome: Standing-seam copper sheet clad over timber framing, exhibiting intense verdigris / malachite-green basic copper carbonate weathering patina.
- Roofs: Diminishing-course Scottish Ballachulish slate in dark charcoal-grey, dressed lead flashing along hips, ridges, and valley gutters.
- Fenestration: Dark- and white-painted timber sash joinery with clear multi-pane float glass.
- Ironwork: Wrought and cast-iron spear-head boundary railings with bitumen/alkyd black enamel coating.
- Ground Plane: Caithness flagstone pedestrian paving slabs, dressed silver-grey granite kerbstones, whinstone gutter channels, and dark asphalt carriageway.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
Peripheral urban specimens (*Acer pseudoplatanus* - Sycamore, and *Tilia × europaea* - Common Lime). Late-June phenological state: dense, fully expanded chlorophyll-saturated foliage forming closed, heavy, convex crown volumes with deep emerald-green palmate and cordate leaf structures; vigorous summer shoot extension with zero defoliation.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All motorized transit vehicles, passenger buses, commercial vans, pedestrians, temporary traffic barriers, road works, and transient refuse storage units are fully excised. Structural curbs, stone paving surfaces, iron railings, boundary walls, and fixed street lamps remain static and plumb.
