# Spatial Domain Analysis: 27 Moray Pl, Edinburgh EH3 6DA, UK

**View Scope:** FRUSTUM

## 1. Geology & Indigenous Lithics
The subterranean terrain rests upon the Lower Carboniferous Inverclyde and Strathclyde Group sedimentary strata (Gullane Formation), characterized by interbedded sandstones, siltstones, and mudstones with localized intrusive volcanic dolerites common to the Edinburgh Basin. Primary above-ground load-bearing masonry is extracted from the historic Craigleith Quarry—an exceptionally dense, quartz-arenite pale buff/grey sandstone bound by siliceous and calcareous cements. Mortar consists of traditional Scottish hydraulic lime and sharp river sand aggregate, deliberately flexible to buffer thermal cycles and subterranean moisture capillary movement along the sloping gorge falling toward the Water of Leith.

## 2. Architecture & Planar Rectification
A grand Georgian / Regency neoclassical dodecagonal circus crescent designed by James Gillespie Graham in 1822. 
- **Planar Rectification & Verticality:** All continuous ashlar elevations are plumbed strictly vertical, rectifying photogrammetry faceting and polygon shearing. Facades form an unbroken, disciplined concave sweep.
- **Storey Heights & Fenestration:** 3 primary storeys over a rusticated/channelled basement floor with a recessed attic storey behind an overarching stone parapet entablature and continuous band course. Rhythmically spaced vertical six-over-six and twelve-over-twelve timber sash-and-case windows set within crisp, sharp rectangular ashlar architraves.
- **Roofline & Chimneystacks:** Pitched Scottish slate roofs interrupted by tall, dressed ashlar chimney stacks with terracotta chimney pots regularly spaced along the party walls, flanked by continuous cast-iron decorative balustrades and dormers.
- **Basement Area Wells:** Sunken lightwells flanked by cast-iron spearhead railings and stone drop drops/steps leading to lower plinths.

## 3. Materials & Environmental Patina
- **Ashlar Facades:** Craigleith fine-grained blonde-to-smoky grey sandstone blocks dressed in smooth ashlar on upper storeys, with horizontal channelled rustication across the ground/basement levels.
- **Environmental Patina & Weathering:** Authentic Scottish maritime-temperate weathering marked by historic carbon deposition (coal soot patina) clinging to sheltered undercrofts, window lintels, and classical cornices; rainwater leaching patterns exposing lighter natural buff quartz hues on exposed faces; delicate green lichen colonies and micro-algae hugging the shadowed lower ashlar basements and area-well retaining walls.
- **Roofing & Ironwork:** Deep slate-blue/charcoal Scottish Ballachulish roofing slates with lead flashing valleys; wrought and cast iron railings coated in oxidized semi-matte black lead paint with micro-rusting at anchor points.

## 4. Landscape Ecology & Botanical Canopy
The foreground and central interior circle feature the protected private Moray Place Gardens:
- Mature, full-canopied deciduous trees including European Beech (*Fagus sylvatica*), Wych Elm (*Ulmus glabra*), Sycamore (*Acer pseudoplatanus*), Horse Chestnut (*Aesculus hippocastanum*), and English Oak (*Quercus robur*).
- Understory dominated by evergreen Holly (*Ilex aquifolium*), *Skimmia japonica*, dense rhododendron thickets, and manicured native Scottish turf grass. Foliage is strictly disambiguated from solid geometry, rendered as dense organic leaf clusters, multi-layered branching, and dappled sunlit green tones.

## 5. Static Civil Fabric Decluttering
All transient visual clutter—including parked modern cars, moving motor traffic, pedestrians, waste bins, temporary road signage, and photogrammetric surface artefacts—has been excised. Fixed civil infrastructure remains preserved: dressed Craigleith sandstone kerbstones, whinstone/granite street gutters, cast-iron streetlamps, perimeter spearhead railings, and perimeter ashlar boundary walls.
